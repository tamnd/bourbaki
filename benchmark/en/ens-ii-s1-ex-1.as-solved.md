Assume first that \(x = y\). Let \(X\) be an arbitrary set. If \(x \in X\), then by the substitutivity of equality in an equalitarian theory (Chapter I, § 2, no. 4) we obtain \(y \in X\). Hence \((x \in X) \Rightarrow (y \in X)\) holds for every \(X\), so \((\forall X)((x \in X) \Rightarrow (y \in X))\) is true.

Conversely, assume \((\forall X)((x \in X) \Rightarrow (y \in X))\). By Axiom A2 the relation \(\mathrm{Coll}_z(z = x \text{ or } z = x)\) is a theorem; therefore, by Definition 2 (03H6), the set \(\{x\}\) exists and the relation \(z \in \{x\}\) is equivalent to \(z = x\). In particular, \(x \in \{x\}\) because \(x = x\) is true. Applying the hypothesis with \(X = \{x\}\) yields \(y \in \{x\}\), and the equivalence \(z \in \{x\} \Leftrightarrow z = x\) gives \(y = x\), i.e. \(x = y\).

Thus the equivalence \((x = y) \Leftrightarrow (\forall X)((x \in X) \Rightarrow (y \in X))\) is a theorem.
