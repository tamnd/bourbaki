**(a)** Let \( E/S \) denote the quotient set of an equivalence relation \( S \) on a preordered set \( E \). The relation \( R(X,Y) \) on \( E/S \) is defined by requiring that for each \( x \in X \in E/S \), there exists \( y \in Y \in E/S \) such that \( x \leqslant y \). To verify \( R \) is a preorder relation:

**Reflexivity**: For any \( X \in E/S \), take \( x \in X \). Since \( x \leqslant x \), there exists \( y = x \in X \) such that \( x \leqslant y \). Thus, \( R(X,X) \).

**Transitivity**: Suppose \( R(X,Y) \) and \( R(Y,Z) \). For each \( x \in X \), there exists \( y \in Y \) with \( x \leqslant y \), and for each such \( y \), there exists \( z \in Z \) with \( y \leqslant z \). Hence \( x \leqslant z \) for each \( x \in X \), so \( R(X,Z) \). Thus, \( R \) is a preorder. This \( R \) is the *quotient* of \( E \) by \( S \).

**(b)** Let \( \varphi: E \to E/S \) be the canonical map. Suppose \( g : E/S \to F \) is a mapping such that \( g \circ \varphi \) is increasing. For \( X \leqslant Y \in E/S \) (i.e., \( R(X,Y) \)), each \( x \in X \) satisfies \( x \leqslant y \) for some \( y \in Y \). Since \( g \circ \varphi \) is increasing, \( \varphi(x) \leqslant \varphi(y) \). But \( \varphi(x) = X \) and \( \varphi(y) = Y \), so \( g(X) \leqslant g(Y) \). Thus, \( g \) is increasing.

We now prove \( \varphi \) is increasing if and only if \( S \) satisfies condition (C).

\( (\Rightarrow) \) Suppose \( \varphi \) is increasing and assume \( x \leqslant y \) and \( x \equiv x' \pmod{S} \). Then \( \varphi(x) = \varphi(x') \), and \( \varphi(x) \leqslant \varphi(y) \) since \( \varphi \) is increasing. By the definition of \( R \), the relation \( R(\varphi(x), \varphi(y)) = R([x],[y]) \) means: for every \( x' \in [x] \), there exists \( y' \in [y] \) such that \( x' \leqslant y' \). Here \( x' \in [x] = [x'] \), so there exists \( y' \in [y] \) with \( x' \leqslant y' \). This is exactly condition (C).

\( (\Leftarrow) \) Suppose (C) holds. To show \( \varphi \) is increasing, assume \( x \leqslant y \) in \( E \). We must show \( R([x],[y]) \), i.e., for each \( x' \in [x] \), there exists \( y' \in [y] \) with \( x' \leqslant y' \). Given \( x' \in [x] \), we have \( x \equiv x' \pmod{S} \). Applying (C) to \( x \leqslant y \) and \( x \equiv x' \pmod{S} \), there exists \( y' \equiv y \pmod{S} \) (so \( y' \in [y] \)) such that \( x' \leqslant y' \). Hence \( \varphi \) is increasing.

Therefore, \( \varphi \) is increasing if and only if \( S \) is weakly compatible.

**(c)** Consider \( E_1 \times E_2 \) with order \( z \leqslant t \) component-wise. Let \( S_1 \) group elements by their first component. For \( z \equiv z' \pmod{S_1} \) (same first component) and \( z \leqslant t \), condition (C) requires a \( t' \equiv t \pmod{S_1} \) (same first component as \( t \)) such that \( z' \leqslant t' \). Since \( z' \) shares \( z \)'s first component and \( t' \) shares \( t \)'s, \( z'_1 = z_1 \leqslant t_1 = t'_1 \), so \( z' \leqslant t' \) holds. Thus, \( S_1 \) is weakly compatible. The projection \( \mathrm{pr}_1 \) factors through \( \varphi_1 \), and \( f_1 \) maps \( \varphi_1(z) \mapsto \mathrm{pr}_1(z) \), yielding an isomorphism with \( E_1 \).

**(d)** Assume \( E \) is ordered and (C') holds. For \( R(X,Y) \) and \( R(Y,X) \) in \( E/S \), each \( x \in X \) has \( y \in Y \) with \( x \leqslant y \), and each \( y \in Y \) has \( x' \in X \) with \( y \leqslant x' \). Then \( x \leqslant y \leqslant x' \). Since \( x \equiv x' \pmod{S} \), (C') implies \( x \equiv y \pmod{S} \), hence \( X = Y \). Thus, \( R \) is antisymmetric, forming an order.

**(e)** Let \( E = \{a, b, c, d\} \) ordered \( a < b < c < d \), with \( S \) defined as \( \{a, c\}, \{b, d\} \). In \( E/S \), classes \([a,c]\) and \([b,d]\) are ordered by \([a,c] \leqslant [b,d]\), making \( E/S \) a poset. Condition (C) fails for \( x = b \equiv x' = d \), \( y = c \), since no \( y' \equiv y \) exists with \( d \leqslant y' \). Condition (C') fails for \( x = a, y = b, z = c \), as \( a \equiv c \pmod{S} \) but \( a \not\equiv b \). Yet \( E/S \) is ordered.

**(f)** For \( f \) increasing, (C') holds since \( f(x) \leqslant f(y) \leqslant f(z) \) and \( f(x) = f(z) \) implies \( f(y) = f(x) \). Condition (C) requires for \( x \equiv x' \pmod{S} \), \( x \leqslant y \), finding \( y' \equiv y \pmod{S} \) with \( x' \leqslant y' \), aligning with the problem statement. The canonical decomposition \( f = g \circ \varphi \) has \( g \) injective and increasing. For \( g \) to be an iso onto \( f(E) \), the converse must hold via the given order conditions.
