Let E be an infinite dimensional hilbertian space, and let $ p, q $ be two integers $ \geq 1 $; let $ p' = \left[ \frac{p}{2} \right], q' = \left[ \frac{q}{2} \right] $ (integral parts). Show that the norm of the bilinear mapping $ (u, v) \mapsto u \wedge v $ from $ \hat{\Lambda}^p(E) \times \hat{\Lambda}^q(E) $ into $ \hat{\Lambda}^{p+q}(E) $ is at least equal to $ \left( \frac{(p'+q')!}{p'! \ q'!} \right)^{1/2} $. (When $ p = 2p' $ and $ q = 2q' $ are even, consider a 2n-dimensional subspace $ E_n $ in E, with an orthonormal basis $ (e_j)_{1 \leq j \leq 2n} $; let $ e'_j = e_{2j-1} \wedge e_{2j} $ for $ 1 \leq j \leq n $; consider the product $ u \wedge v $, where $ u = \sum_H e'_H $, $ v = \sum_{\mathbf{K}} e'_\mathbf{K} $ where $ \mathbf{H} $ (resp. $ \mathbf{K} $) range over the set of all subsets of $ p' $ (resp. $ q' $) elements of $ \{1, 2, ..., n\} $
and $ e'_\mathbf{H} = e'_{i_1} \wedge ... \wedge e'_{i_p} $ (resp. $ e'_\mathbf{K} = e'_{j_1} \wedge ... \wedge e'_{j_q} $) if $ i_1 < ... < i_p $ (resp. $ j_1 < ... < j_q $) is the increasing sequence of elements of $ \mathbf{H} $ (resp. $ \mathbf{K} $). Deduce that the multiplication in $ \Lambda(\mathbf{E}) $ cannot be extended to $ \hat{\Lambda}(\mathbf{E}) $ by continuity.)

§ 4

1) Let E and F be two infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces satisfying the first axiom of countability, $ (a_n) $ an orthonormal basis of E, and $ (b_n) $ an orthonormal basis of F.
a) Let $ u $ be a continuous linear mapping from E into F; let $ u(a_n) = \sum_m \alpha_{mn} b_m $. Show that
$$ \sum_n |\alpha_{mn}|^2 \leq \|u\|^2 \text{ and } \sum_m |\alpha_{mn}|^2 \leq \|u\|^2 \text{ for every } m \text{ and } n. $$
b) Give an example of a double sequence $ (\alpha_{mn}) $ such that $ \sum_m |\alpha_{mn}|^2 \leq 1 $ for all $ n $ and $ \sum_n |\alpha_{mn}|^2 \leq 1 $ for all $ m $, but such that there does not exist any continuous linear mapping $ u $ from E into F such that $ \langle u(a_n)|b_m \rangle = \alpha_{mn} $ for every pair of integers $ (m, n) $. (Show that if $ I \subset \mathbf{N} $ is a set of $ p $ integers, and if $ V_p $ (resp. $ W_p $) is the subspace of E (resp. F) generated by the $ a_n $ (resp. $ b_n $) such that $ n \in I $, then there exists a linear mapping $ u_p $ from $ V_p $ onto $ W_p $ such that $ \langle u_p(a_n)|b_m \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{p}} $ for $ m \in I $ and $ n \in I $, and that $ \|u_p\| \geq \sqrt{p} $.)

¶ 2) Let $ A = (\alpha_{mn})_{(m,n) \in \mathbf{N} \times \mathbf{N}} $ be a double sequence of complex numbers, which we also call an infinite matrix. For every point $ x = (x_n) $ of the direct sum space $ \mathbf{C}^{(\mathbf{N})} $, the sums $ y_m = \sum_n \alpha_{mn} x_n $ are defined; let $ A.x $ be the point $ (y_m) $ of the product space $ \mathbf{C}^\mathbf{N} $, then $ x \mapsto A.x $ is a linear mapping from $ \mathbf{C}^{(\mathbf{N})} $ into $ \mathbf{C}^\mathbf{N} $, and every linear mapping from $ \mathbf{C}^{(\mathbf{N})} $ into $ \mathbf{C}^\mathbf{N} $ is of this form. Let $ E_n $ denote the subspace of $ \mathbf{C}^{(\mathbf{N})} $ generated by the first $ n $ vectors of the canonical basis, $ P_n $ the canonical projection from $ \mathbf{C}^\mathbf{N} $ onto $ E_n $; when $ E_n $ is assigned the norm induced by that of the space $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $, $ \|u\| $ denotes the norm of the linear mapping $ u $ from the finite dimensional hilbertian space $ E_n $ into itself.
a) In order that the image of $ \mathbf{C}^{(\mathbf{N})} $ under the mapping $ x \mapsto A.x $ be contained in $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ and that this mapping extend to a continuous linear mapping from $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ into itself, it is necessary and sufficient that the norms $ \|P_n A P_n\| $ are bounded. This implies that the rows and the columns of $ A $ belong to $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ (exerc. 1).
b) Let $ A^* $ denote the infinite matrix $ (\alpha'_{mn}) $, where $ \alpha'_{mn} = \overline{\alpha}_{nm} $. If the columns of $ A $ belong to $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ (in other words, if $ x \mapsto A.x $ maps $ \mathbf{C}^{(\mathbf{N})} $ into $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $), then the series $ \beta_{mn} = \sum_p \overline{\alpha}_{pm} \alpha_{pn} $ are absolutely convergent, and we put $ A^*A = (\beta_{mn}) $. Show that for $ x \mapsto A.x $ to extend to a continuous linear mapping $ u $ from $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ into itself, it is necessary and sufficient that the norms $ \|P_n (A^*A) P_n\| $ are bounded (we have $ \langle P_n (A^*A) P_n.x|x \rangle = \|AP_n.x\|^2 $ for all $ x \in E_n $). Then $ x \mapsto A^*A.x $ extends to a positive hermitian mapping $ u^*u $ from $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ into itself.
c) For two infinite matrices $ X = (\xi_{mn}), Y = (\eta_{mn}) $, we say that the product $ XY $ is defined if the series $ \zeta_{mn} = \sum_p \xi_{mp} \eta_{pn} $ are absolutely convergent, and then we put $ XY = (\zeta_{mn}) $. We say that a power $ X^k $ ($ k $ integer > 1) is defined if $ X^{k-1} $ and $ X^{k-1}X $ are defined and then we put $ X^k = X^{k-1}X $; in this case, $ X^p X^q = X^k $ for every pair of integers $ p, q $ such that $ p + q = k $.
If $ A $ is an infinite matrix whose columns are in $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ and if the product $ (A^*A)^2 $ is defined, show that for all $ x \in E_n $, we have $ \langle (P_n A^*A P_n)^2.x|x \rangle \leq \langle P_n (A^*A)^2 P_n.x|x \rangle $, and deduce that $ \|P_n A^*A P_n\|^2 \leq \|P_n (A^*A)^2 P_n\| $.
d) In order that an infinite matrix A be such that the image of $ \mathbf{C}^{(\mathbf{N})} $ under $ x \mapsto A.x $ is contained in $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ and that $ x \mapsto A.x $ extends to a continuous linear mapping from $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $ into itself, it is necessary and sufficient that the following three conditions are satisfied:
(i) the rows and columns of $ A $ are in $ \ell_2^\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{N}) $;

(ii) the powers $(A^*A)^k$ are defined for every integer $k > 1$;
(iii) we have
$$
\sup_n (\sup_m |((A^*A)^n_{mm})^{1/n}|) < +\infty
$$
where $(A^*A)^n_{mm}$ denotes the term with indices $(m, m)$ of the matrix $(A^*A)^n$. (Observe that if $C$ is the matrix, with respect to the canonical basis of $E_n$, of a positive hermitian endomorphism of $E_n$, then $\|C\| \leq n \sup_{1 \leq i \leq n} |C_{ii}|$, by considering the trace of $C$ and by diagonalizing $C$. Using the inequality proved in c), show that
$$
\|P_n A^* A P_n\| \leq n^{2-k} \sup_{1 \leq i \leq n} |((A^*A)^{2k}_{ii})|^{2-k}
$$
for every integer $k > 1$, if conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) are satisfied.)
