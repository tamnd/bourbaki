Let $E$ be a complex hilbertian space of finite dimension $d$.
a) For every $u \geq 0$ in $\mathscr{L}(E)$ (V, p. 45), prove that there exists a unique $v \geq 0$ in $\mathscr{L}(E)$ such that $u = v^2$ (diagonalize $u$); we write $v = u^{1/2}$.

b) For every $ u \in \mathcal{L}(E) $, put $ \operatorname{abs}(u) = (u^*u)^{1/2} $. Show that $ u $ and $ \operatorname{abs}(u) $ have the same norm and that we have $ \operatorname{abs}(\Lambda^n(u)) = \Lambda^n(\operatorname{abs}(u)) $ for every integer $ n \leq d $.
c) Let $ s_1(u) \geq s_2(u) \geq \ldots \geq s_d(u) \geq 0 $ be the sequence of eigenvalues of $ \operatorname{abs}(u) $ counted with their order of multiplicity. Show that $ \|u\| = s_1(u) $ and that for every integer $ n \leq d $, $ \|\Lambda^n(u)\| = s_1(u) s_2(u) \ldots s_n(u) $. In order that $ \|\Lambda^n(u)\| = \|u\|^n $ for all $ n $ such that $ 1 \leq n \leq d $, it is necessary and sufficient that $ u^*u $ is a homothety, in other words, that $ u $ is a scalar multiple of a unitary operator.
