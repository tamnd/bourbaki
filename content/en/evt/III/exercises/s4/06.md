A locally convex space E is said to be *relatively bounded* if there exists a bounded barrel in E.
a) In order that E be relatively bounded, it is necessary and sufficient that the topology of E is coarser than a topology defined by a semi-norm. Then there exists a base for the canonical bornology of E consisting of barrels.
b) For E to be bornological and relatively bounded, it is necessary and sufficient that the topology of E is the lower bound of a family of normed space topologies on E (*cf.* III, p. 40, exerc. 1). Further, in order that there exist also a countable base for the canonical bornology of E, it is necessary and sufficient that the topology of E is the lower bound of a sequence of normed space topologies.
