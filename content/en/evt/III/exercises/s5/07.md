Let E, F and G be three topological vector spaces, $ \mathcal{S} $ a set of bounded subsets of E, and H a set of separately continuous bilinear mappings from $ E \times F $ into G. The following properties are equivalent :
$ \alpha) $ For every neighbourhood W of 0 in G and every set $ M \in \mathcal{S} $, there exists a neighbourhood V of 0 in F such that $ u(M \times V) \subset W $ for all $ u \in H $.
$ \beta) $ For every set $ M \in \mathcal{S} $, the image of $ H \times M $ under the mapping $ (u, x) \mapsto u(x, .) $ is an equicontinuous subset of $ \mathcal{L}(F; G) $.
$ \gamma) $ As $ u $ runs through H, the set of mappings $ y \mapsto u(., y) $ from F into $ \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{S}}(E; G) $ is equicontinuous.
We then say that H is a $ \mathcal{S} $-equihypocontinuous set of bilinear mappings (separately continuous) from $ E \times F $ into G. Similarly for a set $ \mathfrak{T} $ of bounded subsets of F, we define the notions of a $ \mathfrak{T} $-equihypocontinuous set and a $ (\mathcal{S}, \mathfrak{T}) $-equihypocontinuous set.
