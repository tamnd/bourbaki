Let $ A $ be a closed set in $ \mathbf{R}^n $ with the following properties; for every $ x \in \mathbf{R}^n $, there exists one and only one point $ y \in A $ such that $ d(x, y) = d(x, A) $, where $ d $ is the euclidean distance. Show that $ A $ is convex. (Argue by reductio ad absurdum, considering a closed segment with end points $ a, b $ in $ A $ containing a point $ c \in \mathcal{C} A $; there is a closed ball $ B $ of centre $ c $ contained in $ \mathcal{C} A $; consider the set $ \mathcal{B} $ of closed balls $ S $ which contain $ B $ and whose interiors do not meet $ A $; show that the radii of these balls is bounded above, and deduce that there exists one of these balls $ S_0 $ whose radius $ \rho $ is the largest possible. Then get a contradiction by proving that $ S_0 $ can only meet $ A $ in a single point, and that this implies the existence in $ \mathcal{B} $ of a ball of radius $ > \rho $.)
