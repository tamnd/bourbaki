Let $ x_0 $ be a point in the closure of A, a non-empty convex subset of a Hausdorff topological vector space E. Let $ f $ be a convex function defined over A. Use $ \mathcal{D} $ to denote the set of closed half lines D which originate at $ x_0 $, for which $ A \cap D $ contains an open segment with end point $ x_0 $. The union C of the half lines $ D \in \mathcal{D} $ is a convex cone with vertex $ x_0 $.
a) Show that, for each fixed $ D \in \mathcal{D} $, as x tends to $ x_0 $ such that $ x \in D \cap A $ and $ x \neq x_0 $, either $ f(x) $ tends to a finite limit or to $ +\infty $.
b) Let $ \mathcal{J} $ be the subset of those $ D \in \mathcal{D} $, for which the limit of $ f(x) $ in a) is $ +\infty $; if $ x_0 \in A $ then $ \mathcal{J} $ is empty. Show that $ \mathcal{J} $ cannot contain two opposite half lines; if D and $ D' $ are two distinct half lines in $ \mathcal{J} $ and P is the plane determined by D and $ D' $ then, either, every half line $ D'' $ of $ \mathcal{D} $ in P belongs to $ \mathcal{J} $, or D and $ D' $ are the only two half lines of $ \mathcal{J} $ lying in P. Deduce that if $ \mathcal{J} \neq \mathcal{D} $, then no half line $ D \in \mathcal{J} $ contains an internal point (II, p. 26) of the cone C relative to the vector subspace generated by C.
c) Let $ \mathcal{F} $ be the set of half lines in $ \mathcal{D} $ that are not in $ \mathcal{J} $. Show that the union of the half lines of $ \mathcal{F} $ is a convex cone, and for each half line $ D \in \mathcal{F} $ the limit of $ f(x) $ defined in a) is independent of D (use exerc. 29 above); further if $ x_0 \in A $ this limit is $ \leq f(x_0) $, and it is equal to $ f(x_0) $ when $ \mathcal{F} $ contains two opposite half lines.
d) Let $ f $ be a non-continuous linear form over E ($ cf. $ II, p. 86, exerc. 17, a)) and take $ A = E $; show that every closed half line, originating at $ x_0 $, belongs to $ \mathcal{F} $, but that
$$
\liminf_{x \to x_0} f(x) = -\infty \quad \text{and} \quad \limsup_{x \to x_0} f(x) = +\infty
$$
(use prop. 21 of II, p. 18).
