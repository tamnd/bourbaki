a) Let $(a_k)_{1 \leq k \leq n}$ be a finite sequence of points in a normed space $E$, and let $(\lambda_k)_{1 \leq k \leq n}$ be a finite sequence of numbers $> 0$ such that $\sum_{k=1}^n \lambda_k < 1$; put $\mu_k = 1 - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \lambda_j$ for every $k$; then
$$
\left\| \sum_{k=1}^n \lambda_k a_k \right\| \leq \frac{\lambda_n}{\mu_{n-1}} \| \mu_{n-1} a_n + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k a_k \| + \frac{\mu_n}{\mu_{n-1}} \left\| \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k a_k \right\|.
$$
b) Let $(a_n)$ be an infinite sequence of points in the unit ball of $E$, and let $(\lambda_n)$ be an infinite sequence of numbers $> 0$ such that $\sum \lambda_n = 1$. For every $n > 0$, put $\mu_n = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k$ and $b_n = \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k a_k + \mu_n a_n$; show that, for all $n \geq 1$, we have
$$
\left\| \sum_{k=1}^n \lambda_k a_k \right\| \leq \mu_n \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{\lambda_k \| b_k \|}{\mu_{k-1} \mu_k}.
$$
(Apply a) by induction.)
c) Let $(C_n)$ be a decreasing sequence of convex sets in $E$, contained in the unit ball, and suppose that $d(0, C_1) \geq \theta > 0$ (hence, *a fortiori* $d(0, C_n) \geq \theta$ for all $n$). Let $(\lambda_n)$ be a sequence of numbers $> 0$ such that $\sum \lambda_n = 1$. Show that there exists a number $\alpha$ such that $\theta \leq \alpha \leq 1$ and a sequence $(x_n)$ of points of $E$ such that $x_n \in C_n$ for all $n$, $\|\sum_n \lambda_n x_n\| = \alpha$ and, for all $n$

$$
\left\| \sum_{k=1}^n \lambda_k x_k \right\| \leq \alpha (1 - \theta \sum_{j=n+1}^\infty \lambda_j).
$$

(Take $x_1$ such that $\|x_1\|$ is arbitrarily close to $d(0, C_1)$, then, by induction, take $x_n$ such that $\left\| \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k x_k + (\sum_{j=n}^\infty \lambda_j) x_n \right\|$ is arbitrarily close to the infimum of the numbers $\left\| \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \lambda_k x_k + (\sum_{j=n}^\infty \lambda_j) y \right\|$ where $y$ ranges over $C_n$. Then use b).)

**T 25**) Let $E$ be a Banach space satisfying the first axiom of countability. Show that the following properties are equivalent :
a) $E$ is not reflexive.
b) For every number $\theta$ such that $0 < \theta < 1$, there exists a sequence $(x'_n)$ in $E'$ such that, $\|x'_n\| \leq 1$ for all $n$, that the sequence $(x'_n)$ converges to 0 for $\sigma(E', E)$ and that the distance of 0 from the convex set generated by the $x'_n$ is $\geq \theta$.
c) For every number $\theta$ such that $0 < \theta < 1$ and every sequence $\lambda_n$ of numbers $> 0$ such that $\sum_n \lambda_n = 1$, there exists a number $\alpha$ such that $\theta \leq \alpha \leq 1$ and a sequence $(y'_n)$ of points of $E'$ such that $\|y'_n\| \leq 1$ for all $n$, $\|\sum_n \lambda_n y'_n\| = \alpha$ and $\left\| \sum_{k=1}^n \lambda_k y'_k \right\| \leq \alpha (1 - \theta \sum_{j=n+1}^\infty \lambda_j)$ for all $n$.
d) There exists $z' \in E'$ such that for no $x \in E$ do we have $|\langle x, z' \rangle| = \|x\| \cdot \|z'\|$ (*Theorem of James-Klee*).
(To see that a) implies b), observe that there exists $z'' \in E''$ such that $\|z''\| < 1$ and $d(z'', E) > \theta$. If $(x_n)$ is an everywhere dense sequence in $E$, find the sequence $(x'_n)$ in $E'$ such that $\|x'_n\| < 1$ and such that $\langle x_k, x'_n \rangle = 0$ for $k \leq n$ and $\langle x'_n, z'' \rangle = \theta$. To see that b) implies c), use exerc. 24. For c) implies d), show that for all $x \in E$ we have $\left| \sum_n \lambda_n \langle x, y'_n \rangle \right| < \alpha$, with the notations of c).

26) A locally convex space $E$ is said to have the property (GDF) if every linear mapping $u$ from $E$ into a Banach space $F$ which satisfies the following property, is continuous : in the product space $E \times F$, every limit of a convergent sequence of points in the graph $\Gamma$ of $u$ again belongs to $\Gamma$. Every Fréchet space has the property (GDF) (I, p. 19, cor. 5); this is also true of every inductive limit of a family of Fréchet spaces (II, p. 34, prop. 10). Show that every Hausdorff locally convex space with the (GDF) property is barrelled. (Let $V$ be a barrel in $E$, $q$ its gauge and $H$ the Hausdorff space associated with $E$ endowed with this semi-norm; show that the canonical mapping $\pi$ from $E$ into the completion $\hat{H}$ is continuous by using the property (GDF) and the fact that every linear form $x' \in V^0$ can be extended uniquely to a continuous linear form on $\hat{H}$, the set of these forms being the unit ball in the dual of $\hat{H}$.)
