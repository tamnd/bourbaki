*Let $ n $ be an integer $ \geq 1 $, $ q $ a power of a prime number, $ K = \mathbf{F}_q $, $ V = K^n $, $ G = \mathbf{GL}(n, K) $ and $ G_1 = \mathbf{SL}(n, K) $. Identify $ G $ with the group $ \mathbf{GL}(V) $. Further, denote by $ S $ the algebra $ S(V) = K[X_1, \ldots, X_n] $ and $ R $ (resp. $ R_1 $) the subalgebra of $ S $ consisting of the elements invariant under $ G $ (resp. $ G_1 $).

a) Let $ e = (e_1, \ldots, e_n) $ be a sequence of integers $ \geq 0 $. Put
$$
L_e = \det(X_i^{q^{e_j}}).
$$
This is an element of S.
Show that
$$
g.L_e = \det(g)L_e \quad \text{for all } g \in G.
$$
(Remark that, if $ g.X_i = \sum_j a_{ij} X_j $, then $ g.X_i^{q^e} = \sum_j a_{ij} X_j^{q^{e_j}} $.) In particular, $ L_e $ belongs to $ R_1 $.

b) Let $ j \in [1, n] $. Put
$$
Z_j = \prod_{(a_{ij})} (X_j + \sum_{i > j} a_{ij} X_i),
$$
the product being over all families $ (a_{ij})_{j < i \leq n} $ of elements of K. Let
$$
T = \prod_{j=1}^n Z_j.
$$
Show that T divides all the $ L_e $. Deduce that $ T = L_{e_n} $, where $ e_n = (0, 1, \ldots, n-1) $, and that T is invariant under $ G_1 $.

c) Denote by $ e_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq n-1 $) the sequence $ (0, 1, \ldots, i-1, i+1, \ldots, n) $, and put $ Y_i = L_{e_i}/T $, cf. b). Show that the $ Y_i $ belong to R and that $ \deg(Y_i) = q^n - q^i $.

d) Let $ S' = K[X_1, \ldots, X_{n-1}] $, and let $ T', Y'_1, \ldots, Y'_{n-2} $ be the elements of $ S' $ defined in the same way as T, $ Y_1, \ldots, Y_{n-1} $ (but replacing n by $ n-1 $). Let $ f : S \to S' $ be the homomorphism defined by
$$
f(X_i) = X_i \quad (1 \leq i \leq n-1), \quad f(X_n) = 0.
$$
Show that
$$
f(T) = 0, \quad f(Y_1) = T'^{q(q-1)}, \quad f(Y_i) = Y'_{i-1}^q \quad \text{for } 2 \leq i \leq n-1.
$$

e) Show that the family $ (T, Y_1, \ldots, Y_{n-1}) $ satisfies condition (iv) of Exerc. 5. (Let $ x = (x_1, \ldots, x_n) $ be a zero of the system $ (T, Y_1, \ldots, Y_{n-1}) $ in an extension $ \overline{K} $ of K. Since $ x $ is a zero of T, the $ x_i $ satisfy at least one non-trivial linear relation with coefficients in K. Transforming $ x $ by an element of $ G_1 $ if necessary, we can assume that $ x_n = 0 $. Conclude by applying d) and arguing by induction on $ n $.)

f) Show that $ R_1 = K[T, Y_1, \ldots, Y_{n-1}] $, and that $ T, Y_1, \ldots, Y_{n-1} $ are algebraically independent ("Dickson's Theorem" – apply e) and Exerc. 5, remarking that the order of $ G_1 $ is equal to $ \deg(T) = \sum_i \deg(Y_i) $).

g) Show that $ R = K[T^{q-1}, Y_1, \ldots, Y_{n-1}] $*
