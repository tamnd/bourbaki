Let $ g $ be a Lie algebra, $ r $ its radical and $ D $ a derivation of $ g $.
(a) If $ D(r) = \{0\} $, $ D $ is inner. (Let $ c $ be the centralizer of $ r $ in $ g $. The adjoint representation of $ g $ defines a representation $ x^* \mapsto \rho(x^*) $ of $ g^* = g/r $ on $ c $. On the other hand, $[D(g), r] \subset D([g, r]) + [g, D(r)] = \{0\}$, hence $ D(g) \subset c $ and hence $ D $ defines a linear mapping $ D^*: g^* \to c $. Show that
$$
D^*([x^*, y^*]) = \rho(x^*)D*y^* - \rho(y^*)D*x^*
$$
for all $ x^*, y^* $ in $ g^* $. Deduce that there exists $ a \in c $ such that $ D^*x^* = \rho(x^*)a $ for all $ x^* \in g^* $ (cf. no. 2, *Remark* 2), whence $ Dx = [x, a] $ for all $ x \in g $.)
(b) If $ D $ coincides on $ r $ with an inner derivation of $ g $, $ D $ is inner. (Apply (a).)
