Let $ G $ be a group and let $ (H_n)_{n \geq 1} $ be a decreasing sequence of subgroups of $ G $; suppose that $ H_1 = G $ and that if we write $ (x, y) = xyx^{-1}y^{-1} $, the relations $ x \in H_i,\ y \in H_j $ imply $ (x, y) \in H_{i+j} $.

(a) Let $ G_i = H_i/H_{i+1} $; show that $ G_i $ is commutative and that the mapping $ x, y \mapsto (x, y) $ defines on passing to the quotient a $ \mathbf{Z} $-bilinear mapping of $ G_i \times G_j $ into $ G_{i+j} $.

(b) Let $ \operatorname{gr}(G) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty G_i $ and extend by linearity the mappings $ G_i \times G_j \to G_{i+j} $ defined in (a) to a $ \mathbf{Z} $-bilinear mapping of $ \operatorname{gr}(G) \times \operatorname{gr}(G) $ into $ \operatorname{gr}(G) $. Show that $ \operatorname{gr}(G) $ is a Lie $ \mathbf{Z} $-algebra with this mapping (to verify the Jacobi identity, use the following formula:

$$
((x, y), z^y) . ((y, z), x^z) . ((z, x), y^x) = e \qquad x, y, z \text{ in } G,
$$

where $ x^y $ denotes $ yxy^{-1} $ and $ e $ denotes the identity element of $ G $.

(c) Suppose that there exists $ n $ such that $ H_n = \{e\} $. Show that $ \operatorname{gr}(G) $ is a nilpotent Lie $ \mathbf{Z} $-algebra.
