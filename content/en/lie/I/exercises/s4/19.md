(a) Let g be a Lie algebra. Let $ \mathfrak{D} $ be the Lie algebra of derivations of g. Characteristic ideals $ g^{[k]} $ of g are defined inductively as follows: $ g^{[0]} = g $ and $ g^{[k+1]} $ is the subspace of g generated by the Dx ($ D \in \mathfrak{D}, x \in g^{[k]} $). The following conditions are equivalent: (1) every derivation of g is nilpotent; (2) $ g^{[k]} = \{0\} $ for k sufficiently large; (3) the holomorph of g is nilpotent. If they are fulfilled, g is said to be *characteristically nilpotent*. Such an algebra is nilpotent.

(b) Verify that an 8-dimensional Lie algebra is defined by the following multiplication table:

$$
[x_1, x_2] = x_3,\quad [x_1, x_3] = x_4,\quad [x_1, x_4] = x_5,\quad [x_1, x_5] = x_6 \\
[x_1, x_6] = x_8,\quad [x_1, x_7] = x_8,\quad [x_2, x_3] = x_5,\quad [x_2, x_4] = x_6 \\
[x_2, x_5] = x_7,\quad [x_2, x_6] = 2x_8,\quad [x_3, x_4] = -x_7 + x_8,\quad [x_3, x_5] = -x_8
$$

and $[x_i, x_j] = 0$ for $i + j > 8$. Show that

$$
\mathcal{C}^2g = \sum_{i=3}^8 Kx_i,\quad \mathcal{C}^3g = \sum_{i=4}^8 Kx_i,\quad \mathcal{C}^4g = \sum_{i=5}^8 Kx_i,\quad \mathcal{C}^5g = \sum_{i=6}^8 Kx_i,
$$
$$
\mathcal{C}^6g = Kx_8,\quad \mathcal{C}^7g = \{0\},\quad [\mathcal{C}^2g, \mathcal{C}^2g] = Kx_7 + Kx_8,
$$

and that the transporter of $\mathcal{C}^2g$ into $\mathcal{C}^4g$ is $\sum_{i=2}^8 Kx_i$. Deduce that every derivation D of g is defined by formulae $Dx_i = \sum_{j=1}^8 u_{ij}x_j$. Then show that $u_{ii} = 0$ for all i if the characteristic of K differs from 2, so that g is characteristically nilpotent.

(c) If the characteristic of K is $\neq 2$, show that the Lie algebra g of (b) is not the derived Lie algebra of any Lie algebra. (If $g = \mathscr{D}\mathfrak{h}$, show first that $\mathfrak{h}$ is nilpotent. Observe that $\dim g/\mathscr{D}g = 2$, which with Exercise 7 (c) leads to a contradiction.)
