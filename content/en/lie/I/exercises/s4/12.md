Let g be a Lie algebra and $ \mathcal{C}^\infty g $ the intersection of the $ \mathcal{C}^g g $.
(a) The Lie algebra $ g / \mathcal{C}^\infty g $ is nilpotent.
(b) Show that there exists a nilpotent subalgebra $ h $ of $ g $ such that $ g = h + \mathcal{C}^\infty g $. (Argue by induction on $ \dim g $. Suppose that $ g $ is not nilpotent. Let $ x \in g $ be such that $ I = \bigcap_k (\mathrm{ad}\, x)^k(g) \neq \{0\} $. Let $ n $ be the union of the kernels of the $ (\mathrm{ad}\, x)^k $. Show that this is a subalgebra of $ g $ and that $ g $ is the direct sum of I and $ n $. By the induction hypothesis $ n $ is the sum of $ \mathcal{C}^\infty n $ and a nilpotent subalgebra $ h $. Finally, $ \mathcal{C}^\infty n \subset \mathcal{C}^\infty g $ and $ I \subset \mathcal{C}^\infty g $.

(c) Verify that the following multiplication table defines a (solvable) Lie algebra $ g $ of dimension 5:

$$
[x_1, x_2] = x_5, \quad [x_1, x_3] = x_3, \quad [x_1, x_4] = -x_4, \quad [x_3, x_4] = x_5
$$

and the other $[x_i, x_j]$ are zero. Show that for this Lie algebra

$$
\mathcal{C}^\infty g = Kx_3 + Kx_4 + Kx_5
$$

but that there exists no supplementary subalgebra of $\mathcal{C}^\infty g$ in $g$.
