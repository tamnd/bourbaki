Let $ g $ be a Lie algebra, $ a $ a subinvariant subalgebra of $ g $, $ r(a) $ the radical of $ a $ and $ \rho $ a finite-dimensional representation of $ a $. For $ \rho $ to admit a finite-dimensional extension to $ g $, it is necessary and sufficient that $ \mathcal{D}g \cap r(a) $ be contained in the largest nilpotency ideal of $ \rho $. (For the sufficiency proceed as follows; let $ g = g_0 \supset g_1 \supset \cdots \supset g_k = a $ be a decomposition series of $ g $ with the properties of Exercise 11 (b) of § 6. Show by induction the existence of an extension $ \rho_i $ of $ \rho $ to $ g_i $, whose largest nilpotency ideal contains $ \mathcal{D}g \cap r(g_i) $. In the notation of Exercise 11 (b) of § 6, the passage from $ \rho_{i+1} $ to $ \rho_i $ follows from Theorem 1 when $ h_i $ is simple, or when $ h_i $ is 1-dimensional and
$$
\mathcal{D}g \cap r(g_{i+1}) = \mathcal{D}g \cap r(g_i).
$$
When $ \mathcal{D}g \cap r(g_{i+1}) \neq \mathcal{D}g \cap r(g_i) $, it can be assumed that $ h_i \subset \mathcal{D}g \cap r(g_i) $. Then $ r(g_i) = r(g) \cap g_i $ ($ \S 5 $, Exercise 10) and Theorem 1 can be applied.)
