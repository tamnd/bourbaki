Let G be a Lie group, $ \phi : U \to G $ an exponential mapping with the properties of Proposition 3, V a neighbourhood of 0 in U such that $ \mathbf{Z}V \subset V $ and $ \psi : V \to G $ a tangent mapping at 0 to $ \phi $ such that $ \psi(nx) = \psi(x)^n $ for all $ n \in V $ and all $ n \in \mathbf{Z} $. If $ p > 0 $, then $ \psi = \phi | V $. (Give L(G) a norm. Let $ x \in V $. Then $ p^n x $ tends to 0 as n tends to $ +\infty $, hence there exists $ \alpha_n > 0 $ such that $ \alpha_n $ tends to 0 and $ \| (\phi^{-1} \circ \psi)(p^n x) - p^n x \| \leq \alpha_n \| p^n x \| $. But $ \psi(p^n x) = \psi(x)^{p^n} $, whence $ \| (\phi^{-1} \circ \psi)(x) - x \| \leq \alpha_n \| x \| $.)
