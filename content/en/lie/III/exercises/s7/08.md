Let $n$ be an integer $\geqslant 1$. We write
$$
M(n) = \prod_l l^{r(l, n)},
$$
where the product is taken over all prime numbers $l$ and the $r(l, n)$ are defined as in Exercises 6 and 7.
Then $M(1) = 2, M(2) = 2^3 \cdot 3 = 24, M(3) = 2^4 \cdot 3 = 48,$
$$
M(4) = 2^7 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 5760.
$$
Deduce from Exercises 6 and 7 that the l.c.m. of the orders of the finite subgroups of $\mathbf{GL}(n, \mathbf{Q})$ (or $\mathbf{GL}(n, \mathbf{Z})$, which amounts to the same) is equal to $M(n)$. Consider the same question for $\mathbf{SL}(n, \mathbf{Q})$ with $M(n)$ replaced by $\frac{1}{2} M(n)$.
