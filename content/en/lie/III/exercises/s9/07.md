(a) Let G be a semi-simple connected real Lie group, Z its centre and $ \rho $ a continuous linear representation of G on a finite-dimensional complex vector space. There exists an integer $ p $ such that, for all $ z \in Z $, $ \rho(z) $ is diagonalizable and all the eigenvalues of $ \rho(z) $ are $ p $-th roots of unity. (It can be assumed that $ \rho $ is irreducible. Then $ \rho(z) $ is scalar by Schur's Lemma. On the other hand, $ \det \rho(g) = 1 $ for all $ g \in G $ because $ G = \mathcal{D}G $.)
    (b) Deduce that, if G admits an injective finite-dimensional continuous linear representation, Z is finite.
