A real Lie group $G$ is said to be *of type* (N) if it is finite-dimensional,

† For more details, cf. J. Tits, Sur une classe de groupes de Lie résolubles, *Bull. Soc. Math. Belg.*, **11** (1959), pp. 100–115 and **14** (1962), pp. 196–209.

nilpotent, connected and simply connected. If G is such a group and g is its Lie algebra, the mapping exp: g → G is an isomorphism when g is given the group structure defined by the Hausdorff law (cf. Chapter II, § 6, no. 5, Remark 3). Let log: G → g denote the inverse isomorphism.

(a) Let V be a vector Q-subspace of g. Show the equivalence of the following conditions:
    (i) V is a Lie Q-subalgebra of g;
    (ii) exp(V) is a subgroup of G.
(Use Exercise 5 of § 6 of Chapter II.)

For a subgroup H of G to be of the form exp(V), where V is a vector Q-subspace of g, it is necessary and sufficient that H be saturated in G (Chapter II, § 4, Exercise 14), i.e. that the relations x ∈ G, x^n ∈ H, n ≠ 0 imply x ∈ H (loc. cit.). If this is so, show that H is an integral subgroup of G if and only if log(H) is a Lie R-subalgebra of g.

(b) Let V be a Lie Q-subalgebra of g of finite dimension m, let (e_1, ..., e_m) be a basis of V over Q and let Λ be the subgroup of V generated by e_1, ..., e_m. Using the fact that the Hausdorff law is polynomial, show that there exists an integer d ≥ 1 such that, for every integer r which is a non-zero multiple of d, exp(rΛ) is a subgroup of G. Let d be such an integer and r a multiple of d. Show that exp(rΛ) is discrete if and only if (e_1, ..., e_m) is a free family over R, i.e. if the canonical mapping of V ⊗_Q R into g is injective. Suppose that this is the case; show that G/exp(rΛ) is compact if and only if V ⊗_Q R → g is bijective, i.e. if V is a Q-form of g. (To show that the condition is sufficient, argue by induction on the nilpotency class of g.)

(c) Conversely, let Γ be a discrete subgroup of G, let $\overline{\Gamma}$ be its saturation in G (Chapter II, loc. cit.) and let $g_{\Gamma} = \log(\overline{\Gamma}) = Q.\log(\Gamma)$ be the corresponding Lie Q-subalgebra. Suppose that R.g = g, i.e. that Γ is contained in no distinct integral subgroup of G. Let z be an element ≠ 1 of the centre of Γ and let x = log (z). Show that x belongs to the centre of g. If X = exp(Rx), show that X/(Γ ∩ X) is compact and that the image of Γ in G/X is discrete. Deduce, arguing by induction on dim G, that G/Γ is compact and that $g_{\Gamma}$ is a G-form of g. If Λ is a lattice of $g_{\Gamma}$, show that there exists an integer d ≠ 0 such that Γ contains exp(dΛ) and that the index of exp(dΛ) in Γ is then finite.

Let H be an integral subgroup of G with Lie algebra h. Show that H/(H ∩ Γ) is compact if and only if h is rational with respect to the Q-structure $g_{\Gamma}$ (in particular if h is one of the terms of the lower—or upper—central series of g).

(d) Let Γ be a discrete subgroup of G, let H be the smallest integral subgroup of G containing Γ and let h be its Lie algebra. Show that H/Γ is compact and that $g \otimes_Q R \to g$ is injective and has image h. (Apply (c) to the nilpotent group H.)

(e) Let Γ be a discrete subgroup of G. Show that there exists a basis $(x_1, ..., x_q)$ of L(G) with the following properties:
    (i) for all $i \in \{1, q\}$, $Rx_i + \cdots + Rx_q$ is an ideal $n_i$ of $Rx_{i-1} + \cdots + Rx_q$;

(iii) there exists $p \in \{1, q\}$ such that $\Gamma$ is the set of products
$$
\exp(m_p x_p) \exp(m_{p+1} x_{p+1}) \ldots \exp(m_q x_q)
$$
where $m_p, \ldots, m_q$ are in $\mathbf{Z}$;
(iii) if $G/\Gamma$ is compact, $\{n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_q\}$ contains the lower central series of $g$. (Using (d) and Proposition 16, reduce it to the case where $G/\Gamma$ is compact. Then argue by induction on $\dim G$, using (c).)

(30) Give an example of a 7-dimensional real nilpotent Lie algebra with no basis with respect to which the constants of structure are rational (cf. Chapter I, § 4, Exercise 18). Deduce that the corresponding group of type (N) (Exercise 29) has no discrete subgroup with compact subgroup. (Use Exercise 29.)

32. Let $G$ be a Lie group of type (N) (Exercise 29) and let $\Gamma$ be a discrete subgroup of $G$. Prove the equivalence of the following conditions:
(a) $G/\Gamma$ is compact;

(c) every integral subgroup of $G$ containing $\Gamma$ is equal to $G$.

¶ 33. Let $\Gamma$ be a group. Prove the equivalence of the following conditions:
(a) $\Gamma$ is nilpotent, torsion-free and finitely generated;
(b) there exists a Lie group of type (N) (Exercise 29) containing $\Gamma$ as a discrete subgroup;
(c) there exists a Lie group $G$ of type (N) (Exercise 29) containing $\Gamma$ as a discrete subgroup and such that $G/\Gamma$ is compact.
(The equivalence of (b) and (c) follows from Exercise 29. The implication (c) $\Rightarrow$ (a) is proved by induction on $\dim(G)$ by the method of Exercise 29 (c). To prove that (a) $\Rightarrow$ (c), show first that the Lie $\mathbf{Q}$-algebra attached to the saturation of $\Gamma$ (cf. Chapter II, § 6, Exercise 4) is finite-dimensional; then take the tensor product of this algebra with $\mathbf{R}$ and the corresponding group of type (N).)

34. Let $G$ be a finite-dimensional connected real or complex Lie group and $\rho$ an analytic linear representation of $G$ on a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$. Suppose that $\rho$ is semi-simple. The group $G$ operates by automorphisms on the algebra $S(V^*)$ of polynomial functions on $V$.
(a) Let $S(V^*)^G$ be the set of $G$-invariant elements of $S(V^*)$. There exists a projector $p$ of $S(V^*)$ onto $S(V^*)^G$ which commutes with the operations of $G$ and which leaves stable every $G$-stable vector subspace of $S(V^*)$.

(b) Let $I, J$ be ideals of $S(V^*)$ which are stable under $G$. Let $A$ (resp. $B$) be the set of zeros of $I$ (resp. $J$) in $V$. Suppose that $A \cap B = \varnothing$. There then exists $u \in I$ such that $u$ is $G$-invariant and that $u = 1$ in $B$. (By *Commutative Algebra*, Chapter 7, § 3, no. 3, Proposition 2, there exists $v \in I, w \in J$ such that $v + w = 1$ Let $u = pv$. Show that $u$ has the required properties.)

35. Let $G$ be a compact subgroup of $\mathbf{GL}(n, \mathbf{R})$.
(a) Let $A, B$ be disjoint compact $G$-invariant subsets of $\mathbf{R}^n$. There exists a $G$-invariant polynomial function $u$ on $\mathbf{R}^n$, such that $u = 1$ on $A$ and $u = 0$ on $B$. (There exists a continuous real-valued function $v$ on $\mathbf{R}^n$ such that $v = 1$ on $A$ and $v = 0$ on $B$. By the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem, there exists a polynomial function $w$ on $\mathbf{R}^n$ such that $|v - w| \leq \frac{1}{3}$ on $A \cup B$. Derive $u$ by an integration process with respect to the normalized Haar measure of $G$.)
(b) Let $x \in \mathbf{R}^n$. Then $Gx$ is the set of zeros in $\mathbf{R}^n$ of a finite number of $G$-invariant polynomials. (Use (a) and Corollary 2 to Theorem 1, no. 8.)

36. In $\mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{R})$, the elements which can be expressed in the form $(a, b)$, where $a, b$ in $\mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{R})$, are the elements $\neq -I$.

¶ 37. Let $G$ be a compact real Lie group and $G'$ a finite-dimensional connected real Lie group. Suppose that $L(G)$ is simple. Let $\rho : G \to G'$ be a homomorphism of abstract groups. Suppose that there exists a neighbourhood $V$ of $e_G$ in $G$ such that $\rho(V)$ is relatively compact. Then $\rho$ is continuous. (Let $V'$ be a neighbourhood of $e_{G'}$ in $G'$. Let $V'' \subset V'$ be a neighbourhood of $e_{G'}$ such that
$$
(x \in V'', x_j \in \rho(V), y_j \in \rho(V) \text{ for } 1 \leq j \leq n) \Rightarrow \left( \prod_{i=1}^n x_i(y_i, x)x_i^{-1} \in V' \right)
$$
where $n = \dim G$. It can be assumed that $\rho$ is non-trivial. Then $\mathrm{Ker}\, \rho$ is finite by Exercise 25. Therefore $\rho(G)$ is not countable and hence not discrete. Hence there exists $g \in G$ with non-open centralizer such that $\rho(g) \in V''$. Using Exercise 9 of § 4 and its notation, $\rho(M(g, n, V)) \subset V'$ and $M(g, n, V)$ is a neighbourhood of $e_G$ in $G$.)

38. Let $G$ be a finite-dimensional real Lie group and $G_0$ its identity component. Consider the following property:
(F) $\mathrm{Ad}(G)$ is closed in $\mathrm{Aut}\, L(G)$.
Show that $G$ has property (F) in each of the following cases:
(i) $G$ is connected and nilpotent;
(ii) every derivation of $L(G)$ is inner;
(iii) $G_0$ has property (F) and $G/G_0$ is finite;
(iv) $G$ is an upper triangular group;
(v) $G_0$ is semi-simple.

39. Let G be a finite-dimensional connected real Lie Group, H an integral subgroup of G and $\overline{H}$ its closure in G. Suppose that H has property (F) (Exercise 38).

(a) Let $x \in \overline{H}$. Then $\mathrm{Ad}_{L(G)}x$ leaves $L(H)$ stable (no. 2, Proposition. 5); let $u(x)$ be its restriction to $L(H)$. Then $u(\overline{H}) = \mathrm{Ad}_{L(H)}(H)$. (Observe that $\mathrm{Ad}_{L(H)}(H)$ is dense in $u(\overline{H})$ and apply property (F).)

(b) Let C be the centre of $\overline{H}$. Then $\overline{H} = C.H$ and C is the closure of the centre of H. (By (a), if $x \in \overline{H}$, there exists $y \in H$ such that $\mathrm{Ad}_{L(H)}x = \mathrm{Ad}_{L(H)}y$. Then $\mathrm{Ad}(x^{-1}y)$ is the identity on $L(H)$, hence $x^{-1}y \in Z_{\overline{H}}(H)$ and $x \in Z_{\overline{H}}(H).H$. By continuity, $Z_{\overline{H}}(H)$ is equal to C. The group C is closed in $\overline{H}$ and hence in G; therefore $\overline{C} \cap \overline{H} \subset C$. Let $x \in C$. There exists a sequence $(x_n)$ in H such that $x_n$ tends to x. Then $\mathrm{Ad}_{L(H)}x_n$ tends to 1 and hence there exists a sequence $(y_n)$ in H such that $y_n$ tends to e and $\mathrm{Ad}_{L(H)}y_n = \mathrm{Ad}_{L(H)}x_n$. Then $y_n^{-1}x_n \in C \cap H$ and $y_n^{-1}x_n$ tends to x.)

(c) $H = \overline{H}$ if and only if the centre of H is closed in G. (Use (b).)

40. Let H be a finite-dimensional real Lie group and $H_0$ its identity component.

(a) Suppose that $H_0$ satisfies condition (F) of Exercise 38, that $H/H_0$ is finite and that the centre of $H_0$ is compact. Let G be a finite-dimensional real Lie group and $f : H \to G$ a continuous homomorphism with discrete kernel. Then $f(H)$ is closed in G. (Reduce it to the case where H is connected. As the kernel of f is discrete, the centre of $f(H)$ is the image under f of the centre of H (Lemma 1) and hence is closed in G. Apply Exercise 39 (c).)

(b) Suppose that $H_0$ is semi-simple and that $H/H_0$ is finite. Then the image of H under a finite-dimensional continuous linear representation is closed in the linear group. (Apply (a) and Exercise 7 (b).)

41. Let G be a finite-dimensional connected real Lie group and $\varphi : G \to \mathbf{GL}(n, \mathbf{C})$ a continuous homomorphism with finite kernel. Then $\varphi((G, G))$ is closed in $\mathbf{GL}(n, \mathbf{C})$. (Using Exercises 7 (b) and 40 (a), reduce it to the case where G is the semi-direct product of a semi-simple group S and its radical R. Every element of $\varphi((G, R))$ is unipotent and hence $\varphi((G, R))$ is closed. The vector subspace $V_1$ of fixed points of $\varphi((G, R))$ is $\neq \{0\}$. It is stable under $\varphi(G)$. By considering $\mathbf{C}^n/V_1$ arguing by induction on n and using the complete reducibility of $\varphi(S)$, it is seen that it is possible to write

$$
\mathbf{C}^n = V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_q
$$

where each $V_i$ is stable under $\varphi(S)$ and $\varphi((G, R))(V_i) \subset V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_{i-1}$ for for all i. On the other hand, $\varphi(S)$ is closed (Exercise 40 (b)). Finally, $\varphi((G, G)) = \varphi(S) \cdot \varphi((G, R))$.)
