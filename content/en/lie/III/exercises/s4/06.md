Let $ G $ be a finite-dimensional Lie group, $ g $ its Lie algebra, $ g^* $ the dual vector space of $ g $, $ \sigma $ the adjoint representation of $ G $ on $ g $ and $ \rho $ the representation of $ G $ on $ g^* $ defined by $ \rho(g) = {}^t \sigma(g)^{-1} $ for all $ g \in G $.

(a) $ L(\rho)(x) = -{}^t(\mathrm{ad}\ x) $ for all $ x \in g $.

(b) Let $ f \in g^* $. Let $ G_f $ denote the stabilizer of $ f $ in $ G $; it is a Lie subgroup of $ G $ and $ g_f = L(G_f) $ is the set of $ x \in G $ such that $ {}^t(\mathrm{ad}\ x)f = 0 $.

(c) For $ x,\ y $ in $ g $, we write $ B_f(x, y) = f([x, y]) $. Show that $ B_f $ is an alternating bilinear form on $ g $, that the mapping $ s_f $ of $ g $ into $ g^* $ left associated with $ B_f $ is the mapping $ x \mapsto {}^t(\mathrm{ad}\ x)f $ and that the orthogonal of $ g $ with respect to $ B_f $ is $ g_f $. Let $ \beta_f $ denote the non-degenerate alternating bilinear form on $ g/g_f $ (which is therefore of even dimension) derived from $ B_f $ when passing to the quotient. We denote by $ \beta_f' $ the inverse form of $ B_f $ on $ (g/g_f)^* $.

(d) Let $ \Omega $ denote an orbit of $ G $ on $ g^* $; we give it the manifold structure derived from that on $ G/G_f $ by transport of structure, where $ f $ is an arbitrary point of $ \Omega $. Then $ G $ operates analytically on $ \Omega $ on the left. Let $ D $ be the associated law of infinitesimal operation. For all $ a \in g $ and all $ f \in \Omega $,
$$
D_a(f) = -({}^t \mathrm{ad}\ a)\ f.
$$

The tangent subspace $ T_f(\Omega) $ is the image of $ s_f $, that is the orthogonal of $ g_f $ in $ g^* $, and is canonically identified with $ (g/g_f)^* $, so that $ s_f $ defines a canonical isomorphism $ r_f $ of $ T_f(\Omega) $ onto its dual. The field $ f \mapsto \beta_f' $ is an analytic differential form $ \omega $ of degree 2 on $ \Omega $, which is invariant under $ G $. For $ f \in \Omega,\ a \in g,\ b \in g,\ \omega(D_a(f), D_b(f)) = f([a, b]) $.

(e) Show that $ d\omega = 0 $.

(f) If $ \alpha $ is a differential form of degree 1 on $ \Omega $, the isomorphisms $ r_f $ allow us to identify $ \alpha $ with a vector field. Let $ A $ be the set of analytic functions on $ \Omega $ with values in $ K $. For $ \phi, \psi $ in $ A $, we write $ [\phi, \psi] = \omega(d\phi, d\psi) \in A $. Show that $ A $ has thus a Lie algebra structure.

(g) For all $ a \in g $, let $ \psi_a $ be the function $ f \mapsto f(a) $ on $ \Omega $. Show that $ a \mapsto \psi_a $ is a homomorphism of $ g $ into the Lie algebra $ A $ and that the form $ d\psi_a $ is identified, by means of the isomorphisms $ r_f $, with the vector field $ D_a $.

(h) Let $ U $ be an open subset of $ g^* $ and $ \phi $ an analytic function on $ U $. For all $ f \in U $, the differential $ d_f \phi $ of $ \phi $ at $ f $ is identified with an element of $ g $. Suppose that $ X\phi = 0 $ for every vector field $ X $ defined by the action of $ G $ on $ g^* $. Show that then, for all $ f \in U $, $ d_f \phi $ belongs to the centre of $ g_f $.

(i) For all $ f \in g^* $, we write $ r_f = \dim g_f $. Let $ r = \inf_{f \in g^*} r_f $. Show that the set $ V $ of $ f \in g^* $ such that $ r_f = r $ is open and dense in $ g^* $. Show that, for all $ f \in V $, $ g_f $ is commutative. (Construct $ r $ functions $ \phi $ satisfying the conditions of (h) in a neighbourhood of $ f $ and such that their differentials at $ f $ are linearly independent.)
