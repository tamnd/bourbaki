Let l be a free Lie algebra admitting a finite basic family with n elements and let u be a surjective endomorphism of l. Show that u is an isomorphism. (Write $\operatorname{gr}^n(l) = \mathcal{C}^n/\mathcal{C}^{n+1}$ and denote by $\operatorname{gr}^n(u)$ the endomorphism of $\operatorname{gr}^n(l)$ derived from u; note that $\operatorname{gr}^n(u)$ is surjective; as $\operatorname{gr}^n(l)$ is a free K-module of finite rank, deduce that $\operatorname{gr}^n(u)$ is bijective since the kernel of u is contained in $\bigcap_n \mathcal{C}^n l$, which is $\{0\}$.)

† When g is not graded, we do not know whether the condition
"H^2(g, M) = \{0\} for every g-module M"
implies that g is a free Lie algebra.

If $(y_1, \ldots, y_m)$ is a generating family of $l$, show that $m \geq n$ and that we have equality if and only if $(y_1, \ldots, y_m)$ is a basic family.

§ 11. Let $Y$ and $Z$ be two disjoint sets (with $Y$ totally ordered), let $X = Y \cup Z$ and $l = L(X)$. Let $a_Y$ be the submodule of $l$ generated by $Z$ and $[l, l]$; it is an ideal of the Lie algebra $l$. We propose to find explicitly a basic family of $a_Y$.

Let $M$ be the set of positive integral functions on $Y$ of finite support. If $m \in M$, we denote by $\theta^m$ the endomorphism of $l$ given by
$$
\theta^m = \prod_{y \in Y} (\operatorname{ad} y)^{m(y)},
$$
where the product is relative to the order relation given on $Y$. Let $\mathcal{S}$ denote the subset of $Y \times M$ consisting of the ordered pairs $(u, m)$ such that there exists $y \in Y$ for which $y > u$ and $m(y) \geq 1$. Show that the elements
$$
\theta^m(u) \quad \text{for} \quad (u, m) \in \mathcal{S} \quad \text{and} \quad \theta^m(z) \quad \text{for} \quad (z, m) \in Z \times M
$$
form a basic family of the Lie algebra $a_Y$.

(Reduce the problem to the case where $Y$ is finite and argue by induction on $\operatorname{Card}(Y)$. Use the Corollary to Proposition 10 applied to the greatest element $y$ of $Y$ and apply the induction hypothesis to $Y - \{y\}$.)

12. Let $X = \{x, y\}$ be a set of two elements. Show that the derived algebra of $L(X)$ is a free Lie algebra admitting as basic family the elements $((\operatorname{ad} y)^q \circ (\operatorname{ad} x)^p)(y)$, for $p \geq 1,\ q \geq 0$. (Use Exercise 11.)

§ 13. (In this exercise we assume that every projective module over $K$ is free; this is so for example if $K$ is a principal ideal domain.)

Let $l = \sum_{n=1}^\infty l_n$ be a graded Lie algebra admitting a basic family $B$ consisting of homogeneous elements and let $b$ be a graded Lie subalgebra of $l$, which is a direct factor of $l$ considered as a module.

(a) For all $i \geq 0$, let $l^{(i)}$ be the graded subalgebra of $l$ such that
$$
l_j^{(i)} = b_j \quad \text{if} \ j \leq i. \\
l_j^{(i)} = l_j \quad \text{if} \ j > i.
$$
Then $l = l^{(0)} \supset l^{(1)} \supset \cdots \supset b$. Show, arguing by induction on $i$, the existence of a basic family $B^{(i)}$ of $l^{(i)}$, consisting of homogeneous elements, such that the elements of $B^{(i-1)}$ and of $B^{(i)}$ of degree $< i - 1$ are the same. (Suppose that $B^{(i-1)}$ has been constructed. Let $m_i$ be the intersection of $l_i = l_i^{(i-1)}$ with the subalgebra generated by the $b_j$ for $j < i$ and let $b_i$ be the submodule of $l_i$ generated by the elements of $B^{(i-1)}$ of degree $i$. The induction hypothesis implies that $l_i = m_i \oplus b_i$. As $m_i \subset b_i$, we can decompose $b_i$ as $b_i = \eta_i \oplus \delta_i$, so that $b_i = m_i \oplus \delta_i$; by the hypothesis on $K$ the modules $\eta_i$ and $\delta_i$ are free;

changing the elements of $B^{(i-1)}$ of degree $i$ if necessary, we can assume that $\mathfrak{z}_i$ is generated by a subset of $B^{(i-1)}$. Applying Exercise 11 to $\mathfrak{l}^{(i-1)}$ and its ideal $\mathfrak{l}^{(i)}$, we obtain a basic family $B^{(i)}$ of $\mathfrak{l}^{(i)}$ with the desired properties.)

(b) Deduce from (a) the fact that $\mathfrak{h}$ has a basic family consisting of homogeneous elements.

¶ 14. Suppose that $K$ is a field. Let $X$ be a set and $x, y$ two elements of $L(X)$ which are linearly independent over $K$. Show that the family $(x, y)$ is free in the Lie algebra $L(X)$. (Let $x_p$ (resp. $y_q$) be the non-zero homogeneous component of $x$ (resp. $y$) of highest degree. Adding if necessary a multiple of $y$ to $x$, we can assume that $x_p$ and $y_q$ are linearly independent. The subalgebra of $L(X)$ generated by $x_p$ and $y_q$ is graded and hence free, cf. Exercise 13. Deduce that $(x_p, y_q)$ is a free family and pass from this to $(x, y)$.)

¶ 15. Let $X = \{x, y\}$ be a set with two elements and let $\sigma$ be an automorphism of the Lie algebra $L(X)$. Show that, if $K$ is a field, $\sigma$ preserves the graduation of $L(X)$ (apply Exercise 14 to the non-zero homogeneous components of $\sigma(x)$ and $\sigma(y)$ of highest degree); deduce an isomorphism of $\mathrm{Aut}(L(X))$ onto $\mathbf{GL}(2, K)$. Extend these results to the case where the ring $K$ has no nilpotent element $\neq 0$. When $K$ contains an element $\varepsilon \neq 0$ of zero square, show that $x \mapsto x, \ y \mapsto y + \varepsilon[x, y]$ can be extended to an automorphism of $L(X)$ which does not preserve the graduation of $L(X)$.

¶ 16. Suppose that $K$ is a $\mathbf{Q}$-algebra. If $g$ is a Lie algebra, we denote by $U(g)$ its enveloping algebra, by $\sigma$ the canonical mapping of $g$ into $U(g)$ and by $S(g)$ the symmetric algebra of the $K$-module $g$. There exists one and only one linear mapping
$$
\eta(g): S(g) \to U(g)
$$
such that $\eta(g)(x^n) = \sigma(x)^n$ for all $x \in g$ and all $n \in \mathbf{N}$. Then
$$
\eta(g)(x_1 \ldots x_n) = \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{s \in \mathfrak{S}_n} \sigma(x_{s(1)} \ldots x_{s(n)}), \qquad x_i \in g.
$$
We propose to show that $\eta(g)$ is bijective.

(a) Prove that $\eta(g)$ is surjective and that it is bijective when the $K$-module $g$ is free (use the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt Theorem).

(b) Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be an ideal of $g$. Let $s_\mathfrak{h}$ (resp. $u_\mathfrak{h}$) denote the ideal of $S(g)$ (resp. the two-sided ideal of $U(g)$) generated by $\mathfrak{h}$ (resp. $\sigma(\mathfrak{h})$). The diagram
$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
0 & \to & s_\mathfrak{h} \to S(g) \to S(g/\mathfrak{h}) \to 0 \\
& & \downarrow \eta(g) \qquad \downarrow \eta(g/\mathfrak{h}) \\
0 & \to & u_\mathfrak{h} \to U(g) \to U(g/\mathfrak{h}) \to 0
\end{array}
$$
is commutative and its rows are exact. Deduce that the image $\tilde{u}_\mathfrak{h}$ of $s_\mathfrak{h}$ under $\eta(g)$ is contained in $u_\mathfrak{h}$ and that $\tilde{u}_\mathfrak{h} = u_\mathfrak{h}$ when $\eta(g)$ and $\eta(g/\mathfrak{h})$ are injective (in particular, when the module $g$ and $g/\mathfrak{h}$ are free).

(c) Take $g$ to be a free Lie algebra with basic family $(X_1, \ldots, X_n, H)$, where $n \geq 0$, and take $h$ to be the ideal of $g$ generated by $H$. Show that $g$ and $g/h$ are free modules. Deduce that, in that case, $\tilde{u}_h = u_h$. In particular, there exists $x \in s_h$ such that $(\eta(g))(x) = \sigma(X_1) \ldots \sigma(X_n) \sigma(H)$.

(d) We return to the general case. Show that $u_h$ is generated over $K$ by the elements of the form $\sigma(x_1) \ldots \sigma(x_n) \sigma(h)$, with $n \geq 0$, $x_i \in g$ and $h \in h$ (note that $u_h$ coincides with the left ideal generated by $h$). Show, using (c), that such an element belongs to $\tilde{u}_h$ (use a suitable homomorphism of a free Lie algebra into $g$). Deduce that $\tilde{u}_h = u_h$.

(e) Show that, if $\eta(g)$ is bijective, so is $\eta(g/h)$. Deduce finally that, for every Lie algebra $g$, $\eta(g)$ is bijective (write $g$ as the quotient of a free Lie algebra) and that the canonical homomorphism

$$
\omega : S(g) \to \operatorname{gr} U(g) \quad \text{(cf. Chapter I, § 2, no. 6)}
$$

is an isomorphism ("Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt Theorem" for Lie algebras over $\mathbf{Q}$-algebras).
