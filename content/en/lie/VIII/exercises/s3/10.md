Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be a semi-simple Lie algebra, and $x$ an element of $\mathfrak{a}$. We say that $x$ is diagonalizable if ad $x$ is diagonalizable (Algebra, Chap. VII), in other words, if there exists a basis of $\mathfrak{a}$ consisting of eigenvectors of ad $x$.

$a)$ Let $\mathfrak{c}$ be a commutative subalgebra of $\mathfrak{a}$ consisting of diagonalizable elements, and let L be the set of weights of $\mathfrak{c}$ in the representation ad$\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{a}}$. The set L is a finite subset of $\mathfrak{c}^*$ containing 0 (unless $\mathfrak{a}= 0)$ and $\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{c})$. Show that there exists a subset M of L such that L **--** $\{0\}$ is the disjoint union of$^{\lambda\in L}$ M and$\bigoplus-M$, and such that $(M + M)\cap L\subset M$. If M has these properties, put $\mathfrak{a}^M=_{\lambda\in M}\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{c})$, and $\mathfrak{p}^M=\mathfrak{a}^0(\mathfrak{c})\oplus \mathfrak{a}^M$. The algebra $\mathfrak{a}^0(\mathfrak{c})$ is the commutant of $\mathfrak{c}$ in $\mathfrak{a}$; it is reductive in $\mathfrak{a}$ (Chap. VII, §1, no. 5), and its Cartan subalgebras are the Cartan subalgebras of $\mathfrak{a}$ (Chap. VII, §2, no. 3). Show that $\mathfrak{p}^M$ is a
