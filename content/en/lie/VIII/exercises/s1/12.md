Let $q\in k$, and let W be a vector space with basis $(e_0, e_1, e_2, . . .)$.

$a)$ Show that there exists a unique representation $\rho_q$ of $\mathfrak{s}$ on W such that

$$
\rho_q(H)e_n= 2e_{n+1},\rho_q(X_+)e_n= (\frac{1}{2}\rho_q(H)-1)^ne_0
$$

$$
\rho_q(X_-)e_n= (1\rho_q(H) + 1)^n(-qe_0+e_1+e_2)
$$

2

We have $\rho_q(C) = 4q$, where $C =H^2+ 2H-4X_-X_+$ (cf. Exerc. 1). The representation $\rho_q$ is simple. The elements $x\in \mathfrak{s}$ such that $\rho_q(x)$ admits an eigenvalue are the multiples of $X_+$. The endomorphism $\rho_q(X_+)$ admits 1 as an eigenvalue, with multiplicity 1.

$b)$ Let $\rho$ be a simple representation of $\mathfrak{s}$ such that $\rho (C) = 4q$ and such that $\rho (X_+)$ has 1 as an eigenvalue. Show that $\rho$ is equivalent to $\rho_q$.
