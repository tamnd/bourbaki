Let $ X $ be a non-empty set and $ W $ a set of permutations of $ X $. Assume given a set $ \mathcal{R} $ of equivalence relations on $ X $, an element $ x_0 \in X $ and a map $ \varphi : H \mapsto s_H $ from $ \mathcal{R} $ to $ W $. Denote by $ \mathcal{R}_0 $ the set of $ H \in \mathcal{R} $ such that $ s_H(x_0) \equiv x_0 \mod. H' $ for all $ H' \neq H $ in $ \mathcal{R} $, and by $ S_0 $ the set of $ s_H $ for $ H $ in $ \mathcal{R}_0 $. We make the following assumptions:

(i) For any $ H \in \mathcal{R} $, there are two equivalence classes modulo $ H $ that are permuted by $ s_H $ and $ s_H^2 = 1 $.

(ii) For all $ H \in \mathcal{R} $ and all $ w \in W $, the transform $ w(H) $ of $ H $ by $ w $ is an equivalence relation belonging to $ \mathcal{R} $ and $ s_{w(H)} = ws_Hw^{-1} $.

(iii) For any $ w \neq 1 $ in $ W $, the set of $ H \in \mathcal{R} $ such that $ w(x_0) \not\equiv x_0 \mod. H $ is finite and meets $ \mathcal{R}_0 $.

a) Prove that $(W, S_0)$ is a Coxeter system (use Prop. 6 of no. 7).

b) Prove that the length $ l_{S_0}(w) $ is equal to the number of elements $ H \in \mathcal{R} $ such that
$$
w(x_0) \not\equiv x_0 \mod. H.
$$

c) Let $ E $ be a finite set and $ X $ the set of total order relations on $ X $. Denote by $ W $ the group of permutations of $ E $, acting in the obvious way on $ E $. Let $ i $ and $ j $ be distinct elements of $ E $; say that two elements $ R $ and $ R' $ of $ X $ are equivalent mod. $ H_{ij} $ if either $ R(i, j) $ and $ R'(i, j) $ or $ R(j, i) $ and $ R'(j, i) $; and denote the transposition of $ i $ and $ j $ by $ s_{ij} $. Let $ \mathcal{R} $ be the set of equivalence relations of the form $ H_{ij} $ and $ \varphi $ the map from $ \mathcal{R} $ to $ W $ defined by $ \varphi(H_{ij}) = s_{ij} $; finally let $ x_0 $ be an arbitrary element of $ X $. Show that the objects thus defined satisfy assumptions (i) to (iii), and recover the results of Exerc. 4.
