Let $ (W, S) $ be a Coxeter system and let $ (\Gamma, f) $ be its Coxeter graph. Let $ k $ be an integer $ \geqslant 3 $. If $ a $ is an edge of $ \Gamma $, put $ f_k(a) = f(a) $ if $ f(a) \neq \infty $ and $ f_k(a) = k $ if $ f(a) = \infty $. Let $ (W_k, S) $ be a Coxeter system whose Coxeter graph is equal to $ (\Gamma, f_k) $ (Chap. V, § 4, no. 3, Cor. of Prop. 4). Show that there exists a unique homomorphism $ \varphi_k $ from $ W $ to $ W_k $ inducing the identity on $ S $. Show that if $ k $ divides $ k' $, there exists a unique homomorphism $ \varphi_{k,k'} $ from $ W_{k'} $ to $ W_k $ such that $ \varphi_k = \varphi_{k,k'} \circ \varphi_{k'} $. Show that the homomorphism $ (\varphi_k) $ from $ W $ to the projective limit of the $ W_k $ is injective (use Exerc. 13) c)), but in general not surjective (for example, in the case of the infinite dihedral group).*

15)⁷ Let $ A $ be a set and $ \mathcal{C} $ a subset of $ \mathfrak{P}(A) $. The elements of $ \mathcal{C} $ are called the chambers of $ A $ and a subset $ F $ of a chamber $ C $ is called a facet, the codimension of $ F $ in $ C $ being the cardinal of $ C - F $. A facet $ F $ is said to be a panel of $ C $ if $ F $ is of codimension 1 in $ C $. Two chambers $ C $ and $ C' $ are said to be adjoining

A gallery of length n is a sequence $ \Gamma = (C_0, C_1, \ldots, C_n) $ of $ n + 1 $ chambers such that $ C_i $ and $ C_{i+1} $ are adjoining for $ 0 \leq i \leq n - 1 $. Then $ C_0 $ and $ C_n $ are called the ends of $ \Gamma $. The gallery $ \Gamma $ is called injective if $ C_i \neq C_{i+1} $ for $ 0 \leq i \leq n - 1 $ and is called minimal if there is no gallery with the same ends and length < n.

The set A (together with $ \mathcal{C} $) is a building if every element of A belongs to at least one chamber and if any two chambers are the ends of a gallery. The distance between two chambers C and C' is the length $ d(C, C') $ of a minimal gallery with ends C and C'.

A sub-building of a building A is a subset D of A such that D together with $ \mathcal{C} \cap \mathfrak{P}(D) $ is a building.

a) Show that if A is a building, a facet has the same codimension in every chamber containing it; this allows us to speak of the codimension of a facet and of a panel of A without reference to a particular chamber. A morphism of a building B to A is a map $ f $ from B to A such that the restriction of $ f $ to every chamber C of B is a bijection from C to a chamber $ f(C) $ of A. Show that the image under $ f $ of a facet of B is a facet of the same codimension.

b) A building is called an apartment if every panel is contained in exactly two chambers. Show that if A is an apartment, every automorphism of A (i.e. every permutation of A preserving $ \mathcal{C} $) that leaves fixed all the points of a chamber is the identity. More generally, let $ \varphi $ be an endomorphism of A and let C be a chamber of A such that $ \varphi(a) = a $ for all $ a \in C $. Let $ (C, C_1, \ldots, C_n) $ be a gallery of A. Show that, either the gallery $ (C, \varphi(C_1), \ldots, \varphi(C_n)) $ is not injective, or $ \varphi(a) = a $ for all $ a $ belonging to the union of the $ C_i $.
