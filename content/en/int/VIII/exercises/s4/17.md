Notations and conventions are those of Exer. 15.

a) Let $ \mu $ be a bounded measure on G; for the endomorphism $ \gamma(\mu): f \mapsto \mu * f $ of $ L^p(G, \beta) $ to be surjective ($ 1 \leq p < +\infty $), it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a $ c_p > 0 $ such that $ \| \nu * g \|_q \geq c_p \| g \|_q $ for all $ g \in L^q(G, \beta) $ (where $ \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1 $) (cf. TVS, IV, §4, No. 2, remarks following Cor. 3 of Th. 1).

b) If $ \mu $ is a measure with base $ \beta $, and G is not discrete, show that $ \gamma(\mu) $ is never surjective for $ 1 \leq p \leq +\infty $ (for $ p < +\infty $, use a), reducing to the case that the density of $ \mu $ with respect to $ \beta $ belongs to $ \mathcal{K}(G) $; for $ p = +\infty $, argue directly by observing that for $ f \in L^1(G, \beta) $ and $ g \in L^\infty(G, \beta) $, $ f * g $ is uniformly continuous for the right uniform structure).

c) If G is abelian, $ 1 \leq p \leq 2 $ and the endomorphism $ \gamma(\mu) $ of $ L^p(G, \beta) $ is surjective, show that it is bijective (using regularization, show that if $ \gamma(\mu) $ is not injective then the endomorphism $ \gamma(\nu) $ of $ L^q(G, \beta) $ is not injective: one makes use of Ch. IV, §6, No. 5, Cor. of Prop. 4).

d) Take $ G = \mathbf{Z} $ and $ \mu = \varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_0 $; show that $ \gamma(\mu) $ is injective in the $ L^p(\mathbf{Z}, \beta) $ for which $ p \neq +\infty $, but not in $ L^\infty(\mathbf{Z}, \beta) $, and it is not surjective for any value of $ p $.
