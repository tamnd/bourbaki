Let G be a locally compact group, $ \beta $ a left Haar measure on G, A a $ \beta $-measurable subset of G, and $ \nu $ a nonzero positive measure on G. Assume that sA is locally $ \nu $-negligible for every $ s \in G $. Show that A is locally $ \beta $-negligible. (Reduce to the case that A is relatively compact and $ \nu $ is bounded. Prove that $ \nu $ and $ \varphi_A \cdot \beta $ are convolvable and that $ \nu * \beta \varphi_A = 0 $, whence $ 0 = \| \nu * \varphi_A \cdot \beta \| = \| \nu \| \cdot \| \varphi_A \cdot \beta \| $.) Show, on admitting the continuum hypothesis, that this result may fail to hold if A is not assumed to be $ \beta $-measurable. (Take $ G = \mathbf{R}^2 $, take for $ \nu $ the Haar measure on the subgroup $ \mathbf{R} \times \{0\} $, and apply Exer. 7 c) of Ch. V, §8.)
