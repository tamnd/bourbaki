Let $ T, X, Y $ be locally compact spaces, $ \pi : T \to X $, $ \pi' : X \to Y $ mappings, $ \pi'' = \pi' \circ \pi $, $ \mu $ a real measure on $ T $. It can happen that $ \pi $ is $ \mu $-proper and $ \pi' $ is $ \pi(\mu) $-proper, without $ \pi'' $ being $ \mu $-proper. (Take $ T = \mathbf{R} \times \{0, 1\} $, $ X = \mathbf{R} $, $ Y = \{0\} $, $ \pi = \mathrm{pr}_1 $; take for $ \mu $ the measure that induces Lebesgue measure (resp. the opposite of Lebesgue measure) on $ \mathbf{R} \times \{0\} $ (resp. $ \mathbf{R} \times \{1\} $) canonically identified with $ \mathbf{R} $.)
