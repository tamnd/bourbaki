Let $ (\mu_n) $ be a sequence of positive measures on T that converges quasi-strongly to $ \mu $ in $ \mathcal{M}(T) $ (Ch. III, §1, Exer. 8).

a) Show that if a mapping $ f $ of T into a topological space G is $ \mu_n $-measurable for every $ n $, then it is $ \mu $-measurable (make use of Exercise 6 b) of Ch. IV, §1).

b) Let $ f $ be a mapping of T into a Banach space F, that is essentially $ \mu_n $-integrable for every $ n $. Show that if the sequence $ (\mu_n^* (|f|)) $ is bounded, then $ f $ is essentially $ \mu $-integrable (Exer. 2). Show by means of an example that one does not necessarily have $ \int f \, d\mu = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int f \, d\mu_n $ (take for $ \mu_n $ a point measure on a compact space, such that $ \lim \| \mu_n \| = 0 $); however, this relation holds when $ f $ is bounded and has compact support.
