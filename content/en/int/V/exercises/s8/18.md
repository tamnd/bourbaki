The notations being the same as in Exer. 17, let $ \nu_\iota $ be a positive measure on $ T_\iota $, of total mass equal to 1, and let $ \nu = \bigotimes_{\iota \in I} \nu_\iota $.

$ a) $ Show that if one of the measures $ \nu_\iota $ is alien to the measure $ \mu_\iota $ with the same index, then $ \nu $ is alien to $ \mu $.

$ b) $ For every $ \iota \in I $, write $ \nu_\iota = \mu'_\iota + \mu''_\iota $, where $ \mu'_\iota $ has base $ \mu_\iota $, and $ \mu''_\iota $ is alien to $ \mu_\iota $ (\S 5, Th. 3); suppose that $ \mu'_\iota \neq 0 $ for all $ \iota \in I $. Show that, for $ \nu $ not to be alien to $ \mu $, it is necessary and sufficient that $ \mu''_\iota = 0 $ except for a countable family of indices, and that the product $ \mu' = \bigotimes_{\iota \in I} \mu'_\iota $ be a measure $ \neq 0 $ with base $ \mu $; $ \mu'' = \nu - \mu' $ is then alien to $ \mu $. (If $ \mu''_\iota \neq 0 $ for an uncountable infinity of indices, show that there exists a number $ \alpha $ such that $ 0 < \alpha < 1 $ and such that, for a countable infinity of indices $ \iota_n $, one has $ \mu'_{\iota_n}(T_{\iota_n}) \leq \alpha $; show then that $ \mu $ is alien to $ \nu $ by using $ a$) ; prove the second part of the proposition by using Exer. 17.)
