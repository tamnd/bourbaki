a) Let $ E $ be a locally compact space, $ \Gamma $ a locally compact group operating on the left continuously in $ E $. Assume that for every $ x \in E $, the mapping $ s \mapsto s \cdot x $ of $ \Gamma $ into $ E $ is proper, and that there exists a nonzero bounded positive measure $ \mu $ on $ E $ invariant under $ \Gamma $. Then $ \Gamma $ is compact. (Let $ (s_n) $ be a sequence of points of $ \Gamma $. Let $ K $ be a compact subset of $ E $ that is not $ \mu $-negligible. Show, using Exer. 15 of Ch. IV, §4, that there exists an $ x_0 \in E $ such that $ s_n x_0 \in K $ for infinitely many values of $ n $. From this, deduce that $ (s_n) $ has a cluster point in $ \Gamma $. Then apply Exer. 6 of GT, II, §4.)

b) Let $ G $ be a locally compact group, $ H $ and $ K $ two closed subgroups of $ G $. Assume that $ G $ and $ H $ are unimodular and that $ G/H $ has finite measure for the measure invariant under $ G $. Show that for $ H $ and $ K $ to satisfy the equivalent conditions of Exer. 11 c) of GT, III, §4, it is necessary and sufficient that $ K $ be compact.
