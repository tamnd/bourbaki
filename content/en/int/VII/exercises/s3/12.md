Let $ K $ be a commutative field, $ E $ a vector space over $ K $, $ F $ a linear subspace of $ E $, $ p $ the canonical homomorphism of $ E $ onto $ E/F $, and $ A $ the set of homomorphisms $ f : E/F \to E $ such that $ p \circ f $ is the identity homomorphism of $ E/F $.

a) If $ f \in A $ and $ h \in \mathrm{Hom}(E/F, F) $, then $ f + h \in A $. Show that if $ f, f' \in A $, there exists one and only one $ h \in \mathrm{Hom}(E/F, F) $ such that $ f + h = f' $. One can therefore regard $ A $ as an affine space of which $ \mathrm{Hom}(E/F, F) $ is the space of translations.

b) Let $ B $ be the set of linear subspaces of $ E $ that are supplementary to $ F $ in $ E $. Show that $ f \mapsto f(E/F) $ is a bijection of $ A $ onto $ B $. One can therefore regard $ B $ as an affine space of which $ \mathrm{Hom}(E/F, F) $ is the space of translations.

c) Show that if $ u $ is an automorphism of $ E $ such that $ u(F) = F $, then the mapping $ f \mapsto u \circ f $ is an affine bijection of $ A $ onto $ A $. (Choose an origin in $ A $, and observe that the mapping $ h \mapsto u \circ h $ is an automorphism of the vector space $ \mathrm{Hom}(E/F, F) $.) From this, deduce that the mapping $ F' \mapsto u(F') $ is an affine bijection of $ B $ onto $ B $.

d) Assume that $ K = \mathbf{R} $ and $ \dim E < +\infty $. Let $ G $ be a compact group, and $ \rho $ a continuous linear representation of $ G $ in $ E $ such that $ \rho(s)(F) = F $ for all $ s \in G $. Show that there exists an $ F' \in B $ such that $ \rho(s)(F') = F' $ for all $ s \in G $. (Make use of c) and Lemma 2 of No. 2.) Obtain this result anew using Prop. 1 of No. 1.
