Let K be a compact space. For every Hausdorff quotient space $K_1$ of K, one identifies the Banach space $\mathscr{C}(K_1)$ with a closed subspace of $\mathscr{C}(K)$ by means of the injection $f \mapsto f \circ \pi$, where $\pi$ is the canonical mapping of K onto $K_1$.

a) Let u be a continuous linear mapping of $\mathscr{C}(K)$ into a quasi-complete Hausdorff locally convex space F. In order that u transform the unit ball of $\mathscr{C}(K)$ into a subset of F that is relatively compact for $\sigma(F, F')$, it suffices that, for every *metrizable* quotient space $K_1$ of K, the restriction of u to $\mathscr{C}(K_1)$ have that property. (Make use of Eberlein’s theorem (TVS, IV, §5, No. 3, Th. 1), and observe that every sequence $(f_n)$ in $\mathscr{C}(K)$ is contained in a subspace $\mathscr{C}(K_1)$, where $K_1$ is a metrizable quotient space of K; for that, one considers the continuous mapping $x \mapsto (f_n(x))$ of K into $\mathbf{R}^N$.)
