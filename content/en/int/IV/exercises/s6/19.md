Let $f$ be a numerical function $\geq 0$ defined in the space $\mathbf{R}_+^* = ]0, +\infty[$ and $p$-th power integrable for Lebesgue measure ($1 < p < +\infty$). Set $F(x) = \int_0^x f(t) \, dt$ for all $x > 0$.

a) Show that as $x$ tends to 0 or to $+\infty$, $F(x) = o(x^{(p-1)/p})$ (use Hölder’s inequality).

b) Show that the function $F(x)/x$ is $p$-th power integrable in $\mathbf{R}_+^*$ and that

$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \left( \frac{F(x)}{x} \right)^p dx \leq \left( \frac{p}{p-1} \right)^p \int_0^{+\infty} (f(x))^p dx
$$

(Hardy’s inequality). (Consider first the case that $f \in \mathscr{K}(\mathbf{R}_+^*)$; for every compact interval $[a, b] \subset \mathbf{R}_+^*$, obtain an upper bound for the integral

$$
\int_a^b \left( \frac{F(t)}{t} \right)^p dt
$$

by integrating by parts and making use of Hölder’s inequality.)
