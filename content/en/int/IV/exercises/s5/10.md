Let $ F $ be a Banach space, $ f $ a measurable mapping of $ X $ into $ F $, such that the set $ A $ of $ x \in X $ where $ f(x) \neq 0 $ is a countable union of integrable sets. Show that there exists a sequence $ (f_n) $ of continuous functions with compact support, with values in $ F $, such that the sequence $ (f_n(x)) $ converges to $ f(x) $ almost everywhere in $ X $ (note on the one hand that $ A $ is the union of a negligible set $ N $ and a sequence of pairwise disjoint compact sets $ K_n $ such that the restriction of $ f $ to each $ K_n $ is continuous; and on the other hand, that there exists a decreasing sequence $ (U_n) $ of open sets containing $ A $ such that $ \mu(U_n \cap \complement A) $ tends to 0 as $ n $ tends to infinity).
