Let $ A $ be a linearly topologized commutative ring. The additive group $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $ is identified with the product group $ A^{N^p} $ and is given the product topology $ \mathcal{T} $.

(a) Show that $ \mathcal{T} $ is compatible with the ring structure on $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $ and is a linear topology for which the mappings $ f \mapsto \partial f / \partial X_i $ are continuous.

(b) For every element $ P = \sum_e \alpha_{e,P} X^e $ of $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $ (notation of § 1, no. 6) and every complete Hausdorff topological A-algebra B ($ \S 2 $, Exercise 28), an element $ x = (x_1, \ldots, x_r) \in B^p $ is said to be substitutable in $ P $ if the family $ (\alpha_{e,P} x^e) $ (where we have written $ x^e = x_1^{e_1} \ldots x_p^{e_p} $ for $ e = (e_1, \ldots, e_p) $) converges to 0 in B with respect to the filter of complements of finite subsets of $ N^p $. This family is then summable and its sum is denoted by $ P(x) $. Show that the set of formal power series $ P \in A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $ such that $ x $ is substitutable in $ P $ is a subring S, of $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $ and the mapping $ P \mapsto P(x) $ is a homomorphism from S, to B.

(c) Show that, if $ x $ is substitutable in $ P $ and $ y = (y_1, \ldots, y_p) $ is an element of $ B^p $ such that the $ y_i $ are topologically nilpotent for $ 1 \leq i \leq p $, then $ x + y $ is substitutable in $ P $.

In particular, if there exists in B a neighbourhood of 0 consisting of topologically nilpotent elements, then, for all $ P \in A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $, the set D of $ x \in B^p $ which are substitutable in $ P $ is open and the mapping $ x \mapsto P(x) $ from D to B is continuous.

(d) Suppose from now on that A is *Hausdorff and complete* and let $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $ be given the topology $ \mathcal{T} $. For a system of q formal power series $ P_1, \ldots, P_q $ of $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $ to be substitutable in a formal power series

$$
Q \in A[[X_1, \ldots, X_q]],
$$

it is necessary and sufficient that the system $ (P_1(0), \ldots, P_q(0)) $ be substitutable in Q.

(e) Suppose that $ x $ is substitutable in each of the $ P_k $ ($ 1 \leq k \leq q $) and that the system $ (P_1, \ldots, P_q) $ is substitutable in Q. Show that $ x $ is substitutable in $ Q(P_1, \ldots, P_q) $, that the system $ (P_1(x), \ldots, P_q(x)) $ is substitutable in Q and that $ Q(P_1(x), \ldots, P_q(x)) = ((Q(P_1, \ldots, P_q))(x)) $ iff further *one* of the following hypotheses assumed to be satisfied: (a) there is in B an ideal n such that the ordered pair $ (B, n) $ satisfies Hensel's conditions and the $ x_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq p $) belong to n; (β) the formal power series Q is restricted.

(f) Take $ B = A = \mathbf{F}_2 $ with the discrete topology, $ P = X + X^2 $, $ Q = \sum_{n=0}^\infty X^{2^n} $; then $ P $ is substitutable in $ Q $, $ x = 1 $ is substitutable in $ P $ and in $ Q(P) $ and $ P(x) $ is substitutable in $ Q $, but $ Q(P(x)) = 0 $ and $ (Q(P))(x) = 1 $.
