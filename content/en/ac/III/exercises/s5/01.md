Let $ A $ be a commutative ring and $ 3 $ an ideal of $ A $. An $ A $-module $ M $ is called absolutely *Hausdorff* with respect to $ 3 $ if, for every finitely generated $ A $-module $ N $, the $ A $-module $ N \otimes_A M $ is Hausdorff with the $ \mathfrak{g} $-adic topology; an absolutely Hausdorff $ A $-module is ideally Hausdorff.

(a) For $ M $ to be absolutely Hausdorff with respect to $ \mathfrak{g} $, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every finitely generated $ A $-module $ N $ and every submodule $ N' $ of $ N $, $ \operatorname{Im}(N' \otimes_A M) $ be closed in $ N \otimes_A M $ with the $ 3 $-adic topology.

(b) Let $ B $ be a commutative $ A $-algebra and $ \mathfrak{L} $ an ideal of $ B $ containing $ \mathfrak{g}B $. If $ M $ is an absolutely Hausdorff $ B $-module with respect to $ \mathfrak{L} $, $ M $ is an absolutely Hausdorff $ A $-module with respect to $ 3 $.
