Let $ X $ be a completely regular topological space and $ \beta X $ its Stone-Čech compactification (General Topology, Chapter IX, § 1, Exercise 7). For all $ x \in X $, let $ \mathfrak{g}(x) $ denote the maximal ideal of the ring $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ consisting of those $ f \in \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ such that $ x $ belongs to the closure of the sub set $ \overline{f}(0) $ of $ X $ (General Topology, Chapter X, § 4, Exercise 15); let $ \mathfrak{u}(x) $ denote the ideal consisting of those $ f \in \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ such that $ \overline{f}(0) $ is the trace on $ X $ of a neighbourhood of $ x $ in $ X $; $ \mathfrak{u}(x) $ is equal to its radical.

An ideal $ a $ of $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ is called isolated (resp. absolutely isolated) if, for all $ f \geq 0 $ on $ a $, every $ g $ such that $ 0 \leq g \leq f $ (resp. $ |g| \leq f $) belongs to $ a $ (cf. Algebra, Chapter IV, § 1, Exercise 4); then $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})/a $ possesses an order structure (resp. a lattice-order structure) compatible with its ring structure and for which the elements $ \geq 0 $ are the classes mod. $ a $ of the elements $ \geq 0 $ to $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ (loc. cit.).

(a) Show that an ideal $ a $ of $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ which is equal to its radical is absolutely isolated (if $ f \in a $ and $ |g| \leq |f| $, consider the function equal to 0 when $ f(x) = 0 $ and to $ (g(x))^2/f(x) $ when $ f(x) \neq 0 $). In this case for $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})/a $ to be totally ordered, it is necessary and sufficient that $ a $ be prime; the set of prime ideals of $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})/a $ is then totally ordered by inclusion.

(b) For an isolated ideal $ a $ of $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ to be such that $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})/a $ is totally ordered, it is necessary that $ a $ be contained in a single maximal ideal $ \mathfrak{g}(x) $ and then $ a $ necessarily contains $ \mathfrak{u}(x) $; it is sufficient that $ a $ contain a prime ideal and then $ a $ is absolutely isolated (note that the relation $ fg = 0 $ then implies $ f \in a $ or $ g \in a $).

(c) Take $ X = \mathbf{R} $, $ x = 0 $; show that the principal ideal generated by the function $ t \mapsto |t| $ is contained in $ \mathfrak{g}(0) $ and contains $ \mathfrak{u}(0) $ but is not isolated; the principal ideal generated by the identity mapping $ t \mapsto t $ is contained in $ \mathfrak{g}(0) $, contains $ \mathfrak{u}(0) $ and is isolated but not absolutely isolated.

(d) Show that, if $ p $ and $ q $ are two prime ideals in $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $, then $ pq = p \cap q $ (and in particular $ p^2 = p $) and $ p + q $ is prime or equal to $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $. (If $ p + q \neq \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $, observe that $ p + q $ is absolutely isolated and equal to its radical and use (a).)

(e) For $ \mathfrak{u}(x) $ to be prime, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every function $ f \in \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $, the closure in $ \beta X $ of one of the two sets $ \overline{f^1}([0, +\infty[) $, $ \overline{f^1}([-\infty, 0]) $ is a neighbourhood of $ x $. Deduce an example where $ \mathfrak{u}(x) $ is prime and not maximal (consider the topological space associated with a non-trivial ultrafilter; cf. General Topology, Chapter I, § 6, no. 5).

(f) Suppose that $ x \in X $. For $ \mathcal{U}(x) = \mathfrak{F}(x) $, it is necessary and sufficient that every countable intersection of neighbourhoods of $ x $ in $ X $ be a neighbourhood of $ x $ in $ X $. (Cf. General Topology, Chapter I, § 7, Exercise 7.)

(g) Show that $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ is canonically identified with the total ring of fractions of $ \mathcal{C}^\infty(X; \mathbf{R}) $.
