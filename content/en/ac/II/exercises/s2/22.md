Let A be a ring (not necessarily commutative) and S a multiplicative subset of A.

(a) Let $\mathscr F$ be the set of left ideals I of A such that, for all $a\in A$, there exists $s\in S$ such that $sa\in I$; this implies in particular that $I\cap S\ne\varnothing$. Show that $\mathscr F$ is topologizing and idempotent.

(b) Let B be a ring and $\phi:A\longrightarrow B$ a ring homomorphism. The ordered pair $(B,\phi)$ is called a *ring of left fractions* of A with denominators in S if it satisfies the following conditions:

(I) If $\phi(a)=0$, there exists $s\in S$ such that $sa=0$.

(II) If $s\in S$, $\phi(s)$ is invertible in B.

(III) Every element of B is of the form $(\phi(s))^{-1}\phi(a)$, where $a\in A$.

Show that the following properties are equivalent:

$(a)$ The ring A possesses a *ring of left fractions* with denominators in S.

$(\beta)$ The following conditions hold:

$(\beta_1)$ For all $s\in S$, $a\in A$, there exists $t\in S$ and $b\in A$ such that $ta=bs$.

$(\beta_2)$ If $a\in A$ and $s\in S$ satisfy $as=0$, there exists $t\in S$ such that $ta=0$.

$(\gamma)$ The canonical images of the elements of S in $A_{\mathscr F}$ are invertible.

Moreover these properties imply the following:

$(\delta)$ The principal ideals $As$, where $s\in S$, belong to $\mathscr F$ and every ideal $m\in\mathscr F$ contains one of these principal ideals.

$(\epsilon)$ The left annihilator of every $s\in S$ is contained in $\mathscr F A_{,}$.

$(\zeta)$ For every exact sequence $0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{h}N\xrightarrow{p}P\longrightarrow0$ of A-modules, the sequence $0\longrightarrow M_{\mathscr F}\xrightarrow{h_{\mathscr F}}N_{\mathscr F}\xrightarrow{p_{\mathscr F}}P_{\mathscr F}\longrightarrow0$ is exact.

(Show that $(a)\Rightarrow(\beta)\Rightarrow(\gamma)\Rightarrow(a)$. To see that $(\gamma)$ implies $(a)$, note that, for every A-module M and every $x\in\mathscr F M$, there exists $s\in S$ such that $sx=0$. To see that $(\beta)$ implies $(\gamma)$, prove first that $(\beta)$ implies $(\delta)$, $(\epsilon)$ and $(\zeta)$. To show that $(\beta)$ implies $(\zeta)$, establish, using $(\delta)$, that, if $m\in\mathscr F$ and

$$
u\in\operatorname{Hom}_A(m,P/\mathscr F P),
$$

132 then there exists s \in S such that As \subset m and v \in \mathrm{Hom}_A(As, N/\mathcal{F}N) such that the diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
As & \xrightarrow{v} & N/\mathcal{F}N \\
i \downarrow & & \downarrow p' \\
m & \xrightarrow{\pi} & P/\mathcal{F}P
\end{array}
$$

is commutative, i being the canonical injection and $ p' $ the mapping derived from $ p $ by taking quotients. Consider finally, for all $ s \in S $, the exact sequence

$$ 0 \longrightarrow N \longrightarrow A \xrightarrow{\mu_s} A \longrightarrow A/As \longrightarrow 0 $$

where $ \mu_s $ is the homothety $ \lambda \mapsto \lambda s $; use (E), (\zeta) and Exercise 4 of Algebra, Chapter I, § 8.)

(c) Deduce from (b) that, if A possesses a ring of left fractions (B, $ \phi $) with denominators in S, this ring has the following universal property: for every ring homomorphism $ f : A \to C $ such that $ f(s) $ is invertible in C for all $ s \in S $, there exists a unique homomorphism $ g : B \to C $ such that $ f = g \circ \phi $. In particular B is canonically isomorphic to $ A_{\mathcal{F}} $.

(d) The notion of a *ring of right fractions* of A with denominators in S is similarly defined. Deduce from (c) that, if there exist both a ring of left fractions and a ring of right fractions of A, these two rings are isomorphic.

(e) Let E be a vector space over a field K, $ (e_i)_{i \in I} $ a basis of E and T the tensor algebra of E. Let J be a non-empty subset of I distinct from I and S the multiplicative subset of T generated by the $ e_i $ such that $ i \in J $. Show that T admits no ring of left or right fractions with denominators in S.

(f) With the notation of (e) suppose that I = N and consider the quotient ring A of T by the two-sided ideal generated by the elements $ e_0^2 $ and $ e_0 e_i - e_{i+1} e_0 $, for all $ i > 0 $, and $ e_i e_j - e_j e_i $, for $ i > 0, j > 0 $. Let S' be the multiplicative set generated by the canonical images of the $ e_i $ in A, $ \epsilon $ or $ i > 0 $; show that A admits a ring of left fractions, but not a ring of right fractions, with denominators in S'.

(g) Suppose that A admits a ring of left fractions with denominators in S. In the set S x A, let R be the relation between $ (s, x) $ and $ (t, y) $: "there exists $ r \in S $, $ u \in A $, $ v \in A $ such that $ r = us = vt $ and $ ux = vy $". Show that R is an equivalence relation and define on the set $ B = (S \times A)/R $ a ring structure such that the ordered pair consisting of S and the mapping $ \phi : A \to B $, which maps $ x \in A $ to the class of $ (1, x) $, is a ring of left fractions of A with denominator in S (use the fact that, for $ s \in S $ and $ t \in S $, there exists $ u \in A $ and $ v \in A $ such that $ r = us = tv $ belongs to S). Consider the case $ S = A - \{0\} $ (cf. Algebra, Chapter I, § 9, Exercise 8).
