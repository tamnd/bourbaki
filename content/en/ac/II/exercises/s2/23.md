(a) Let A be a left Noetherian ring with no divisor of 0. Show that A possesses a field of left fractions (use Exercise 14 (a) to show that condition $(\beta_1)$ of Exercise 22 (b) is fulfilled for $S = A - \{0\}$).

(b) Let $A$ be a left and right Noetherian ring with no divisor of 0. Show that every finitely generated left $A$-module $M$, each of whose elements $\neq 0$ is free, is a submodule of a free $A$-module. (Embed $M$ canonically in $K \otimes_A M$, where $K$ is the field of fractions (left or right) of $A$ considered as a right $A$-module; if $(x_i)$ is a basis of the left vector $K$-space $K \otimes_A M$, show that there exists $a \neq 0$ in $A$ such that $M$ is contained in the sub-$A$-module of $K \otimes_A M$ generated by the $a^{-1} x_i$.)

\textbf{¶24.} Let $A$ be a ring, commutative or not, and $\mathcal{F}$ the set of left ideals 1 of $A$ such that $l \cap m \neq 0$ for \emph{every} left ideal $m \neq 0$ of $A$.

In order that $l \in \mathcal{F}$, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every element $a \neq 0$ of $A$, there exist $b \in A$ such that $ba \neq 0$ and $ba \in l$. Every ideal $l$ of $\mathcal{F}$ contains the \emph{left socle} of $A$ (\emph{Algebra}, Chapter VIII, § 5, Exercise 9) and, if $A$ is Artinian, $\mathcal{F}$ consists of the ideals containing the left socle of $A$.

(a) Show that $\mathcal{F}$ is a topologizing set (Exercise 16). Deduce that the set $a$ of $a \in A$ such that $la = 0$ for some $l \in \mathcal{F}$ (union of the right annihilators of the $l \in \mathcal{F}$) is a two-sided ideal of $A$, which contains no idempotent. If $A$ is left Noetherian, show that $a$ is nilpotent (prove first that every element $a \in a$ is nilpotent, by considering the left annihilators of the $a^n$; then use Exercise 26 (b) of \emph{Algebra}, Chapter VIII, § 6).

Let $K$ be a commutative field and $B$ the ring of polynomials with coefficients in $K$ in indeterminates $Y$ and $X_i$, ($i \geq 1$). Let $b$ be the ideal of $B$ generated by the $X_i X_j$ ($i \# j$) and the $Y^{l-1} X_i$ and let $A$ be the quotient ring $B/b$; show that in $A$ the ideal $a$ defined above contains the class of $Y$, which is not nilpotent.

(b) Show that, for every left ideal $m$ of $A$, there exists a left ideal $n$ of $A$ such that $m \cap n = 0$ and $m + n \in \mathcal{O}$ (Take $n$ to be an ideal which is maximal amongst the left ideals $l$ such that $m \cap l = 0$).

(c) A ring $A$ is called (left) \emph{neat} if the two-sided ideal $a$ defined in (a) is reduced to 0. If this is so, the set $\mathcal{F}$ is idempotent (let $l, m, n$ be left ideals of $A$ such that $n \in \mathcal{F}$, $l \subset n$, $n/l$ being F-negligible, and $m \neq 0$. If $x \neq 0$ belongs to $m \cap n$, there exists $r \in \mathcal{F}$ such that $r.x \subset l \cap m$).

\textbf{¶25.} With the notation of Exercise 24, suppose that $A$ is a left neat ring.

(a) Show that the canonical mapping $j_1 : A \to A_{\mathcal{F}}$ is injective, which allows us to identify $A$ with a subgroup of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$. Let $\mathcal{F}'$ be the set of left ideals $l'$ of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ such that $l'_{\mathcal{F}} = A_{\mathcal{F}}$; show that $\mathcal{F}'$ is the set of left ideals $l'$ of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ such that $l' \cap m' \neq 0$ for every left ideal $m' \# 0$ of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$. (Show first that, for every left ideal $m' \neq 0$ of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$, $A \cap m' \neq 0$; note on the other hand that the ideals $l' \in \mathcal{F}'$ are precisely the left ideals of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ containing an ideal $m \in \mathcal{F}$.) Show that the ring $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ is left neat.

(b) Show that $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ is an \emph{injective} $A$-module, that is (\emph{Algebra}, Chapter II, §2, Exercise 11) that, for every left ideal $l$ of $A$ and all
$$
u \in \operatorname{Hom}_A(l, (A_{\mathcal{F}})_s),
$$

there exists $a' \in A_{\mathcal{F}}$ such that $u(x) = xa'$ for all $x \in 1$. (Use Exercise 24 (b) and Exercise 19 (c).) Deduce that every endomorphism of the A-module $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ is of form $u : x \mapsto xa'$ where $a' \in A_{\mathcal{F}}$ (note that $\operatorname{Hom}_A(A, A_{\mathcal{F}}) = \operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\mathcal{F}}}(A_{\mathcal{F}}, A_{\mathcal{F}})$ by Exercise 19 (e)); also $\operatorname{Ker}(u) = (\operatorname{Ker}(u))_{\mathcal{F}}$.

(c) Show that, for every left ideal 1 of A, $1_{\mathcal{F}}$ is a direct factor of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$. (Let m be a left ideal of A such that $m \cap 1 = 0$ and $m + 1 \in \mathcal{F}$; extend the projection of $m + 1$ onto m, which is zero on 1, to an endomorphism $p$ of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$; show that $p^2 = p$ and that $\operatorname{Ker}(p) = 19$.)

(d) Show that $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ is an absolutely flat ring (Chapter I, § 2, Exercise 17). (Reduce it to the case where $A = A_{\mathcal{F}}$. If $u$ is the endomorphism $x \mapsto xa$ of the left A-module $A_s$, then $\operatorname{Ker}(u) = (\operatorname{Ker}(u))_{\mathcal{F}}$, and $\operatorname{Ker}(u)$ therefore admits a complementary ideal m in A.; note that $\operatorname{Im}(u)$ is isomorphic to m, hence injective (Algebra, Chapter II, § 2, Exercise 11) and therefore a direct factor of A, (loc. cit.). Finally, use Exercise 13 of Algebra, Chapter 11, § 1.)

¶26. (a) Show that every Zorn ring A (Algebra, Chapter VIII, § 6, Exercise 13) with no nil ideal $\neq 0$ is left neat; in particular, every absolutely flat ring and every primitive ring whose socle is not reduced to 0 is a left neat ring.

(b) Let A be a left primitive ring whose socle is not reduced to 0; show that, if A is represented as a dense subring of a ring of endomorphisms $\operatorname{End}_D(V)$ of a vector space (Algebra, Chapter VIII, § 5, Exercise 10), the ring $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ corresponding to A (Exercise 25) is isomorphic to $\operatorname{End}_D(V)$.

(c) Show that every quasi-simple ring A (Algebra, Chapter VIII, § 5, Exercise 5) is left and right neat and that the corresponding ring $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ is quasi-simple. Deduce that, for every ring B not reduced to 0, there exists a homomorphism $\phi : B \to R$ to a ring R which is quasi-simple, absolutely flat and such that $R_s$ is an injective R-module.

(d) Let A be a left Noetherian ring with no nilpotent ideals. Show that A is left neat and that the corresponding ring $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ is a semi-simple ring ("Goldie's Theorem"; use Exercise 24 (a) and, on the other hand, use Algebra, Chapter VIII, § 2, Exercise 7, noting that in the absolutely flat ring $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ there cannot exist any family of idempotents which are pairwise orthogonal, unless A contained an infinite direct sum of left ideals $\neq 0$). Show that, if $s \in A$ is not a right divisor of 0 in A, it is invertible in $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ and conversely; the set S of these elements is a multiplicative subset of A. Show that $\mathcal{F}$ is the set of left ideals of A which meet S (to see that the ideals of A meeting S belong to $\mathcal{F}$, note that the elements of S are not left divisors of 0 in A; to see that every $f \in \mathcal{F}$ contains an element of S, represent each simple component of $A_{\mathcal{F}}$ as an endomorphism ring of an n-dimensional vector space E and define by induction n elements $u_1, \ldots, u_n$ such that $u_i u_j = 0$ for $j < i$ and $u_i^2 \neq 0$, the $u_i$ being of rank 1; finally show that the kernel of $s = u_1 + \ldots + u_n$ is zero). Deduce that $(A_{\mathcal{F}}, j_A)$ is a ring of left fractions of A with denominators in S (Exercise 22).

27. (a) Let $A$ be a ring and $M$ a finitely generated faithful $A$-module. Show that, if $a, b$ are two ideals of $A$ such that $aM = bM$, the radicals of $a$ and $b$ are equal (use no. 2, Corollary 2 to Proposition 4).

(b) Deduce from (a) that, if in an integral domain $A$ $p$ is a finitely generated prime ideal, then $p^m \neq p^n$ for $m \neq n$.

(c) Let $K$ be a field and $A$ the polynomial ring $K[X, Y]$ in two indeterminates. Let $a$ be the ideal $AX^4 + AX^3Y + AXY^3 + AY^4$, $b$ the ideal $a + AX^2Y^2$ of $A$; show that $b \neq a$ and $b^2 = a^2$.
