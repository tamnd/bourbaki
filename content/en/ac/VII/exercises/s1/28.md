An integrally closed domain A is said to be *of finite character and rational type* if there exists a family $ (v_i)_{i \in I} $ of valuations on its field of fractions K satisfying properties (AK,,) and (AK_{IM}) and whose value groups are *subgroups of the additive group Q*. Show that the family of essential valuations for A of height 1 satisfy (AK,,) and (AK,). (For all $ i \in I $, let $ p_i $ be the prime ideal of A consisting of the x such that $ v_i(x) > 0 $ and let $ V_i $ be the ring of the valuation $ v_i $. Show that, if there are two indices $ \alpha, \beta $ such that $ p_\beta \subset p_\alpha $, then A is the intersection of the $ V_i $ of index $ i \neq \alpha $. For this, argue by *reductio ad absurdum* by showing that otherwise there would be an element $ x \in K^* $, an element $ y \in p_\beta $ and two positive integers $ r, s $ such that $ v_\beta(x^r y^s) > 0 $, $ v_\alpha(x^r y^s) = 0 $ and $ v_i(x^r y^s) \geq 0 $ for all $ i \in I $, contrary to the hypothesis.)
