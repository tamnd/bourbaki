Let $ \mathbf{A} $ be an integral domain.

(a) For a divisorial ideal $ a $ of $ \mathbf{A} $ to be such that $ \mathrm{div}(a) $ is invertible in $ D(A) $, it is necessary and sufficient that $ a: a = \mathbf{A} $ (cf. Exercise 5 and Algebra, Chapter VI, § 1, Exercise 30). In particular, in order that $ a: a = \mathbf{A} $ for every divisorial ideal $ a $ of $ \mathbf{A} $, it is necessary and sufficient that $ \mathbf{A} $ be completely integrally closed.

(b) In order that $ a: a = \mathbf{A} $ for every finitely generated ideal $ a \neq 0 $ of $ \mathbf{A} $, it is necessary and sufficient that $ \mathbf{A} $ be integrally closed.
