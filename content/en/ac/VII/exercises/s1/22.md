(a) Let $ \Gamma' $ be a lattice-ordered additive group and $ A $ the algebra of $ \Gamma $ over a field $ k $; $ A $ is an integral domain (*Algebra*, Chapter II, § 11, no. 4, Proposition 8). Every element $ x \neq 0 $ in $ A $ may be written uniquely as
$$
x = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i e^{v_i},
$$
where the $ v_i $ are distinct elements of $ \Gamma $, the $ e^{v_i} $ the corresponding elements of the canonical basis of $ A $ over $ k $ and the $ a_i $ elements $ \neq 0 $ of $ k $. Write $ \phi(x) = \inf(v_1, \ldots, v_n) $ in $ \Gamma $; show that $ \phi(x+y) \geq \inf(\phi(x), \phi(y)) $ if $ x, y $ and $ x+y $ are $ \neq 0 $ in $ A $ and $ \phi(xy) = \phi(x) + \phi(y) $ if $ xy \neq 0 $ in $ A $. (To show the second assertion, establish first the following lemma: given a finite family $ (\xi_j) $ of elements of $ \Gamma $, in order that $ \inf(\xi_j) = 0 $, it is necessary and sufficient that for all $ \eta > 0 $ in $ \Gamma' $ there exist an index $ j $ and an element $ \zeta \in \Gamma $ such that $ 0 < \zeta \leq \eta $ and $ \inf(\xi_j, \zeta) = 0 $. Apply this lemma reducing it to the case where $ \phi(x) = \phi(y) = 0 $.)

(b) Deduce from (a) that, if $ K $ is the field of fractions of $ A $, $ \phi $ can be extended to a homomorphism from $ K^* $ to $ \Gamma $ (also denoted by $ \phi $) such that
$$
\phi(x+y) \geq \inf(\phi(x), \phi(y))
$$
if $ x, y $ and $ x+y $ are $ \neq 0 $ in $ K $. Deduce that, if $ B $ is the set of $ x \in K $ such that $ x = 0 $ or $ \phi(x) \geq 0 $, $ B $ is a domain whose field of fractions is $ K $ and such that, if $ U $ is the group of invertible elements of $ B $, $ K^*/U $ is isomorphic to $ \Gamma $ (and in particular $ B $ is a pseudo-Bezoutian domain). Derive examples of a pseudo-Bezoutian domain which is not completely integrally closed, a completely integrally closed pseudo-Bezoutian domain which is not pseudo-principal (cf.

Algebra, Chapter VI, § 1, Exercise 31) \* and a pseudo-principal domain which is not factorial. \*

(c) Derive from (b) an example of an integral domain which is the union of a right directed family of factorial subdomains and is completely integrally closed but not pseudo-Bezoutian. (Let $ \theta $ be an irrational number in $[0, 1]$ and for every integer $ j $ let $ q_j $ be the greatest integer such that $ q_j / 2^j < 0 $. For all $ j $ define on the product group $ \mathbf{Q} \times \mathbf{Q} $ a lattice-ordered group structure by taking the set $ (\mathcal{G}_j)_+ $ of elements $ \geq 0 $ of this group to be the ordered pairs $ (\xi, \eta) $ such that $ \xi \geq 0 $ and $ 0 \leq \eta \leq (q_j 2^{-j}) \xi $. *)
