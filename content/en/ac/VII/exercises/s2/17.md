(a) Let A be an integral domain; show that in the ring $ A[X_{ij}] $, where $ (X_{ij}) $ is a family of $ n^2 $ indeterminates $ (1 \leq i \leq n, 1 \leq j \leq n) $, the element $ \det(X_{ij}) $ is extremal. (Reduce it to the case where A is a field; observe that the factors of $ \det(X_{ij}) $ would necessarily be homogeneous polynomials and argue by induction on n)
(b) Let K be an infinite field and F a polynomial in $ K[Y_1, \ldots, Y_m] $, which is also written as $ F(Y) $; for every square matrix $ s = (\alpha_{ij}) $ of order m with elements in K let $ F(s, Y) $ denote the polynomial F, where the element $ \sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_{ij} Y_j $ has been substituted for each $ Y_j $. Show that, if F is extremal, so is $ F(s, Y) $ for every invertible matrix s. If there exists an integer $ k \geq 0 $ such that

$$
F(s, Y) = (\det(s))^k F(Y)
$$

for every invertible matrix s, F is necessarily homogeneous in each of the $ Y_j $;

moreover, if $ F = GH $ where G and H are two polynomials in $ K[Y_1, \ldots, Y_m] $, there exist two integers $ p $ and $ q $ such that $ p + q = k $ and
$$
G(s, Y) = (\det(s))^p G(Y), \qquad H(s, Y) = (\det(s))^q H(Y)
$$
for every invertible matrix $ s $ (use (a)).

(c) Let A be a ring which is not reduced to 0; consider the polynomial ring $ A[X_{ij}] $, where the $ X_{ij} $ are $ n(n+1)/2 $ (resp. $ 2n(2n-1)/2 $) indeterminates with $ 1 \leq i \leq j \leq n $ (resp. $ 1 \leq i < j \leq 2n $); let $ U = (\xi_{ij}) $ (resp. $ V = (\eta_{ij}) $) be the square matrix of order n (resp. $ 2n $) over $ A[X_{ij}] $ such that $ \xi_{ij} = X_{ij} $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq j \leq n $ and $ \xi_{ij} = X_{ji} $ for $ i > j $ (resp. $ \eta_{ii} = 0 $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq 2n $, $ \eta_{ij} = X_{ij} $ for $ 1 \leq i < j \leq 2n $, $ \eta_{ij} = -X_{ji} $ for $ i > j $). Show that $ \det(U) $ (resp. $ \mathrm{Pf}(V) $) is an extremal element in $ A[X_{ij}] $ (argue as in (b), considering $ \det(s \cdot U \cdot t_s) $ and $ \mathrm{Pf}(s \cdot V \cdot t_s) $).
