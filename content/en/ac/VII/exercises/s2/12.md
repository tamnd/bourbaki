Let $A$ be an integral domain and $K$ its field of fractions. Show that the following properties are equivalent:
(a) For every prime ideal $p$ of $A$, the local ring $A_p$ is a valuation ring.
(b) For every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$, the local ring $A_m$ is a valuation ring.

(y) Every finitely generated ideal $\neq 0$ in $\mathbf{A}$ is invertible (and therefore the finitely generated fractional ideals $\neq 0$ form a group).

(6) Every torsion-free finitely generated $\mathbf{A}$-module is projective.

(E) For all $x \in K$, the fractional ideal $\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}x$ is invertible.

($\zeta$) For all $x \neq 0$ in $K$, there exists $y \in K$ such that $y \equiv 0 \pmod{1}$, $y \equiv 0 \pmod{x}$ and $y \equiv 1 \pmod{1-x}$.

($\eta$) If $(a_i)$ is a finite family of ideals of $A$, for the system of congruences $x \equiv c_i \pmod{a_i}$ to have a solution it is necessary and sufficient that $c_i \equiv c_j \pmod{(a_i + a_j)}$ for every pair of indices $i, j$ ("Chinese remainder theorem").

($\theta$) If $a, b, c$ are three ideals of $\mathbf{A}$, then $a \cap (b + c) = a \cap b + a \cap c$.

(t) If $a, b, c$ are three ideals of $\mathbf{A}$, then $a + (b \cap c) = (a + b) \cap (a + c)$.

(x) $\mathbf{A}$ is integrally closed and every finitely generated ideal of $\mathbf{A}$ is divisorial.

($\lambda$) $\mathbf{A}$ is integrally closed and for $z \neq 0$ in $K$ there exist $x, y$ in $\mathbf{A}$ such that $z = x + yz^2$.

(p) $\mathbf{A}$ is integrally closed and, if $a, b, c$ are three finitely generated fractional ideals $\neq 0$, the relation $ab = ac$ implies $b = c$.

(v) For every finitely generated $\mathbf{A}$-module $M$, the torsion submodule of $M$ is a direct factor of $M$.

($\sigma$) Every ring $B$ such that $\mathbf{A} \subset B \subset K$ is an integrally closed domain.

Then $\mathbf{A}$ is called a *Prüferian* domain (or a *Prüfer domain*).

(To prove the equivalence of ($a$), ($\beta$), (y) and ($\delta$), use Chapter II, § 5, no. 2, Theorem 1 and no. 6, Theorem 4. To prove that ($E$) implies (y), use the identity $(a + b)(b + c)(c + a) = (a + b + c)(ab + bc + ca)$ between three fractional ideals in an integral domain. To prove that ($\zeta$) implies ($\eta$), argue by induction on the number of $a_i$. The equivalence of (q), (l) and (t) has been shown in Algebra, Chapter VI, § 1, Exercise 25. Prove that (A) implies ($E$) by noting that ($\lambda$) implies the relation

$$
x(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}z) \subset z(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}z),
$$

using Exercise 6 (b) of § 1 and noting that

$$
(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}z)(Ay + \mathbf{A}x/z) = \mathbf{A}.
$$

To prove that (p) implies (A) noting that always

$$
z(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}z) \subset (\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}z^2)(\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{A}z).
$$

Prove that (x) implies ($\lambda$) noting that every fractional principal ideal $\mathbf{A}$ which contained $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{A}z^2$ also contains $\mathbf{A}z$. To prove that (v) implies (y), consider, for a finitely generated integral ideal $a \neq 0$ of $\mathbf{A}$, a non-zero element $c \in a(A : a)$ and apply Exercise 32 of § 1 to the ideal $b = ca$. To prove that ($s$) implies ($\beta$), reduce it to the case where $\mathbf{A}$ is a local ring, consider an element $z \in K - \mathbf{A}$ and show that $z^{-1} \in \mathbf{A}$, noting that the hypothesis implies that $z \in \mathbf{A}[z^2]$.

§ 13. (a) In a Priiferian domain A, let $a, b$ be two finitely generated ideals; show that, if $b \notin a$, there exists a finitely generated ideal $c$ such that $a \subset c$, $a \neq c$ and $bc \subset a$ (consider the ideal $a + b$).

(b) Deduce from (a) that, if $a$ is a finitely generated ideal in a Priiferian domain A, then in the ring $A/a$ every element which is not a divisor of zero is invertible. In particular, a prime ideal of A cannot be finitely generated if it is maximal.

(c) Show that in a Priiferian domain A two prime ideals $p, p'$ neither of which is contained in the other are relatively prime (consider, for every maximal ideal $m$ of A, the ideal $pA_m + p'A_m$).

(d) In a Priiferian domain A let $a$ be a finitely generated ideal. For $a$ to be primal (Chapter IV, § 2, Exercise 33), it is necessary and sufficient that $a$ be contained only in a single maximal ideal of A (use (b)); $a$ is then irreducible and quasi-prime (Chapter IV, § 2, Exercise 34), so that, for finitely generated ideals, these three notions coincide. For $a$ to be primary, it is necessary and sufficient that it be contained only in a single prime ideal (necessarily maximal) $m$; for $a$ to be strongly primary (Chapter IV, § 2, Exercise 27), it is necessary and sufficient also that $mA_m$ be principal.

¶ 19. An integral domain $A$ is called *pseudo-Priiferian* if the set of finitely generated divisors of $D(A)$ ($\S 1$, Exercise 11) is a *group*. A pseudo-Priiferian domain is regularly integrally closed ($\S 1$, Exercise 29). A pseudo-Bezoutian domain ($\S 1$, Exercise 21) is pseudo-Priiferian; a Priiferian domain is pseudo-Priiferian; a Krull domain is pseudo-Priiferian.

(a) Show by examples that the converses of the last three assertions are not necessarily true.

(b) Let $\theta$ be an irrational number $> 0$ and $\Gamma$ the group $\mathbf{R}^2$ ordered by taking the set of ordered pairs $(a, \beta)$ such that $a \geq 0$ and $\beta \geq \theta a$ as the set of positive elements; the ordered group $\Gamma$ is a complete lattice. Let $B$ be the pseudo-principal domain derived from $\Gamma$ by the procedure of $\S 1$, Exercise 22 and let $A$ be the subring the intersection of $B$ and the algebra over $k$ of the subgroup $\mathbf{Q}^2$ of $\mathbf{R}^2$. Show that $A$ is a completely integrally closed domain but is not pseudo-Priiferian (note that, if $K$ is the field of fractions of $A$ and $U$ the group of invertible elements of $A$, the ordered group $K^*/U$ is isomorphic to the group $\Gamma \cap \mathbf{Q}^2$, ordered by the ordering induced by that on $\Gamma$).

(c) If the domain $\mathbf{A}$ is pseudo-Pruferian, show that the polynomial domain $\mathbf{A}[X]$ is pseudo-Pruferian (cf. § 1, Exercise 30 (c)).

(d) Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a pseudo-Pruferian domain and $\mathbf{K}$ its field of fractions. Show that the integral closure $\mathbf{B}$ of $\mathbf{A}$ in an algebraic extension $\mathbf{L}$ of $\mathbf{K}$ is a pseudo-Pruferian domain (method of Exercise 16, using § 1, Exercise 29 (a)).

§ 3

1. For an integral domain to be factorial, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a mapping $x \mapsto s(x)$ of $\mathbf{A} - \{0\}$ to $\mathbf{N}$ such that $s(xy) = s(x) + s(y)$, the relation $s(x) = 0$ implies that $x$ is invertible in $\mathbf{A}$ and finally, for any two elements $x, y$ of $\mathbf{A} - \{0\}$, neither of which divides the other, there exist elements $a, b, z, t$ of $\mathbf{A} - \{0\}$ such that $ax + by = zt, s(z) < s(x)$, $t$ being prime to $x$ and toy. (If this condition is fulfilled, show first that every non-zero element of $\mathbf{A}$ is a product of extremal elements and then that, for every extremal element $p, Ap$ is a prime ideal; conclude with the aid of Theorem 1 (d)).

2. (a) Let $\mathbf{A}$ be an integral domain, the union of a right directed family $(\mathbf{A}_\lambda)$ of subrings. Suppose that each of the $\mathbf{A}_\lambda$ is factorial and that, if $\lambda \leq \mu$, every extremal element of $\mathbf{A}_\lambda$ is extremal in $\mathbf{A}_\mu$. Show that every domain of formal power series $\mathbf{A}[[X_\lambda]]_{\lambda \in L}$ in any family of indeterminates (Algebra, Chapter IV, § 5, Exercise 1) is factorial (same method).

3. (a) Let $\mathbf{A} = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathbf{A}_n$ be a graded algebra with positive degrees over a field $k$; suppose that $\mathbf{A}_0 = k$ and that $\mathbf{A}$ is a Krull domain.
