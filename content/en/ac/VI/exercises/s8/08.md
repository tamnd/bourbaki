Let $ K' $ be a field, $ B $ the ring of a valuation $ v $ on $ K' $, $ G $ a finite group of automorphisms of $ K' $, $ K $ the subfield of $ K' $ consisting of the elements invariant under $ G $ and $ A = K \cap B $, which is a valuation ring of $ K $, corresponding to the valuation $ w = v|K $.

(a) The valuations on $ K' $ which extend $ w $ are the $ v \circ \sigma $, where $ \sigma \in G $ (no. 6, Corollary 1 to Proposition 6) and the integral closure $ A $ of $ A $ in $ K' $ is the intersection of the $ \sigma . B $, where $ \sigma $ runs through $ G $. If $ p(B) $ is the intersection of $ A' $ and the maximal ideal $ m(B) $, then $ \sigma . p(B) = p(\sigma . B) $. Show that the decomposition group $ G^Z(p(B)) $ (Chapter V, § 2, no. 2) is the subgroup of $ G $ consisting of the $ \sigma $ such that $ \sigma . B = B $; write $ G^Z = G^Z(p(B)) $ and denote by $ K^Z $ the subfield of $ K' $ consisting of the elements invariant under $ G^Z $ and by $ B^Z $ the ring of the valuation induced on $ K^Z $ by $ v $, which is equal to $ B \cap K^Z $; recall that the residue fields of $ B^Z $ and $ A $ are the same and that the maximal ideal $ m(B^Z) $ is generated by $ m(A)B^Z $ (Chapter V, § 2, no. 2, Proposition 4).

(b) Let $ v^Z $ denote the restriction of $ v $ to $ K^Z $ and $ \Gamma $ and $ \Gamma^Z $ the order groups of $ w $ and $ v^Z $. Show that $ \Gamma = \Gamma^Z $. (Let $ G^D \supset G^Z $ be the subgroup of $ G $ consisting of the $ \sigma \in G $ such that $ \sigma . B $ is dependent on $ B $ (§ 7, no. 2). Argue by induction on the order of $ G $. If $ (G/G^D) > 1 $, let $ K'' $ be the field of invariants of $ G^D $ and $ A'' = K'' \cap B $; show that the order group of $ v|K'' $ is equal to $ \Gamma $: for this, use the Approximation Theorem (§ 7, no. 2, Theorem 1) in order to prove that, for all x \in K", there exists y \in K" such that v(y) = v(x) and v(y_i) = 0 for the conjugates y, of y with respect to K, distinct from y; then $ \mathcal{G} $ may be replaced by $ \mathcal{G}^D $ and the induction hypothesis may be applied. If $ \mathcal{G}^D = \mathcal{G} $, let B, be the valuation ring of K' generated by the $ \sigma . B $, where $ \sigma \in \mathcal{G} $; let $ \overline{K}' = \kappa(B_0) $ be its residue field, $ x : B, \to \overline{K}' $ the canonical homomorphism, $ \overline{B} = \pi(B) $ a valuation ring of $ \overline{K}' $ and $ \bar{v} $ the corresponding valuation on $ \overline{K}' $ such that $ v = \bar{v} \circ \pi $ in B, so that the order group $ \overline{\Delta} $ of $ \bar{v} $ is a subgroup of the order group A of v; if $ A_1 = \Delta / \overline{\Delta} $ and $ \overline{\omega} : A \to A_1 $, is the canonical homomorphism, $ v_0 = \overline{\omega} \circ v $ is a valuation on K' corresponding to B_0 and invariant under $ \mathcal{G} $. By taking quotients, $ \mathcal{G} $ defines a group of automorphisms $ \overline{\mathcal{G}} $ of $ \overline{K}' $; let $ \mathcal{N} $ be the kernel of the canonical homomorphism $ \mathcal{G} \to \overline{\mathcal{G}} $; show that $ \mathcal{N} \subset \mathcal{G}^z $ and deduce that, if $ \mathcal{N} \neq \{ e \} $, $ \mathcal{G} $ may be replaced by $ \mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N} $ and the induction hypothesis may be applied. Suppose finally that $ \mathcal{N} = \{ e \} $; let $ A_1 = K \cap B $, and $ w_0 $ be the restriction of $ v_0 $ to K; show that the order group of $ w_0 $ is A, and that $ \pi(A_0) = \overline{K} $ is the field of invariants of $ \overline{\mathcal{G}} $ (cf. no. 1, Lemma 2); if $ \bar{w} $ is the restriction to $ \overline{K} $ of $ \bar{v} $ and $ \overline{\Gamma} $ its order group, A, is canonically isomorphic to $ \Gamma/\overline{\Gamma} $. On the other hand, the group $ \mathcal{G}^z(p(\overline{B})) $ is the canonical image of $ \mathcal{G}^z(p(B)) $ in $ \overline{\mathcal{G}} $; observe that $ \mathcal{G}^D \neq \overline{\mathcal{G}} $ and that the induction hypothesis may therefore be applied to prove that $ \overline{\Gamma} $ is equal to the order group $ \overline{\Gamma}^z $ of the restriction of $ \bar{v} $ to $ \overline{K}^z $.

(c) Let $ \mathcal{G}^T = \mathcal{G}^T(p(B)) $ be the inertia group of $ p(B) $ (Chapter V, § 2, no. 2) and let $ K^T $ denote the subfield of K' consisting of the invariants of $ \mathcal{G}^T $ and $ B^T $ the ring of the valuation induced on $ K^T $ by $ u $, which is equal to $ B \cap K^T $; recall that, if k and k' are the residue fields of A and B, the residue field $ k^T $ of $ B^T $ is the greatest separable extension of k contained in the quasi-Galois extension $ k' $ of k and that $ \mathcal{G}^z/\mathcal{G}^T $ is canonically isomorphic to the group of k-automorphisms of $ k' $ (or $ k^T $) (Chapter V, § 2, Theorem 2 and Proposition 5). Let $ v^T $ be the restriction of $ v $ to $ K^T $; show that the order group of $ v^T $ is also equal to $ \Gamma $ (apply Lemma 2 of no. 1 to the extension $ K^T $ of $ K^z $).
