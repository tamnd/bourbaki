Let K be a field, v a valuation on K and A the ring of v.

(a) Suppose that A is Henselian (Chapter III, § 4, Exercise 3) in which case we also say, by an abuse of language, that K is *Henselian for v*. Then, for every algebraic extension L of K and every valuation v' on L extending v, the ring A' of v' is Henselian (Chapter III, § 4, Exercise 4).

(b) If K is complete with v and v is of height 1, A is Henselian. Give an example where K is complete with v and v is of height 2, but A is not Henselian (cf. Exercise 1).

(c) If K is *linearly compact* with v, show that K is Henselian. (In the notation condition (H) of Chapter III, § 4, Exercise 3, let $ \mathcal{L} $ denote the set of prime ideals of A (totally) ordered by inclusion and $ \mathfrak{L} $ the set of ordered pairs ($ \mathfrak{B}_+ $), where $ \mathfrak{B} $ is a well ordered subset (with respect to the relation $ \supset $) of $ \mathcal{L} $ and $ \phi : p \mapsto (Q_p, Q'_p) $ a mapping from $ \mathfrak{B} $ to the set $ A[X] \times A[X] $, with the following properties: (1) Q, and $ Q'_p $ are monic of respective degrees $ \deg(\overline{Q}) $ and $ \deg(\overline{Q'}) $; (2) if p $ \supset q $ are two elements of $ \mathfrak{B} $, the coefficients of the polynomials $ Q - Q_p $ and $ Q'_p - Q'_a $ belong to p; (3) the coefficientsof $ P - Q_p Q'_p $ belong to p; (4) $ \tilde{f}(Q_p) = \overline{Q} $, $ \tilde{f}(Q'_p) = \overline{Q}' $. Define on $ \mathcal{L} $ an ordering by setting $ (\mathfrak{B}, \phi) \leq (\mathfrak{B}', +') $ when $ \mathfrak{B} \subset \mathfrak{B}' $ and $ \phi' $ is an extension of $ \phi $ to $ \mathfrak{B}' $.Prove that $ \mathcal{L} $ admits a maximal element $ (\mathfrak{B}_0, \phi_0) $ and that $ \mathfrak{B}_0 $ has a last element equal to (0): obtain a contradiction from the opposite by considering two cases, according to whether or not $ \mathfrak{B}_0 $ has a last element; in the former case, if p is this last element, consider an element c $ \in A $ such that $ v(c) $ is the least value taken by v on the set of coefficientsof $ P - Q_p Q'_p $ and the least prime ideal p' of A containing c; if $ p' = p $, use Exercise 2 (a) of § 4 and the fact that a quotient of a linearly compact module by a closed submodule is linearly compact and hence complete. If on the contrary $ \mathfrak{B}_0 $ has no last element, use directly the hypothesis that A is linearly compact.)

(d) In the notation of Exercise 5, show that for $ A $ to be Henselian, it is necessary and sufficient that $ A' $ and $ A $ be so. (Note that, if $ P \in A'[X] $, there exists $ a \in A $ such that $ a^n P(X/a) \in A[X] $ (where $ n $ is the degree of $ P $); to verify axiom (H) of Chapter III, § 4, Exercise 3 for $ A' $, attention may be confined to the polynomials of $ A[X] $; use the fact that $ A' = A_p $, where $ p $ is a prime ideal of $ A $ contained in the maximal ideal $ m $, and note that, if two polynomials in $ (A/p)[X] $ are strongly relatively prime, so are their images in $ (A/m)[X] $.)

(e) Suppose that $ A $ is Henselian; then, for every algebraic extension $ L $ of $ K $, there exists (up to equivalence) only one valuation on $ L $ extending $ v $. (If $ P \in A[X] $ is a monic irreducible polynomial, $ x_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq m $) the distinct roots of $ P $ in its splitting field $ N $, show that, for every valuation $ v' $ on $ N $ extending $ v $, the $ v'(x_i) $ are all equal for $ 1 \leq i \leq m $.) Conversely, if the valuation ring $ A $ has this property, it is Henselian (observe that, in the integral closure $ A' $ of $ A $ in $ L $, there can only exist one maximal ideal lying above $ m $ (Chapter V, § 1, Exercise 13) and conclude that, if $ P \in A[X] $ is a monic irreducible polynomial its image in $ (A/m)[X] $ cannot be a product of two relatively prime polynomials).
