(a) Let L be a field and T an indeterminate; in the field of formal power series $ L((T)) $, consider the series

$$
s = c_0 + c_1 T^{1!} + c_2 T^{2!} + \ldots + c_n T^{n!} + \ldots
$$

where the $ c_i \in L $ are all $ \neq 0 $. Show that s is transcendental over the subfield $ L(T) $ of $ L((T)) $. (Argue by reductio ad absurdum by supposing that there exists a relation $ a_0(T) + a_1(T)s + \ldots + a_q(T)s^q = 0 $ where $ a_i(T) $ are polynomials in $ L(T) $ such that $ a_i \neq 0 $; construct the equation satisfied by

$$
s' = s - c_0 - c_1 T^{1!} - \ldots - c_{n-1} T^{(n-1)!}
$$

and show that for n sufficiently large its constant term is a polynomial of degree $ < n! $, whence our contradiction.)

(b) Let k be a field, X, Y, Z, T four indeterminates, $ K = k(X, Y, Z) $ and E an algebraic closure of $ k(X) $; in E, for all n, let $ x_n $ denote an element such that $ x_n^n = X $. In the field of formal power series $ E((T)) $, consider the element

$$
s = x_1 T + x_2 T^{2!} + \ldots + x_n T^{n!} + \ldots
$$

The elements s and T are algebraically independent over E by (a). Consider the homomorphism $ f : K \to E((T)) $ such that $ f(X) = X, f(Y) = T, f(Z) = s $. If v is the discrete valuation on $ E((T)) $ defined by the order of formal power series (no. 3, Example 3), $ v \circ f $ is a discrete valuation on $ K $; show that the residue field of $ v \circ f $ is the subfield $ k(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n, \ldots) $ of $ E $ and is therefore infinite over $ k $.
