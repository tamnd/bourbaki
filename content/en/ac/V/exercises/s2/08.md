A local ring $ A $ is called unibranche if its integral closure is a local ring.
(a) Let $ A $ be a local integral domain and $ K $ its field of fractions. For $ A $ to be unibranche, it is necessary and sufficient that every sub-$ A $-algebra of $ K $ which is a finitely generated $ A $-module be a local ring.
(b) Show that, if the completion of a Noetherian local ring $ A $ is an integral domain, $ A $ is unibranche (if $ B $ is a finite $ A $-algebra contained in $ K $, show that the subring of the field of fractions of $ \hat{A} $ generated by $ B $ and $ \hat{A} $ is isomorphic to $ B \otimes_A \hat{A} $, using the flatness of the $ A $-module $ \hat{A} $ and the fact that $ B $ is contained in a finitely generated free $ A $-module contained in $ K $; then use (a) and Chapter III, § 2, no. 13, Corollary to Proposition 19).
