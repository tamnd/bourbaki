$a)$ Let $g$ be a function defined and admitting a continuous $k^{th}$ derivative on ]0, $+\infty[$, such that $g^{(k)}$ is decreasing on this interval and that $\lim_{x \to +\infty} g^{(k)}(x) = 0$. Show that there exists one and only one solution $u$ of the equation $u(x + 1) - u(x) = g(x)$ which admits an increasing $k^{th}$ derivative on ]0, $+\infty[$ and takes a given value at a given point (use exerc. 1 b)).

$b)$ Let $\alpha$ be an arbitrary real number. Let $S_\alpha$ be the function defined on ]0, $+\infty[$ satisfying the relation
$$
S_\alpha(x + 1) - S_\alpha(x) = x^\alpha
$$
such that $S_\alpha(1) = 0$, and, further, that the derivative of $S_\alpha$ of order equal to the integer part of $(\alpha + 1)^+$ is increasing (it is a unique function by $a$) ). Show that
$$
S'_\alpha(x) - S'_\alpha(1) = \alpha\, S_{\alpha - 1}(x),
$$
and
$$
S_\alpha \left( \frac{x}{p} \right) + S_\alpha \left( \frac{x + 1}{p} \right) + \cdots + S_\alpha \left( \frac{x + p - 1}{p} \right) = \frac{1}{p^\alpha}\, S_\alpha(x) + C_\alpha
$$
for every integer $p \geq 1$, where $C_\alpha$ is a constant, which, when $\alpha \neq -1$, is equal to
$$
\left( \frac{1}{p^\alpha} - p \right) \frac{S'_{\alpha + 1}(1)}{\alpha + 1}
$$
(cf. VII, p.318, prop. 2 and VI, p. 291, exerc. 3).
