a) Let $ (f_n) $ be a sequence of increasing continuous functions belonging to $ \mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{F}, \mathbf{R}) $ and such that $ f_n \ll f_{n+1} $ for every $ n $. Show that there exists a function $ f $ that is continuous, increasing, belongs to $ \mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{F}, \mathbf{R}) $, and is such that $ f \gg f_n $ for every $ n $ (reduce to the case where $ f_n \leq f_{n+1} $ and define $ f $ so that $ f_n(x) \leq f(x) \leq f_{n+1}(x) $ for $ n \leq x \leq n+1 $).

b) Let $ (f_n) $ be a sequence of increasing continuous functions belonging to $ \mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{F}, \mathbf{R}) $ and such that $ 1 \ll f_{n+1} \ll f_n $ for every $ n $. Show that there exists a function $ f $ that is continuous, increasing, belongs to $ \mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{F}, \mathbf{R}) $, and is such that $ 1 \ll f \ll f_n $ for every $ n $ (reduce to the case where $ f_{n+1} \leq f_n $ and show that one can define an increasing sequence $ (x_n) $ of real numbers and a continuous increasing function $ f $ such that $ f_{n+1}(x) \leq f(x) \leq f_n(x) $ for $ x_n \leq x \leq x_{n+1} $).

c) Let $ (f_n), (g_n) $ be two sequences of continuous increasing functions belonging to $ \mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{F}, \mathbf{R}) $ such that $ f_n \ll f_{n+1}, g_m \gg g_{m+1} $ and $ f_n \ll g_m $ for all $ m $ and $ n $; show that there exists a function $ h $, continuous, increasing, belonging to $ \mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{F}, \mathbf{R}) $, such that $ f_n \ll h \ll g_m $ for all $ m $ and $ n $ (similar method).

In particular, show that there exists a continuous decreasing function $ f $ in $ \mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{F}, \mathbf{R}) $ such that *no* logarithmic criterion allows one to determine whether the integral $ \int_a^{+\infty} f(t)\,dt $ converges or is infinite ("*theorems of Du Bois-Reymond*"").
