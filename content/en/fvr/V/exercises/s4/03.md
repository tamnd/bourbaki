Show that, for every convergent series $(u_n)$ with terms $> 0$, there exists a series $(v_n)$ with infinite sum, and terms $> 0$, such that $\liminf_{n \to \infty} v_n / u_n = 0$.

(u_n) be a decreasing sequence of numbers > 0; if there exists an integer k such $u_{kn} \geq u_n$ for all n from some point on, then the series with general term $u_n$ has an e sum.

(u_n) be a series with terms > 0 from some point on. Show that if
$$
\limsup_{n \to \infty} \left( \frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n} \right)^n < \frac{1}{e}
$$
he series converges, and if $\liminf_{n \to \infty} \left( \frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n} \right)^n \geq \frac{1}{e}$ the series has an infinite sum.

(u_n) be a series of reals $\neq 0$ such that $\lim_{n \to \infty} u_n = 0$. Show that if there exists a $r > r$ such that $0 < r < 1$ and if from some point on one has $-1 \leq u_{n+1}/u_n \leq r$, he series (u_n) converges.

(u_n) be a convergent series with terms > 0.
Show that
$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{u_1 + 2u_2 + \cdots + nu_n}{n} = 0.
$$
Show that
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{u_1 + 2u_2 + \cdots + nu_n}{n(n+1)} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} u_n
$$
$$
\frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1}
$$
duce from a) and b) that
$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} n(u_1 u_2 \ldots u_n)^{1/n} = 0
$$
at
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(n! u_1 u_2 \ldots u_n)^{1/n}}{n+1} < \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} u_n
$$
the inequality (8) of III, p. 93).

) Let (z_n) be a sequence of complex numbers. Show that if the series with general $z_n, z_n^2, \ldots, z_n^{q-1}, |z_n|^q$ converge, then the infinite product with general factor $1 + z_n$ converges.

$z_n$ is real and if the series with general term $z_n$ converges, then the infinite product general factor $1 + z_n$ converges if the series with general term $z_n^2$ converges, and one
$$
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty} (1 + z_n) = 0 \quad \text{if} \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} z_n^2 = +\infty.
$$
every integer $p \geq 2$ put
$$
k_p = [\log \log p], \quad h_p = \sum_{j=1}^{p-1} k_j, \quad \omega_p = \frac{2\pi}{k_p}.
$$

Define the sequence $(z_n)$ of complex numbers in the following way: for $n = h_p + m$, $0 \leq m \leq k_p - 1$, put $z_n = e^{m i \omega_p} / \log p$. Show that for *every* integer $q > 0$ the series with general term $z_n^q$ converges, but that the product with general factor $1 + z_n$ does not converge.

9) *a*) Show, with the help of Stirling’s formula, that the maximum of the function $f_n(x) = \left| e^{2x} - e^{-x} \sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^k \frac{x^k}{k!} \right|$ over the interval $[0, +\infty[$ tends to 0 with $1/n$.

*b*) Deduce, by induction on the integer $p$, that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists a polynomial $g(x)$ such that $\left| e^{px} - e^{-x} g(x) \right| \leq \varepsilon$ for every $x \geq 0$ (replace $x$ by $px/2$ in *a*), and apply the induction hypothesis to $e^{(p-1)x/2}$.

10) For every number $\alpha > 0$ derive the formula
$$
1^{\alpha n} + 2^{\alpha n} + \cdots + n^{\alpha n} \sim \frac{n^{\alpha n}}{1 - e^{-\alpha}}
$$
as $n$ tends to $+\infty$.

11) For every number $\alpha > 0$ derive the formula
$$
(1!)^{-\alpha/n} + (2!)^{-\alpha/n} + \cdots + (n!)^{-\alpha/n} \sim \frac{1}{\alpha} \frac{n}{\log n}
$$
as $n$ tends to $+\infty$ (compare each term $(p!)^{-\alpha/n}$ to $n^{-\alpha p/n}$).
