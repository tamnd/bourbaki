Let $ f $ be a continuous and differentiable map of $ \mathbf{R} $ into a complete normed algebra $ E $ over $ \mathbf{R} $ having a unit element $ e $; suppose that $ f(0) = e $ and that, identically, $ f'(x) = f(x)c $ where $ c $ is an invertible element of $ E $. Show that $ f $ is a homomorphism of the additive group $ \mathbf{R} $ into the multiplicative group of invertible elements of $ E $. (Consider the largest open interval $ I $ containing 0 such that $ f(z) $ is invertible for all $ z \in I $, and prove that for $ x, y $ and $ x + y $ in $ I $ one has $ f(x + y)(f(x)f(y))^{-1} = e $ keeping $ x $ fixed and letting $ y $ vary; then deduce that $ I = \mathbf{R} $.)
