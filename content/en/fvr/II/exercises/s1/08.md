Show that there exists a continuous function on $ [-1, +1] $ admitting a finite derivative at every point of this interval, this derivative being equal to $ \sin \left( \frac{1}{\sin 1/x} \right) $ at points $ x $ different from $ 1/n\pi $ ($ n $ an integer $ \neq 0 $) and from 0. (On a neighbourhood of $ x = 1/n\pi $ make the change of variable $ x = \frac{1}{n\pi + \operatorname{Arc}\sin u} $ and use exerc. 7; by means of the same change of variable, show that there exists a constant $ a > 0 $ independent of $ n $ such that

$$
\left| \int_{2/(2n-1)\pi}^{2/(2n+1)\pi} \sin \left( \frac{1}{\sin \frac{1}{x}} \right) dx \right| \leq \frac{a}{n^3};
$$

and deduce that if one puts

$$
g(x) = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \int_x^1 \sin \left( \frac{1}{\sin \frac{1}{t}} \right) dt,
$$

then $ g $ has a derivative equal to 0 at the point $ x = 0 $.)
