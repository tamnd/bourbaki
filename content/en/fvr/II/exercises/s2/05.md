a) Let $ \mathbf{f} $ be a regulated function on a *bounded* open interval ]$a, b$*; suppose that there exists a real function $ g $, decreasing on ]$a, b$*, such that $ \| \mathbf{f}(x) \| \leqslant g(x) $ on ]$a, b$* and such that the integral $ \int_a^b g(t)\,dt $ converges. Show that if $ (\varepsilon_n) $ is a sequence of numbers $ > 0 $, tending to 0, and such that $ \inf_{n \geqslant 1} n \varepsilon_n > 0 $, one has
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \mathbf{f}\left(a + \varepsilon_n + k \frac{b-a}{n}\right) = \int_a^b \mathbf{f}(t)\,dt.\tag{*}
$$
b) Give an example of a real regulated function $ f > 0 $ on ]0, 1] such that the integral $ \int_0^1 f(t)\,dt $ converges, and yet the relation (*) does not hold for $ \varepsilon_n = 1/n $ (take $ f $ so that its value for $ x = 2^{-n} $ is $ 2^{2^n} $).

c) With the same hypotheses as in a) show that
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n (-1)^k \mathbf{f}\left(a + k \frac{b-a}{n}\right) = 0.
$$
