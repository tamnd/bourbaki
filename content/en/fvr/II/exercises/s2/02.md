$ a) $ Let $ \mathbf{f} $ be a regulated function on an interval $[0, a]$ such that the integral $ \int_0^a \frac{\mathbf{f}(t)}{t}\, dt $ is convergent. Show that the integral $ g(x) = \int_0^x \mathbf{f}(t)\, dt $ is convergent and that $ g $ admits a right derivative at the point $ x = 0 $ (integrate suitably by parts).

$ b) $ Give an example of a real function $ f $ such that the integral $ g(x) = \int_0^x f(t)\, dt $ is convergent and admits a right derivative at the point 0, and yet $ f(t)/t $ does not have an integral on ]0, a] (take for $ f(x)/x $ the derivative of a function of the form $ \cos \varphi(x) $, where $ \varphi $ tends to $ +\infty $ as $ x $ tends to 0).
