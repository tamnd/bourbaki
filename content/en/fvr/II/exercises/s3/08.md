Let I and J be two arbitrary intervals in $ \mathbf{R} $, and $ \mathbf{f} $ a function defined and continuous on $ I \times J $, with values in a complete normed space E over $ \mathbf{R} $. Suppose that:

1 the integral $ \int_I \mathbf{f}(x, y) dx $ is uniformly convergent when y runs through an arbitrary compact interval contained in J;

2 the integral $ \int_J \mathbf{f}(x, y) dy $ is uniformly convergent when x runs through an arbitrary compact interval contained in I;

3 if, for every compact interval H contained in I, one puts $ u_H(y) = \int_H \mathbf{f}(x, y) dx $, the integral $ \int_J u_H(y) dy $ is uniformly convergent for $ H \in \mathfrak{K}(I) $ (the right directed ordered set of compact intervals contained in I).

Under these conditions, show that the integrals $ \int_I dx \int_J \mathbf{f}(x, y) dy $ and $ \int_J dy \int_I \mathbf{f}(x, y) dx $ exist and are equal.
