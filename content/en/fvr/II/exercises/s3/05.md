Let $ f $ be a continuous vector function on a compact interval $ I = [0, a] $. Show that if, at the point $ \alpha_0 \in I $, there is an $ \varepsilon > 0 $ such that $ (f(x) - f(\alpha_0))/|x - \alpha_0|^{1/2 + \varepsilon} $ remains bounded as $ x $ tends to $ \alpha_0 $, then the function $ g(\alpha) = \int_0^\alpha f(x)\,dx/\sqrt{\alpha - x} $ admits a derivative equal to
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_0}} f(\alpha_0) - \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\alpha_0} \frac{f(x) - f(\alpha_0)}{(\alpha_0 - x)^{3/2}}\,dx
$$
at the point $ \alpha_0 $ for $ \alpha_0 > 0 $, and to 0 for $ \alpha = 0 $.

When $ f $ is the real function $ \sqrt{\alpha_0 - x} $, show that the function $ g $ has an infinite derivative at the point $ \alpha_0 $.
