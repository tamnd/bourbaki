Show by an example that a linear equation $ d\mathbf{x}/dt = A.\mathbf{x} + e^{\alpha t}\mathbf{p}(t) $, where $ A \in \mathcal{L}(E) $ is independent of $ t $ and $ \mathbf{p} $ is a polynomial (with coefficients in E) can have an integral which is equal to a polynomial of the *same degree* as $ \mathbf{p} $, even when $ \alpha $ is a characteristic root of $ A $.
