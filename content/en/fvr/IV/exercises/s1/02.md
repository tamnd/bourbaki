Given two numbers $ b > 0, M > 0 $ and an arbitrary number $ \varepsilon > 0 $, give an example of a scalar differential equation $ x' = g(x) $ such that $ |g(x)| \leq M $ for $ |x| \leq b $ and which admits an integral $ x = u(t) $ continuous on the interval $ ] - b/M - \varepsilon, b/M + \varepsilon[ $, but which does not have a finite limit at the point $ x = \frac{b}{M} + \varepsilon $ (define $ g $ so that the integral considered has a continuous derivative on $ ] - b/M - \varepsilon, b/M + \varepsilon[ $, this derivative being equal to the constant $ M $ on $ ] - b/M, b/M[ $).
