(a) Let $S_{n,p}$ denote the number of mappings of $[1,n]$ onto $[1,p]$. Prove that

$$
S_{n,p}=p^n-\binom{p}{1}(p-1)^n+\binom{p}{2}(p-2)^n-\cdots+(-1)^{p-1}\binom{p}{p-1}.
$$

(Note that $p^n=S_{n,p}+\binom{p}{1}S_{n,p-1}+\binom{p}{2}S_{n,p-2}+\cdots+\binom{p}{p-1}$ and use Exercise 3.)

(b) Prove that $S_{n,p}=p(S_{n-1,p}+S_{n-1,p-1})$ (method of no. 8, Proposition 13).

(c)   Prove that

$$\mathrm{S}_{n+1,n} = \frac{n}{2}\,(n+1)!\qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathrm{S}_{n+2,n} = \frac{n(3n+1)}{24}\,(n+2)!$$

(consider the elements $r$ of $[1, n]$ whose inverse image consists of more than one element).

(d)   If $\mathrm{P}_{n,p}$ is the number of partitions into $p$ parts of a set of $n$ elements, show that $\mathrm{S}_{n,p} = p!\mathrm{P}_{n,p}$.
