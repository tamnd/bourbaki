Let E be an ordered set. A subset X of E is said to be *free* if no two distinct elements of X are comparable. Let $\mathfrak{J}$ be the set of free subsets of E. Show that, on $\mathfrak{J}$, the relation "given any $x \in \mathrm{X}$, there exists $y \in \mathrm{Y}$ such that $x \leqslant y$" is an order relation between X and Y, written $\mathrm{X} \leqslant \mathrm{Y}$. The mapping $x \to \{x\}$ is an isomorphism of E onto a

subset of the ordered set $\mathfrak{J}$. If $\mathrm{X} \subset \mathrm{Y}$, where $\mathrm{X} \in \mathfrak{J}$ and $\mathrm{Y} \in \mathfrak{J}$, show that $\mathrm{X} \leqslant \mathrm{Y}$. The ordered set $\mathfrak{J}$ is totally ordered if and only if E is totally ordered, and then $\mathfrak{J}$ is canonically isomorphic to E.
