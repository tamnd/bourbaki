Let $A$ and $R$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ be a letter distinct from the constants of $\mathscr{T}$. If $R \Rightarrow A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, then

$$(\exists x)R \Leftrightarrow (\exists_A x)R$$

is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. If (not $R$) $\Rightarrow A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, then

$$(\forall x)R \Leftrightarrow (\forall_A x)R$$

is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. In particular, if $A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$,

$$(\exists x)R \Leftrightarrow (\exists_A x)R \qquad \text{and} \qquad (\forall x)R \Leftrightarrow (\forall_A x)R$$

are theorems in $\mathscr{T}$.
