Let $\mathscr{T}$ be a theory, let $A_1$, $A_2$, ..., $A_n$ be its explicit axioms and $a_1$, $a_2$, ..., $a_h$ its constants.

(a) Let $\mathscr{T}'$ be the theory whose signs and schemes are those of $\mathscr{T}$, and whose explicit axioms are $A_1$, $A_2$, ..., $A_{n-1}$. The axiom $A_n$ is said to be *independent* of the other axioms of $\mathscr{T}$ if $\mathscr{T}'$ is not equivalent to $\mathscr{T}$. This will be so if and only if $A_n$ is not a theorem of $\mathscr{T}'$.

(b) Let $\mathscr{T}''$ be a theory whose signs and schemes are the same as those of $\mathscr{T}$. Let $T_1$, $T_2$, ..., $T_h$ be terms of $\mathscr{T}$ such that

$$(T_1|a_1)(T_2|a_2) \ \ldots \ (T_h|a_h)A_i$$

is a theorem of $\mathscr{T}''$ for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n-1$, and such that "not $(T_1|a_1)(T_2|a_2) \ \ldots \ (T_h|a_h)A_n$" is a theorem of $\mathscr{T}''$. Then either $A_n$ is independent of the other axioms of $\mathscr{T}$, or else $\mathscr{T}''$ is contradictory.
