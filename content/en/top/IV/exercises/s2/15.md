a) Let $ X $ be a connected Hausdorff space such that, for each $ x \in X $, $ C\{x\} $ has exactly two components. Show that, if $ K $ is either of these components, $ K = K \cup \{x\} $ [Chapter I, § 11, Exercise 4 a)]. Let $ x, y $ be two distinct points of $ X $, let $ A $ and $ B $ be the components of $ C\{x\} $ and let $ A', B' $ be the components of $ C\{y\} $. Show that one of the two sets $ A, B $ is contained in $ A' $ or in $ B' $.

b) Let $ x_0 $ be a point of $ X $ and let $ A(x_0), B(x_0) $ be the components of $ C\{x_0\} $. For each $ x \neq x_0 $ there is exactly one of the two components of $ C\{x\} $ which is either contained in $ A(x_0) $ or contains $ A(x_0) $; denote this component by $ A(x) $. Show that the relation $ A(x) \subset A(y) $ is a linear order relation compatible (Exercise 14) with the topology of $ X $.

c) Extend the conclusion of b) to the case where $ C\{x\} $ has two components for all $ x \in X $ except for one point $ a \in X $, for which $ C\{a\} $ is connected.

*d) Let $ X $ be the subspace of $ \mathbf{R}^2 $ consisting of the points $ a = (0, 1) $, $ b = (0, -1) $ and all pairs $(x, y)$ such that $ x \neq 0 $ and $ y = \sin(1/x) $. Show that $ X $ is connected in the topology $ \mathcal{T} $ induced on $ X $ by the topology of $ \mathbf{R}^2 $, that $ C\{a\} $ and $ C\{b\} $ are connected and that, for each point $ x $ of $ X $ other than $ a $ and $ b $, $ C\{x\} $ has exactly two components; but that there exists no linear ordering on $ X $ compatible with the topology $ \mathcal{T} $ *.
