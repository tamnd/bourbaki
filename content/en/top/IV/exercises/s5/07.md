Let $ f $ be a real-valued function defined on a closed subset $ A $ of $ \overline{\mathbf{R}} $. Let $ \lim \sup_{x \to a, x \geqslant a} f(x) $ [resp. $ \lim \sup_{x \to a, x \leqslant a} f(x) $] denote the upper limit of $ f(x) $ as $ x $ tends to a point $ a \in A $ while remaining in $ A \cap [a, +\infty] $ (resp. $ A \cap [-\infty, a] $). Show that the set of points $ a \in A $ such that $ \limsup_{x \to a, x \geq a} f(x) \neq \limsup_{x \to a, x \leq a} f(x) $ is countable. [Prove that, for each pair of rational numbers $ p, q $ such that $ p < q $, the set $ G_{p,q} $ of points $ a \in A $ such that
$$
\limsup_{x \to a, x \geq a} f(x) \leq p < q \leq \limsup_{x \to a, x \leq a} f(x)
$$
is countable, by using Exercise 1 of § 2].
