Let $(u_n)$ be a sequence of finite real numbers such that the series whose general term is $u_n$ is convergent but not absolutely convergent; let $s = \sum_{n=0}^\infty u_n$. Show that, for each number $s' \geq s$, there exists a permutation $\sigma$ of $\mathbf{N}$ such that $\sigma(n) = n$ for those indices $n$ such that $u_n \geq 0$, and such that $ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} u_{\sigma(n)} = s' $. [Show by induction on $ m $ that there is a permutation $ \sigma_m $ of $ \mathbf{N} $ such that $ \sigma_m(k) = k $ for all $ k $ for which $ u_k \geq 0 $ and such that, if $ u_k^{(m)} $ denotes $ u_{\sigma_m(k)} $, there is an integer $ p_m $ with the property that $ \left| s' - \sum_{i=0}^k u_i^{(m)} \right| \leq 1/m $ for all $ k \geq p_m $. Moreover, $ \sigma_{m+1} $ is such that $ \sigma_{m+1}(k) = \sigma_m(k) $ for all $ k $ such that $ \sigma_m(k) < p_m $ and all $ k $ such that $ u_k \leq -1/m $.]
