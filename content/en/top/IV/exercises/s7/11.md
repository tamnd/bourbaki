Consider a sequence $ (a_n) $ every term of which is the sum of a finite sequence of finite real numbers
$$
a_n = b_{n,1} + b_{n,2} + \cdots + b_{n,k_n}.
$$

For each pair $(n, p)$ of positive integers such that $p \leq k_n$, if $m = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} k_i + p$, put $c_m = b_{n,p}$. Show that if the series whose general term is $a_n$ is convergent in $\mathbf{R}$, and if
$$
d_n = |b_{n,1}| + |b_{n,2}| + \cdots + |b_{n,k_n}|
$$
tends to 0 as $n$ tends to infinity, then the series whose general term is $c_m$ is convergent and has the same sum as the series whose general term is $a_n$.

§ 12) Let $(u_n)$ be a sequence of finite real numbers. For each permutation $\sigma$ of the set $\mathbf{N}$, put
$$
r(n) = |\sigma(n) - n| \cdot \sup_{m \geq n} |u_m|.
$$
a) If the series with general term $u_n$ is convergent, and if $\lim_{n \to \infty} r(n) = 0$, show that the series with general term $v_n = u_{\sigma(n)}$ is convergent to the same sum. [Consider the difference $\sum_{k=0}^n v_k - \sum_{k=0}^n u_k$ for large values of $n$, and if $h$ is the smallest integer $\leq n$ such that $\sigma(h) > n$, note that in this difference there are at most $\sigma(h) - h$ terms in each of the two sums which do not cancel out].
b) Suppose that the series whose general term is $u_n$ is convergent. Give an example of a permutation $\sigma$ such that $\lim_{n \to \infty} r(n) = 0$ but such that the criterion of Chapter III, § 5, Exercise 6 a) is not satisfied. [Define a family of mutually disjoint intervals $I_k = [n_k - 2k, n_k + 2k]$ in $\mathbf{N}$, and take $\sigma$ such that $\sigma(n) = n$ whenever $n$ does not belong to any of the $I_k$, and such that $\sigma(n_k - 2j) = n_k + 2j$, $\sigma(n_k + 2j) = n_k - 2j$ for all $k$ and $0 \leq j \leq k$].

§ 13) Let $(u_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers $\geq 0$ such that $\lim_{n \to \infty} u_n = 0$ and $\sum_{n=0}^\infty u_n = +\infty$. Let $(p_n)$ be a strictly increasing sequence of numbers $> 0$ such that $\lim_{n \to \infty} p_n = +\infty$. Show that there exists a permutation $\sigma$ of $\mathbf{N}$ such that for each $n \in \mathbf{N}$, $\sum_{k=0}^n u_{\sigma(n)} \leq p_n$.

§ 14) Let $(u_n)$ be a sequence of finite real numbers such that the series whose general term is $u_n$ is convergent but not absolutely convergent; let $s = \sum_{n=0}^\infty u_n$. Show that, for each number $s' \geq s$, there exists a permutation $\sigma$ of $\mathbf{N}$ such that $\sigma(n) = n$ for those indices $n$ such that $u_n \geq 0$, and such that $ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} u_{\sigma(n)} = s' $. [Show by induction on $ m $ that there is a permutation $ \sigma_m $ of $ \mathbf{N} $ such that $ \sigma_m(k) = k $ for all $ k $ for which $ u_k \geq 0 $ and such that, if $ u_k^{(m)} $ denotes $ u_{\sigma_m(k)} $, there is an integer $ p_m $ with the property that $ \left| s' - \sum_{i=0}^k u_i^{(m)} \right| \leq 1/m $ for all $ k \geq p_m $. Moreover, $ \sigma_{m+1} $ is such that $ \sigma_{m+1}(k) = \sigma_m(k) $ for all $ k $ such that $ \sigma_m(k) < p_m $ and all $ k $ such that $ u_k \leq -1/m $.]

15) Let $ (u_n) $ be a sequence of finite real numbers such that $ \lim_{n \to \infty} u_n = 0 $ and $ \sum_n u_n^+ = \sum_n u_n^- = +\infty $. If $ a $ and $ b $ are any two real numbers (finite or not) such that $ a \leq b $, show that there is a permutation $ \sigma $ of $ \mathbf{N} $ such that, if we put $ s_n = \sum_{k=0}^n u_{\sigma(k)} $, we have $ \liminf_{n \to \infty} s_n = a $ and $ \limsup_{n \to \infty} s_n = b $. Show that the set of cluster points of the sequence $ (s_n) $ is then the interval $[a, b]$.

16) Let $ (u_n) $ be a sequence of finite real numbers such that the series whose general term is $ u_n $ is convergent but not absolutely convergent. Show that there exists a permutation $ \sigma $ of $ \mathbf{N} $ satisfying the condition of Chapter III, § 5, Exercise 6 a) but such that the series whose general term is $ u_{\sigma^{-1}(n)} $ is not convergent. [Let $ h, k, m $ be three integers with the following properties : if $ p_1, \ldots, p_r $ are the integers $ n $ such that $ h \leq n < h + m $ and $ u_n \geq 0 $, then $ u_{p_1} + \cdots + u_{p_r} > 2 $ and $ h + m < k - r $; moreover if $ \mu, \nu $ are any integers such that $ h + m \leq \mu \leq \nu $, then $ \left| \sum_{n=\mu}^{\nu} u_n \right| \leq 1 $. Put $ s = m - r $ and let $ q_1, \ldots, q_s $ denote the integers $ n $ such that $ h \leq n < h + m $ and $ u_n < 0 $. Consider the permutation $ \pi $ of the interval $[h, k + s]$ of $ \mathbf{N} $ such that $ \pi(p_i) = k - i + 1 $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq r $, $ \pi(q_j) = k + j $ for $ 1 \leq j \leq s $, $ \pi(k + j) = h + s - j $ for $ 1 \leq j \leq s $, $ \pi(k - i + 1) = h + s + i - 1 $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq r $ and finally $ \pi(n) = n $ for $ h + m \leq n \leq k - r $; show that we have
$$
\left| \sum_{i=h}^k u_i - \sum_{i=h}^k u_{\pi^{-1}(i)} \right| \geq 1.
$$

17) If $ u_{mn} = 1/(m^2 - n^2) $ for $ m \neq n $ and $ u_{mm} = 0 $, show that
$$
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \left( \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} u_{mn} \right) = - \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} u_{mn} \right) \neq 0.
$$
(Use Exercise 8.)

18) Let $ (1 + u_i) $ be a family of real numbers all belonging to the interval $[0, +\infty[$ (resp. ]$0, +\infty$]). Then the family $(1 + u_i)$ is multipliable in $\mathbf{R}$ if and only if *one* of the following conditions is satisfied:
a) At least one of the numbers $1 + u_i$ is equal to $0$ (resp. $+\infty$).
b) The family $u_i$ is summable in $\overline{\mathbf{R}}$.

19) Let $(1 + u_i)_{i \in I}$ be a multipliable family in $\mathbf{R}$. For each non-empty subset $H$ of $I$, let $v_H$ denote $\prod_{i \in H} u_i$. Show that the family $(v_H)_{H \in \mathcal{P}(I)}$ is summable in $\mathbf{R}$ and that
$$
1 + \sum_H v_H = \prod_{i \in I} (1 + u_i).
$$
Is the converse true?

Deduce that, if $-1 < x < 1$, then $\prod_{n=0}^\infty (1 + x^{2^n}) = 1/(1 - x)$.

20) Let $(u_n)$ be a sequence of finite real numbers $\geqslant 0$ such that $u_0 > 0$, and let $s_n = \sum_{k=0}^n u_k$ for each $n \geqslant 0$. Then the sequence $(u_n)$ is summable in $\mathbf{R}$ if and only if the sequence $(u_n/s_n)$ is summable in $\mathbf{R}$ [apply Theorem 4 to the sequence $(1 - u_n/s_n)]$. The same holds if the sequence $(u_n/s_n)$ is replaced by the sequence $(u_n/s_{n-1})$.

21) Let $u_n = (-1)^n/\sqrt{n}$ for $n \geqslant 2$. The product whose general factor is $1 + u_n$ is not convergent, but the series whose general term is $u_n$ is convergent.

22) For $n \geqslant 2$, let $u_{2n-1} = -1/\sqrt{n}$ and $u_{2n} = (1 + \sqrt{n})/n$. The product whose general factor is $1 + u_n$ is convergent, but the series whose general term is $u_n$ is not convergent.
