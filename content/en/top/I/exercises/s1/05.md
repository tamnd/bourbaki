If $A$ is any subset of a topological space $X$, the frontier of $A$ is denoted by $\mathrm{Fr}(A)$.

a) Show that $\mathrm{Fr}(\overline{A}) \subset \mathrm{Fr}(A), \mathrm{Fr}(\mathring{A}) \subset \mathrm{Fr}(A)$, and give an example *on the real line* where these three sets are distinct.

b) Let $A, B$ be two subsets of $X$; show that
$$
\mathrm{Fr}(A \cup B) \subset \mathrm{Fr}(A) \cup \mathrm{Fr}(B)
$$
and give an example *on the real line* where these sets are distinct. If $\overline{A} \cap \overline{B} = \varnothing$, show that $\mathrm{Fr}(A \cup B) = \mathrm{Fr}(A) \cup \mathrm{Fr}(B)$.

c) If $A$ and $B$ are open in $X$, show that
$$
(A \cap \mathrm{Fr}(B)) \cup (B \cap \mathrm{Fr}(A)) \subset \mathrm{Fr}(A \cap B)
$$
$$
\subset (A \cap \mathrm{Fr}(B)) \cup (B \cap \mathrm{Fr}(A)) \cup (\mathrm{Fr}(A) \cap \mathrm{Fr}(B))
$$
and give an example *on the real line* where these three sets are distinct.
