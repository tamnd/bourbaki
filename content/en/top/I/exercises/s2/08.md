Let $ X $ be a set, and $ c $ a cardinal number which is either infinite or equal to 1. The set of subsets of $ X $ consisting of $ X $ and those $ M \subset X $ such that $ \operatorname{card}(M) < c $ is the set of closed subsets of $ X $ for a topology $ \mathcal{T}_c $; $ \mathcal{T}_1 $ is the coarsest topology on $ X $ and $ \mathcal{T}_c $ is the discrete topology if $ c > \operatorname{card}(X) $. Every permutation of $ X $ is an automorphism of the topological space obtained by giving $ X $ a topology $ \mathcal{T}_c $.

Show that conversely any topology $ \mathcal{T} $ on $ X $, which has the property that every permutation of $ X $ is continuous with respect to $ \mathcal{T} $, is necessarily one of the topologies $ \mathcal{T}_c $. [Let $ c $ be the smallest cardinal such that $ \operatorname{card}(F) < c $ for each closed subset $ F \neq X $ in the topology $ \mathcal{T} $; observe that when $ X $ is infinite and $ c > \mathrm{card}(X) $, $ \mathcal{T} $ is necessarily the discrete topology.]
