On an ordered set $ X $ let $ \mathcal{T}_0(X) $ (resp. $ \mathcal{T}_+(X) $, $ \mathcal{T}_-(X) $) denote the topology generated by the set of all open intervals (resp. the set of right half-open intervals, the set of left half-open intervals), bounded or not.

a) Show that if $ X $ is linearly ordered, then the open (resp. right half-open, left half-open) intervals form a base of $ \mathcal{T}_0(X) $ (resp. $ \mathcal{T}_+(X) $, $ \mathcal{T}_-(X) $); and that the closed intervals are closed sets in each of these three topologies.

b) On a linearly ordered set $ X $, show that the topology $ \mathcal{T}_+(X) $ is finer than the right topology ($ \S 1 $, Exercise 2), that the topology $ \mathcal{T}_-(X) $ is finer than the left topology, and that the topology $ \mathcal{T}_0(X) $ is the intersection of $ \mathcal{T}_+(X) $ and $ \mathcal{T}_-(X) $.

c) Let $ X $ be a linearly ordered set. Show that a necessary and sufficient condition for $ \mathcal{T}_0(X) $ to have a countable base is that there should be is a countable subset $ D $ of $ X $ such that, for each $ x \in X $ and each interval $ ]a, b[ $ containing $ x $, there exist $ \alpha \in D $ and $ \beta \in D $ such that

$$
a \leq \alpha < x < \beta \leq b
$$

[cf. Chapter IV, $ \S 2 $, Exercise 11 c)].

d) Show that if $ X $ is well-ordered (*Set Theory*, Chapter III, $ \S 2 $) then the topology $ \mathcal{T}_+(X) $ is the discrete topology, and the topologies $ \mathcal{T}_0(X) $ and $ \mathcal{T}_-(X) $ are identical.

§ 6) A topology on a set $ X $ is said to be *quasi-maximal* if it is maximal in the set of topologies in which $ X $ has no isolated points.

a) Show that the following properties of a topology $ \mathcal{T} $ on $ X $ are equivalent: $ \alpha) $ $ \mathcal{T} $ is quasi-maximal; $ \beta) $ if $ X $ carries the topology $ \mathcal{T} $, then $ X $ has no isolated point and every subset of $ X $ which has no isolated points is open.

b) Let $ X $ be a Kolmogoroff space ($ \S 1 $, Exercise 2) in which every non-empty open set is infinite. Show that there exists a quasi-maximal topology on $ X $ which is finer than the given topology (show that the set of topologies on $ X $ in which all the non-empty open sets are infinite is inductive, and use Exercise 2 d) of $ \S 1 $).

§ 7) If $ X $ is any set, let $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ denote the set of non-empty subsets of $ X $.

a) Let $ X $ be a non-empty topological space. Let $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega $ (resp. $ \mathcal{T}_\Phi $) denote the coarsest topology on $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ such that for every non-empty open (resp. closed) subset $ A $ of $ X $, $ \mathfrak{P}_0(A) $ is open (resp. closed) in $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $. Show that in general these two topologies are not comparable, and that the mapping $ x \to \{x\} $ is a homeomorphism of $ X $ onto a subspace of $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ if this latter set carries either of the topologies $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega, \mathcal{T}_\Phi $.

b) Let $ D $ be a dense subset of $ X $. Show that the set $ \mathfrak{G}(D) $ of finite non-empty subsets of $ D $ is dense in $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ for either of the topologies $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega, \mathcal{T}_\Phi $.

c) If $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ is ordered by inclusion, show that the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega $ is coarser than the left topology on $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ (\S 1, Exercise 2); that $ A \in \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ is isolated in the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega $ if and only if $ A = \{x\} $, where $ x $ is an isolated point of $ X $; and that $ A \in \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ is isolated in the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Phi $ if and only if $ X $ is a finite discrete space and $ A = X $.

d) Prove that if $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ carries the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega $ (resp. $ \mathcal{T}_\Phi $) and
$$
\mathfrak{P}_0(X) \times \mathfrak{P}_0(X)
$$
carries the topology which is the product of $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega $ (resp. $ \mathcal{T}_\Phi $) with itself, then the mapping $ (M, N) \to M \cup N $ of $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) \times \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ into $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ is continuous.

e) Show that if $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ carries the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Phi $, then the mapping $ M \to \overline{M} $ is continuous.

f) Let $ X, Y $ be two topological spaces, $ f : X \to Y $ a continuous map. Show that if both $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ and $ \mathfrak{P}_0(Y) $ carry the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega $, and if both carry the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Phi $, then the mapping $ M \to f(M) $ of $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ into $ \mathfrak{P}_0(Y) $ is continuous.

g) Let $ X, Z $ be two topological spaces. Show that a mapping $ f : Z \to \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ is continuous when $ \mathfrak{P}_0(X) $ carries the topology $ \mathcal{T}_\Omega $ (resp. $ \mathcal{T}_\Phi $) if and only if the set of all $ z \in Z $ such that $ f(z) \cap A \neq \emptyset $ is closed (resp. open) in $ Z $ for each closed (resp. open) subset $ A $ of $ X $.

§ 8) Let $ X $ be a set, and $ c $ a cardinal number which is either infinite or equal to 1. The set of subsets of $ X $ consisting of $ X $ and those $ M \subset X $ such that $ \operatorname{card}(M) < c $ is the set of closed subsets of $ X $ for a topology $ \mathcal{T}_c $; $ \mathcal{T}_1 $ is the coarsest topology on $ X $ and $ \mathcal{T}_c $ is the discrete topology if $ c > \operatorname{card}(X) $. Every permutation of $ X $ is an automorphism of the topological space obtained by giving $ X $ a topology $ \mathcal{T}_c $.

Show that conversely any topology $ \mathcal{T} $ on $ X $, which has the property that every permutation of $ X $ is continuous with respect to $ \mathcal{T} $, is necessarily one of the topologies $ \mathcal{T}_c $. [Let $ c $ be the smallest cardinal such that $ \operatorname{card}(F) < c $ for each closed subset $ F \neq X $ in the topology $ \mathcal{T} $; observe that when $ X $ is infinite and $ c > \mathrm{card}(X) $, $ \mathcal{T} $ is necessarily the discrete topology.]

9) a) With the hypotheses of Proposition 4 of no. 3, show that the coarsest topology on $ X $ for which the $ f_i $ are continuous is also the finest topology $ \mathcal{T} $ on $ X $ which has the following property: every mapping $ g $ of a topological space $ Z $ into $ X $ such that the $ f_i \circ g $ are continuous is continuous (with respect to $ \mathcal{G} $).

b) With the hypotheses of Proposition 6 of no. 4, show that the finest topology on $ X $ for which the $ f_i $ are continuous is also the coarsest topology $ \mathcal{T} $ on $ X $ which has the following property: every mapping $ g $ of $ X $ into a topological space $ Z $ such that the $ g \circ f_i $ are continuous is continuous (with respect to $ \mathcal{T} $).

10. On a set $ X $, let $ \mathfrak{M} $ be a directed set of topologies without isolated points. Show that the least upper bound of $ \mathfrak{M} $ in the set of all topologies on $ X $ is a topology without isolated points.

§ 11. A topological space is said to be solvable if it is the disjoint union of two dense subsets.

a) If a topological space $ X $ is solvable, show that $ X $ has no isolated points and that every open subspace of $ X $ is solvable. Show also conversely that if $ X $ is a topological space and $ \mathcal{B} $ is a set of non-empty solvable open subspaces of $ X $ such that every non-empty open subset of $ X $ contains a set belonging to $ \mathcal{B} $, then $ X $ is solvable (consider a maximal set of mutually disjoint open sets belonging to $ \mathcal{B} $).

b) If a Kolmogoroff space $ X $ is solvable, then every non-empty open subset of $ X $ is infinite [use Exercise 2 d) of § 1].

c) Let $ X $ be a topological space such that 1) the smallest cardinal $ \mathfrak{d} $ of a non-empty open subset of $ X $ is infinite, and 2) there is a base $ \mathcal{B} $ of the topology of $ X $ such that $ \mathrm{card}(\mathcal{B}) \leq \mathfrak{d} $. Show that $ X $ is solvable. (Use transfinite induction to construct two disjoint dense subsets of $ X $). By the method of Set Theory, Chapter III, § 6, Exercise 24 a). In particular, the rational line is solvable.

d) A topological space $ X $ which has no isolated points is said to be isodyne if $ \mathrm{card}(U) = \mathrm{card}(X) $ for every non-empty open subset of $ X $. Show that in a space $ X $ with no isolated points, every non-empty open set in $ X $ contains a non-empty open subspace which is isodyne. Deduce that if every isodyne open subspace of $ X $ is soluble, then $ X $ is soluble.
