Let $ A, B $ be two subsets of a topological space $ X $.

a) Show that if $ A $ and $ B $ are closed in $ X $, and $ A \cup B $ and $ A \cap B $ are connected, then $ A $ and $ B $ are connected. Show by an example that this statement is no longer necessarily true when one of the sets $ A, B $ is not closed.

b) Suppose that $ A $ and $ B $ are connected and $ \overline{A} \cap B \neq \varnothing $. Show that $ A \cup B $ is connected.

§ 4) Let $ X $ be a connected space of at least two points.

a) Let $ A $ be a connected subset of $ X $, $ B $ a subset of $ \mathbf{C}A $ which is both open and closed in $ \mathbf{C}A $. Show that $ A \cup B $ is connected. (Apply Exercise 5 of § 3 to $ Y = A \cup B $ and $ Z = \mathbf{C}A $.)

b) Let $ A $ be a connected subset of $ X $ and $ B $ a component of the set $ \mathbf{C}A $. Show that $ \mathbf{C}B $ is connected [use a)].

c) Deduce from b) that there exist two connected subsets $ M, N $ of $ X $ which are distinct from $ X $ and such that $ M \cup N = X $ and $ M \cap N = \varnothing $.

§ 21) a) Show that the following conditions on a topological space $ X $ are equivalent: (i) for each open set $ U $ in $ X $, $ \overline{U} $ is open; (ii) for each closed set $ F $ in $ X $, $ \dot{F} $ is closed; (iii) for each disjoint pair of open sets $ U, V $ in $ X $, we have $ \overline{U} \cap \overline{V} = \varnothing $.
