a) Let $ X $ be a topological space. Show that the following three statements are equivalent:

(Q) If $ x, y $ are any two distinct points of $ X $, there is a neighbourhood of $ x $ which does not contain $ y $.

(Q') Every subset of $ X $ which consists of a single point is closed in $ X $.

(Q'') For every $ x \in X $ the intersection of the neighbourhoods of $ x $ consists of the point $ x $ alone.

A space $ X $ is said to be *accessible* if it satisfies these conditions.

b) An ordered set $ X $ with the right topology (with respect to which $ X $ is a Kolmogoroff space [§ 1, Exercise 2]) is accessible if and only if no two distinct elements of $ X $ are comparable, and then the right topology on $ X $ is the discrete topology.
