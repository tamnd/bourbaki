Let $ X $ be a Hausdorff space with no isolated points, and let $ \mathcal{T}_0 $ be the topology of $ X $. Let $ \mathfrak{M} $ denote the set of complements of countable subsets $ A $ of $ X $ such that $ \overline{A} $ has only a finite number of non-isolated points. Show that if $ \mathcal{T} $ is the topology on $ X $ generated by the union of $ \mathfrak{M} $ and the set of subsets which are open in $ \mathcal{T}_0 $, then $ \mathcal{T}^* = \mathcal{T}_0 $ (Exercise 20) and that every sequence which converges with respect to $ \mathcal{T} $ has only a finite number of distinct terms (which implies that, with respect to $ \mathcal{T} $, no point of $ X $ has a countable fundamental system of neighbourhoods, although $ X $ itself may be countable and therefore every point of $ X $ is then the intersection of a countable family of neighbourhoods of this point).
