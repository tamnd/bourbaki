a) Let $ X $ be a compact space, let $ (f_n) $ be a sequence of continuous mappings $ X \to X $, which converges pointwise but not uniformly in $ X $ to a continuous function (\$ 1, no. 6, Remark 2). Show that the set of mappings $ f_n $ is relatively compact in $ C_s(X; X) $ but not in
$$
C_u(X; X) = C_c(X; X).
$$

b) Let $ I $ be the interval $[-\mathbf{1}, +\mathbf{1}]$ of $ \mathbf{R} $ and for each integer $ n > 0 $ let $ u(x_n) = \sin \sqrt{x + 4n^2 \pi^2} $ for all $ x \geq 0 $. Show that the set $ H $ of functions $ u_n $ is an equicontinuous subset of $ C(\mathbf{R}_+; I) $ and is relatively compact in $ C_c(\mathbf{R}_+; I) $ but not in $ C_u(\mathbf{R}_+; I) $. (Note that the sequence $ (u_n) $ converges pointwise to 0.)
