Let $ X $ be a set, let $ Y $ be a Hausdorff uniform space containing more than one point, and let $ \mathcal{S}_1, \mathcal{S}_2 $ be two sets of subsets of $ X $ which satisfy conditions $ (F'_I), (F'_{II}) $ of no. 2. Show that if $ \mathcal{S}_1 \subset \mathcal{S}_2 $ and $ \mathcal{S}_1 \neq \mathcal{S}_2 $, then the uniformity of $ \mathcal{S}_1 $-convergence is strictly coarser than the uniformity of $ \mathcal{S}_2 $-convergence. In particular:

(i) If $ X $ is a non-compact Hausdorff topological space, the uniformity of compact convergence is strictly coarser than the uniformity of uniform convergence.

(ii) If $ X $ is a Hausdorff topological space which has infinite compact subsets (cf. Chapter I, § 9, Exercise 4), then the uniformity of pointwise convergence is strictly coarser than the uniformity of compact convergence.
