Let $X$ be a topological space. A set $\mathcal{S}$ of subsets of $X$ is said to be *saturated* if it satisfies conditions $(F'_I)$ and $(F'_{II})$ of no. 2 and if the closure of every set of $\mathcal{S}$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}$.

a) Show that if $X$ is normal and if $\mathcal{S}$ is saturated and covers $X$, then the set $\mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})$ is dense in the space $\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathcal{S}}(X; \mathbf{R})$ (no. 6, Theorem 2, Corollary 2) with respect to the topology of $\mathcal{S}$-convergence. In particular, $\mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})$ is dense in $\mathcal{F}_s(X; \mathbf{R})$. $\mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})$ is closed in $\mathcal{F}_s(X; \mathbf{R})$ only if $X$ is discrete.

b) Let $X$ be a completely regular space (Chapter IX, § 1, no. 5) and let $\mathcal{S}_1, \mathcal{S}_2$ be two saturated sets of subsets of $X$, such that $\mathcal{S}_1 \subset \mathcal{S}_2$ and $\mathcal{S}_1 \neq \mathcal{S}_2$. Show that the topology of $\mathcal{S}_1$-convergence on $\mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R})$ is strictly coarser than the topology of $\mathcal{S}_2$-convergence.
