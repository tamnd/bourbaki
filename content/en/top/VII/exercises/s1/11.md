Let $ a_i = (a_{ij}) $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq m,\ 1 \leq j \leq n $) be $ m $ points of $ \mathbf{R}^n $ and let $ q $ be an integer > o. For each point $ k = (k_i) $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq m $) of $ \mathbf{R}^m $ with integer coordinates, *not all zero*, satisfying the inequalities $ 0 \leq k_i \leq q $, consider the point $ x_k = (x_{j,k}) $ ($ 1 \leq j \leq n $) of $ \mathbf{R}^n $, all of whose coordinates belong to $[0, 1[$ and which is congruent mod $ \mathbf{Z}^n $ to $ \sum_{i=1}^m k_i a_i $, i.e., the point with coordinates

$$
x_{j,k} = \sum_{i=1}^m k_i a_{ij} - \left[ \sum_{i=1}^m k_i a_{ij} \right].
$$

If $ p $ is the smallest integer such that $ q + 1 \geq p^{n/m} $, show that there are two distinct points $ k, k' $ such that $ x_k $ and $ x_{k'} $ both belong to the same cube of side $ 1/p $ (same method as Exercise 10). Deduce that there exist $ m $ integers $ p_i $, not all zero, and $ n $ integers $ r_j $ ($ 1 \leq j \leq n $) such that $ 0 \leq p_i \leq q $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq m $, and

$$
\left| \sum_{i=1}^m p_i a_{ij} - r_j \right| \leq \frac{1}{p} \quad (1 \leq j \leq n).
$$

Use this result to give another proof of Proposition 2 of no. 1 (the "pigeon-hole principle").
