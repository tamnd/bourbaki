Let $ f $ be the mapping of $ S_2 $ into $ \mathbf{R}^4 $ such that
$$
f(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (x_1^2 - x_2^2, x_1 x_2, x_1 x_3, x_2 x_3).
$$
This function has the same value at diametrically opposite points of $ S_2 $. Show that on passing to the quotient it defines a homeomorphism of $ P_2 $ onto a subspace of $ \mathbf{R}^4 $.

Show likewise that the mapping $ g $ of $ S_3 $ into $ \mathbf{R}^6 $ defined by
$$
g(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) = (x_1^2 - x_2^2, x_1 x_2, x_1 x_3 + x_2 x_4, x_3^2 - x_4^2, x_3 x_4, x_1 x_4 - x_2 x_3)
$$
defines, on passing to the quotient, a homeomorphism of $ P_3 $ into $ \mathbf{R}^6 $.
