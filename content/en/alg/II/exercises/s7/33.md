Let $ A $ be an integral domain. An $ A $-module $ E $ is called *divisible* if for all $ x \in E $ and all $ \alpha \neq 0 $ in $ A $, there exists $ y \in E $ such that $ \alpha y = x $.

(a) Let $ E, F $ be two $ A $-modules. Show that if $ E $ is divisible, so is $ E \otimes_A F $. If $ E $ is divisible and $ F $ is torsion-free, $ \operatorname{Hom}_A(E, F) $ is torsion-free. If $ E $ is a torsion $ A $-module and $ F $ is divisible, then $ E \otimes_A F = \{0\} $. If $ E $ is a torsion $ A $-module and $ F $ is torsion-free, then $ \operatorname{Hom}_A(E, F) = \{0\} $.

(b) Show that every injective $ A $-module ($ \S 2 $, Exercise 11) is divisible and conversely that every divisible torsion-free $ A $-module is injective (use criterion ($ \delta $) of $ \S 2 $, Exercise 11).
