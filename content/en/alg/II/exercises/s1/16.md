Let A be a ring such that there exists an A-module M with a generating system of n elements but containing a free system of $ n + 1 $ elements.

(a) Show that there exists in $ A_s^n $ a free system of $ n + 1 $ elements.

(b) Deduce that there exists in M an infinite free system (construct such a system by induction using (a)).

(c) Let C be a ring, E the free C-module $ C_s^{(N)} $, $ (e_n) $ its canonical basis and A the endomorphism ring of E. Let $ u_1 $ and $ u_2 $ denote the endomorphisms of E defined by the conditions $ u_1(e_{2n}) = e_n, \quad u_1(e_{2n+1}) = 0, \quad u_2(e_{2n+1}) = e_n, \quad u_2(e_{2n}) = 0 $ for all $ n \geq 0 $. Show that $ u_1 $ and $ u_2 $ form a basis of the A-module $ A_s $ and deduce that in the A-module $ A_s $ there exist infinite free systems.

*(d) Let A be the tensor algebra of a vector space E of dimension $ \geq 2 $ over a commutative field (cf. III, § 5, no. 1). Show that in the A-module $ A_s $ there exist infinite free systems (observe that two linearly independent vectors in E are linearly independent in $ A_s $ and use (b)). On the other hand, for every integer $ n > 0 $, every basis of the A-module $ A_s^n $ has $ n $ elements (cf. § 7, no. 2, Proposition 3).*
