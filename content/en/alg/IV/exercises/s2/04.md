Suppose that the additive group of $ A $ contains an infinite subgroup $ G $ whose non-zero elements are not divisors of zero in $ A $. Show that the mapping $ f \mapsto \tilde{f} $ of $ A[X_1, \ldots, X_p] $ into the algebra of mappings of $ A^p $ into $ A $ is injective (observe that a polynomial of degree $ n $ in one indeterminate cannot have more than $ n $ distinct roots lying in $ G $). This holds in particular when there is in $ A $ an element $ x $, not a divisor of $ 0 $ and of infinite order in the additive group of $ A $.
