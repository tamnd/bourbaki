There exists a unique polynomial $P_{p,q}(T_0, \ldots, T_p, U_0, \ldots, U_q)$ with integer coefficients such that for every ring A and any polynomials $f = t_p X^p + \ldots + t_0, g = u_q X^q + \ldots + u_0$ of $\mathbf{A}[X]$ we have

$$
\operatorname{res}_{p,q}(f, g) = P_{p,q}(t_0, \ldots, t_p, u_0, \ldots, u_q).
$$

If we assign the weight i to $T_i$ and $U_i$, then $P_{p,q}$ is isobaric of weight pq.

33) Let $n \geq 1$ be an integer, and order the elements of $\mathbf{N}^n$ lexicographically (notation $a \leq \beta$). For each $a \in \mathbf{N}^n$ let $T_a$ be the set of elements of $\mathbf{Z}[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$ of the form $X^\alpha + \sum u_{\alpha \beta} X^\beta$ (with $u_{\alpha \beta} \in \mathbf{Z}$).

a) Show that the product of an element of $T_a$ by an element of $T_\beta$ lies in $T_{\alpha + \beta}$.

b) Let $\varepsilon_k$ be the element $(1, 1, \ldots, 1, 0, \ldots, 0)$ of $\mathbf{N}^n$. Show that $s_k \in T_{\varepsilon_k}$. Deduce that
$$
S(\alpha) = \prod_{k=1}^n s_k^{\alpha_k - \alpha_k + 1}
$$
belongs to $T_a$, for $a \in \mathbf{N}^n$ such that $a_1 \geq a_2 \geq \ldots \geq a_n$. (Set $a_{n+1} = 0$.)

c) With the notation of IV, p. 66, deduce that $c_s = d_s = 1$ for $a \in D_k$ and $c_{\alpha \beta} = d_{\alpha \beta} = 0$ if $\alpha < \beta$.

14) Let $L$ be a finite ordered set and $A$ a commutative ring. In the ring $M$ of square matrices over $A$ with index set $L$, the set of matrices $m = (m_{uv})$ such that $m_{uu} = 0$ except when $u \leq v$ is a subring $B$.

a) An element $m = (m_{uv})$ of $B$ is invertible in $B$ if and only if $m_{uu}$ is invertible in $A$ for all $u \in L$.

b) Suppose henceforth that $A = \mathbf{Z}$. Let $\zeta$ be the element of $B$ defined by $\zeta(u, v) = 1$ if $u \leq v$ and $\zeta(u, v) = 0$ otherwise. Show that $S$ has an inverse $\mu$ (also written $\mu_L$) in $B$ (called the Mobius function of $L$). Let $f$ and $g$ be two mappings of $L$ into $\mathbf{Z}$. Show that the relations
$$
f(x) = \sum_{y \geq x} g(y) \quad \text{for all } x \in L
$$
and
$$
g(x) = \sum_{y \geq x} \mu(x, y) f(y) \quad \text{for all } x \in L
$$
are equivalent.

c) Assume that $L$ is the product of a finite family of ordered sets $L_1, \ldots, L_r$. Show that
$$
\mu_L(x, y) = \prod_{i=1}^r \mu_{L_i}(x_i, y_i)
$$
for $x = (x_1, \ldots, x_r)$ and $y = (y_1, \ldots, y_r)$ such that $x \leq y$.

d) Let $m \geq 1$ be an integer and let $D$ be the set of divisors of $m$, equipped with the order relation « $x$ divides $y$ ». Show that $\mu_D(x, y) = (-1)^s$ where $s$ is the cardinal of the set of prime numbers $p$ dividing $y/x$ when $y/x$ is not divisible by the square of an integer, and $\mu_D(x, y) = 0$ otherwise. (By applying c) reduce to the case where $m$ is a power of a prime number.)

e) Suppose that the ordered set $L$ is lattice-ordered. Let $a, b, c$ be distinct elements of $L$ such that $a \leq b \leq c$, and let $\Phi$ be the set of $x \in L$ such that $a \leq x$ and $\sup(x, b) = c$, and $\Psi$ the corresponding set defined by $y \leq c$ and $\inf(y, b) = a$. Show that we have the relation
$$
\sum_{x \in \Phi} \mu(a, x) = \sum_{y \in \Psi} \mu(y, c) = 0.
$$

f) Let $T$ be a finite set and $\mathcal{R}(T)$ the set of equivalence relations on $T$, ordered by the relation « $R$ is finer than $S$ », written $R \leq S$. Show that $\mathcal{R}(T)$ is lattice-ordered. Let $R, S$ in $\mathcal{R}$ be such that $R \leq S$; let $U_1, \ldots, U_k$ be the equivalence classes under $S$ and for $1 \leq i \leq k$, let $\alpha_i$ be the number of equivalence classes under $R$ contained in $U_i$. Show that
$$
\mu_{\mathcal{R}(T)}(R, S) = \prod_{i=1}^k (-1)^{\alpha_i - 1} (a_i - 1)!
$$
(if $A_i$ is the set of equivalence classes under $R$ contained in $U_i$, observe that the interval $(R, S)$ of $\mathcal{R}(T)$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{R}(A_1) \times \ldots \times \mathcal{R}(A_r)$, apply c) and reduce to the case where $R$ is the least element of $\mathcal{R}(T)$ and $S$ the greatest ; treat this last case by using e)).

15) Let $T$ be a finite set with $k$ elements. We denote by $D_k$ the set of elements $(a_1, \ldots, a_k)$ of $\mathbf{N}^k$ such that $a_1 + \ldots + a_k = k$ and $a_1 \geq a_2 \geq \ldots \geq a_k$. With every equivalence relation R in T we associate an element $\lambda(R)$ of $D_k$ as follows: if $U_1, ..., U_l$ are the equivalence classes under R, numbered so that $\mathrm{Card}(U_i) \geq ... \geq \mathrm{Card}(U_l)$, put $\lambda(R) = (\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_k)$ with $\alpha_i = \mathrm{Card}(U_i)$ if $1 \leq i \leq l$ and $\alpha_i = 0$ if $1 < i \leq k$. Let $X_n$ ($n \in \mathbf{N}$) be indeterminates; for each $R \in \mathcal{H}(T)$ put $r(R) = \sum_{f} \prod_{i \in I} X_{f(i)}$, where the summation is extended to the set of mappings $f : T \to \mathbf{N}$ such that R is the equivalence relation associated with $f$. Put $s(R) = \sum_{S \neq R} r(S)$.

a) Prove that $r(R) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} r_i . M(\lambda(R))$ if $r_i$ of the equivalence classes under R arc of cardinal i for $1 \leq i \leq k$. (See formula (11) of IV, p. 65 for the definition of $M(\alpha)$.)

b) Prove the relation $S(R) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} p_i^{r_i}$ where the $r_i$ are as in a) and $p_m = \sum_{n \in \mathbf{N}} X_n^m$ for every integer $m \geq 1$.

c) Show that $r(R) = \sum_{S \neq R} \mu(R, S) s(S)$, where $\mu(R, S)$ is defined as in Exercise 14.

d) Hence deduce an expression of the $s_k$ as a polynomial in $p_1, ..., p_k$ with rational coefficients, where $s_k = \sum_{i_1 < ... < i_k} X_{i_1} ... X_{i_k}$.

e) For $a \in D_k$ put $S'(\alpha) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} s_{\alpha_i}$. Show that $S'(\alpha) = \sum_{\beta \in D_k} c'_{\alpha \beta} . M(\beta)$, where the integer $c'_{\alpha \beta}$ is defined as follows: suppose that $r_i$ (resp. $s_i$) of the components of $a$ (resp. $\beta$) are equal to i (for $1 \leq 1 \leq k$). Then $c'_{\alpha \beta}$ is the product $Nr,!... r_k!s_1!... s_k!/k!$, where N is the number of pairs of equivalence relations R, S on T such that $\lambda(R) = a, \lambda(S) = \beta$ and such that the intersection of an equivalence class under R with an equivalence class under S has at most one element. Deduce the symmetry relation $c'_{\alpha \beta} = c'_{\beta \alpha}$.

f) Show that there is an involution $a \mapsto \tilde{\alpha}$ in $D_k$, characterized by the fact that the relations $i \leq a,$ and $j \leq \tilde{\alpha}_i$ are equivalent, for i, j in the range from 1 to k. Show that $S'(\alpha) = S(\tilde{\alpha})$ and $c'_{\alpha \beta} = c_{\tilde{\alpha} \tilde{\beta}}$. Hence obtain the relation $c_{\alpha \beta} = c_{\tilde{\alpha} \tilde{\beta}}$ (with the notation of IV, p. 66).

Conventions (cf. page IV, 66).

A symbol such as $p^\alpha \cdot q^\beta \ldots$, where $p, q, \alpha, \beta, \ldots$ are integers $\geq 1$, denotes an element of $D_k$ where $k = p\alpha + q\beta + \ldots$ having $\alpha$ components equal to $p$, $\beta$ components equal to $q$, etc. Thus $3 \cdot 1^2$ denotes the element of $D_5$ equal to $(3, 1, 1, 0, 0)$, while $1^4$ denotes the element $(1, 1, 1, 1)$ of $D_r$.

On the right of the row of index $\alpha$ of the matrix C the expression of $S(\alpha)$ as monomial in $s_1, \ldots, s_k$ is given. For example, for $k = 3$ and $\alpha = 2 . 1$ we have

$$
S(2 . 1) = s_1 s_2 = M(2 . 1) + 3M(1^3)
$$

We have used a corresponding convention for the columns of the matrix D. For example, for $k = 4$ and $\alpha = 2^2$ we have

$$
M(2^2) = 2s_4 - 2s_1 s_3 + s_2^2 .
$$

We recall that $M(2^2)$ is the sum of all monomials of the form $X_i^2 X_j^2$ for $1 \leq i < j \leq n$ in the ring $\mathbf{Z}[X_1, \ldots, X_n]$.
