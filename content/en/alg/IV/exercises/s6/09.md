a) Let K be a field and $ X_1, \ldots, X_n $ indeterminates. Show that for any exponents $ v_1 < v_2 < \ldots < v_n $ the determinant $ \det(X_i^{v_j}) $ is non-zero in $ K(X_1, \ldots, X_n) $.

b) Let $ s_k $ ($ 1 \leq k \leq n $) be the elementary symmetric polynomials in the $ X_i $ and $ r_1, \ldots, r_n $ n elements of the field $ K(X_1, \ldots, X_n) $. We put (with a new indeterminate T)

$$
R(T) = \frac{1 + r_1 T + \cdots + r_n T^n}{1 + s_1 T + \cdots + s_n T^n} = 1 + u_1 T + \cdots + u_k T^k + \cdots.
$$

Show that if $ n $ of the coefficients $ u_k $ are zero, then all the $ u_k $ are zero. (Decompose $ R(T) $ into simple fractions.)

*c) Suppose that K is of characteristic zero. Let $ \{v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n\} $ be a set of n elements $ \geq 1 $ of $ \mathbf{N} $, and let M be the complement of this set in $ \mathbf{N} $; suppose also that M is stable under addition. Show that if $ y_1, \ldots, y_n $ are n elements of $ K(X_1, \ldots, X_n) $ such that

$$
P_{v_j}(y_1, \ldots, y_n) = P_{v_j}(X_1, \ldots, X_n)
$$

for $ 1 \leq j \leq n $, then $ y_j = X_j $ for $ 1 \leq j \leq n $ except possibly for a permutation. (Consider the expansion of $ \log R(T) = v_1 T + v_2 T^2 + \cdots + v_k T^k + \cdots $, observe that $ v_{v_j} = 0 $ for $ 1 \leq j \leq n $ and examine the relations between the $ v_k $ and the $ u_k $.)
