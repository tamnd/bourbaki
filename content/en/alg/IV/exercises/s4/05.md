Let $ E = A[X_1, \ldots, X_p] $, $ F = A[[X_1, \ldots, X_p]] $.
a) If D is a Z-derivation of E into F, we have $ \omega(Du) \geq \omega(u) - 1 $ for every non-zero $ u $ in E.
b) If $ D' $ is a Z-derivation of F, then $ \omega(D'v) \geq \omega(v') - 1 $ for every non-zero $ v $ in F.
c) If E is equipped with the topology induced by that of F, then D and $ D' $ are continuous.
d) Every Z-derivation of E into F extends in a unique fashion to a Z-derivation of F.
e) Let $ \mathcal{D} $ be the F-module of A-derivations of F. If $ D \in \mathcal{D} $ and $ DX_i = u_i $, then
$$
D = \sum_{i=1}^p u_i D_i.
$$
f) $ (D,, \ldots, D_p) $ is a basis of the F-module $ \mathcal{D} $.
