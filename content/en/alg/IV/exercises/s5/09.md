(Polynomial laws) Denote by E and F two modules over the commutative ring k. By a polynomial law of E in F we understand, for each (associative commutative and unital) k-algebra L, a mapping $ \varphi_L $ of $ L \otimes_k E $ into $ L \otimes_k F $ satisfying the condition:
If L and R are two k-algebras and f is a unitary homomorphism of L into R, then
$$
(f \otimes 1) \circ \varphi_L = \varphi_R \circ (f \otimes 1)
$$
a) Let Q be a polynomial law of E into F, let $ L = k[(T_i)_{i \in I}] $ be the polynomial algebra in a family of indeterminates $ T_i $ over k and let $ (x_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of finite support of elements of E. Show that there exists a unique family of finite support $ (y_\xi)_{\xi \in \mathbf{N}^{(I)}} $ of elements of F such that
$$
\varphi_L \left( \sum_i T_i \otimes x_i \right) = \sum_{\xi \in \mathbf{N}^{(I)}} T^\xi \otimes y_\xi
$$
Show that if R is an algebra over k, then for every family $ (a_i)_{i \in I} $ of elements of R we have
$$
\varphi_R \left( \sum_i a_i \otimes x_i \right) = \sum_{\xi \in \mathbf{N}^{(I)}} a^\xi \otimes y_\xi.
$$

b) A family $ (\varphi^j)_{j \in J} $ of polynomial laws of E into F is called summable if for every algebra L over k and every $ u \in L \otimes E $, the family $ \varphi^j_L(u) $ has finite support. Show that if $ (\varphi^j)_{j \in J} $ is a summable family of polynomial laws of E into F, then there exists a unique polynomial law Q of E into F such that for every k-algebra L and every $ u \in L \otimes E $ we have $ \sum_i \varphi^i_L(u) = \varphi_L(u) $. This polynomial law is called the sum of the laws $ \varphi^j $ and is denoted by $ \sum_j \varphi^j $.

c) Let E, F, G be three k-modules, $ \varphi $ a polynomial law of E into F and $ \psi $ a polynomial law of F into G. Show that there exists a unique polynomial law $ \eta $ of E into G such that for every k-algebra L we have $ \eta_L = \psi_L \circ \varphi_L $. This law $ \eta $ is called the composition of Q and $ \psi $ and is denoted by $ \psi \circ Q $.

d) Let p be an integer $ \geq 0 $. A polynomial law Q of E into F is said to be homogeneous of degree $ p $ if for every algebra L over k we have $ \varphi_L(au) = a^p \varphi_L(u) $ for any $ a \in L $ and $ u \in L \otimes E $. Show that if Q is homogeneous of degree p, then in formula (1) we have $ y_\xi = 0 $ for $ |\xi| = \sum_i \xi(i) \neq p $. Determine all homogeneous polynomial laws of degrees 0 and 1.

e) With the same notation as in c), show that if Q and $ \psi $ are homogeneous laws of degrees p and q respectively, then $ \psi \circ Q $ is a homogeneous law of degree $ pq $.

f) Let p be an integer $ \geq 0 $. For every commutative k-algebra L we denote by $ \varepsilon_L $ the injection $ a \mapsto aT $ of L into the polynomial algebra $ L[T] $ and by $ \beta_L^p $ the homomorphism of the L-module $ L[T] $ into L which associates with each element $ \sum_k a_k T^k \in L[T] $ the coefficient $ a_p $. Let Q be a polynomial law of E into F. Show that the mappings $ \varphi_L^p = (\beta_L^p \otimes \mathrm{Id}_L) \circ \varphi_{L[T]} \circ (\varepsilon_L \otimes \mathrm{Id}_L) $ form a polynomial law of E into F which is homogeneous of degree p. This polynomial law is called the homogeneous component of degree $ p $ of the law Q. Show that the homogeneous components of Q form a summable family with sum $ \varphi $.
