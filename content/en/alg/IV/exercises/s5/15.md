With the notation as in Ex. 14, let $ E, F, G $ be three k-modules, $ \sigma \in S(E, F) $, $ \tau \in S(F, G) $, and suppose that $ \sigma $ is of order $ \geq 1 $. The series $ \tau \circ A(\sigma) \in S(E, G) $ is called the composition of $ a $ and $ \tau $; it is denoted by $ \tau \circ a $.
a) Let $ \varphi $ be a polynomial law of $ E $ into $ F $ (Ex. 9) and $ \psi $ a polynomial law of $ F $ into $ G $, and denote by $ a_\varphi $ and $ \sigma_\psi $ the series associated with $ \varphi $ and $ \psi $ respectively. Show that if the homogeneous component of degree 0 of $ \varphi $ is zero, then $ \sigma_\varphi $ is of order $ \geq 1 $ and $ \sigma_\psi \circ \sigma_\varphi $ is the series associated with the polynomial law $ \psi \circ \varphi $.
b) Show that if $ a $ is of order $ \geq p $ and $ \tau $ is of order $ \geq q $, then $ \tau \circ \sigma $ is of order $ \geq pq $.
c) Show that for every integer $ p $, $ (\tau \circ \sigma)^{(p)} = \tau^{(p)} \circ A(\sigma) $. Deduce that if $ \tau $ is of order $ \geq 1 $ and if $ H $ is a k-module, then for any $ \rho \in S(G, H) $ we have $ (\rho \circ \tau) \circ a = \rho \circ (\tau \circ \sigma) $.
d) Suppose that $ G $ is a k-algebra. Show that the mapping $ \tau \mapsto \tau \circ A(\sigma) $ is a homomorphism of the algebra $ S(F, G) $ into the algebra $ S(E, G) $.
