A $ \pi $-primary module M is a direct sum of cyclic submodules if and only if there exists a pseudofree subset H in M such that, if N is the pure submodule of M generated by H, then $ M(\pi) = N(\pi) $.
b) The module M is a direct sum of cyclic submodules if and only if M is the union of an increasing sequence $ (P_k) $ of submodules, such that the heights in M of the nonzero elements in each $ P_k $ are bounded. (To see that the condition is sufficient, use a) and Ex. 11, b).)
c) Deduce from b) that if M is a direct sum of cyclic submodules, then every submodule of M is a direct sum of cyclic submodules.
d) Let M be a $ \pi $-primary module with no elements of infinite height, having a countable system of generators $ (x_i) $. Show that M is a direct sum of cyclic submodules (if

P_k is the submodule generated by x, for i ≤ k, then apply b) to the increasing sequence (P_k), using Ex. 5 applied to the decreasing sequence (P_k ∩ π^iM) (for fixed k)).
e) Let M be a τ-primary module having a countable system of generators. Then M is a direct sum of indecomposable submodules if and only if the submodule N of elements of infinite height in M is pure (use d) and Ex. 7, c)).
