Two pairs $ (A_1, A_2) $ and $ (B_1, B_2) $ of square matrices of order $ n $ over a commutative field $ K $ are said to be *equivalent* if there exist two invertible square matrices $ P $ and $ Q $ over $ K $ such that $ A_1 = PB_1Q $ and $ A_2 = PB_2Q $. If $ A_1 $ and $ B_1 $ are invertible, show that the pairs are equivalent if and only if the matrices $ XA_1 + A_2 $ and $ XB_1 + B_2 $ are equivalent over the ring $ K[X] $.
