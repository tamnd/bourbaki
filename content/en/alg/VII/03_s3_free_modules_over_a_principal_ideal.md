## § 3. FREE MODULES OVER A PRINCIPAL IDEAL DOMAIN

#### Theorem 1 {#alg-vii-s3-thm-1 .statement}

— *Let $ A $ be a ring such that every left ideal of $ A $ is projective (II, p. 231, Def. 1) as an $ A $-module. Then every submodule $ M $ of a free left $ A $-module $ L $ is a direct sum of modules isomorphic to ideals of $ A $.*

Let $ (e_i)_{i \in I} $ be a basis for $ L $, and let $ p_i $ be the coordinate functions corresponding to this basis. Choose a *well-ordering* (*Set Theory*, III, p. 153) of $ I $ and let $ L_\alpha $ denote the submodule generated by the $ e_i $, for $ \alpha \leq i $; put $ M_\alpha = M \cap L_\alpha $. The coordinate function $ p_i $ maps $ M_\alpha $ onto an ideal $ a_\alpha $ of $ A $; since $ a_\alpha $ is a projective $ A $-module, there exists (II, p. 231, Prop. 4) a submodule $ N_\alpha $ of $ M $, such that the map $ x \mapsto p_i(x) $ from $ N_\alpha $ into $ a_\alpha $ is bijective. Let $ M'_\alpha $ be the submodule of $ L $ generated by the $ N_\alpha $, for $ \alpha \leq i $; we will show that $ M'_\alpha = M_\alpha $ for all $ \alpha $, which will imply that $ M $ is generated by the family $ (N_\alpha)_{\alpha < i} $. In fact, suppose $ M'_\lambda = M_\lambda $ for all $ \lambda < i $; then $ p_\lambda(x) \in a_\lambda $, for all $ x \in M_\lambda $; thus there exists $ y \in N_\lambda $ such that $ x - y $ is a linear combination of finitely many elements $ e_i $, with $ \alpha < \lambda $; in other words $ x - y $ is an element of $ M_\alpha $, for some $ \lambda < i $; the inductive hypothesis shows that $ x - y \in M'_\lambda \subset M'_\alpha $, that is that $ x \in M'_\alpha $, and so $ M'_\alpha = M_\alpha $. It remains to show that the sum of the $ N_\alpha $ is direct; now suppose there exists a linear relation $ \sum a_\alpha = 0 $, with $ a_\alpha \in N_\alpha $, where the $ a_\alpha $ (all but finitely many of which are zero) are not all zero. Let $ p $ be the greatest index $ \alpha $ such that $ a_\alpha \neq 0 $; since $ p_\mu(a_\lambda) = 0 $ for $ \lambda < \mu $, we have $ p_\mu(a_\mu) = p_\mu\left( \sum a_\alpha \right) = 0 $, so $ a_\mu = 0 $, contradicting the choice of $ p $.

#### Corollary 1 {#alg-vii-s3-thm-1-cor-1 .statement}

— *If every left ideal of $ A $ is projective, then every submodule of a projective left $ A $-module is projective.*

Indeed every projective $ A $-module is a submodule of a free $ A $-module (II, p. 231, Prop. 4), and Th. 1 applies.

#### Corollary 2 {#alg-vii-s3-thm-1-cor-2 .statement}

— *Every submodule of a free module over a principal ideal domain is free.*
    This follows immediately from Th. 1, since every ideal of a principal ideal domain is free.

#### Corollary 3 {#alg-vii-s3-thm-1-cor-3 .statement}

— *Every projective module over a principal ideal domain is free.*
*Remark.* — The proof of Th. 1 shows that every submodule of $ A^{(1)} $ is isomorphic to a direct sum $ \bigoplus a_i $, where each $ a_i $ is an ideal of $ A $.

PROPOSITION I. — *If L is a free module of finite rank n over a principal ideal domain A, then every submodule M of L is a free module of rank $ \leq n $.*
    Indeed M is a free module by Cor. 2 to Th. 1, and it has rank $ \leq n $ by the previous remark, or by the following lemma:

#### Lemma 1 {#alg-vii-s3-lem-1 .statement}

— *Let L be a module over a commutative ring A, generated by n elements, and let M be a free submodule of L; then M has rank $ \leq n $.*
    Suppose first that L is free. Let i denote the canonical injection of M into L. By III, p. 520, Cor., the homomorphism $ \bigwedge^n i : \bigwedge^n M \to \bigwedge^n L $ is injective; by III, p. 511, Prop. 6, $ \bigwedge^n L = \{0\} $, so $ \bigwedge^n M = \{0\} $; it follows that M has rank $ \leq n $ (III, p. 518, Cor. 1). Now consider the general case; the module L is a quotient of a free module L' of rank n. There exists a submodule M' of L isomorphic to M (II, p. 218, Prop. 21). By the first part of the argument M' has rank $ \leq n $, and the result follows.

#### Corollary {#alg-vii-s3-n0-cor-1 .statement}

— *Let E be a module over a principal ideal domain A, generated by n elements. Then any submodule F of E can be generated by at most n elements.*
    Indeed there exists a homomorphism f from $ A^n $ onto E (II, p. 216, Cor. 3), and $ f^{-1}(F) $, which is a free module of rank $ m \leq n $, is generated by n elements; the images of these elements under f generate F.
