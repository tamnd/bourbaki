Let K be a field of characteristic q, $ \Omega $ an algebraically closed extension of K, x an element of $ \Omega $ such that $ x \notin K $ and M a maximal element of the set of subextensions of $ \Omega $ not containing x (V, p. 156, Ex. 9); suppose further that M is perfect so that $ M(x) $ is a Galois extension of prime degree p of M (*loc. cit.*).
a) Show that either $ p = 2 $ and $ \Omega = M(i) $ or for each integer r $ \geq 1 $, there exists a single extension of degree $ p^r $ of M; this extension M, is cyclic over M, $ \Omega $ is the union of the M, and the M, are the only extensions of finite degree of M. (Note that if a p-group $ \Gamma $ of order $ p^r $ has only one subgroup of order $ p^{r-1} $, it is necessarily cyclic, arguing by induction on r and using I, p. 133, Ex. 10, and I, p. 77, Cor. of Prop. 11).
b) Suppose that $ \Omega \neq M(i) $ and that $ p \neq q $. Show that M contains the p-th roots of unity; we thus have $ M(x) = M_1 = M(\alpha) $ with $ \alpha^p \in M $. Show that if a is the p-th power of an element of $ M_1 $, then $ M_1 = M(\zeta) $, where $ \zeta $ is $ p^2 $-th root of unity (if $ a = \beta^p $ for $ \beta \in M_1 $, consider the minimal polynomial of $ \beta $ in $ M[X] $).
c) Under the hypotheses of b) show that if a is not a p-th power of an element of $ M_1 $ then $ M_r $ is the splitting field of the polynomial $ X^{p^{r-1}} - a $ of $ M_1[X] $ (use b)).
d) Under the hypotheses of b), show that if a is the p-th power of an element of $ M_1 $, there exists $ y \in M_1 $ such that $ \gamma \notin M $ and that $ M_2 $ is the splitting field of $ X^p - \gamma $; show that y is not the p-th power of an element of $ M_2 $ and deduce that $ M_r $ is the splitting field of the polynomial $ X^{p^{r-1}} - \gamma $ of $ M_1[X] $.
