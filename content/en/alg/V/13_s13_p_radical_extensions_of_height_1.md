## § 13. $p$-RADICAL EXTENSIONS OF HEIGHT $\leq 1$

Throughout this paragraph $p$ denotes a prime number. All the fields considered are of characteristic $p$.

### 1. $p$-free subsets and pbases

#### Definition 1 {#alg-v-s13-def-1 .statement}

Let $K$ be a field and $L$ a $p$-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of $K$. A family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ of elements of $L$ is said to be $p$-free over $K$ (resp. a $p$-basis of $L$ over $K$) if $x_i \notin K$ for all $i \in I$ and the homomorphism of $\bigotimes_{i \in I} K(x_i)$ into $L$ derived from the canonical injections $u_i : K(x_i) \to L$ is injective (resp. bijective).

If $a$ is an element of $L - K$, it is $p$-radical of height 1, and so its minimal polynomial over $K$ is $X^p - a^p$ ($V$, p. 24, Prop. 1); therefore $\{1, a, \ldots, a^{p-1}\}$ is a basis of $K(a)$ over $K$. Let $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of elements of $L - K$ and $A$ the subset of $\mathbf{N}^{(I)}$ consisting of all families of finite support $a = (\alpha_i)_{i \in I}$, such that $\alpha_i < p$ for all $i \in I$; Prop. 9 (III, p. 471) shows that the elements $\bigotimes_{i \in I} x_i^{\alpha_i}$ as $a$ runs over $A$ form a basis of the vector space $\bigotimes_{i \in I} K(x_i)$ over $K$. Moreover, the canonical homomorphism of $\bigotimes_{i \in I} K(x_i)$ into $L$ has as image $K(x_i)_{i \in I}$ ($V$, p. 18, Cor. 1), we thus have the following proposition:

#### Proposition 1 {#alg-v-s13-prop-1 .statement}

Let $K$ be a field, $L$ a $p$-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of $K$ and $x = (x_i)_{i \in I}$, a family of elements of $L$. Then the vector space $K(x_i)_{i \in I}$ over $K$ is generated by the products $x'' = \prod_{i \in I} x_i^{\alpha_i}$ for $a$ in $A$. For $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ to be $p$-free (resp. a $p$-basis) it is necessary and sufficient that the family $(x^a)_{a \in A}$ should be free over $K$ (resp. a basis of $L$ over $K$).

#### Corollary {#alg-v-s13-n1-cor-1 .statement}

— Let L' be an extension of a field K, generated by two linearly disjoint subextensions K' and L. Suppose that L is p-radical of height $\leq 1$ over K; *then* L' is p-radical of height $\leq 1$ over K'. Moreover, for a family of elements of L to be p-free over K (resp. a p-basis of $L$ over K) it is necessary and sufficient that it should be p-free over K' (resp. a p-basis of L' over K').

We have ${L'}^p \subset K \subset K'$ and $L' = K'(L)$, whence ${L'}^p = {K'}^p({L'}^p) \subset K'$. In other words, L' is a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of K'. The other assertions of the corollary follow at once from Prop. 1 and from V, p. 14, Prop. 5.

#### Remark {#alg-v-s13-n1-rem-1 .statement}

Let K be a field, L a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of K and $(x_i)_i$, a family of elements of L. Let A be the polynomial algebra $K[(X_i)_{i \in I}]$, a the ideal of A generated by the polynomials $X_i^p - x_i^p$ and $\varphi : A \to L$ the K-algebra homomorphism such that $\varphi(X_i) = x_i$ for each $i \in I$. The family $(x_i)_i$, is p-free if and only if the kernel of $\varphi$ is equal to a. For $K[(X_i)]/a$ may be identified with the algebra $\bigotimes_{i \in I} K[X_i]/(X_i^p - x_i^p)$.

Let L be a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of the field K. A subset S of $L$ is said to be p-free (resp. a p-basis) if the family defined by the identity mapping of S onto itself is p-free (resp. a p-basis). For a family $(x_i)_i$, of elements of $L$ to be p-free (resp. a p-basis) it is necessary and sufficient that the mapping $i \mapsto x_i$ should be a bijection of I on a p-free subset (resp. a p-basis) of L. By Prop. 1, every subset of a p-free set is p-free; conversely, if S is a subset of L all of whose finite subsets are p-free, then S is p-free. Finally, a p-basis of L over K is a p-free subset B such that $L = K(B)$.

#### Proposition 2 {#alg-v-s13-prop-2 .statement}

— Let K be a field, $L$ a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of K and S a subset of L. For S to be p-free it is necessary and sufficient that $K(T) \neq K(S)$ for every subset $T \neq S$ of S.

Suppose first that S is p-free and let $T \neq S$ be a subset of S. Denote by A the set of families with finite support $a = (\alpha_s)_{s \in S}$ of integers between 0 and $p-1$; let $A'$ be the subset of A consisting of the families $a = (\alpha_s)_{s \in S}$ such that $a_s = 0$ for all s in $S - T$. We also put $u_a = \prod_{s \in S} s^{\alpha_s}$ for $a \in A$. Then (V, p. 98, Prop. 1) the family $(u_a)_{a \in A'}$ is a basis of $K(S)$ over K and the subfamily $(u_a)_{a \in A'}$ is a basis of $K(T)$ over K. Since $A' \neq A$, we have $K(T) \neq K(S)$.

Suppose now that S is not p-free. Then there exists an integer $n \geq 1$ and a sequence of elements $x_1, ..., x_n$ of S such that $(x_1, ..., x_{n-1})$ is p-free but $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ is not. We have $[K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1}):K] = p^{n-1}$ and $[K(x_1, ..., x_n):K] < p^n$. Since $[K(x_1, ..., x_n):K]$ is a multiple of $[K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1}):K]$, we thus have

$$
[K(x_1, ..., x_n):K] = [K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1}):K].
$$

whence $x_n \in K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1})$. This shows that $K(S - \{x_n\}) = K(S)$.

#### Proposition 3 {#alg-v-s13-prop-3 .statement}

— Let K be a field, L a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of K and S, T two subsets of L. The following conditions are equivalent:
a) The subset S is p-free over K and T is p-free over K(S).
b) $S \cap T = \varnothing$ *and* S U T is p-free over K.
If T is p-free over K(S), we have $T \cap K(S) = \varnothing$ and a fortiori $S \cap T = \varnothing$. We may therefore assume S and T disjoint. Let A be the subset of $N^{(S \cup T)}$ consisting of all families $a = (a_x)_{x \in S \cup T}$ with $a_x < p$ for all $x \in S \cup T$, and define subsets $A'$ of $N^{(S)}$ and $A''$ of $N^{(T)}$ in similar fashion. We can in a natural way, identify $N^{(S \cup T)}$ with $N^{(S)} \times N^{(T)}$, and then A is identified with $A' \times A''$. For $a \in A$ put $u_a = \prod_{x \in S \cup T} x^{\alpha_x}$, and similarly define $u'_\beta$ and $u''_\gamma$ for $\beta \in A'$ and $\gamma \in A''$. We have $u_a = u'_\beta u''_\gamma$ for $a = (\beta, \gamma)$ in $A = A' \times A''$. Moreover $(u'_\beta)_{\beta \in A'}$ generates the vector K-space K(S) (V, p. 94, Prop. 1). For S U T to be p-free over K it is necessary and sufficient that the family $(u'_\beta u''_\gamma)_{\beta \in A', \gamma \in A''}$ should be free over K (V, p. 98, Prop. 1); it comes to the same (II, p. 222) to suppose that the family $(u'_\beta)_{\beta \in A'}$ is free over K and the family $(u''_\gamma)_{\gamma \in A''}$ free over K(S). Now the equivalence of a) and b) follows from Prop. 1 (V, p. 98).

#### Corollary {#alg-v-s13-n1-cor-2 .statement}

— Let L be a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of K and M a subextension of L. Then L is p-radical of height $\leq 1$ over M and M is p-radical of height $\leq 1$ over K. Moreover, if B is a p-basis of M over K and C is a p-basis of L over M, then $B \cap C = \varnothing$ and $B \cup C$ is a p-basis of L over K.

Let K be a field. A family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$, is said to be a (absolute) p-basis of K if it is a p-basis of K over $K^p$. For every integer $n \geq 1$ we denote by $\Lambda(n)$ the subset of $N^{(1)}$ consisting of all $a = (\alpha_i)_{i \in I}$, such that $\alpha_i < p^n$ for all $i \in I$.

#### Proposition 4 {#alg-v-s13-prop-4 .statement}

— Let $x = (x_i)_{i \in I}$ be a p-basis of K. For every integer $n \geq 1$, the family $(x^u)_{u \in \Lambda(n)}$ is a basis of K over $K^{p^n}$.

For $n = 1$ the assertion reduces to Prop. 1 (V, p. 98). The set $\Lambda(n)$ consists of elements of $N^{(1)}$ of the form $a = \beta + p^{n-1}\gamma$ with $\beta \in \Lambda(n-1)$ and $\gamma \in \Lambda(1)$. Such a decomposition is unique and we have $x^\alpha = x^\beta (x^\gamma)^{p^{n-1}}$. Moreover, the family $(x_i^{p^{n-1}})_{i \in I}$ is clearly a p-basis of $K^{p^{n-1}}$ over $K^{p^n}$, hence the family $(x^\gamma)^{p^{n-1}}$ is a basis of $K^{p^{n-1}}$ over $K^{p^n}$. We thus obtain the conclusion by induction, using II, p. 222, Prop. 25.

### 2. Differentials and pbases

Let K be a field (of characteristic p) and V a vector space over K. We recall (III, p. 552) that a derivation of K into V is a mapping D of K into V satisfying the relations
$$
\begin{align*}
D(x + y) &= D(x) + D(y) \\
D(xy) &= xD(y) + yD(x)
\end{align*}
$$

for $x,\ y \in K$. It follows that $D(1) = 0$ and $D(x^n) = nx^{n-1}D(x)$ for all $x \neq 0$ and all $n \in \mathbf{Z}$ (III, p. 557 and 558). Since $K$ is of characteristic $p$, we thus have for all $x \in K$

$$
D(x^p) = 0 .
$$

Moreover (III, p. 558), we have for $x,\ y \in K,\ y \neq 0$,

$$
D(x/y) = (yD(x) - xD(y))/y^2
$$

By the above formulae the kernel of $D$ is a subfield $E$ of $K$ containing $K^p$. Let $M$ be a subfield of $K$; we say that $D$ is an $M$-derivation if it is $M$-linear ; by (2) it comes to the same to assume that the restriction of $D$ to $M$ is zero.

We have (III, p. 570) defined the module $\Omega_M(K)$ of $M$-differentials of $K$ and the canonical $M$-derivation $d = d_{K/M}$ of $K$ into $\Omega_M(K)$. The image of $d_{K/M}$ generates the vector $K$-space $\Omega_M(K)$. For a mapping $D$ of $K$ into $V$ to be an $M$-derivation it is necessary and sufficient that there should exist a $K$-linear mapping $u : \Omega_M(K) \to V$ such that $D = u \circ d_{K/M}$; this mapping $u$ is uniquely determined.

#### Proposition 5 {#alg-v-s13-prop-5 .statement}

*Let $K$ be a field and $L$ an extension of $K$ generated by an element $x$ such that $x \notin K,\ x^p \in K$. Let $V$ be a vector space over $L$ and $A$ a derivation of $K$ into $V$ such that $\Delta(x^p) = 0$. Then there exists a unique derivation $D$ of $L$ into $V$ extending $A$ and such that $D(x) = 0$.

By $V$, p. 24, Prop. 1, $\{1,\ x,\ \ldots,\ x^{p-1}\}$ is a basis of $L$ over $K$. For every element $u = a_0 + a_1x + \ldots + a_{p-1}x^{p-1}$ of $L$ (with $a_0,\ \ldots,\ a_{p-1}$ in $K$) we put $D(u) = A(a_0) + x\Delta(a_1) + \ldots + x^{p-1}\Delta(a_{p-1})$. It is clear that $D$ extends $A$ and satisfies (1); so it is enough to establish the relation

$$
D(uv) = u \cdot D(v) + v \cdot D(u)
$$

when $u$ is of the form $ax^i$ and $v$ of the form $bx^j$ with $a,\ b$ in $K,\ 0 \leq i < p$ and $0 \leq j < p$.

When $i + j < p$ we have $uv = x^{i+j}ab$, whence $D(uv) = x^{i+j}\Delta(ab)$. Otherwise we have $0 \leq i + j - p < p$ and so $uv = x^{i+j-p}(abx^p)$, with $abx^p \in K$, but since $A(x^p) = 0$, we have $A(abx^p) = x^pA(ab)$, whence again $D(uv) = x^{i+j}\Delta(ab)$. So we have in all cases

$$
\begin{align*}
D(uv) &= x^{i+j}\Delta(ab) = x^i x^j (a \cdot A(b) + b \cdot \Delta(a)) \\
&= (ax^i)x^j \cdot \Delta(b) + (bx^j)x^i \cdot \Delta(a) = u \cdot D(v) + v \cdot D(u) .
\end{align*}
$$

#### Corollary 1 {#alg-v-s13-prop-5-cor-1 .statement}

*Let $K$ be a field, $L$ a $p$-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of $K$ and $V$ a vector space over $L$. Every derivation $A$ of $K$ into $V$ vanishing on $L^p$ extends to a derivation of $L$ into $V$.

By Zorn's lemma (E, III, p. 20) there exists a maximal extension of $A$ to a derivation $D_0 : L_0 \to V$, where $L_0$ is a subfield of $L$ containing $K$. Let $x \in L$; we have $x^p \in L_0$ and $A(x^p) = 0$, whence $D_0(x^p) = 0$. By Prop. 5,

D_0 extends to a derivation defined on L_0(x); by the maximal character of D_0 we thus have L_0(x) = L, whence x ∈ L_0. Since x was arbitrary we conclude that L_0 = L.

#### Corollary 2 {#alg-v-s13-prop-5-cor-2 .statement}

—Let L be a p-radical extension of height ≤ l of a field K and let E be a subextension of L. Let U be the subspace of Ω_K(L) generated by the differentials of elements of E. Then E consists of the elements of L whose differential belongs to U. In particular we have d_{L/K}x ≠ 0 for all x ∈ L − K.

Let x be an element of L not belonging to E. Then {1, x, ..., x^{p^{l-1}}} is a basis of E(x) over E. Let A be the E-linear mapping of E(x) into L such that Δ(x^i) = ix' for 0 ≤ i < p. We verify at once that A is an E-derivation of E(x) into L, and in particular a K-derivation. By Cor. 1 to Prop. 5 there exists a K-derivation D of L into L extending A. By the universal property of Ω_K(L), there exists a linear form u on this vector L-space such that D = u ∘ d_{L/K}. We have D(x) = x and D|E = 0; therefore we have u(d_{L/K}x) ≠ 0 and u|U = 0, whence d_{L/K}x ∉ U.

The last assertion is the particular case E = K; then we have U = 0.

#### Theorem 1 {#alg-v-s13-thm-1 .statement}

—Let L be a p-radical extension of height ≤ l of a field K and (a_i)_{i ∈ I}, a family of elements of L.

a) For (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to be p-free over K it is necessary and sufficient that the family (dx_i)_{i ∈ I} should be free in the vector L-space Ω_K(L).

b) For (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to generate L over K it is necessary and sufficient that the family (dx_i)_{i ∈ I} should generate the vector L-space Ω_K(L).

c) For (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to be a p-basis of L over K it is necessary and sufficient that the family (dx_i)_{i ∈ I} should be a basis of the vector L-space Ω_K(L).

To begin with let us remark that the differential dx of an element of the field K((x_i)_{i ∈ I}) is a linear combination with coefficients in L of the differentials dx_i, i ∈ I. For the family (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to be p-free it is necessary and sufficient that x_i ∉ K(x_j)_{j ∈ I - {i}} for all i ∈ I (V, p. 99, Prop. 2). By Cor. 2 of Prop. 5 this means that dx_i is not a linear combination of the dx_j for j ≠ i in I. Assertion a) follows from this. Now b) follows at once from Cor. 2 to Prop. 5 and c) follows from a) and b).

The following corollary makes Cor. 1 of Prop. 5 more specific:

#### Corollary {#alg-v-s13-n2-cor-1 .statement}

—Let (x_i)_{i ∈ I} be a p-basis of L over K. Let V be a vector space over L, A a derivation of K into V which vanishes on L^p and (u_i)_{i ∈ I} a family of elements of V. Then there exists a unique derivation D of L in V which extends A and maps x_i to u_i for all i ∈ I.

By Cor. 1 of Prop. 5 there exists a derivation D_0 of L into V extending A. The derivations of L in V extending A are precisely the mappings of the form D = D_0 + u ∘ d_{L/K} where u is an L-linear mapping of Ω_K(L) into V. We have D(x_i) = u_i if and only if the linear mapping u satisfies the conditions u(dx_i) = u_i − D_0(x_i). Since the family dx_i is a basis of Ω_K(L), this determines u uniquely and the corollary follows.

Let L be a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of K and $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ a p-basis of L over K. By the Cor. to Th. 1 there exists for each $i \in I$ a K-derivation $D_i$ of L into L characterized by $D_i(x_j) = \delta_{ij}$ (Kronecker symbol); $D_i$ is sometimes called the *partial derivation with respect to* $x_i$. When $I$ is *finite*, the family $(D_i)_{i \in I}$ is a basis of the vector space over L consisting of the K-derivations of L into L.

Th. 1 allows the study of p-bases to be reduced to the study of bases of a vector space. For example we have the following result:

#### Theorem 2 {#alg-v-s13-thm-2 .statement}

— *Let L be a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of a field K.*
  *a)* *There exist p-bases of L over K. More precisely, if S is a p-free subset of L over K and T a subset of L such that $S \subset T$ and $L = K(T)$, then there exists a p-basis B of L over K such that $S \subset B \subset T$.*
  *b)* *Two p-bases of L over K have the same cardinal.*
  *c)* *For $[L:K]$ to be finite it is necessary and sufficient that the vector space $\Omega_K(L)$ over L should be of finite dimension over L and then we have*

$$
[L : K] = p^{[\Omega_K(L) : L]}
$$

Assertions *a)* and *b)* are immediate (II, p. 292). If $[L : K]$ is finite, there exists by *a)* a finite p-basis $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ of $L$ over $K$; then the monomials $x_1^{\alpha_1} ... x_n^{\alpha_n}$ with $0 \leq \alpha_i < p$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$ form a basis of $L$ over $K$ and the differentials $dx_1, ..., dx_n$ form a basis of $\Omega_K(L)$ over $L$. We thus have $[L : K] = p^n$ and $[\Omega_K(L) : L] = n$. Conversely if $\Omega_K(L)$ is of finite dimension over $L$, there exists a finite p-basis of L over K (V, p. 102, Th. 1) and $[L : K]$ is finite.

#### Corollary {#alg-v-s13-n2-cor-2 .statement}

— *For each $x \in L - K$, there exists a p-basis of L over K containing X.*

For $\{x\}$ is a p-free subset of $L$ over $K$ and so it is enough to apply Th. 2, *a)*.

Let K be a field and $L$ an extension of K. If $D$ is a K-derivation of L with values in a vector L-space, then we have $D(x^p) = 0$ for all $x \in L$, and so $D$ is a $K(L^p)$-derivation; it follows that $\Omega_K(L) = \Omega_{K(L^p)}(L)$. Since $L$ is a p-radical extension of height $\leq 1$ of $K(L^p)$, we can apply the above results. For example, from Th. 1, *c)* we deduce the following: let $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of elements of $L$; for $(dx_i)_{i \in I}$ to be a basis of the vector L-space $\Omega_K(L)$ it is necessary and sufficient that $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ should be a p-basis of L over $K(L^p)$. Similarly, Cor. 2 of Prop. 5 implies:

#### Proposition 6 {#alg-v-s13-prop-6 .statement}

— *Let K be a field (of characteristic p) and L an extension of K.*
  *a)* *Let $x \in L$; the differential $d_{L/K}x$ vanishes if and only if x belongs to $K(L^p)$.*
  *b)* $\Omega_K(L) = 0$ if and only if $L = K(L^p)$.*
  *c)* *Suppose that L is p-radical of finite height over K; then $\Omega_K(L) = 0$ if and only if $L = K$.*

### 3. The correspondence between subfields and Lie algebras of derivations

We denote by E a field and by g the set of all derivations of E into itself. We recall that g is a vector space over E, with operations defined by

$$(D + D')(x) = D(x) + D'(x), \quad (aD)(x) = a \cdot D(x)$$

for $D, D'$ in $g$ and $a, x$ in $E$. Moreover, if $D$ and $D'$ are derivations of E into E, the same is true of $[D, D'] = DD' - D'D$ (III, p. 554). Finally, Leibniz's formula (III, p. 556) gives

$$D^p(xy) = x \cdot D^p(y) + \sum_{j=1}^{p-1} \binom{p}{j} D^j(x) D^{p-j}(y) + D^p(x) \cdot y$$

($x, y$ in $E$); since the binomial coefficients $\binom{p}{j}$ for $1 \leq j \leq p-1$ are divisible by $p$ ($V$, p. 4, Lemma 1), we see that $D^p$ is a derivation of E into E. We immediately find the relation (for $a, a' \in E$)

$$[aD, a'D'] = aa' \cdot [D, D'] + (aD(a')) \cdot D' - (a'D'(a)) \cdot D.$$

In particular, the mapping $(D, D') \mapsto [D, D']$ of $g \times g$ into $g$ is $E^p$-linear.

We denote by $\mathcal{C}$ the set of subfields $K$ of $E$ such that $E^p \subset K$ and $[E : K]$ is finite; for each $K \in \mathcal{C}$ we denote by $g(K)$ the set of K-derivations of E. Further, we denote by $\mathcal{L}$ the set of vector subspaces $l$ of $g$ of finite dimension over E and such that $[D, D'] \in h$ and $D^p \in h$ for any $D, D'$ in $h$; for each $h \in \mathcal{L}$ we denote by $I(h)$ the set of $x \in E$ such that $D(x) = 0$ for all $D \in h$.

#### Theorem 3 (Jacobson) {#alg-v-s13-thm-3 .statement}

— *The mappings $K \mapsto g(K)$ and $l \mapsto I(h)$ are bijections of $\mathcal{C}$ onto $\mathcal{L}$ and of $\mathcal{L}$ onto $\mathcal{C}$ respectively, which are mutually inverse. If $K \in \mathcal{C}$ and $h \in \mathcal{L}$ correspond to each other, then $[E : K] = p^{[h : E]}$*.

The proof requires several preliminary lemmas.

#### Lemma 1 {#alg-v-s13-lem-1 .statement}

— *Let L be a field, V a vector space over L and u an endomorphism of V such that $u^p = u$. For each $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$ let $V_i$ be the kernel of $u - i$. Then we have*

$$\mathbf{V} = \bigoplus_{i \in \mathbf{F}_p} V_i.$$

For each $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$ denote by $P_i(X)$ the polynomial $- \prod_{j \neq i} (X - j)$. We have

$$(X - i) P_i(X) = X - X^p$$

by Formula (2) ($V$, p. 94). On deriving the quoted formula (2), we find

$$\sum_{i \in \mathbf{F}_p} P_i(X) = 1$$

The formulae (8) and (9) show that the endomorphism $P_i(u)$ of $V$ maps $V$ into $V_i$ and that $\sum_{i \in \mathbf{F}_p} P_i(u) = 1$, whence $V = \sum_{i \in \mathbf{F}_p} V_i$. It remains to show that the sum is direct. For each $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$ let $v_i \in V_i$; it is clear that $P_i(u)$ annihilates $v_j$ for all $j \neq i$ and we have $P(u)v_i = av_i$ with $a = -\prod_{n \in \mathbf{F}_p^*} n \neq 0$. The relation $\sum_{i \in \mathbf{F}_p} v_i = 0$ thus implies $v_i = 0$ for all $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$ and so the sum is direct.

#### Lemma 2 {#alg-v-s13-lem-2 .statement}

*Let D be a derivation of E such that $D^p = D$ and let K be the kernel of D. Suppose that there exists a non-zero x in E such that $D(x) = x$. Then K is a subfield of E containing $E^p$ and we have $[E : K] = p$.*

It is clear that K is a subfield of E containing $E^p$. Denote by $K_i$ the kernel of $D - i$ for $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$. We have $K_0 = K$ and Lemma 1 shows that $E = \bigoplus_{i \in \mathbf{F}_p} K_i$. Let $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$ and let $u$ be in $K_i$; we have
$$
D(xu) = D(x) \cdot u + x \cdot D(u) = xu + x(iu) = (i + 1)xu
$$
whence $xu \in K_{i+1}$. Since x is non-zero, multiplication by x is an automorphism of the vector K-space E which maps $K_i$ onto $K_{i+1}$ for all $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$. Since $[K_0 : K] = 1$, we have $[K_i : K] = 1$ for all $i \in \mathbf{F}_p$ whence $[E : K] = p$.

#### Lemma 3 {#alg-v-s13-lem-3 .statement}

*Let $\mathfrak{h} \in \mathcal{L}$ be of dimension s over E. Then $I(\mathfrak{h})$ belongs to $\mathcal{C}$ and we have $[E : I(\mathfrak{h})] = p^s$.*

It is clear that $I(\mathfrak{h})$ is a subfield of $E$ containing $E^p$. For each $x \in E$ let $f_x$ be the E-linear form $D \mapsto D(x)$ on I). Since the intersection of the kcrncls of these linear forms is equal to 0, they generate the vector space dual to $\mathfrak{h}$ (II, p. 301, Th. 7); hence there exist $x_1, \ldots, x_s$ in $E$ such that the linear forms $f_{x_1}, \ldots, f_{x_s}$ form a basis of this dual. Let $(A,, \ldots, A,)$ be the basis of $\mathfrak{h}$ characterized by $\Delta_i(x_j) = f_{x_j}(\Delta_i) = \delta_{ij}$. Put $D_i = x_i \Delta_i$, then $(D_1, \ldots, D_s)$ is a basis of I) over E and we have $D_i(x_j) = x_i \delta_{ij}$. The derivations $D_i^p - D_i$ and $[D_i, D_j]$ for $i, j = 1, \ldots, s$ belong to I) and annihilate $x_1, \ldots, x_s$; we thus have
$$
D_i^p = D_i , \quad [D_i, D_j] = 0
$$
For i in the range 0 to s let us denote by $K_i$ the intersections of the kernels of the derivations $D_j$ for $1 \leq j \leq i$. Then $K_i$ is a subfield of E and we have
$$
E = K_0 \supset K_1 \supset \ldots \supset K_{s-1} \supset K_s = I(\mathfrak{h}) .
$$
Let i be between 0 and $s-1$; then $K_i$ is stable under $D_{i+1}$ because $D_{i+1}$ commutes with $D_1, \ldots, D_i$. Moreover we have $D_{i+1}^p = D_{i+1}$, $D_{i+1}(x_{i+1}) = x_{i+1} \neq 0$ and $x_{i+1} \in K_i$. Hence Lemma 1 implies that $[K_i : K_{i+1}] = p$, whence finally $[E : K] = [K_0 : K_s] = p^s$.

We now come to the proof of the theorem. Let I) $\in \mathcal{L}$ be of dimension s over E and put $K = I(\mathfrak{h})$; then $[E : K] = p^s$ by Lemma 3, whence $[\Omega_K(E) : E] = s$ by

Th. 2, c) (V, p. 103). By the universal property of the module of differentials the mapping $u \mapsto u \circ d_{E/K}$ is an isomorphism of the dual of $\Omega_K(E)$ onto $g(K)$, hence $[g(K):E] = s$. Now we have $[\mathfrak{h}:E] = s$ and $\mathfrak{h} \subset g(K)$, whence $\mathfrak{h} = g(K)$, that is $\mathfrak{h} = g(I(\mathfrak{h}))$.

Conversely, for every field $K \in \mathcal{C}$ it is clear that $g(K)$ belongs to $\mathcal{L}$ (V, p. 103, Th. 2, c)). *If* $x$ belongs to $I(g(K))$, we have $u(d_{E/K}x) = 0$ for each linear form $u$ on $\Omega_K(E)$, whence $d_{E/K}x = 0$ and so finally $x \in K$ by Cor. 2 of Prop. 5 (V, p. 102). Thus we have $K = I(g(K))$.

#### Remark {#alg-v-s13-n3-rem-1 .statement}

— 1) The mutually inverse bijections $K \mapsto g(K)$ and $\mathfrak{h} \mapsto I(\mathfrak{h})$ are inclusion reversing; therefore $\mathfrak{h} \mapsto I(\mathfrak{h})$ is an isomorphism of the ordered set $\mathcal{L}$ onto the ordered set opposite to $\mathcal{C}$. Hence we obtain the relation $I(\mathfrak{h} \cap \mathfrak{h}') = E^p(I(\mathfrak{h}), I(\mathfrak{h}'))$ for $\mathfrak{h}, \mathfrak{h}'$ in $\mathcal{L}$, because $\mathfrak{h} \cap \mathfrak{h}'$ is the largest element of $\mathcal{L}$ contained both in $\mathfrak{h}$ and in $\mathfrak{h}'$.

2) It can be shown that every subspace of finite dimension of $g$ which is stable under the mapping $D \mapsto D^p$ is also stable under the bracket operation.
