Let $ \tau $ be a law of composition on a set E. Denoting by F the sum set (Set Theory, II, § 4, no. 8) of E and a set $ \{e\} $ consisting of a single element and identifying E and $ \{e\} $ with the corresponding subsets of F, show that it is possible in one and only one way to define on F a law of composition $ \overline{\tau} $ which induces on E the law $ \tau $ and under which $ e $ is identity element; if $ \tau $ is associative, the law $ \overline{\tau} $ is associative. (F is said to be derived from E "by adjoining an identity element".)
