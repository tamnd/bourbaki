(a) Given a set E, let $ \Phi $ be the set of mappings into E of any subset of E; if $ f, g, h $ are three elements of $ \Phi $, show that if the composition $ (f \circ g) \circ h $ is defined so is $ f \circ (g \circ h) $, but that the converse is not true; if these two compositions are defined they are equal.

(b) Let $ \mathcal{F} $ be a family of non-empty subsets of E such that no two have an element in common and let $ \Psi $ be the subset of $ \Phi $ consisting of the bijective mappings of a set of $ \mathcal{F} $ onto a set of $ \mathcal{F} $. Show that, under the law induced by the law $ f \circ g $ on $ \Psi $, the condition of Exercise 3(a) holds.
