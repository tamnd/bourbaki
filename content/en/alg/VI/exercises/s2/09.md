Let q $ \geq 0 $ be a non-square natural number; let A denote the set $ \{ 1, \sqrt{q} \} $ in the field $ K = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{q}) $: show that K is A-orderable. Show that there exists an extension E of K such that E is pythagorean, orderable, and that every odd degree polynomial over E has a root in E, but that E does not admit the structure of a maximal ordered field (consider a maximal A-orderable extension of K).
