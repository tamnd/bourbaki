Let I be the semilattice ordered monoid of ideals of a commutative ring A (Ex. 24). For a finite system of congruences $ x \equiv a_i (a_i,) $ to admit a solution whenever any two of them admit a common solution (that is to say whenever

$$
a_i \equiv a_j (a_i + a_j)
$$

for every pair of indices i, j), it is necessary and sufficient that each of the laws

$$
(a, b) \mapsto a \cap b, (a, b) \mapsto a + b,
$$

in I be distributive over the other ("Chinese remainder theorem"). One may proceed as follows:
a) If $ (a, \cap a_2) + (a, \cap a_3) = a, \cap (a, + a_3) $ and if any two of the three congruences $ x \equiv a_i (a_i,) $ ($ i = 1, 2, 3 $) admit a common solution, then the three congruences have a common solution (let $ x_{12} $ be a common solution of $ x \equiv a_1(a_1) $ and $ x \equiv a_2(a_2) $ and let $ x_{13} $ be a common solution of $ x \equiv a_1(a_1) $ and $ x \equiv a_3(a_3) $; show that the congruences $ x = x_{12}(a_1 \cap a_2) $ and $ x \equiv x_{13}(a_1 \cap a_3) $ have a common solution).
b) If any system of three congruences, any two of which have a common solution, admits a common solution, show that the following distributive laws hold

$$
a + (b \cap c) - (a \cap b) \cap (a + c), \quad \text{and} \quad a \cap (b + c) - (a \cap b) + (a \cap c).
$$

(For the first, note that for all $ x \in (a + b) \cap (a + c) $, there exists $ y \in b \cap c $ such that $ y \equiv x(a) $; for the second, note that for all $ x \in a \cap (b + c) $, there exists $ y \in a \cap b $ such that $ y \equiv x(c) $.) Note that by a) and b), the second distributive law implies the first (cf. Set Theory, III, p. 217, Ex. 16).
c) Prove the « Chinese remainder theorem » by induction on the number of congruences under consideration, by an analogous argument to that of a).
d) *Translation in the case of principal ideal domains.*
