Let $F$ be a set, $C$ a subset of $F\times F$ and $m: C\rightarrow F$ a mapping. We make the following hypotheses:

(i) The mappings from C into $F\times F$ given by $(f, g)\mapsto (f, m(f, g))$ and $(f, g)\mapsto (m(f, g), g)$ are injective ;

(ii) If $f,g,h\in F$ are such that the pairs $(f, g)$ and $(g, h)$ belong to C, then the pairs $(f, m(g, h))$ and $(m(f, g), h)$ belong to C and one has $m(f, m(g, h)) =m(m(f, g), h)$ ;
