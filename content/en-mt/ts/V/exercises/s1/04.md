Let G be a topological group. One says that G is amenable if there exists a linear form $\lambda :\mathscr{C}_b(G;\mathbf{R})\rightarrow \mathbf{R}$ such that

(i) If $f\geqslant 0$, then $\lambda (f)\geqslant 0$.

(ii) One has $\lambda (1) = 1$.

(iii) For every $g\in G$ and every $f\in \mathscr{C}_b(G;\mathbf{R})$, one has $\lambda (\boldsymbol{\gamma }(g)f) =\lambda (\boldsymbol{\delta }(g)f)$, where

$$
\boldsymbol{\gamma }(g)f(x) =f(g^{-1}x),\boldsymbol{\delta }(g)f(x) =f(xg)
$$

If G is a discrete group, this definition coincides with that of EVT, IV, p. 73, exerc. 4.

a) The topological group G is amenable if and only if for every integer $n\in \mathbf{N}$, every finite family $(f_i)_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}$ in $\mathscr{C}_b(G;\mathbf{R})$, every families $(g_i)_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}$ and $(h_i)_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}$ in G, every real number $a$, the condition

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n(f_i(x)-f_i(g_ixh_i))\geqslant a
$$

for every $x\in G$ implies that $a\leqslant 0$. (To prove that this condition is sufficient, consider the set of functions of the form

$$
x\mapsto \sum_{i=1}^n(f_i(x)-f_i(g_ixh_i))
$$

and apply Hahn–Banach theorem.)

b) Every compact topological group is amenable, every abelian topological group is amenable.

c) Let G be a non-commutative free group. The discrete group G is not amenable.

d) For every Hilbert space E and every bounded continuous representation of G in E, there exists a Hilbert norm $q$ on E such that the representation of G in the Hilbert space E endowed with $q$ is unitary.
