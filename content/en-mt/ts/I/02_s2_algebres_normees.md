## § 2. NORMED ALGEBRAS

In this section, K denotes one of the fields $\mathbf{R}$ or $\mathbf{C}$.

### 1. Generalities

Recall (cf. TG, IX, p. 37, def. 9) that a normed algebra is an algebra A over K provided with a norm $x\mapsto  \|x\|$ such that:

$$
\|xy\|\leqslant \|x\| \|y\| \tag{1}
$$

for all $x,y\in A$. If A is complete, one says that A is a Banach algebra.

Let A be a complete normable algebra over K. The topology of A can be defined by a norm satisfying (1) (cf. TG, IX, p. 38). When A is provided with the normed algebra structure defined by such a norm, one will also say that the algebra A is a Banach algebra.

We also recall the following facts (cf. TG, IX, p. 38–39):

(1) If A is a non-zero normed algebra and has a unit element $e$, then $\|e\|\geqslant 1$.

(2) Let A be a normed K-algebra. The opposite algebra of A, provided with the same norm, is a normed algebra. The completion of A, provided with the norm obtained by extension by continuity of the norm of A, is a Banach K-algebra. Every subalgebra of A, provided with the induced norm, is a normed algebra. If I is a closed two-sided ideal of A, the algebra $A/I$, provided with the norm defined by $\|\dot{x}\|=$ inf$_{x\in\dot{x}}\|x\|$ for every $\dot{x}\in A/I$, is a normed algebra.

(3) Let $(A_i)_{\in I}$ be a family of normed algebras and B the product algebra of the algebras $A_i$. The subalgebra of the elements $(x_i)_{i\in I}$ of B such that $\|(x_i)\|=$ sup$_{i\in I}\|x_i\|<+\infty$, is a normed algebra, called the product normed algebra of the algebras $(A_i)_{i\in I}$. If $A_i$ is a Banach algebra for every $i$, then A is a Banach algebra.

Let A be a normed algebra. Let $(y_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of elements of A; the smallest closed subalgebra B of A containing the elements $y_i$ is called the closed subalgebra of A generated by the $y_i$; if B = A, one says that the $y_i$ topologically generate the normed algebra A, or that the family $(y_i)_{i\in I}$ is a topological generating system of the normed algebra A.

Similarly, if A is a unital normed algebra, the smallest closed unital subalgebra containing the elements $y_i$ is called the closed unital subalgebra of A generated by the $y_i$. If it is equal to A, one says that the $y_i$ topologically generate the unital normed algebra A.

Let A be a normed K-algebra. Denote by $\widetilde{A}$ the algebra obtained from A by adjunction of a unit element. On $\widetilde{A}$, define a norm by putting $\|(\lambda , x)\|=|\lambda |+\|x\|$ for every $\lambda \in K$ and every $x\in A$. We have

$$
\|(\lambda , x)(\mu, y)\|=|\lambda \mu|+\|xy+\mu x+\lambda y\|
$$

$$
\leqslant |\lambda | |\mu|+\|x\| \|y\|+|\mu| \|x\|+|\lambda | \|y\|
$$

$$
=\|(\lambda , x)\| \|(\mu, y)\|
$$

Thus $\widetilde{A}$ becomes a normed algebra, called the unital normed algebra deduced from A by adjunction of a unit element. The algebra A is identified with the closed two-sided ideal $\{0\} \times A$ of $\widetilde{A}$.

#### Definition 1 {#ts-i-s2-def-1 .statement tag=025W}

Let A be a normed algebra. For $a\in A$, denote by $\boldsymbol{\gamma }_a$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta }_a$ the mappings $x\mapsto ax$, and $x\mapsto xa$ from A into A. The mapping $\boldsymbol{\gamma }:a\mapsto \boldsymbol{\gamma }_a$ is a representation of A in A, called the left regular representation of A. The mapping $\boldsymbol{\delta }:a\mapsto \boldsymbol{\delta }_a$ is a representation of the opposite algebra of A in A, called the right regular representation of A.

#### Lemma 1 {#ts-i-s2-lem-1 .statement tag=025X}

Let A be a normed algebra. The left regular representation of A and the right regular representation of A are continuous of norm $\leqslant 1$.

If the algebra A is unital, the mappings $x\mapsto  \|\boldsymbol{\gamma }_x\|$ and $x\mapsto  \|\boldsymbol{\delta }_x\|$ are norms on A, defining the topology of A. They satisfy the inequality (1).

If the unital normed algebra A is non-zero, that is, if $e\not= 0$, we also have $\|\boldsymbol{\gamma }_e\|=\|\boldsymbol{\delta }_e\|= 1$.

It is immediate that $\|\boldsymbol{\gamma }_x\|\leqslant \|x\|$ and that $\|\boldsymbol{\delta }_x\|\leqslant \|x\|$.

If A has a unit element $e$, then we have $x=\boldsymbol{\gamma }_xe=\boldsymbol{\delta }_xe$, hence: (2) $\|x\|\leqslant \|\boldsymbol{\gamma }_x\| \cdot  \|e\|\|x\|\leqslant \|\boldsymbol{\delta }_x\| \cdot  \|e\|$.

In this case, $x\mapsto  \|\boldsymbol{\gamma }_x\|$ and $x\mapsto  \|\boldsymbol{\delta }_x\|$ are therefore norms equivalent to the norm of A. They satisfy (1) since $\boldsymbol{\gamma }$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta }$ are representations.

The last assertion follows from the fact that $\boldsymbol{\gamma }_e=\boldsymbol{\delta }_e=$ Id$_A$.

### 2. Examples

1) Let E be a normed space. Equip E with the product defined by $ab= 0$ for all $a$ and $b$ in E; this defines on E a structure of normed algebra.

2) Let X be a set. We denote by $\mathscr{B}(X; K)$ the normed algebra of bounded functions on X with values in K, endowed with the norm

$\|f\|=$ sup$_{x\in X}|f(x)|$

(TG, X, p. 22). It is a unital Banach algebra over K (TG, X, p. 21, cor. 1). It is commutative. Let $f$ be an element of $\mathscr{B}(X; K)$. Then $f$ is invertible in $\mathscr{B}(X; K)$ if and only if one has

inf$_{x\in X}|f(x)|>0$.

The spectrum of $f$ is therefore the set of the $\lambda \in K$ such that

inf$_{x\in X}|f(x)-\lambda |= 0$,

that is to say the closure in K of the set $f(X)$ of the values of $f$.

3) Let X be a topological space. We denote by $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K)$ the unital subalgebra of $\mathscr{B}(X; K)$ of continuous and bounded functions on X with values in K$,\mathscr{C}_0(X; K)$ the subalgebra of $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K)$ consisting of functions which tend to 0 at infinity (cf. INT, III, §1, n$^o2$ and TG, X, p. 40, cor. 2). We recall that $\mathscr{K}(X; K)$ denotes the subalgebra of $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K)$ of continuous functions with compact support.

The algebras $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K)$ and $\mathscr{C}_0(X; K)$ are commutative Banach algebras over K; indeed, they are closed subspaces of $\mathscr{B}(X; K)$ (TG, X, p. 21, cor. 2 and INT, III, §1, n$^o2$).

The inverse of an everywhere nonzero continuous function being continuous, the subalgebra $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K)$ is a full subalgebra of $\mathscr{B}(X; K)$. In particular, the spectrum of an element $f$ of $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K)$ is equal to $\overline{f(X)}$.

If X is discrete, one has $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K) =\mathscr{B}(X; K)$.

If X is compact, one has $\mathscr{C}_b(X; K) =\mathscr{K}(X; K) =\mathscr{C}(X; K)$, the unital normed algebra $\mathscr{C}(X; K)$ of continuous functions on X with values in K. In this case, an element $f\in \mathscr{C}(X; K)$ is invertible if and only if $f$ does not take the value 0, and the spectrum of $f$ is equal to the set $f(X)$ of the values of $f$.

Suppose now that X is not compact. The algebra $\mathscr{C}_0(X; K)$ is then not unital; the algebra obtained from it by adjunction of a unit element is identified with the subalgebra $K\cdot 1\oplus \mathscr{C}_0(X; K)$ of $\mathscr{C}(X; K)$ formed by the functions which have a limit at infinity. For an element of this subalgebra to be invertible, it is necessary and sufficient that it does not vanish and that its limit at infinity is not zero. It follows that for every $f\in \mathscr{C}_0(X; K)$, the spectrum of $f$ is equal to $f(X)\cup  \{0\}$.

The spectrum of $f\in \mathscr{K}(X; K)$ is equal to $f(X)$ (indeed, if X is not compact, 0 belongs to $f(X)$).

4) Let $n\geqslant 0$ be an integer. Let $A_n$ be the algebra of functions $f: [0,1]\rightarrow K$ admitting continuous derivatives in $[0,1]$ up to order $n$, endowed with the norm

$\|f\|=\sum^nk1$! sup$_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}|f^{(k)}(t)|$.

$k=0$

If $f, g\in A_n$, one has

$\|f g\|=\sum^nk1$! sup$|(f g)^{(k)}(t)|=\sum^nk1$! sup$|\sum^k(ks)f^{(s)}(t)g^{(k-s)}(t)|$

$k=0k=0s=0$

$\leqslant \sum^n\sum^ks$!($k1-s$)! sup$_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}|f^{(s)}(t)|$ sup$_{0\leqslant t\leqslant 1}|g^{(k-s)}(t)|=\|f\| \|g\|$,

$k=0s=0$

by the Leibniz formula (FVR, I, p. 28, prop. 2), hence $A_n$ is a unital commutative Banach algebra.

5) Let E be a Banach space whose norm is denoted by $p$. The algebra $\mathscr{L}(E)$ of continuous endomorphisms of E, endowed with the norm

$\|u\|=$ sup$_{p(x)\leqslant 1}p(u(x))$

is a unital Banach algebra (EVT, III, p. 14 and p. 24, cor. 2 ; TG, X, p. 23, formula (3)).

6) Let G be a locally compact group. Let $e$ denote its unit element. Let $\mathscr{M}^1(G)$ be the Banach space of bounded complex measures on G (INT, III, p. 57). The convolution product (INT, VIII, p. 120, def. 1) endows $\mathscr{M}^1(G)$ with a complex Banach algebra structure (INT, VIII, §3, no$^o1$, prop. 2) admitting as unit element the measure $\varepsilon_e$ defined by the unit mass placed at the point $e$. If G is commutative, this Banach algebra is commutative. The space $\mathscr{C}'(G)$ of measures with compact support is a subalgebra of $\mathscr{M}^1(G)$ (loc. cit.).

7) Let G be a locally compact group endowed with a Haar measure $\mu$. Then $L^1_K(G, \mu)$ is a Banach algebra for the convolution product (INT, VIII, prop. 12, p. 166). If $K =\mathbf{C}$, the mapping defined by $f\mapsto f \mu$ permits one to identify $L^1_{\mathbf{C}}(G, \mu)$ with a subalgebra of the Banach algebra $\mathscr{M}^1(G)$.

If G is commutative, the Banach algebra $L^1_K(G, \mu)$ is commutative.

8) Take $G =\mathbf{Z}$ and $K =\mathbf{C}$ in Example 7. Then $L^1_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{Z})$ is the complex commutative Banach algebra of sequences $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}$ such that $\sum_n|x_n|<+\infty$, the product of the elements $(x_n)$ and $(y_n)$ being $(z_n)$, where

$$

and the norm $\|(x_n)\|=\sum_n|x_n|$. This algebra admits as unit element the sequence $\varepsilon = (\varepsilon_n)$ such that $\varepsilon_0= 1$ and $\varepsilon_n= 0$ for $n\not= 0$.

Let $\mathbf{U}$ denote the unit circle in $\mathbf{C}$. If $x= (c_n)$ is an element of A, let $\varphi (x)$ be the continuous function on $\mathbf{U}$ whose value at $e^{it}$ is

$$
\varphi (x)(e^{it}) =\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}c_ne^{int}
$$

One verifies that $\varphi$ is a morphism of $L^1_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{Z})$ onto an algebra A of continuous functions on $\mathbf{U}$, the multiplication in A being the usual multiplication. By integrating term by term the equality

$$
((\sum_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}c_me^{imt})\cdot e^{-int}=\varphi (x)(e^{it})\cdot e^{-int}
$$

there results

$$
c_n=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^1\varphi (x)(e^{it})e^{-int}dt
$$

In particular, it follows that the morphism $\varphi$ is injective. The algebra A, endowed with the norm deduced from that of $L^1_{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{Z})$ by $\varphi$, is called the Banach algebra of absolutely convergent Fourier series. It admits as unit element the function $1 =\varphi (\varepsilon )$.

9) Let Δ be the disk of complex numbers $z$ satisfying $|z|\leqslant 1$. The algebra A of continuous functions on Δ analytic in the interior of Δ (VAR, R1, p. 26, 3.2.1) is endowed with the norm $\|f\|=$ sup$_{z\in\Delta}|f(z)|$. Then A is a commutative unital Banach algebra.

### 3. Spectral radius

#### Lemma 2 (Fekete Lemma) {#ts-i-s2-lem-2 .statement tag=025Y}

Let $(a_n)_{n\geqslant 1}$ be a sequence of real numbers. Suppose that

$$
a_{n+m}\leqslant a_n+a_m
$$

for all $n\geqslant 1$ and all $m\geqslant 1$. Then the sequence $(a_n/n)_{n\geqslant 1}$ converges and satisfies

lim$_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}=$ inf$_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{a_n}{n}$.

Put $a_0= 0$ ; the inequality $a_{n+m}\leqslant a_n+a_m$ remains valid for all $n\geqslant$ 0 and all $m\geqslant 0$. Fix an integer $m\geqslant$ 1. For every integer $n\geqslant 1$, let $q(n)$ and $r(n)$ be the integers such that $n=q(n)m+r(n)$ and $0\leqslant r(n)< m$ (E, III, p. 39, th. 1). The hypothesis then implies

$$
\frac{a_n}{n}\leqslant \frac{a_{q(n)m}}{n}+\frac{a_{r(n)}}{n}\leqslant \frac{q(n)a_m}{n}+\frac{a_{r(n)}}{n}\leqslant \frac{q(n)}{n}a_m+\frac{m}{n}
$$

Letting $n$ tend to $+\infty$, we deduce that lim sup$_n(a_n/n)\leqslant a_m/m$ since $q(n)/n\rightarrow 1/m$. Since this holds for every $m\geqslant 1$, we therefore have

lim sup$_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}\leqslant$ inf$_{m\geqslant 1}\frac{a_m}{m}\leqslant$ lim inf$_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}$.

These inequalities prove the convergence of the sequence $(a_n/n)_{n\geqslant 1}$ as well as the formula lim $a_n/n=$ inf$_{n\geqslant 1}a_n/n$.

#### Proposition 1 {#ts-i-s2-prop-1 .statement tag=025Z}

Let A be a normed algebra. For every $x\in A$, the sequence $(\|x^n\|^{1/n})_{n\geqslant 1}$ is convergent and its limit $\varrho (x)$ is equal to inf$_{n\geqslant 1}\|x^n\|^{1/n}$. Moreover, for every norm $x\mapsto  \|x\|_1$ defining the topology of A, one also has

$\varrho (x) =$ lim$_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|x^n\|^{1/n}_1=$ inf$_{n\geqslant 1}\|x^n\|^{1/n}_1$.

If $x$ is nilpotent, one has $\|x^n\|^{1/n}_1= 0$ for every sufficiently large integer $n$ and every norm $x\mapsto  \|x\|_1$ defining the topology of A.

Suppose now that $x$ is not nilpotent, and put $\alpha_n=$ $\|x^n\|$. One has $\alpha_n>0$ for every integer $n\geqslant 1$, and $\alpha_{n+m}\leqslant \alpha_n\alpha_m$ for all $n, m\in \mathbf{N}$ according to (1). Lemma 2, applied to the sequence $a_n=$ log($\alpha_n$), shows the existence of the limit $\varrho (x)$ and the formula $\varrho (x) =$ inf$_{n>0}\alpha_n^{1/n}$.

Let $x\mapsto  \|x\|_1$ be a norm defining the topology of A. There exist real numbers $a >0$ and $b >0$ such that

$$
a\|x\|\leqslant \|x\|_1\leqslant b\|x\|
$$

for all $x\in A$ (EVT, II, p. 7, cor. 2). Consequently,

$$
a^{1/n}\|x^n\|^{1/n}\leqslant \|x^n\|^{1/n}_1\leqslant b^{1/n}\|x^n\|^{1/n}
$$

for all $n\geqslant 1$, whence, by passing to the limit, or by taking the greatest lower bound, the equality

$\varrho (x) =$ lim$_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|x^n\|^{1/n}_1=$ inf$_{n>0}\|x^n\|^{1/n}_1$.

#### Definition 2 {#ts-i-s2-def-2 .statement tag=0260}

For every element $x$ of a normed algebra A, the real number

$\varrho (x) =$ lim$_{n\rightarrow \infty}\|x^n\|^{1/n}=$ inf$_{n>0}\|x^n\|^{1/n}$

is called the spectral radius of $x$.

For every element $x$ of A, one has

$$
\varrho (x)\leqslant \|x\| \tag{3}
$$

(4) $\varrho (x^n) =\varrho (x)^n$, for every integer $n\geqslant 1$.

#### Definition 3 {#ts-i-s2-def-3 .statement tag=0261}

An element $x$ of A is quasi-nilpotent if $\varrho (x) = 0$.

This amounts to saying that, whatever $\lambda \in K$ may be, the numbers $\|(\lambda x)^n\|$ are bounded for $n\geqslant 1$; or again that, whatever $\lambda \in K$ may be, the sequence $(\lambda x)^n$ tends to 0 when $n\rightarrow +\infty$.

#### Remark 1 {#ts-i-s2-n3-rem-1 .statement tag=0262}

Let A be a normed algebra. If an element $x\in A$ satisfies $\varrho (x) =\|x\|$, one has $\|x^n\|=\|x\|^n$ for every $n\in \mathbf{N}$, by (3) and (4).

Conversely, suppose that $\|x^2\|=\|x\|^2$ for every $x\in A$. Then, for every integer $n\geqslant 0$, one has the equality $\|x^{2^n}\|=\|x\|^{2^n}$, hence $\|x\|=$ $\|x^{2^n}\|^{2^{-n}}$; when $n$ tends to $+\infty$, one obtains $\|x\|=\varrho (x)$.

#### Remark 2 {#ts-i-s2-n3-rem-2 .statement tag=0263}

The function $x\mapsto \varrho (x)$ on A, being the greatest lower bound of the continuous functions $x\mapsto  \|x^n\|^{1/n}$ for $n\geqslant 1$, is upper semi-continuous (TG, IV, p. 31, cor.), but in general it is not continuous. It may even happen (cf. exerc. 12 of I, p. 157) that a sequence of nilpotent elements of A tends to an element which is not quasi-nilpotent.

### 4. Inverses

Let A be a unital Banach algebra, whose unit element is denoted by 1. Recall (TG, IX, prop. 14, p. 40) that the group G of invertible elements of A is an open subset of A, that the topology induced on G by that of A is compatible with the group structure and that the topological group G is complete.

#### Proposition 2 {#ts-i-s2-prop-2 .statement tag=0264}

Let A be a Banach algebra and $x$ an element of A. The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^nx^n$, considered as a power series in $\lambda$, has radius of convergence $\varrho (x)^{-1}$. If A is unital and if $\varrho (x)<1$, then $1-x$ is invertible and has as inverse $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n$.

The series $\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\lambda^nx^n$ has radius of convergence

(lim sup$_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|x^n\|^{1/n}$)$^{-1}=\varrho (x)^{-1}$

(cf. VAR, R1, p. 23, 3.1.4). Suppose that A admits a unit element. If $\varrho (x)<1$, the series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n$ is therefore absolutely convergent. Since

$$
(1-x)(\sum_{n=0}^kx^n)=(\sum_{n=0}^kx^n)(1-x) = 1-x^{k+1}
$$

for every integer $k\geqslant 0$, the element $1-x$ is invertible and its inverse is equal to $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n$.

#### Corollary 1 {#ts-i-s2-prop-2-cor-1 .statement tag=0265}

If A is a unital Banach algebra, then the group of invertible elements of A contains the open ball with center 1 and radius 1.

This is immediate since $\|x\|<1$ implies $\varrho (x)<1$.

#### Corollary 2 {#ts-i-s2-prop-2-cor-2 .statement tag=0266}

Let A be a Banach algebra and I a regular maximal left ideal (resp. right ideal) of A. Then I is closed.

Let $(\widetilde{A}, e)$ be the unital Banach algebra deduced from A by adjunction of a unit element. There exists a maximal left ideal (resp. right) J of $\widetilde{A}$ such that $J\cap A = I$ (A, VIII, p. 428, prop. 4). Then J is disjoint from the open ball of center $e$ and of radius 1 (cor. 1), and therefore $\overline{J}\not=\widetilde{A}$. Since J is a maximal ideal, this implies that $\overline{J}= J$, and consequently that $I = J\cap A =\overline{J}\cap A$ is closed in A.

#### Corollaire 3 {#ts-i-s2-prop-2-cor-3 .statement tag=0267}

The radical of a Banach algebra is closed.

Indeed the radical is the intersection of the regular maximal left ideals (A, VIII, p. 430, déf. 3).

#### Proposition 3 {#ts-i-s2-prop-3 .statement tag=0268}

Let A be a unital Banach algebra.

a) If $x\in A$ admits a left inverse (resp. right) $y$, every element $x'\in A$ such that $\|x'-x\|<\|y\|^{-1}$ admits a left inverse (resp. right).

b) The set of elements of A which are invertible (resp. left, resp. right) is open in A.

c) Let $(x_n)$ be a sequence of elements of A admitting left inverses (resp. right)$y_n$, and converging to an element $x\in A$. If the sequence $(y_n)$ is bounded, then $x$ is invertible on the left (resp. right).

It suffices to treat the case of left inverses; that of right inverses follows by considering the opposite algebra.

Let $x, y, x'\in A$ be such that $yx= 1$ and $\|x'-x\|<\|y\|^{-1}$. We have

$$
\|1-yx'\|=\|yx-yx'\|\leqslant \|y\| \cdot  \|x-x'\|<1
$$

therefore $yx'$ is invertible: there exists $z\in$ A such that $z(yx') = 1$. Thus the element $x'$ is left invertible with inverse $zy$. This proves assertion a) and assertion b) follows from it immediately.

Let $(x_n)$ be a sequence of elements of A admitting left inverses $y_n$, which converges to an element $x\in$ A, and such that the sequence $(y_n)$ is bounded. If $M\geqslant 1$ is a real number such that $\|y_n\|\leqslant M$ for all $n\geqslant 1$, then we have $\|x_n-x\|<M^{-1}\leqslant \|y_n\|^{-1}$ for $n$ sufficiently large, and therefore $x$ admits a left inverse according to a).

#### Définition 4 {#ts-i-s2-def-4 .statement tag=0269}

Let A be a normed algebra, let $x$ be an element of A. Let us denote by $\boldsymbol{\gamma }_x$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta }_x$ the mappings $y\mapsto xy$ and $y\mapsto yx$ of A into A. We say that $x$ is a topological zero divisor on the left (resp. right) if $\boldsymbol{\gamma }_x($resp. $\boldsymbol{\delta }_x)$ is not a homeomorphism of A onto $\boldsymbol{\gamma }_x(A)$ (resp. onto $\boldsymbol{\delta }_x(A)$).

#### Remarque {#ts-i-s2-n4-rem-1 .statement tag=026A}

According to TG, IX, p. 36, cor. $2,x$ is a topological zero divisor on the left (resp. right) if and only if there exists a sequence $(z_n)$ in A such that $\|z_n\|= 1$ and such that $xz_n$ tends to 0 (resp. that $z_nx$ tends to 0) when $n\rightarrow +\infty$.

A left zero divisor (resp. right) is a topological zero divisor on the left (resp. right). Suppose that A is nonzero and unital. A topological zero divisor on the left (resp. right) $x$ is not left invertible (resp. right). Indeed, if for example $yx= 1$ and if $xz_n$ tends to 0, then $z_n=y(xz_n)$ tends to 0 and one cannot have $\|z_n\|= 1$ for all $n$.

#### Proposition 4 {#ts-i-s2-prop-4 .statement tag=026B}

Let A be a unital Banach algebra. Let $x$ be an element of A which is not left invertible. If there exists a sequence $(x_n)$ of left invertible elements of A which converges to $x$, then $x$ is a topological right zero divisor.

Let $y_n$ be a left inverse of $x_n$. By Prop. 3 (ii), $\|y_n\|$ tends to $+\infty$. Let $z_n=\|y_n\|^{-1}y_n$. We have $\|z_n\|= 1$, and $z_nx_n=\|y_n\|^{-1}$ tends to 0, hence $z_nx=z_nx_n+z_n(x-x_n)$ tends to 0. One then concludes with the aid of the remark following Definition 4.

#### Proposition 5 {#ts-i-s2-prop-5 .statement tag=026C}

Let A be a unital Banach algebra and let B be a full subalgebra of A. Then B is a full subalgebra of A.

In fact, let $x$ be an element of $\overline{B}$ invertible in A, and $(x_n)$ a sequence of elements of B tending to $x$. Then, for $n$ sufficiently large, $x_n$ is invertible in A and $x^{-1}_n$ tends to $x^{-1}$. Since the subalgebra B is full, we have $x^{-1}_n\in B$, whence $x^{-1}\in \overline{B}$.

Let A be a unital Banach algebra and let $(y_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of elements of A. Let B be the full subalgebra of A generated by the elements $y_i$. Then $\overline{B}$ is the smallest closed full subalgebra of A containing the $y_i$. It is called the closed full subalgebra generated by the elements $y_i$.

### 5. Spectrum of an element in a normed algebra

In this number, it is assumed that $K =\mathbf{C}$.

#### Theorem 1 {#ts-i-s2-thm-1 .statement tag=026D}

Let A be a unital Banach algebra and $x\in A$.

a) The set Sp$_A(x)$ is a compact subset of $\mathbf{C}$;

b) The spectral radius $\varrho (x)$ is the radius of the smallest closed disk with center 0 in $\mathbf{C}$ which contains Sp$_A(x)$ ;

c) The resolvent $\lambda \mapsto R(x, \lambda ) = (\lambda -x)^{-1}$ of $x$ is holomorphic in $\mathbf{C}-$ Sp$_A(x)$ and zero at infinity. Moreover, for every integer $k\geqslant 0$, we have the formula

$\frac{\partial^k}{\partial \lambda^k}R(x, \lambda ) = (-1)^kk$! $R(x, \lambda )^{k+1}$;

d) For every complex number $\lambda$ such that $|\lambda |>1/\varrho (x)$, we have

$$
R(x, \lambda ) =\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-n-1}x^n
$$

The complement of the spectrum Sp$_A(x)$ is the inverse image of the group G of invertible elements of A by the continuous mapping $\lambda \mapsto$ $x-\lambda$ of $\mathbf{C}$ into A; by Proposition 3, b) of I, p. 23, the spectrum Sp$_A(x)$ is a closed subset of $\mathbf{C}$. On the other hand, let $\lambda \in \mathbf{C}$ be such that $|\lambda |> \varrho (x)$. We have $\lambda -x=\lambda (1-\lambda^{-1}x)$. Since $\varrho (\lambda^{-1}x) =|\lambda |^{-1}\varrho (x)<1$, the element $1-\lambda^{-1}x$, hence also the element $\lambda -x$, is invertible and

$$
R(x, \lambda ) = (\lambda -x)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-n-1}x^n \tag{5}
$$

(I, p. 22, Proposition 2). In particular, $\lambda \notin$ Sp$_A(x)$. This proves that Sp$_A(x)$ is contained in the disc with center 0 and radius $\varrho (x)$. Hence Sp$_A(x)$ is compact. This formula (5) also proves that the resolvent of $x$ is defined and holomorphic in the complement of the closed disc $\Delta_{\varrho(x)}$ with center 0 and radius $\varrho (x)$, and tends to 0 at infinity.

Let $\lambda_0\in \mathbf{C}-$ Sp$_A(x)$. Put $y=\lambda_0-x$. Let $\mu\in \mathbf{C}$ be such that $|\lambda_0-\mu|<\|y^{-1}\|^{-1}$. We have

$$
\mu-x=y-(\lambda_0-\mu) =y(1-(\lambda_0-\mu)y^{-1})
$$

therefore $\mu-x$ is invertible and has inverse

$$
(\mu-x)^{-1}=y^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\lambda_0-\mu)^ny^{-n} \tag{6}
$$

by Proposition 2 of I, p. 22. Therefore the resolvent of $x$ is defined and holomorphic in the open disc with center $\lambda_0$ and radius $\|y^{-1}\|^{-1}$. Hence the resolvent of $x$ is a holomorphic mapping of $\mathbf{C}-$ Sp$_A(x)$ into A.

Formula (1) of I, p. 4 implies $\frac{\partial}{\partial \lambda}R(x, \lambda ) =-R(x, \lambda )^2$, whence, by induction on $k$,

$\frac{\partial^k}{\partial \lambda^k}R(x, \lambda ) = (-1)^kk$! $R(x, \lambda )^{k+1}$.

Let $a >0$ be a real number such that Sp$_A(x)$ is contained in the closed disc $\Delta_a$ with center 0 and radius $a$. The function $\lambda \mapsto (\lambda^{-1}-x)^{-1}$ is then defined and holomorphic for $0<|\lambda |< a^{-1}$ and tends to 0 when $\lambda$ tends to 0. The unique continuous function on the open disc with center 0 and radius $a^{-1}$ which extends this holomorphic function is then holomorphic (VAR, R1, 3.3.9), hence the radius of convergence of the series (5) which defines it is $\geqslant a^{-1}$ (VAR, R1, 3.2.9). By Proposition 2 of I, p. 22, we therefore have $a\geqslant \varrho (x)$.

#### Remark 1 {#ts-i-s2-n5-rem-1 .statement tag=026E}

The spectrum of an element in a unital Banach algebra may be any nonempty compact subset F of $\mathbf{C}($cf. Example 3 of I, p. 17; for $A =\mathscr{C}(F;\mathbf{C})$ and $f\in A$ the canonical injection of F into $\mathbf{C}$, we have Sp$_A(f) = F$).

#### Remark 2 {#ts-i-s2-n5-rem-2 .statement tag=026F}

Let A be a unital Banach algebra and let $x\in A$. By Theorem 1 of I, p. 24$,\mathbf{C}-$ Sp$_A(x)$ is an open subset of $\mathbf{C}$, hence is locally connected. Therefore the connected components of $\mathbf{C}-$ Sp$_A(x)$ are open. By Theorem 1, one of these connected components contains the set of $\lambda \in \mathbf{C}$ such that $|\lambda |> \varrho (x)$; all the other connected components are therefore bounded.

#### Corollary 1 {#ts-i-s2-thm-1-cor-1 .statement tag=026G}

Let A be a nonzero unital normed algebra. For every $x\in A$, the spectrum Sp$_A(x)$ is nonempty.

Suppose first that A is complete. If one had Sp($x$) $=\emptyset$, the resolvent of $x$ would be holomorphic in $\mathbf{C}$ and zero at infinity, hence identically zero (VAR, R., 3.3.6, p. 29). Since $R(x, \lambda ) = (\lambda -x)^{-1}$ is invertible, it would follow that 1 = 0 and therefore that $A =\{0\}$.

In the general case, let $\widehat{A}$ be the completed algebra of A; the relation Sp$_A(x) =\emptyset$ would entail Sp$_{\widehat{A}}(x) =\emptyset$, whence $\widehat{A} =\{0\}$ and $A =\{0\}$.

#### Corollary 2 (Gelfand-Mazur Theorem) {#ts-i-s2-thm-1-cor-2 .statement tag=026H}

Let A be a normed algebra over $\mathbf{C}$. If A is a field, then $A =\mathbf{C}\cdot 1$.

If $x\in A$, there exists $\lambda \in \mathbf{C}$ such that $x-\lambda$ is not invertible (cor. 1), whence $x-\lambda = 0$ and $x\in \mathbf{C}\cdot 1$.

#### Corollary 3 {#ts-i-s2-thm-1-cor-3 .statement tag=026I}

Let A be a unital Banach algebra and $x$ an invertible element of A such that $\|x\|=\|x^{-1}\|= 1$. Then Sp($x$)$\subset \mathbf{U}$.

Let Δ be the disc with center 0 and radius 1 in $\mathbf{C}$. By theorem 1 b) and the fact that $\varrho (x)\leqslant \|x\|$, one has Sp($x$)$\subset \Delta$. Analogously, Sp($x$)$^{-1}$ = Sp($x^{-1}$)$\subset \Delta$, whence the corollary (cf. I, p. 2, remark 4).

#### Corollary 4 {#ts-i-s2-thm-1-cor-4 .statement tag=026J}

Let E be a complex Banach space, $\mathscr{L}(E)$ the Banach algebra of continuous endomorphisms of E and A a nonzero subalgebra of $\mathscr{L}(E)$ such that E is a simple A-pseudomodule (A, II, p. 176, Appendix).

a) Let $u$ be an endomorphism of E, not necessarily continuous, which commutes with A. Then $u$ is a homothety;

b) Let $u$ be an endomorphism of E, not necessarily continuous. For every integer $n\geqslant 1$ and every $(\xi_1, . . . , \xi_n)\in E^n$, there exists $v\in A$ such that

$$
(v(\xi_1), . . . , v(\xi_n)) = (u(\xi_1), . . . , u(\xi_n))
$$

Let us prove a). Let $\widetilde{A}$ be the algebra obtained from A by adjunction of a unit element. Since E is a simple A-pseudomodule, it is a simple $\widetilde{A}$-module.

Let B be the commutant of $\widetilde{A}$ in the ring of endomorphisms of the $\mathbf{C}$-vector space E. The algebra B contains 1 and is the endomorphism algebra of the $\widetilde{A}$-module E. Since E is a simple $\widetilde{A}$-module, Schur’s Lemma (A, VIII, p. 43, corollary) shows that B is a field.

Let $\xi_0\in E$ be such that $A\xi_0\not=\{0\}$. One has therefore $A\xi_0= E$. For every $u\in B$, let $A_u$ be the set of $v\in A$ such that $v(\xi_0) =u(\xi_0)$. This set is nonempty, since $A\xi_0= E$. One then puts

$\|u\|_B=$ inf$_{v\in A_u}\|v\|$.

The mapping $u\mapsto  \|u\|_B$ is a seminorm on B.

Let us prove that this mapping is a norm. Let $u$ be a nonzero element of B. For every $v\in A_u$, one has $\|v\|\geqslant \|v(\xi_0)\|/\|\xi_0\|=\|u(\xi_0)\|/\|\xi_0\|$, so that $\|u\|_B\geqslant \|u(\xi_0)\|/\|\xi_0\|$. It is therefore enough to prove that $u(\xi_0)\not= 0$. Let $\xi_1\in E$ be such that $u(\xi_1)\not= 0$. Since $A\xi_0= E$, there exists $w\in A$ such that $\xi_1=w(\xi_0)$. Then $wu(\xi_0) =uw(\xi_0) =u(\xi_1)\not= 0$, hence $u(\xi_0)\not= 0$.

On the other hand, let $u$ and $u'$ be elements of B. For every $\varepsilon  >0$, there exist $v, v'\in A$ such that $v(\xi_0) =u(\xi_0),v'(\xi_0) =u'(\xi_0)$ and $\|v\|\leqslant \|u\|_B+\varepsilon$, $\|v'\|\leqslant \|u'\|_B+\varepsilon$. Then one has $vv'(\xi_0) =vu'(\xi_0) =u'v(\xi_0) =u'u(\xi_0)$, whence

$$
\|u'u\|_B\leqslant \|v'v\|\leqslant \|v\|\|v'\|\leqslant (\|u\|_B+\varepsilon )(\|u'\|_B+\varepsilon )
$$

and finally $\|u'u\|_B\leqslant \|u\|_B\|u'\|_B$. This shows that B, endowed with the norm $u\mapsto  \|u\|_B$, is a normed algebra. Since it is a field, corollary 2 implies that $B =\mathbf{C}\cdot 1$, which is the desired conclusion.

Let us prove b). According to a), the commutant of A in End$_{\mathbf{C}}(E)$ is reduced to the homotheties of E. Its bicommutant is therefore End$_{\mathbf{C}}(E)$. Assertion b) therefore results from Jacobson's density theorem (theorem 1 of A, VIII, p. 434).

#### Corollary 5 {#ts-i-s2-thm-1-cor-5 .statement tag=026K}

Let A be a Banach algebra and $x\in A$.

a) Sp$'(x)$ is a compact subset of $\mathbf{C}$;

b) The spectral radius $\varrho (x)$ is the radius of the smallest closed disk with center 0 in $\mathbf{C}$ that contains Sp$'(x)$;

c) In order that $x$ be quasi-nilpotent, it is necessary and sufficient that one have Sp$'(x) =\{0\}$.

Assertions a) and b) result from th. 1 by considering the Banach algebra deduced from A by adjunction of a unit element. Assertion c) results from b).

### 6. Spectrum relative to a subalgebra

In this No., it is assumed that $K =\mathbf{C}$.

#### Lemma 3 {#ts-i-s2-lem-3 .statement tag=026L}

Let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be compact subsets of $\mathbf{C}$. If $X_2$ is contained in $X_1$ and if the boundary of $X_1$ in $\mathbf{C}$ is contained in $X_2$, then $X_1$ is the union of $X_2$ and of certain bounded connected components of the complement of $X_2$ in $\mathbf{C}$.

Let U be a connected component of $\mathbf{C}-X_2$. Every boundary point of $X_1\cap U$ in the open set U is also a boundary point of $X_1$ in $\mathbf{C}$, hence belongs to $X_2$ by hypothesis; since $U\cap X_2=\emptyset$, one sees that $X_1\cap U$ has no boundary point in the space U. Since U is connected, the intersection $X_1\cap U$ is either empty or equal to U (TG, I, p. 82, cor.), and the lemma results.

#### Proposition 6 {#ts-i-s2-prop-6 .statement tag=026M}

Let A be a unital Banach algebra and B a closed unital subalgebra of A. For every $x\in B$, one has Sp$_B(x)\supset$ Sp$_A(x)$, and the boundary of Sp$_A(x)$ in $\mathbf{C}$ contains the boundary of Sp$_B(x)$ in $\mathbf{C}$. In particular, if Sp$_B(x)\subset \mathbf{R}$, then one has Sp$_B(x) =$ Sp$_A(x)$.

One has Sp$_B(x)\supset$ Sp$_A(x)$ (remark 6 of I, p. 3). If $\lambda$ is a point of the boundary of Sp$_B(x)$ in $\mathbf{C}$, there exists a sequence $(\lambda_n)$ of points exterior to Sp$_B(x)$ tending to $\lambda$. Then $x-\lambda_n$ is invertible in B and tends to $x-\lambda$, which is not invertible in B; hence $x-\lambda$ is a left or right topological zero divisor in B (Proposition 4 of I, p. 24), hence in A. Thus, $\lambda \in$ Sp$_A(x)$. But since Sp$_A(x)\subset$ Sp$_B(x)$, the complex number $\lambda \in$ Fr$_{\mathbf{C}}$(Sp$_B(x)$) cannot be interior to Sp$_A(x)$, and therefore belongs to its boundary.

#### Corollary {#ts-i-s2-n6-cor-1 .statement tag=026N}

The set Sp$_B(x)$ is the union of Sp$_A(x)$ and of certain bounded connected components of $\mathbf{C}-$ Sp$_A(x)$.

This follows from Proposition 6 and lemma 3.

This corollary will be completed by Propositions 13 of I, p. 46 and 14 of I, p. 46.
