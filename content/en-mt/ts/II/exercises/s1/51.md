We identify $\mathbf{T}$ with the interval $[0,1[$. For $n\in \mathbf{Z}$, let $e_n$ be the unitary character of $\mathbf{T}$ defined by $t\mapsto$ exp(2$i\pi nt$). For every measure $\mu\in \mathscr{M}^1(\mathbf{T})$ and every integer $m\geqslant 1$, one sets

$$
S_m(\mu) =\sum_{h=-m}^m\widehat{\mu}(h)e_h\in \mathscr{C}(\mathbf{T})
$$

for the $m$-th symmetric partial sum of the Fourier series of $\mu$.

A finite family $(y_1, . . . , y_k)$ of real numbers is said to be $\mathbf{T}$-independent if $(1, y_1, . . . , y_k)$ is linearly independent over $\mathbf{Q}$. This amounts to saying that the equation

$$
\sum_{j=1}^km_jy_j= 0
$$

in $\mathbf{T}$, with $m_j\in \mathbf{Z}$, has for unique solution the zero family. For $t\in \mathbf{R}$, one denotes by $t-(y_1, . . . , y_k)$ the family $(t-y_j)_j$.
