For every complex number $s$ such that $\mathscr{R}(s)>1$, put

$$
\zeta (s) =\sum\frac{1}{n^s}
$$

$n\geqslant 1$ (“Riemann zeta function”).

a) The function $\zeta$ is holomorphic on the open subset of $\mathbf{C}$ formed by the complex numbers $s$ such that $\mathscr{R}(s)>1$.

b) For $y\in \mathbf{R}_+^*$, put

$$
\theta (y) =\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}}e^{-\pi n^2y}
$$

this series converges absolutely and uniformly on the compact subsets of $\mathbf{R}_+^*$. For every $s$ such that $\mathscr{R}(s)>1$, one has

$$
\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma (s/2)\zeta (s) =\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{+\infty}\theta (y)y^{s/2}\frac{dy}{y}
$$

(see FVR, VII, p. 10 for the gamma function in the complex plane).

d) For every $s$ such that $\mathscr{R}(s)>1$, one has

$$
\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma (s/2)\zeta (s) =\frac{1}{s(s- 1)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_1^{+\infty}(\theta (y)-1)(y^{s/2}+y^{(1-s)/2})dyy
$$

e) There exists a unique holomorphic function on $\mathbf{C}-\{1\}$ whose restriction to the open set of complex numbers with real part $>1$ coincides with $\zeta$. This function, still denoted by $\zeta$, satisfies $(s-1)\zeta (s)\rightarrow 1$ when $s\rightarrow 1$ (*in other words, it has a simple pole of residue 1 at $s= 1$*).

f) The function Λ defined by

$$
\Lambda (s) =\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma (s/2)\zeta (s)
$$

is holomorphic on $\mathbf{C}-\{0,1\}$; it has simple poles of residue 1 at $s= 0$ and $s= 1$. One has $\Lambda (1-s) = \Lambda (s)$ for every $s\in \mathbf{C}-\{0,1\}$.
