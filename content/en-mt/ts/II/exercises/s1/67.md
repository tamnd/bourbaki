Let $k\geqslant 2$ and $\ell  > k$ be natural numbers. For every integer $N\geqslant 1$, let $r_{k,\ell}(N)$ denote the number of families $(n_1, . . . , n_{\ell})\in \mathbf{N}^{\ell}$ such that

$$
n^k_1+\cdots +n^k_{\ell}= N
$$

The object of this exercise is to prove that, if $\ell$ is sufficiently large as a function of $k$, then $r_{k,\ell}(N)\geqslant 1$ ("Waring's problem") for every $N\geqslant 1$.
