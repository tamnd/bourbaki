Let $I = [a, b]$ be a compact interval of $\mathbf{R}$. Let $f$ be a continuous function from I into $\mathbf{R}^2$ which is injective on the interior of I, and which is piecewise $C^1$ (that is to say, there exists a finite partition of I into intervals J such that $f|J$ is differentiable, and $f(f|J)'$ is continuous). One says that $f$ is a parametrized curve in the plane. One says that

$$
\ell (f) =\int_I|f'(t)|dt
$$

(where $f'(t)$ is identified with a complex number) is the length of the parametrized curve $f$. If moreover $f(a) =f(b)$, one says that $f$ defines a Jordan curve. There then exists a unique bounded connected component C of $\mathbf{R}^2-f(I)$ (TA, to appear). The Lebesgue measure of C is called the area enclosed by the Jordan curve $f$, and is denoted by $A(f)$.

(a) Suppose that $f$ is a Jordan curve. For every $t\in I$, the restriction $f_t$ of $f$ to $[a, t]$ is a parametrized curve and the function $s:t\mapsto \ell (f_t)$ is a strictly increasing bijection from I onto $J = [0, \ell (f)]$.
