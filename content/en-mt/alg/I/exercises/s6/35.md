Let $G$ be a group such that the derived group $D = D(G)$ is contained in the center C of G.
a) Prove that the mapping $(x, y) \mapsto (x, y)$ induces a mapping $\varphi : (G/C) \times (G/C) \to D$ such that, on writing these groups additively, one has:
$$
\begin{align*}
\varphi(\alpha + \beta, \gamma) &= \varphi(\alpha, \gamma) + \varphi(\beta, \gamma) & \varphi(\alpha, \beta) &= -\varphi(\beta, \alpha) \\
\varphi(\alpha, \beta + \gamma) &= \varphi(\alpha, \beta) + \varphi(\alpha, \gamma) & \varphi(\alpha, \alpha) &= 0
\end{align*}
$$
for all $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ in $G/C$.
b) Prove that, for every integer $n$, one has
$$
(yx)^n = y^n x^n (x, y)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
$$

for $x, y$ in $G$. Deduce that, if $n$ is odd and if $d^n = 1$ for every $d \in D$, the mapping $x \mapsto x^n$ defines, by passing to the quotient, a homomorphism $\theta : G/D \to C$.
