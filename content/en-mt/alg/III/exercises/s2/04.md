2. Let G be a group, H a normal subgroup of G. Now prove that, if $a$ is the two-sided ideal of the group algebra $A^{(G)}$, generated by the elements $ts - s$, where t runs through H and s runs through G, the group algebra $A^{(G/H)}$ is isomorphic to the quotient algebra $A^{(G)}/a$.
3. Let A be a commutative ring, S a commutative monoid with identity element e. Suppose given on A an action law $(s, x) \mapsto x^s$ having S as domain of operators, such that, for every $s \in S$, the mapping $x \mapsto x^s$ is an automorphism of the ring A, and that $(x^s)^t = x^{st}$ whatever $x \in A, s$ and $t$ in S may be (which implies that e is an identity operator for the action law considered). That being so, on the A-module $A^{(S)}$, whose canonical basis will be denoted by $(b_s)$, one defines a multiplicative composition law by the relation

( s ∑  x s  b s  )( s ∑  y s  b s  )= s ∑  ( tu=s ∑  a t,u  x t  y u s  )b s

where the $a_{s,t}$ belong to A.

Now prove that this law and the addition in $A^{(S)}$ define on this set a ring structure, provided that the $a_{s,t}$ satisfy the conditions
$$
a_{s,t} a_{st,u} = a_{tu}^s a_{s,tu}
$$
for all $s, t, u$ in S. The element $b_e$ is unit element for this ring if one has in addition $a_{e,s} = a_{s,e} = 1$ for all $s \in S$; in this case one can identify A with a subring of $A^{(S)}$, and if C denotes the subring of A formed by the elements invariant under all the automorphisms $x \mapsto x^s$, $A^{(S)}$ is a C-algebra, which is called the cross product of the ring A and the monoid S, relative to the system of factors $(a_{s,t})$. If the system of factors $(a_{s,t})$ is replaced by $\left( \frac{c_s c_t^s}{c_{st}} a_{s,t} \right)$, where, for every $s \in S$, $c_s$ is an invertible element of A and $c_e = 1$, the new cross product obtained is isomorphic to the cross product defined by the system of factors $(a_{s,t})$.

If one takes in particular for A a quadratic algebra over a ring C, for S a cyclic group of order 2, namely $\{e, s\}$, such that $x^s = \bar{x}$ for $x \in A$, prove that every cross product of A and S is a quaternion algebra over C.

¶ 13) Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $E$ an $A$-module. We define recursively an $A$-module $E_n$ by setting
$$
E_1 = E, \qquad E_n = \bigoplus_{p+q=n} (E_p \otimes_A E_q) \qquad \text{for } n \geq 2.
$$
We set $ME = \bigoplus_{n=1}^\infty E_n$, and we define on $ME$ an $A$-algebra structure by setting, for $x_p \in E_p$ and $x_q \in E_q$, $x_p x_q = x_p \otimes x_q \in E_{p+q}$.
(a) Now prove that for every $A$-algebra $B$ and every $A$-linear mapping $f : E \to B$, there exists one and only one $A$-homomorphism of algebras $ME \to B$ which extends $f$. We say that $ME$ is the free algebra of the $A$-module $E$.
(b) We define recursively on the integer $n$ an integer $a(n)$ by the formulas
$$
a(1) = 1, \qquad a(n) = \sum_{p+q=n} a(p)a(q) \qquad \text{if } n \geq 2.
$$
Now prove that $E_n$ is isomorphic to the direct sum of $a(n)$ modules isomorphic to $E^{\otimes n}$.
(c) Let $f(T)$ be the formal series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a(n)T^n$. Now prove that $f(T) = T + (f(T))^2$. \* Deduce the formulas

b) If A is a ring without divisor of 0 and if σ is an isomorphism of A onto a subring of A, show that E is a ring without divisor of 0 and that one has $ω(uv) = ω(u) + ω(v)$ for $u \neq 0$ and $v \neq 0$.
