We call a simplicial diagram a pair $(K,\mathscr F)$, where $K$ is a set and $\mathscr F$ a set of finite parts of $K$, called faces of $K$, such that any subset of a face is a face and that every point of $K$ belongs to at least one face. A simplicial map of a simplicial scheme $(K,\mathscr F)$ into a simplicial scheme $(L,\mathscr G)$ is a map of $K$ into $L$ which transforms any face of $K$ into a face of $L$.

a) Let $n$ be an integer $\geq0$. We call $n$-simplex of the simplicial scheme $K$ any sequence $(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ of elements of $K$ such that the $\alpha_i$ belong to the same face of $K$. We note $S_n(K,A)$ the $A$-free module generated by the $n$-simplexes for $n\geq0$, and we set $S_n(K,A)=0$ for $n<0$. We define a $A$-homomorphism

$$
d_n:S_n(K,A)\rightarrow S_{n-1}(K,A)
$$

by setting $d_n=0$ for $n\leq0$ and

$$
d_n(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^i(\alpha_0,\ldots,\widehat{\alpha_i},\ldots,\alpha_n)
$$

for any $n$-simplex $(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ (the $\widehat{\ }$ sign on a letter means it should be omitted). Show that $d_{n-1}\circ d_n=0$ for all $n$, so that $(S(K,A),d)$ is a complex of $A$-modules. We similarly define a $A$-complex $C(K,A)$ by setting $C^n(K,A)=\operatorname{Hom}_A(S_n(K,\mathbf Z),A)$, $\delta^n=\operatorname{Hom}(d_{n+1},1_A)$ for all $n$. For $n\geq0$, we sometimes note $H_n(K,A)$ (resp. $H^n(K,A)$) the $A$-module $H_n(S(K,A))$ (resp. $H^n(C(K,A))$).

b) Let $K,L$ be two simplicial schemes, $f:K\rightarrow L$ be a simplicial map. Show that $f$ induces morphisms of complexes $S(f):S(K,A)\rightarrow S(L,A)$ and $C(f):C(L,A)\rightarrow C(K,A)$, hence $A$-homomorphisms $f_*:H(K,A)\rightarrow H(L,A)$ and $f^*:H(L,A)\rightarrow H(K,A)$.

c) Let $g:K\rightarrow L$ be a second simplicial map. We say that $f$ and $g$ are simplicially homotopic if for any face $\sigma$ of $K$, the set $f(\sigma)\cup g(\sigma)$ is a face of $L$. Show that the morphisms $S(f)$ and $S(g)$ (resp. $C(f)$ and $C(g)$) are then homotopy (define a homotopy $h$ by setting

$$
h(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^i
(f(\alpha_0),\ldots,f(\alpha_i),g(\alpha_i),\ldots,g(\alpha_n))
$$

for any $n$-simplex $(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)$).

d) We say that the simplicial diagram $K$ is conic if there exists a point $s$ of $K$ such that for every face $\sigma$ of $K$, $\sigma\cup\{s\}$ is a face. Show that there then exist homotopisms of $S(K,A)$ and of $C(K,A)$ on $A$.

e) We note $D(K,A)$ the subcomplex of $S(K,A)$ generated by the simplices $(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ for which two of the $\alpha_i$ are equal, and by the elements of the form

$$
(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)-\varepsilon(\sigma)(\alpha_{\sigma(0)},\ldots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)})
$$

for any $n$-simplex $(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ and any $\sigma$ permutation of $\{0,\ldots,n\}$. Show that $D(K,A)$ is a subcomplex of $S(K,A)$, and that the application of passing to the quotient

$$
p:S(K,A)\rightarrow S(K,A)/D(K,A)
$$

is a homotopism (a reciprocal homotopism is obtained by choosing a total order on $K$ and associating with the class modulo $D(K,A)$ of a simplex $(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_n)$ the simplex

$$
\varepsilon(\sigma)(\alpha_{\sigma(0)},\ldots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)})
$$

where $\sigma$ is the permutation such that $\alpha_{\sigma(0)}<\cdots<\alpha_{\sigma(n)}$ if all $\alpha_i$ are different, and $0$ otherwise).
