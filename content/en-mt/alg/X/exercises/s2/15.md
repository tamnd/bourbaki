Let $E$ be a spectral sequence of A-modules, approaching a graded A-module G (Exercise 14).
a) Suppose that $E_2^{p,q} = 0$ for $p < 0$. Now prove that the mappings $\alpha_r$ induce for every $q$ injective A-homomorphisms:

$$
E_{\infty}^{0,q} \to \cdots \to E_3^{0,q} \to E_2^{0,q}
$$

whence, by composition with $(\beta^{0q})^{-1}$ and with the mapping of passing to the quotient, an A-homomorphism $\gamma^q : G^q \to E_2^{0,q}$.
b) Suppose that $E_2^{pq} = 0$ for $q < 0$. Now prove that the mappings $\alpha_r^{-1}$ induce surjective homomorphisms:

$$
E_2^{p,0} \to E_3^{p,0} \to \cdots \to E_{\infty}^{p,0}
$$

whence by composition with $\beta^{p,0}$ an A-homomorphism $\delta^p : E_2^{p,0} \to G^p$.
c) Suppose that the hypotheses of a) and b) are satisfied, that is to say $E_2^{pq} = 0$ if $p < 0$ or $q < 0$. Now prove that the sequence

$$
0 \to E_2^{1,0} \xrightarrow{\delta^1} G^1 \xrightarrow{\gamma^1} E_1^{0,1} \xrightarrow{d_2} E_2^{2,0} \xrightarrow{\delta^2} G^2
$$

is exact.
d) Suppose that $E_2^{pq} = 0$ if $p > 0$ or $q > 0$; define an exact sequence analogous to that of c).
e) Suppose that there exist integers $r, d$, with $d \geqslant r \geqslant 2$, such that $E_r^{pq} = 0$ if $p \neq 0, d$. Define an exact sequence:

$$
\cdots \to E_r^{d,n-d} \to G^n \to E_r^{0,n} \to E_r^{d,n+1-d} \to G^{n+1} \to \cdots .
$$

If analogously $E_r^{pq} = 0$ for $q \neq 0, d$, now prove that there exists an exact sequence:

$$
\cdots \to E_r^{n,0} \to G^n \to E_r^{n-d,d} \to E_r^{n+1,0} \to \cdots .
$$

f) Suppose that $E_2^{pq} = 0$ if $q \neq 0$ (resp. $p \neq 0$). Now prove that the homomorphism

$$
\delta^n : E_2^{n,0} \to G^n \text{ (resp. } \gamma^n : G^n \to E_2^{0,n})
$$

is bijective for every $n$.
