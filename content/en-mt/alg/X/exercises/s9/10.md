Soient A un anneau, M un a-module, $x = (x_1, ..., x_n)$ a sequence of elements of A, p an integer $\leq n$.
We denote by $x' = (x_1, ..., x_p)$, $x'' = (x_{p+1}, ..., x_n)$ and $M' = M/(x')$ M.
a) Let k, k’ be two integers such that $k \leq k'$. Suppose that the sequence x’ is k’-sécante for M (Exercise 9). Prove that the sequence x is k-sécante for M if and only if the sequence x” is k-sécante for M’.
b) Prove that if the sequence x is 1-sécante for M, the sequence x” is 1-sécante for M’.
† 11) Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, m its maximal ideal, $k = A/m$. Let M be a nonzero a-module of finite type. We call the depth of M, and denote it by prof (M), the supremum of the integers n for which there exists a sequence $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ of elements of m which is completely sécante for M.
a) Prove that $\operatorname{prof}(M) = 0$ if and only if m is associated to M (X, p. 172, Exercise 28), in other words, if M contains a submodule isomorphic to k (observe that an ideal contained in a union of prime ideals is necessarily contained in one of them).

b) Prove that prof (M) is the greatest lower bound of the integers $n$ for which $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(k, M)$ is nonzero (reason by induction on $n$). If $\operatorname{prof}(M) = n$, any sequence $(x_1, ..., x_p)$ of elements of m completely sécante for M ($p \leq n$) can be extended to a sequence $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ in m completely sécante for M.

c) If $(x_1, ..., x_k)$ is a sequence of elements of m completely sécante for M, prove that
$$
\operatorname{prof}\left(M/(x_1 M + \cdots + x_k M)\right) = \operatorname{prof}(M) - k .
$$

d) Suppose that $M$ is of *finite projective dimension*. Prove the equality $\mathrm{dp}_A(M) + \operatorname{prof}(M) = \operatorname{prof}(A)$ (reason by induction on $\mathrm{dp}_A(M)$).

e) Prove that $\operatorname{prof}(A)$ is the supremum of the projective dimensions of the a-modules which are of *finite type* and of *finite projective dimension*.

†12) Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, m its maximal ideal.
a) Prove that if $0 < \operatorname{dh}(A) < \infty$, there exists a non-zero-divisor element in $m - m^2$ (show as in Exercise 11, *a*) that m would otherwise be associated to $A$, whence an exact sequence
$$
0 \to k \to A \to N \to 0 ;
$$
arrive at a contradiction using Exercise 16, p. 203).
b) Let $n$ be an integer. Prove that one has $\operatorname{dh}(A) = n$ if and only if there exists a sequence $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ completely sécante for $A$, generating m (reason by induction on $n$, using *a*) and Exercise 14, p. 203).
One says that a local ring is *regular* if it is noetherian and of finite homological dimension.

13) Let $A$ be a regular local ring (Exercise 14) of homological dimension $n$; we denote by m its maximal ideal and $k = A/m$.
a) Prove that one has $\dim_k(m/m^2) = n$.
b) Prove that the $k$-algebra $\bigoplus_{r \geq 0} (m^r/m^{r+1})$ is isomorphic to an algebra of polynomials in $n$ indeterminates.
c) If $n = 0$, $A$ is a field. If $n = 1$, prove that $A$ is a principal ideal domain *and therefore a discrete valuation ring* (prove that $A$ is integral by observing that $m \cap m^n = \bigcap m^n$, whence $\bigcap m^n = 0$).
d) Prove that the ring $A[[T]]$ is regular.

14) Let $A$ be a ring, $a$ an ideal of $A$. Consider the alternating graded $(A/a)$-algebra $\operatorname{Tor}^A(A/a, A/a)$ (*cf.* X, p. 201, exercise 9) and the homomorphism of graded $(A/a)$-algebras $\theta : \Lambda_{A/a}(a/a^2) \to \operatorname{Tor}^A(A/a, A/a)$ deduced from the isomorphism $a/a^2 \to \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/a, A/a)$ (X, p. 73). Prove that if the ideal $a$ is generated by a completely transverse sequence in $A$, $\theta$ is an isomorphism.

15) Let $A$ be a *noetherian* ring, $a$ an ideal contained in the radical of $A$. Suppose that the $(A/a)$-module $a/a^2$ is free and that the homomorphism $\theta_2 : \Lambda_{A/a}^2(a/a^2) \to \operatorname{Tor}_2^A(A/a, A/a)$ (exercise 14) is surjective. Prove that $a$ is generated by a completely transverse sequence in $A$. (If $x$ is a minimal family of generators of $a$, show with the aid of exercise 7 (or directly) that $\operatorname{Coker}(\theta_2)$ is isomorphic to $H_1(x, A) \otimes_A A/a$.)

16) Let $A$ be a *local noetherian* ring, $m$ its maximal ideal, $k = A/m$. Prove that the homomorphism of graded $k$-algebras $\theta : \Lambda_k(m/m^2) \to \operatorname{Tor}^A(k, k)$ is injective: if $\theta_2$ is bijective, the ring $A$ is *regular* (exercise 12).
(If $x$ is a minimal system of generators of $m$, show that the complex $K(x, A)$ satisfies the conditions of exercise 14, p. 182; use exercises 4 and 15.)

†17) Let $A$ be a ring such that the $A$-module $A_s$ is of *finite injective dimension* (X, p. 202, exercise 4; a *regular* local ring (exercise 12) satisfies this property).
a) Let $x$ be an element which is not a zero divisor and is not invertible in $A$. Prove that one has
$$
\operatorname{di}_{A/Ax}(A/Ax) = \operatorname{di}_A(A) - 1 .
$$
(Let $0 \to A \to I^0 \to I^1 \to \cdots$ be a *minimal* injective resolution (X, p. 182, exercise 13) of $A$; show that the complex $\operatorname{Hom}_A(A/Ax, I^1) \to \operatorname{Hom}_A(A/Ax, I^2) \to \cdots$ defines a minimal injective resolution of the $(A/Ax)$-module $A/Ax$.)
b) Suppose now that the ring $A$ is *local noetherian*. Prove the equality $\operatorname{di}(A) = \operatorname{prof}(A)$ (exercise 11; reason by induction on $\operatorname{di}(A)$).
c) Let $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ be a completely transverse sequence for $A$, contained in the maximal ideal of $A$, with $n = \operatorname{di}(A)$. Prove that the ring $A$ is *autoinjective* (X, p. 171, exercise 26).

Soient $A$ a noetherian ring, $\mathcal{C}$ a stable set of classes of $A$-modules (X, p. 40), $x = (x_1, ..., x_n)$ a sequence of elements of $A$, $x$ the ideal generated by $x$. We denote by $\mathcal{C}_x$ the set of elements of $\mathcal{C}$ which are annihilated by $x$. Let $M$ be a finitely generated $A$-module.
a) Exercise prove that for every $i \geqslant 0$, there exists an element $M_i$ of $\mathcal{C}_x$ such that one has
$$
\sum_{k \geqslant 0} (-1)^k [H_{i+k}(x, M)] = [M_i]
$$
in $K(\mathcal{C}_x)$ (reason by induction on $n$, using Proposition 4, p. 157; in the case $n = 1$, consider the successive powers of the endomorphism $(x_1)_M$).
b) Suppose that $A/x$ is of finite length. Show that there exist positive integers $m_i$ ($i \geqslant 0$) such that one has $\operatorname{long} H_i(x, M) = m_i + m_{i+1}$.
