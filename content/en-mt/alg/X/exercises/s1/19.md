Let A and B be two rings, $\varphi : A \to B$ a homomorphism br, M a B-module. We provide $B \otimes_A M$ and $\mathrm{Hom}_A(B, M)$ with B-module structures on the left deduced from the B-module structure of B. We say that M is *projective relative to A*, or *$\varphi$-projective*, if the canonical surjection $B \otimes_A M \to M$ admits a B-linear section; we say that M is *injective relative to A*, or *$\varphi$-injective*, if the canonical injection
$$
M \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(B, M)
$$
admits a B-linear retraction.
a) If M is projective (resp. injective) as A-module and $\varphi$-projective (resp. $\varphi$-injective) then M is a projective (resp. injective) B-module.
b) For any A-module N, the B-module $B \otimes_A N$ (resp. $\mathrm{Hom}_A(B, N)$) is $\varphi$-projective (resp. $\varphi$-injective).
c) We suppose that $\varphi(A)$ is contained in the center of B. Let N be an A-module, P be a $\varphi$-projective B-module. Show that the B-module $P \otimes_A N$ (resp. the B-module on the right $\mathrm{Hom}_A(P, N)$) is $\varphi$-projective (resp. $\varphi$-injective).
