Soit A an absolutely flat ring (Exercise 16).

a) Let P be a projective A-module. Now prove that every finitely generated submodule of P is a direct factor of P (reduce to the case where P is free of finite type).
b) Now prove that every projective A-module is a direct sum of monogeneous submodules, isomorphic to monogeneous ideals of A. (Use Kaplansky's theorem (II, p. 183, exercise 2) to reduce to the case where P admits a countable family of generators, then use a).)
c) Now prove that every ideal of A generated by a countable family of generators is projective.
d) Give an example of an absolutely flat ring A and of a finitely generated nonprojective A-module.
†18) Let M be an A-module. Now prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
α) M is of finite type (resp. of finite presentation).
β) For every inductive system (N_i, u_{ij}) of A-modules relative to a filtering preordered set I, the canonical homomorphism $\lim_{\longrightarrow i \in I} \mathrm{Hom}_A(M, N_i) \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(M, \lim_{\longrightarrow i \in I} N_i)$ is injective (resp. bijective).
γ) For every family $(P_j)_{j \in J}$ of right A-modules, the canonical homomorphism
$$
(\prod_{j \in J} P_j) \otimes_A M \to \prod_{j \in J} (P_j \otimes_A M)
$$
(II, p. 61) is surjective (resp. bijective).
δ) For every set I, the canonical homomorphism $A_d^1 \otimes_A M \to M^I$ is surjective (resp. bijective).
19) Let A and B be two rings, $\varphi : A \to B$ a homomorphism, M a B-module. We equip $B \otimes_A M$ and $\mathrm{Hom}_A(B, M)$ with the structures of left B-module deduced from the B-module structure of B. We say that M is *projective relatively to A*, or *$\varphi$-projective*, if the canonical surjection $B \otimes_A M \to M$ admits a B-linear section; we say that M is *injective relatively to A*, or *$\varphi$-injective*, if the canonical injection
$$
M \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(B, M)
$$
admits a B-linear retraction.
a) If M is projective (resp. injective) as an A-module and $\varphi$-projective (resp. $\varphi$-injective) then M is a projective (resp. injective) B-module.
b) For every A-module N, the B-module $B \otimes_A N$ (resp. $\mathrm{Hom}_A(B, N)$) is $\varphi$-projective (resp. $\varphi$-injective).
c) Suppose that $\varphi(A)$ is contained in the center of B. Let N be an A-module, P a $\varphi$-projective B-module. Now prove that the B-module $P \otimes_A N$ (resp. the right B-module $\mathrm{Hom}_A(P, N)$) is $\varphi$-projective (resp. $\varphi$-injective).
20) Let A be a principal ideal domain, a a nonzero element of A, $B = A/Aa$. Now prove that the B-module $B_s$ is injective.
21) Now prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
α) A is noetherian.
β) Every inductive limit A-module of injective A-modules is injective.
γ) Every direct sum A-module of injective A-modules is injective.
(To prove that γ) implies α), let $(a_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be an increasing sequence of left ideals of A, $a = \bigcup_n a_n$, $I_n$ an injective envelope of $a/a_n$. The natural homomorphism of a into $\bigoplus I_n$ is the restriction of a homomorphism $f : A \to \bigoplus I_n$; consider $f(1)$.)
22) Let A be a principal ideal domain, K its field of fractions, $\pi$ a prime element of A. Now prove that the $\pi$-primary component of the A-module $K/A$ is an injective envelope of the A-module $A/\pi^n A$ for all $n \geq 1$.
†23) Let I be an injective A-module, E the ring $\mathrm{End}_A(I)$, r the set of elements $u$ of E such that I is an injective envelope of $\mathrm{Ker}(u)$.
a) Now prove that r is a two-sided ideal and that the ring $E/r$ is absolutely flat (exercise 16). (Let $u$ be an element of E, N a submodule of I such that $N \cap \mathrm{Ker}\,u = 0$ and maximal for this property; now prove that there exists $v \in E$ such that $vu(x) = x$ for all $x \in N$, and deduce that $uvu - u \in r$.)
b) Now prove that r is the radical of E (the inclusion $r(E) \subset r$ follows from a); now prove that for all $u \in r$, $1 - u$ is bijective).
c) Suppose that I is a direct sum of a finite family of indecomposable injectives. Now prove that the ring $E/r$ is semisimple.
†24) Let $i : A \to I$ be an injective envelope of the A-module $A_s$. We put $E = \mathrm{End}_A(I)$ and $B = \mathrm{End}_E(I)$, so that B is the bicommutant of the A-module I (VIII, § 3, def. 1). We consider A as a subring of B. We note $j : B \to I$ and $p : E \to I$ the mappings defined by $j(b) = b(i(1))$ and $p(u) = u(i(1))$ for $b \in B,\ u \in E$.
a) Now prove that p is a surjection and j an injection.

EXERCISES

b) Prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
α) The mapping $p$ is bijective.
β) The mapping $j$ is bijective.
γ) The A-module B is injective.
If these conditions are satisfied, the mapping $j^{-1} \circ p$ is an isomorphism of the ring E onto the opposite ring of B.
c) Let $\mathfrak{F}$ be the set of left ideals $a$ of A such that $a \cap b \neq (0)$ for every nonzero left ideal $b$.
We denote by $s(A)$ the set of elements $a$ of A for which there exists $a \in \mathfrak{F}$ such that $aa = 0$. Prove that $s(A)$ is a two-sided ideal of A.
d) Prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
α) $s(A) = 0$.
β) The ring E is without radical.
γ) The ring B is absolutely flat.
If these conditions are satisfied, the equivalent properties of b) are satisfied.
(Let $s(I)$ be the set of elements $x$ of I for which there exists $a \in \mathfrak{F}$ such that $ax = 0$. Using exercise 23, prove that $p^{-1}(s(I))$ is equal to the radical r of E, whence the implication $\beta$) $\Rightarrow \alpha)$. Prove next that $\alpha)$ implies $r = \mathrm{Ker}\,(p) = 0$, which entails $\beta)$ and $\gamma)$ (in view of exercise 23) as well as condition $\alpha)$ of b). Finally prove that $\gamma)$ implies $\alpha)$.)
e) If A is left noetherian, the ideal $s(A)$ is nilpotent (first prove that every element $a$ of $s(A)$ is nilpotent, by considering the left annihilators of the $a^n$; then use exercise 8, b) of VIII, § 9).
† 25) Suppose that the ring A is left noetherian and contains no nilpotent two-sided ideals.

b) We identify A with a subring of B. Prove the following properties:
(i) Every element of A which is left cancellable is invertible in B (therefore cancellable in A).
(ii) Every element $b$ of B can be written $s^{-1} a$, with $s \in A$, $a \in A$ and $s$ cancellable,
It is sometimes said that B is the total ring of left fractions of A.
(To prove (ii), reduce to the case where B is simple, Prove that then the ideal (0) of A is prime, Let $a$ be an ideal of $\mathfrak{F}$, $a$ an element of $a$ for which the right ideal $aB$ is maximal among the ideals $a'$ B ($a' \in a$), $x$ an element of B such that $axa = a$. $$
(B(1 - ax) \cap a) \cdot (B(1 - xa) \cap a) = (0);

† 27) Suppose the ring A is left noetherian.

c) Suppose A is commutative. Prove that every indecomposable injective A-module I is the injective envelope of $A/\mathcal{U}(I)$. f) Let $k$ be a field, $\mathbf{Q}^+$ the additive monoid of rational numbers $\geqslant 0$, $A$ the algebra $k^{(\mathbf{Q}^+)}$, $a$ a nonzero element of $A$, $I$ the injective envelope of $A/Aa$. Show that the $A$-module $I$ is indecomposable, but does not contain a submodule isomorphic to $A/p$ for $p$ prime (note that the set of ideals of $A$ is totally ordered and that $A$ contains only two prime ideals).
28) Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ an $A$-module, $I$ an injective envelope of $M$, direct sum of a family $(I_\alpha)_{\alpha \in J}$ of indecomposable submodules. We denote by $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$ the set of two-sided prime ideals $\mathfrak{U}(I_\alpha)$, for $\alpha \in J$ (exercise 27); if $p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$, we say that $p$ is associated with $M$.
a) One has $\operatorname{Ass}(M) \neq \varnothing$ if $M \neq 0$; if $M$ is of finitely generated type, the set $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$ is finite.
b) Show that a two-sided prime ideal $p$ is associated with $M$ if and only if $M$ contains a submodule $N$ such that $\operatorname{Ann}(N') = p$ for every nonzero submodule $N'$ of $N$. If $A$ is commutative, $p$ is associated with $M$ if and only if $M$ contains a submodule isomorphic to $A/p$,
c) Let $M'$ be a submodule of $M$. Show that one has
$$
\operatorname{Ass}(M') \subset \operatorname{Ass}(M) \subset \operatorname{Ass}(M') \cup \operatorname{Ass}(M/M').
$$
d) Put $J_p = \sum_{\mathfrak{U}(I_\alpha) = p} I_\alpha$ and $Q(p) = \left( \sum_{q \neq p} J_q \right) \cap M$. Show that one has $\bigcap_{p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)} Q(p) = 0$ and $\bigcap_{p \in E} Q(p) \neq 0$ for every subset $E$ of $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$ distinct from $\operatorname{Ass}(M)$. For every $p \in \operatorname{Ass}(M)$, one has $\operatorname{Ass}(M/Q(p)) = \{ p \}$.
e) Let $a$ be an element of the center of $A$. Show that for the homothety of ratio $a$ to be injective in $M$, it is necessary and sufficient that $a$ belong to none of the prime ideals associated with $M$.

30) Suppose that the ring $A$ is local, commutative and noetherian; denote by $m$ its maximal ideal, $k = A/m$. Let $I$ be an $A$-module such that every element of $I$ is annihilated by a power of $m$. For every $A$-module $M$, denote by $T(M)$ the $A$-module $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, I)$ and $c_M : M \to T(T(M))$ the homomorphism defined by
$$
(c_M(m))(u) = u(m) \quad \text{for} \quad m \in M, \quad u \in T(M).
$$
Show that the following properties are equivalent:
$\alpha$ For every $A$-module $M$ of finite length, the $A$-module $T(M)$ is of finite length and the homomorphism $c_M : M \to T(T(M))$ is bijective.
$\beta$ For every $A$-module $M$ of finite length, one has $\operatorname{long} T(M) = \operatorname{long} M$.
$\gamma$ The $A$-module $I$ is injective and the $A$-modules $k$ and $T(k)$ are isomorphic.
$\delta$ The $A$-module $I$ is isomorphic to the injective envelope of $k$.
When these conditions are verified, one sometimes says that $I$ is a dualizing module for the ring $A$.
(Show that the property $\gamma$ is equivalent to each of the other three: to see that $\alpha$ or $\beta$ implies $\gamma$, use exercise 29.)

In particular for every ideal $a$ of $A$, the submodule of $I$ formed by the elements annihilated by $a$ is a dualizing module for $A/a$.
