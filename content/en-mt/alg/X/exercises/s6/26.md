We note $A^e$ the k-algebra $A \otimes_k A^0$; every $(A, A)$-bimodule is naturally provided with a $A^e$-module structure on the left and also with a $A^e$-module structure on the right. Let M be a $(A, A)$-bimodule, $n$ an integer $\geqslant 0$; we put:
$$
H_n(A, M) = \mathrm{Tor}_n^{A^e}(M, A) \qquad H^n(A, M) = \mathrm{Ext}_{A^e}^n(A, M).
$$
a) The k-module $H_0(A, M)$ is isomorphic to the quotient of M by the sub-k-module generated by the elements $(am - ma)$ for $a \in A, m \in M$; the k-module $H^0(A, M)$ is isomorphic to the subk-module of M formed by elements m such that $am = ma$ for all $a \in A$. The k-module $H^1(A, M)$ is identified with the quotient of the k-module of the derivations $D_k(A, M)$ by the subk-module of the interior derivations, that is to say the derivations $d_m$, for $m \in M$, defined by $d_m(a) = am - ma$ for all $a \in A$ (*cf.* III, p. 132).
b) We assume that A is a *projective* k-module. Show that the standard resolution B(A) (X, p. 58) defines a projective resolution of the $A^e$-module on the left A. Deduce that the k-modules $H^n(A, M)$ are isomorphic to the homology modules of the complex $C(A, M)$, where, for $p \geqslant 0$, $C^p(A, M)$ is the k-module of the applications k-multilinears of $A^p$ in M, $C^p(A, M) = 0$ for $p < 0$, the differential being given by the formula:
$$
(df)(x_0, ..., x_p) = x_0 . f(x_1, ..., x_p) + \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} (-1)^{i+1} f(x_0, ..., x_i . x_{i+1}, ..., x_p)
$$
$$
+ (-1)^{p+1} f(x_0, ..., x_{p-1}) . x_p
$$
for $f \in C^p(A, M)$, $x_0, ..., x_p \in A$.
Give an analogous expression for the k-modules $H_n(A, M)$.
c) Under the assumption of b), let $\rho : k \to k'$ be a homomorphism br of commutative rings, $A'$ the $k'$-algebra $k' \otimes_k A$, $M'$ a $(A', A')$-bimodule. Show that the k-modules $H_n(A, M')$ and $H_n(A', M')$ (resp. $H^n(A, M')$ and $H^n(A', M')$) are isomorphic.
d) We now assume that k is a field. Let B be a second k-algebra (associative and unified), M a $(A, A)$-bimodule, N a $(B, B)$-bimodule. For all $n \geqslant 0$, define an isomorphism of k-modules
$$
H_n(A \otimes_k B, M \otimes_k N) \to \bigoplus_{p+q=n} (H_p(A, M) \otimes_k H_q(B, N)).
$$
If we further assume that A and B are of finite dimension on k, show that there also exists a k-isomorphism
$$
H^n(A \otimes_k B, M \otimes_k N) \to \bigoplus_{p+q=n} (H^p(A, M) \otimes_k H^q(B, N)) \quad \text{pour tout } n \geqslant 0
$$
(use X, p. 76, th. 3).

e) Let M, N be two A-modules on the left, P be an A-module on the right, so that the k-modules Hom_k(M, N) and M $\otimes_k$ P have natural structures of (A, A)-bimodules. Define for all $n \geq 0$ isomorphisms of k-modules:

$$
H^n(A, \mathrm{Hom}_k(M, N)) \to \mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M, N) \quad \text{et} \quad H_n(A, M \otimes_k P) \to \mathrm{Tor}_n^A(P, M).
$$
