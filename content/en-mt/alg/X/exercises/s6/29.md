We keep the notations from the previous exercise; we further assume that $k$ is a field of characteristic zero, and that the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is semisimple (LIE, I, § 6). Let $M$ be a $\mathfrak{g}$-module of finite dimension on $k$.

a) If $M$ is simple and non-trivial, we have $H^p(\mathfrak{g},M)=0$ for all $p>0$ (the bilinear form on $\mathfrak{g}$ associated with $M$ is non-degenerate (LIE, I, § 6, no 1, prop. 1); observe that the multiplication by the corresponding Casimir element (LIE, I, § 3, no 7) is zero in $k$ and bijective in $M$).

b) Show that $H^1(\mathfrak{g},M)=0$ whatever $M$ (use a) and the fact that $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}$). Conversely, a $k$-Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$ (of finite dimension) such that $H^1(\mathfrak{h},M)=0$ for every $\mathfrak{h}$-module $M$ of finite dimension is semisimple.

c) We provide $k$ with the $\mathfrak{g}$-module structure corresponding to the trivial representation. Show that, for all $p>0$, the $k$-vector space $H^p(\mathfrak{g},k)$ is isomorphic to the vector subspace of $C^p(\mathfrak{g},k)$ formed of alternating $p$-linear forms $f$ which are invariant, that is to say such that

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{p}f(x_1,\ldots,[x,x_i],\ldots,x_p)=0\quad\text{pour tous }x,x_1,\ldots,x_p\in\mathfrak{g}.
$$

(Deduce from the semi-simplicity of the representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ and from exercise 28, d) that $H^p(\mathfrak{g},k)$ is isomorphic to $H^p(C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}),k)$, where $C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the subcomplex of $C(\mathfrak{g},k)$ canceled by all endomorphisms $\theta(x)$ for $x\in\mathfrak{g}$; then show that $C_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g})$ has zero differential.)

d) Deduce from c) that $H^2(\mathfrak{g},M)=0$ for every $\mathfrak{g}$-module $M$ of finite dimension over $k$. (Show that any bilinear form invariant on $\mathfrak{g}$ is symmetric, reducing to the case where $\mathfrak{g}$ is simple and $k$ algebraically closed.) \*
