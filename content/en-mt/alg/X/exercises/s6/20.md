Let G be a finite group, M a $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module.
a) We assume that the Z-module M is free. Show that the $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module M is projective if and only if for any Sylow subgroup H $\subset G$, the $\mathbf{Z}^{(H)}$-module M is cohomologically trivial.
(To proving that the condition is sufficient, consider a resolution $0 \to R \to L \to M \to 0$ of the $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module M, where L is free; show that the $\mathbf{Z}^{(H)}$-module Q = Hom$_\mathbf{Z}$(M, N) is cohomologically trivial by observing that Q/pQ is cohomologically trivial and using exercise 19. Deduce from exercise 17, e) that we have $H^1(G, Q) = 0$, and conclude that M is projective.)
b) Show that the following conditions are equivalent:
α) The $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module M is cohomologically trivial.
β) The $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module M admits a finite projective resolution.
γ) The $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module M admits a projective resolution of length one.
(To show that α) entails γ), consider an exact sequence $0 \to R \to L \to M \to 0$, where L is a free $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module, and apply a) to R.)
c) We assume that the Z-module M is divisible. Show that the $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module M is cohomologically trivial if and only if it is injective (consider an exact sequence $0 \to M \to I \to Q \to 0$, where I is an injective $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module, and show using b) that the $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module Hom$_\mathbf{Z}$(Q, M) is cohomologically trivial).
d) For a $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module to be cohomologically trivial, it is necessary and sufficient that it admits an injective resolution of length 1.
