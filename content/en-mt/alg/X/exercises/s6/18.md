Let G be a finite group and M a $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module. We say that M is cohomologically trivial if for every subgroup H of G and for every $q \in \mathbf{Z}$, the group $\hat{H}^q(H, M)$ is zero.
a) Now prove that a $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module projective relatively to $\mathbf{Z}$ (in particular an induced module, cf. exercise 12) is cohomologically trivial.
b) Let n, r, k be three integers such that r divides $(k^n - 1)$; let G be a cyclic group of order n, $\sigma$ a generator of G. We endow the $\mathbf{Z}$-module $M = \mathbf{Z}/r\mathbf{Z}$ with the structure of $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module defined by $\sigma m = km$ for every $m \in M$. Prove that M is projective relatively to $\mathbf{Z}$ if and only if n and r are relatively prime.
c) With the notations of b), show that for $\hat{H}^q(G, M) = 0$ for every $q \in \mathbf{Z}$ to hold, it is necessary and sufficient that one has $(r, k - 1)(r, 1 + k + \cdots + k^{n-1}) = r$. In particular if $r = k^n - 1$, show that M is cohomologically trivial.
d) Deduce from b) and c) an example of a $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module that is cohomologically trivial which is not projective relatively to $\mathbf{Z}$, and of a $\mathbf{Z}^{(G)}$-module M such that $\hat{H}^q(G, M) = 0$ for every $q \in \mathbf{Z}$ which is not cohomologically trivial.
