Let $G$ be a real or complex connected finite-dimensional Lie group, $\alpha$ a differential form of degree $p$ invariant on the left on G. For $\alpha$ to be invariant on the right as well, it is necessary and sufficient that $d\alpha = 0$. (Observe that the condition of invariance on the right is written

$$
\wedge^p(t\mathrm{Ad}(s)) . \alpha(e) = \alpha(e)
$$

for all $s \in G$, and is therefore equivalent to the condition

$$
\sum_{j=1}^p \langle \alpha(e), u_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge u_{j-1} \wedge [u, u_j] \wedge u_{j+1} \wedge \cdots \wedge u_p \rangle = 0
$$

whatever $u, u_1, \ldots, u_p$ in $L(G)$ may be). In particular, the differential forms of degree 1 invariant on the right and on the left on $G$ form a vector space of dimension $\dim L(G) - \dim[L(G), L(G)]$.
