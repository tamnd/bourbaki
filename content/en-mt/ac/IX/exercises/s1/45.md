By a pre-$\lambda$-ring is meant a ring A endowed with mappings $\lambda_n : A \to A$ ($n \in \mathbf{N}$) such that
(i) $\lambda_0(a) = 1$,
(ii) $\lambda_1(a) = a$,
(iii) $\lambda_n(a + b) = \sum_{p=0}^n \lambda_p(a) \lambda_{n-p}(b)$,
for all $a, b$ in A. Conditions (i) and (iii) can also be expressed by saying that
$$
\lambda(a) = \sum_{n \geq 0} \lambda_n(a) T^n
$$
defines a homomorphism of the additive group of A into the multiplicative group $\Lambda(A)$.

By $\lambda$-morphisms from a pre-$\lambda$-ring A into another B is meant a ring homomorphism $\rho : A \to B$ such that $\rho(\lambda_n(a)) = \lambda_n(\rho(a))$ for all $a \in A, n \in \mathbf{N}$, or in other words such that the diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Lambda(A) & \xrightarrow{\Lambda(\rho)} & \Lambda(B) \\
\downarrow \lambda & & \uparrow \lambda \\
A & \xrightarrow{\rho} & B
\end{array}
$$

is commutative (the mapping $\Lambda(\rho)$ transforms the series $1 + \sum_{n \geq 1} a_n T^n$ into the series $1 + \sum_{n \geq 1} \rho(a_n) T^n$).

Let A be a ring. We propose to endow $\Lambda(A)$ with a structure of pre-$\lambda$-ring. Let $E^A : U(A) \to \Lambda_T(A)$ denote the ring isomorphism defined in Exercise 43. Let S be another indeterminate.
a) Show that the two composite isomorphisms
$$
U(U(A)) \xrightarrow{U(E^A)} U(\Lambda_T(A)) \xrightarrow{E^{\Lambda_T(A)}} \Lambda_S(\Lambda_T(A))
$$
and
$$
U(U(A)) \xrightarrow{E^{U(A)}} \Lambda_S(U(A)) \xrightarrow{\Lambda_S(E^A)} \Lambda_S(\Lambda_T(A))
$$
coincide; they will be denoted by $\tilde{E}^A$.
The mapping $\lambda^A$ is defined by the commutativity of the diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
U(U(A)) & \xrightarrow{\tilde{E}^A} & \Lambda_S(\Lambda_T(A)) \\
\uparrow \mu^A & & \uparrow \lambda^A \\
U(A) & \xrightarrow{E^A} & \Lambda_T(A)
\end{array}
$$

where $\mu^A$ denotes the homomorphism defined in Exercise 41, d), p. 55.
