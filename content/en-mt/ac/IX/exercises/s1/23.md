Let $A$ be a ring and $I$ the set of negative integers. Let $m$ be an integer $\geqslant 1$ and $[a_0, ..., a_{m-1}]$ an element of $W_m(A)$. Associate to it the element $(b_i)_{i \in I}$ of $A^I$ defined by
$$
\left\{
\begin{array}{ll}
b_i = a_{i+m-1} & \text{for } 1-m \leqslant i \leqslant 0 \\
b_i = 0 & \text{for } i \leqslant -m .
\end{array}
\right.
$$

Thus $W_m(A)$ is identified with a subset of $A^1$.
a) For every integer $n \geqslant 0$, the mapping $V_n : W_n(A) \to W_{n+1}(A)$ induced by the shift is a group homomorphism. The identifications of the groups $W_n(A)$ with subsets of $A^1$ are compatible with the mappings $V_n$ and make it possible to identify the group $\lim \overrightarrow{W_n(A)}$, the inductive limit of the groups $W_n(A)$ with respect to the $V_n$, with the subset $CW^u(A)$ of $A^1$ formed by the elements all but a finite number of whose components are zero, elements which shall be called *unipotent Witt covectors*. By transport of structure, one obtains on this subset a group structure.
b) For $a = (a_i)_{i \in I}$ and $b = (b_i)_{i \in I}$ in $CW^u(A)$, one has
$$
a + b = (c_i)_{i \in I} \quad \text{where} \quad c_{-n} = S_m(a_{-m-n}, ..., a_{-n-1}, a_{-n}; b_{-m-n}, ..., b_{-n-1}, b_{-n})
$$
for every integer $m$ sufficiently large.
c) For every ring homomorphism $\varphi : A \to B$, the mapping $CW^u(\varphi) : CW^u(A) \to CW^u(B)$ defined by $CW^u(\varphi)(a_i)_{i \in I} = (\varphi(a_i))_{i \in I}$ is a group homomorphism.
