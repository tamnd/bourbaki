Let $A$ be a $\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}$-algebra. We denote by $\mathcal{D}_A$ the algebra over $W(A)$ generated by two indeterminates $F$ and $V$ subject to the relations
$$
\begin{align*}
Fa &= (Fa).F & \text{for } a \in W(A) \\
aV &= V(Fa) & \text{for } a \in W(A) \\
FV &= p \\
VaF &= Va & \text{for } a \in W(A),
\end{align*}
$$
where $F$ and $V$ denote respectively the Frobenius and shift homomorphisms of $W(A)$.
a) Let $B$ be an $A$-algebra. Making $F$ and $V$ act on $W(B)$ by the Frobenius and shift homomorphisms of $W(B)$, we endow $W(B)$ with a $\mathcal{D}_A$-module structure. Analogously, for each integer $n \geqslant 1$, $W_n(B)$ is a $\mathcal{D}_A$-module.
b) For every element $x$ of $\mathcal{D}_A$, there exists a finite-support family $(a_i)_{i \in \mathbf{Z}}$ of elements of $W(A)$, characterized by the equality
$$
x = \sum_{n \geqslant 1} a_{-n} F^n + a_0 + \sum_{n \geqslant 1} V^n a_n.
$$
c) Suppose $W(A)$ is an integral domain. Let $x \in \mathcal{D}_A$. We denote by $\delta(x)$ the greatest integer $n$ such that $a_n$ is nonzero. If $x$ and $y$ are two nonzero elements of $\mathcal{D}_A$, one has $\delta(xy) = \delta(x) + \delta(y)$. Deduce that $\mathcal{D}_A$ is an integral domain.
d) If $A$ is a perfect ring of characteristic $p$, one can replace the condition $VaF = Va$ for $a \in W(A)$ by the condition $VF = p$. The left ideal $\mathcal{D}_A V$ of $\mathcal{D}_A$ is two-sided.
e) If $k$ is a perfect field of characteristic $p$, $\mathcal{D}_k$ is noetherian. (Consider $\mathcal{D}_k$ as quotient of the ring generated over $W(k)$ by two indeterminates $X$ and $Y$ subject to the relations $XY = YX$ and $Xa = (Fa)X, aY = Y(Fa)$ for $a \in W(k)$. Then apply III, § 2, No. 8, corollary 2 to Theorem 1 and Exercise 10.)
