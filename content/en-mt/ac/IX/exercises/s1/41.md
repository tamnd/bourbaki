Let $A$ be a ring.
a) Let $\mu : A \to U(A)$ be a ring homomorphism such that $\Phi_1 \circ \mu = \mathrm{Id}_A$. Put $\sigma = \Phi \circ \mu$ and $\sigma(a) = (\sigma_n(a))_{n \in J}$ for $a \in A$. The mappings $\sigma_n : A \to A$ ($n \in J$) satisfy the following conditions:
(1) $\sigma_n$ is a ring homomorphism for every $n \in J$, and $\sigma_1 = \mathrm{Id}_A$.
(2) For every prime number $p$ and for every $a \in A$, one has $\sigma_p(a) \equiv a^p \mod. pA$ and
$$
\sigma_p(\sigma_n(a)) \equiv \sigma_{pn}(a) \mod. p^{w_p(pn)}A \quad \text{for every } n \in J .
$$
b) Suppose that the additive group of $A$ has no $\mathbf{Z}$-torsion. Let $\sigma = (\sigma_n)_{n \in J}$ be a family of mappings $\sigma_n : A \to A$ satisfying conditions (1) and (2) of $a$. There exists a unique ring homomorphism $\mu : A \to U(A)$ such that $\Phi_1 \circ \mu = \mathrm{Id}_A$ and $\Phi \circ \mu = \sigma$. (Apply Exercises 32 and 33, d), p. 51.)
c) Suppose that the additive group of $A$ has no $\mathbf{Z}$-torsion. There exists a unique ring homomorphism
$$
\mu^A : U(A) \to U(U(A))
$$
such that $\Phi^{U(A)} \circ \mu^A$ is the mapping
$$
F^A : U(A) \to U(A)^J
$$
defined by $F^A(a) = (F_n(a))_{n \in J}$. (Reduce to the case where $A = \mathbf{Z}[X]$. Apply $b$ and Exercises 36 and 38, b), p. 52 and 53.)
d) Let $X = (X_n)_{n \in J}$ be a family of indeterminates considered as an element of $U(\mathbf{Z}[X])$. Put
$$
\mu^{Z[X]}(X) = (\mu_n(X))_{n \in J} = ((\mu_{n,m}(X))_{m \in J})_{n \in J}
$$
where $\mu_{n,m}(X) \in \mathbf{Z}[X]$ for $n, m$ in $J$. For every ring $A$ define $\mu^A : U(A) \to U(U(A))$ by $\mu^A(a) = ((\mu_{n,m}(a))_{m \in J})_{n \in J}$. Now prove that $\mu^A$ is a ring homomorphism such that the following diagram is commutative

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
U(A) & \xrightarrow{\mu^A} & U(U(A)) \\
\downarrow \Phi^A & & \downarrow \Phi^{U(A)} \\
A^J & \xrightarrow{f^A} & (A^J)^J .
\end{array}
$$

(1)

Here by definition, $f^A(a) = (f_n(a))_{n \in J}$ for $a \in A^J$, and $\Phi^A$ (resp. $\Phi^{U(A)}$) is the homomorphism $\Phi$ associated with the ring $A$ (resp. $U(A)$).
e) For every ring homomorphism $\rho : B \to A$ the diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
U(B) & \xrightarrow{U(\rho)} & U(A) \\
\downarrow \mu^B & & \downarrow \mu^A \\
U(U(B)) & \xrightarrow{U(U(\rho))} & U(U(A)) .
\end{array}
$$

is commutative.

f) Now prove that the following diagram is commutative

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
U(A) & \xrightarrow{\mu^A} & U(U(A)) \\
\downarrow & & \downarrow \mu^{U(A)} \\
U(U(A)) & \xrightarrow{U(\mu^A)} & U(U(U(A))) .
\end{array}
$$

(Reduce to the case where $A = \mathbf{Q}[X]$. Then $A$ is a $\mathbf{Q}$-algebra, hence $\Phi^A : U(A) \to A^J$ is an isomorphism and $U(A)$ is also a $\mathbf{Q}$-algebra. If, with the aid of diagram (1) and by transport of structure, one replaces $\mu^A$ by $f^A$ when $A$ is a $\mathbf{Q}$-algebra, one is reduced to proving the commutativity of the diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
A^J & \xrightarrow{f^A} & (A^J)^J \\
\downarrow f^A & & \downarrow f^{(A^J)} \\
(A^J)^J & \xrightarrow{(f^A)^J} & ((A^J)^J)^J .
\end{array}
$$

One has

$$
f^{(A^J)}(f^A(X)) = f^{(A^J)}((f_n(X))_{n \in J}) = ((f_m(f_n(X)))_{n \in J})_{m \in J} = ((f_{mn}(X))_{n \in J})_{m \in J} ,
$$
and
$$
(f^A)^J(f^A(X)) = (f^A)^J((f_n(X))_{n \in J}) = (f^A(f_n(X)))_{n \in J} = ((f_{mn}(X))_{m \in J})_{n \in J} .
$$

g) For all $a \in A$, one has
$$
\mu_n^A(\tau(a)) = 0 \quad \text{for} \quad n \geq 2 .
$$

h) One has the following commutative diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
U(A) & \xrightarrow{\mu^A} & U(U(A)) \\
\downarrow \varphi & & \downarrow \psi \\
W(A) & \xrightarrow{s_A} & W(W(A))
\end{array}
$$

where the mapping $s_A$ is that defined in Exercise 15, p. 44, where the mapping $\varphi$ from $U(A)$ into $W(A)$ is obtained by identifying $W(A)$ and $U_{p^\infty}(A)$ (p. 53, Exercise 39), and the mapping $\psi$ is the composite of $U(\varphi)$ and the mapping from $U(W(A))$ into $W(W(A))$ obtained by identifying $W(W(A))$ with $U_{p^\infty}(W(A))$.)
