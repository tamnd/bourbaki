Let $J$ be the set of integers $\geqslant 1$. For every $n \in J$, let us denote by $J_n$ the set of integers $d \geqslant 1$ which divide $n$. Let $A$ be a ring. We make $A^J$ into a product ring. We define the mappings $\Phi, f_n, v_n (n \in J)$ of $A^J$ into itself by the following formulae. For $a = (a_j)_{j \in J}$, put
$$
\Phi(a) = (\Phi_n(a))_{n \in J}, \quad \text{where} \quad \Phi_n(a) = \sum_{d \in J_n} da_d^{n/d},
$$
$$
v_n(a) = (na_{m/n})_{m \in J}, \quad f_n(a) = (a_{mn})_{m \in J};
$$
it being understood that $a_{m/n} = 0$ if $m/n \notin J$. Note that $\Phi_n$ depends only on the $a_d$ ($d \in J_n$). We shall sometimes write $\Phi_n((a_d)_{d \in J_n})$ in place of $\Phi_n(a)$.

For every prime number $p$, we denote by $w_p$ the $p$-adic valuation of $\mathbf{Q}$. *These notations will be retained in the exercises that follow in § 1.*
a) $f_n$ is an endomorphism of the ring $A^J$, and $f_n \circ f_m = f_{nm}$ for all $n, m$ in $J$.
b) $v_n$ is an endomorphism of the additive group of $A^J$, and $v_n \circ v_m = v_{nm}$ for all $n, m$ in $J$.
Let $n, m$ be in $J$ and $d = \mathrm{pgcd}(m, n)$
c) One has $f_n \circ v_m = d \cdot v_{m/d} \circ f_{n/d}$. In particular $f_n \circ v_n = n.\mathrm{Id}$, and $f_n \circ v_m = v_m \circ f_n$ if $d = 1$.
d) For all $a, b$ in $A^J$ one has
$$
v_n(a) \cdot v_m(b) = d \cdot v_{nm/d}(f_{m/d}(a) \cdot f_{n/d}(b))
$$
$$
((q)^{p/wf})^{p/u} \cdot (v)^u a = ((q)^{w f} \cdot v)^u a \frac{p}{u}
$$
$$
\frac{n}{d} v_n(f_m(b)) = v_n(1) \cdot v_{n/d}(f_{m/d}(b)) .
$$
(The second formula follows from the first, and the third from the second by taking $a = 1$.)
In particular, one has
$$
v_n(a) \cdot v_n(b) = n v_n(a \cdot b),
$$

and
$$
v_n(a) \cdot v_m(b) = v_{nm}(f_m(a) \cdot f_n(b))
$$
if $d = 1$.
