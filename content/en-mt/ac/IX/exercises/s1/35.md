Let A be a ring.
a) The set $A^J$, endowed with the addition
$$
a + b = S(a, b)
$$
and with the multiplication
$$
a \times b = P(a, b),
$$
is a commutative ring, denoted by U(A). The identity element for addition is the sequence $0$ all of whose terms are zero; the identity element for multiplication is the sequence $1$ all of whose terms are zero except that of index 1, which is equal to $1_A$. The opposite of an element $a$ of U(A) is $I(a)$.
b) Let $\rho : B \to A$ be a ring homomorphism. Then $U(\rho) : U(B) \to U(A)$ defined by $U(\rho)(b_n)_{n \in J} = (\rho(b_n))_{n \in J}$ is a ring homomorphism.
c) The mapping $\Phi : U(A) \to A^J$ is a ring homomorphism. In other words $\Phi_n : U(A) \to A$ is a ring homomorphism for every $n \in J$.
d) If the additive group of A has no $\mathbf{Z}$-torsion, the additive group of U(A) also has none.
e) Let $q \in J$. If multiplication by $q$ is bijective in A, it is still bijective in U(A). (Use Exercise 32, c), p. 51.)
