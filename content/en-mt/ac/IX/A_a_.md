### 1. Inductive limit of local rings

Let I be a nonempty right filtering preordered set and let $(A_\alpha, \varphi_{\beta\alpha})$ be an inductive system of rings relative to I. Suppose that, for every $\alpha \in I$, the ring $A_\alpha$ is local, with maximal ideal $m_\alpha$, that the homomorphisms $\varphi_{\beta\alpha}$ are local and flat, and that one has $\varphi_{\beta\alpha}(m_\alpha)$ $A_\beta = m_\beta$ for $\beta \geq \alpha$. Let A denote the inductive limit of the $A_\alpha$, and for every $\alpha \in I$, let $\varphi_\alpha : A_\alpha \to A$ be the canonical homomorphism.

#### Proposition 1 {#ac-ix-a0-prop-1 .statement}

a) The ring A is local, with maximal ideal $m = \lim \overrightarrow{m_\alpha}$. For every $\alpha \in I$, the homomorphism $\varphi_\alpha$ is local and flat, and one has $\varphi_\alpha(m_\alpha)$ $A = m$.

b) If $A_\alpha$ is noetherian for every $\alpha \in A$, then A is noetherian.

a) Put $m = \lim \overrightarrow{m_\alpha}$; this is an ideal of A. The quotient ring $A/m$ is the inductive limit of the fields $A_\alpha/m_\alpha$, hence is a field (A, I, p. 116, Prop. 3). On the other hand, every element of $A - m$ is invertible in A: in fact, let $x \in A - m$; there exist $\alpha \in I$ and $\xi \in A_\alpha$ such that $x = \varphi_\alpha(\xi)$; one has $\xi \notin m_\alpha$, hence $\xi$ is invertible in $A_\alpha$ and x is invertible in A. Consequently, A is a local ring, with maximal ideal m. Let $\alpha \in I$. From the relations $\varphi_{\beta\alpha}(m_\alpha)$ $A_\beta = m_\beta$ for $\beta \geq \alpha$, one deduces, by passing to the inductive limit, $\varphi_\alpha(m_\alpha)$ $A = m$; finally, the homomorphism $\varphi_\alpha$ is flat by I, § 2, No. 7, Prop. 9.

(b) Let $\hat{A}$ be the separated completion of A for the $m$-adic topology and $\pi$ the canonical mapping of A into $\hat{A}$. Suppose that the rings $A_\alpha$ are noetherian. Fix $\alpha \in I$ and prove that the ring $\hat{A}$ is noetherian and flat over $A_\alpha$. By hypothesis, $m_\alpha$ is a finitely generated ideal of $A_\alpha$, hence $m = \varphi_\alpha(m_\alpha)$. A is a finitely generated ideal of A. It follows that the maximal ideal $\hat{m}$ of $\hat{A}$ is equal to $mA$ (III, § 2, No. 12, Cor. 2 to Prop. 16 and No. 13, Prop. 19), hence is finitely generated. Consequently, the ring $\hat{A}$ is noetherian (loc. cit., No. 10, Cor. 5 to Th. 2). On the other hand, for every $n \in \mathbf{N}$, the quotient $\hat{A}/\hat{m}^n$ is isomorphic to $A/m^n$ (loc. cit., No. 12, Cor. 2 to Prop. 16 and formula (21)), which means that $\hat{A}/\pi \circ \varphi_\alpha(m_\alpha^n)$ $\hat{A}$ is isomorphic to $A \otimes_A (A_\alpha/m_\alpha^n)$; since A is a flat $A_\alpha$-module, the $(A_\alpha/m_\alpha^n)$-module $\hat{A}/\pi \circ \varphi_\alpha(m_\alpha^n)$ $\hat{A}$ is flat for every $n \in \mathbf{N}$. By III, § 5, No. 4, Prop. 2, the $A_\alpha$-module $\hat{A}$ is ideally separated for $m_\alpha$; by loc. cit., No. 2, Th. 1, the $A_\alpha$-module $\hat{A}$ is therefore flat. It follows by passing to the inductive limit that $\hat{A}$ is (faithfully) flat over A (I, § 2, No. 7, Prop. 9), hence that A is noetherian (I, § 3, No. 5, corollary to Prop. 8).

### 2. Inflation of a local ring

Let A be a local ring.

We denote by A]X[ the local ring of the polynomial ring A[X] at the prime ideal $m_A A[X]$. It is a local ring with maximal ideal $m_A A[X]$, the canonical homomorphism $A \to A[X]$ is local and flat, and the residue field of $A[X]$ is the pure extension of $\kappa_A$ generated by the class of $X$.

#### Lemma 1 {#ac-ix-a0-lem-1 .statement}

Let $P \in A[X]$ be a monic polynomial whose image $\overline{P}$ in $\kappa_A[X]$ is irreducible. Then the $A$-algebra $B = A[X]/(P)$ is local and finite over $A$, with maximal ideal $m_A B$, the canonical homomorphism $\rho : A \to B$ is local and flat, the residual extension $\kappa_A \to \kappa_B$ is algebraic and generated by the class $x$ of $X$, and the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $\kappa_A$ is $\overline{P}$.

Since the polynomial $P$ is monic, the $A$-module $B$ is free of finite type (A, IV, p. 10). The ring $B/m_A B$ is identified with $\kappa_A[X]/(\overline{P})$, and therefore is a field; hence the ideal $m_A B$ is maximal. Let $q$ be a maximal ideal of $B$; then the ideal $\rho^{-1}(q)$ is maximal (V, § 2, no 1, prop. 1); hence we have $\rho^{-1}(q) = m_A$, whence $q \supset m_A B$ and finally $q = m_A B$. Thus the ring $B$ is local. Lemma 1 follows at once.

#### Definition 1 {#ac-ix-a0-def-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a local ring. An $A$-algebra $B$ is said to be an elementary enlargement of $A$ if $B$ is isomorphic to the $A$-algebra $A[X]$, or else if there exists a monic polynomial $P$ in $A[X]$, whose image in $\kappa_A[X]$ is irreducible, such that $B$ is isomorphic to the $A$-algebra $A[X]/(P)$.

Let $B$ be an elementary enlargement of $A$. From the preceding there result the following properties:

a) The ring $B$ is local and the canonical homomorphism from $A$ into $B$ is local and flat, and in particular injective (I, § 3, no 5, prop. 8).

b) The residue field $\kappa_B$ of $B$ is a monogenic extension of the residue field $\kappa_A$ of $A$. If $\kappa_A$ is of finite degree $d$ over $\kappa_A$, then $B$ is a free $A$-module of rank $d$.

c) We have $m_B = m_A B$. In particular, if $A$ is a field, the same is true of $B$. An extension of fields is an elementary enlargement if and only if it is monogenic.

d) If $A$ is noetherian, the same is true of $B$.

#### Definition 2 {#ac-ix-a0-def-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a local ring. An $A$-algebra $B$ is said to be an enlargement of $A$ if there exists a well-ordered set $\Lambda$ having a greatest element $\omega$, and an increasing family $(B_\lambda)_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ of subalgebras of $B$ satisfying the following conditions:

a) We have $B_\omega = B$ and the ring $B_\lambda$ is local for every $\lambda \in \Lambda$.

b) If $\alpha$ is the smallest element of $\Lambda$, the $A$-algebra $B_\alpha$ is isomorphic to $A$.

c) Let $\nu \neq \alpha$ in $\Lambda$ and let $S_\nu$ be the set of $\lambda \in \Lambda$ such that $\lambda < \nu$. If $S_\nu$ has no greatest element, one has $B_\nu = \bigcup_{\lambda \in S_\nu} B_\lambda$; if $S_\nu$ has a greatest element $\mu$, then $B_\nu$ is an elementary inflation of $B_\mu$.

Let $B$ be a ring and $\rho : A \to B$ a ring homomorphism. One says that $\rho$ is an inflation (resp. an elementary inflation) if the $A$-algebra defined by $\rho$ has this property. If this is so, $\rho$ is injective.

#### Example 1 {#ac-ix-a0-n2-exa-1 .statement}

Every field extension is an inflation. Let indeed K be an extension of a field k. Let us furnish K with a well-ordering for which 0 is the greatest element, and for $\lambda \in K$, let $K_\lambda$ be the sub-$k$-extension of K generated by the elements $\beta$ of K such that $\beta < \lambda$. The verification of conditions a), b), c), for $k, K$ and the family $(K_\lambda)_{\lambda \in K}$, is immediate.

#### Example 2 {#ac-ix-a0-n2-exa-2 .statement}

Let A be a local ring, and I a set of indices. Denote by $A[(X_i)_{i \in I}]$ the local ring of the polynomial ring $A[(X_i)_{i \in I}]$ at the prime ideal $m_A A[(X_i)_{i \in I}]$. The A-algebra $A[(X_i)_{i \in I}]$ is an inflation of A. In fact, furnish the set I with a well-ordering; let $\Lambda$ be the well-ordered set obtained by adjoining to I a greatest element $\omega$. For $i \in I$, identify $A[(X_j)_{j < i}]$ with a subalgebra $B_i$ of $B = A[(X_i)_{i \in I}]$, and put $B_\omega = B$. The family $(B_\lambda)_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ satisfies conditions a), b), c).

#### Remark {#ac-ix-a0-n2-rem-1 .statement}

With the notation of Def. 2, the ring $B_\mu$ is an inflation of $B_\lambda$ when $\lambda \leq \mu$.

#### Proposition 2 {#ac-ix-a0-prop-2 .statement}

Let A be a local ring and B an inflation of A.

a) The ring B is local and one has $m_A B = m_B$.

b) The A-algebra B is faithfully flat.

c) The canonical homomorphism

$$
\gamma_B : \operatorname{gr}(A) \otimes_{\kappa_A} \kappa_B \to \operatorname{gr}(B)
$$

is bijective.

d) If A is noetherian, the same is true of B and the Hilbert-Samuel series (VIII, § 4, No. 3) of A and B are equal.

Let $(B_\lambda)_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ be a family of subalgebras of B satisfying conditions a), b) and c) of Def. 2.

Let $\Lambda'$ be the set of indices $\lambda \in \Lambda$ such that, for every $\mu \leq \lambda$ in $\Lambda$, the A-algebra $B_\mu$ is local and faithfully flat, and such that one has $m_{B_\mu} = m_A B_\mu$. Suppose that $\Lambda' \neq \Lambda$ and let $v$ be the smallest element of $\Lambda - \Lambda'$. One has $\alpha \in \Lambda'$, hence $v \neq \alpha$. Now $S_v$ is contained in $\Lambda'$. If $S_v$ has no greatest element, one has $B_v = \bigcup_{\lambda \in S_v} B_\lambda$ and $v$ belongs to $\Lambda'$ by Prop. 1 of No. 1. If $S_v$ has a greatest element $\mu$, one has $\mu \in \Lambda'$ and $B_v$ is an elementary inflation of $B_\mu$: one again has $v \in \Lambda'$ by the remarks following Def. 1, whence a contradiction.

When A is noetherian, one proves analogously that the set $\Lambda''$ of indices $\lambda \in \Lambda$ such that the ring $B_\lambda$ is noetherian is equal to $\Lambda$.

Thus one has $\omega \in \Lambda'$, whence assertions a) and b). When A is noetherian, one has $\omega \in \Lambda''$, hence $B = B_\omega$ is noetherian.

Assertion c) follows from a), b), and Theorem 1 of III, § 5, No. 2. Suppose that A (hence B) is noetherian; since one has

$$
[m_B^n / m_B^{n+1} : \kappa_B] = [m_A^n / m_A^{n+1} : \kappa_A]
$$

for every $n \in \mathbf{N}$, the Hilbert-Samuel series of A and B are equal.

#### Corollary {#ac-ix-a0-n2-cor-1 .statement}

Suppose that A is noetherian.
a) One has dim(A) = dim(B).
b) Suppose that A is regular, and let $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ be a system of coordinates of A. Then B is regular and the sequence $(x_1 1_B, ..., x_n 1_B)$ is a system of coordinates of B.
This follows from Prop. 1 of VIII, § 5, No. 1.

#### Proposition 3 {#ac-ix-a0-prop-3 .statement}

Let A, B, C be three local rings and $u : A \to B, v : B \to C$ two inflations. Then $v \circ u$ is an inflation.
Let $(B_\lambda)_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ and $(C_\mu)_{\mu \in M}$ be families of sub-A-algebras of B and of sub-B-algebras of C respectively, having properties a), b), c) of Def. 2. On the set N sum of $\Lambda$ and M, consider the order relation inducing on $\Lambda$ and M the given orders and such that one has $\lambda < \mu$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda, \mu \in M$. This is a well-ordering relation. For $\lambda \in \Lambda \subset N$, put $C_\lambda = v(B_\lambda)$. Then the family $(C_v)_{v \in N}$ satisfies conditions a), b), c) of Def. 1 with respect to the A-algebra C.

#### Theorem 1 {#ac-ix-a0-thm-1 .statement}

Let $f : A \to A'$ be a surjective local homomorphism of local rings and let $B'$ be an inflation of $A'$. There exists an inflation B of A and an isomorphism of A-algebras of $B \otimes_A A'$ onto $B'$.
A) Suppose that $B'$ is an elementary inflation of $A'$. Let us distinguish two cases:
1) If $B'$ is finite over $A'$, choose an isomorphism of $A'-algebras$ $\varphi : A'[X]/(P') \to B'$, where $P' \in A'[X]$ is a monic polynomial whose image is irreducible in $\kappa_{A'}[X]$. Choose a monic polynomial $P \in A[X]$ whose image in $A'[X]$ is $P'$. It is necessarily irreducible modulo the maximal ideal of A. Put then $B = A[X]/(P)$. The A-algebra B is an elementary inflation of A and $\varphi$ induces an isomorphism of A-algebras of $B \otimes_A A'$ onto $B'$.
2) If $B'$ is not finite over $A'$, choose an isomorphism of $A'-algebras$ $\psi : A'[X] \to B'$. Put $B = A[X][$. The A-algebra B is an elementary inflation of A, and $B \otimes_A A'$ is canonically isomorphic to $A'[X][$. Hence $\psi$ induces an isomorphism of A-algebras of $B \otimes_A A'$ onto $B'$.
B) We pass to the general case. Let $(B'_\lambda)_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ be a family of sub-$A'$-algebras of $B'$ having with respect to $A'$ and $B'$ properties a), b), c) of Def. 2. We are going to define by transfinite induction an inductive system $(\tilde{B}_\lambda, i_{\mu \lambda})$ indexed by $\Lambda$ of local rings and injective local homomorphisms, and isomorphisms $u_\lambda : \tilde{B}_\lambda \otimes_A A' \to B'_\lambda$ such that, for $\lambda \leq \mu$, $u_\mu \circ (i_{\mu \lambda} \otimes \mathrm{Id}_{A'}) \circ u_\lambda^{-1}$ is the canonical injection of $B'_\lambda$ into $B'_{\mu}$.
If $\alpha$ is the smallest element of $\Lambda$, we put $\tilde{B}_\alpha = A, i_{\alpha \alpha} = \mathrm{Id}_A$ and take for $u_\alpha$ the canonical isomorphism $A \otimes_A A' \to A'$.
Let $\nu \in \Lambda$, and suppose that $\tilde{B}_\lambda, u_\lambda$ and $i_{\mu \lambda}$ have been constructed when $\lambda \leq \mu < \nu$. Let $S_\nu$ be the set of elements $\varepsilon$ of $\Lambda$ such that $\varepsilon < \nu$. If $S_\nu$ has no greatest element, we take for $\tilde{B}_\nu$ the inductive limit of the $\tilde{B}_\lambda$ for $\lambda \in S_\nu$, for $u_\nu$ the composite isomorphism $\tilde{B}_\nu \otimes_A A' \to \lim (\tilde{B}_\lambda \otimes_A A') \to \lim B'_\lambda \to B'_\nu$, and for $i_{\nu \lambda}$, when $\lambda \in S_\nu$, the canonical mapping of $\tilde{B}_\lambda$ into $\tilde{B}_\nu$. If $S_\nu$ has a greatest element $\mu$, then $B'_\nu$ is an elementary inflation of $B'_{\mu}$. By A), there exists an elementary inflation i_{v\mu}: \tilde{B}_{\mu} \to \tilde{B}_{v} et un isomorphisme de $\tilde{B}_{\mu}$-algèbres de $\tilde{B}_{v} \otimes_{\tilde{B}_{\mu}} B'_{\mu}$ sur $B'_{v}$. We take for $u_{v}$ the composite isomorphism of A-algebras
$$
\tilde{B}_{v} \otimes_{A} A' \to \tilde{B}_{v} \otimes_{\tilde{B}_{\mu}} (\tilde{B}_{\mu} \otimes_{A} A') \to \tilde{B}_{v} \otimes_{\tilde{B}_{\mu}} B'_{\mu} \to B'_{v}
$$
and for $i_{v\lambda}$, when $\lambda \in S_{v}$, the homomorphism $i_{v\mu} \circ i_{\mu\lambda}$.

Let us then put $B = \tilde{B}_{\omega}$ and, for every $\lambda \in \Lambda$, denote by $B_{\lambda}$ the image of $\tilde{B}_{\lambda}$ under the canonical injection $\tilde{B}_{\lambda} \to B$. The family $(B_{\lambda})_{\lambda \in \Lambda}$ satisfies conditions a), b), c) of Definition 2, and B is an inflation of A. On the other hand, the homomorphism $u_{\omega}$ is an A'-isomorphism of $B \otimes_{A} A'$ onto $B'$.

#### Corollary {#ac-ix-a0-n2-cor-2 .statement}

Let A be a local ring and K an extension of its residue field $\kappa_{A}$. Then there exist a local ring B and an inflation $A \to B$ such that the $\kappa_{A}$-algebra $\kappa_{B}$ is isomorphic to K.

In fact, the homomorphism $\kappa_{A} \to K$ is an inflation (Example 1). Applying Theorem 1 with $A' = \kappa_{A}$ and $B' = K$, one obtains the existence of an inflation B of A and of an A-isomorphism of $B/m_{A}B$ onto K, whence the corollary.

### 3. Existence of $p$-rings

#### Proposition 4 {#ac-ix-a0-prop-4 .statement}

Let p be a prime number, k a field of characteristic p, and let n be an integer $\geq 1$, or $+ \infty$. Then there exists a $p$-ring ($\S$ 2, No. 1, Definition 1) of length n whose residue field is isomorphic to k.

We may regard k as an extension of the residue field $\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z}$ of the local ring $\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}$. By the corollary to Theorem 1, there exists a local ring B, an inflation of $\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}$, such that $\kappa_{B}$ is isomorphic to k. The local ring $\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}$ is regular and $\{ p \}$ is a system of coordinates of $\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}$. By the corollary to Proposition 2 of No. 2, the ring B is regular and $\{ pl_{B} \}$ is a system of coordinates of B. In other words, B is a discrete valuation ring, with maximal ideal $pB$. The completion C of B is then a $p$-ring of infinite length and the residue field $\kappa_{C}$ is isomorphic to $\kappa_{B}$, hence to k. Moreover, for every integer $n \geq 1$, $C/p^{n}C$ is a $p$-ring of length n, with residue field isomorphic to $\kappa_{C}$, hence to k.
