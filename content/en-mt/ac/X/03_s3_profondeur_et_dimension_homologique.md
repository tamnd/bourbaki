## § 3. DEPTH AND HOMOLOGICAL DIMENSION

### 1. Projective dimension, injective dimension, homological dimension

Let A be a ring, M an A-module. Recall (A, X, p. 134, Def. 1) that the projective dimension of M, denoted by dp_A(M), is the greatest lower bound (in $\overline{\mathbf{Z}}$) of the set of lengths of projective resolutions of M. One has dp_A(0) = -∞ and dp_A(M) ≥ 0 if M is nonzero. In order that M be projective, it is necessary and sufficient that one have dp_A(M) ≤ 0.

#### Example {#ac-x-s3-n1-exa-1 .statement}

Let J be an ideal of A generated by an A-regular sequence $x = (x_1, \ldots, x_r)$. One has dp_A(A/J) ≤ r. In fact, this is clear if A is zero; in the contrary case, the Koszul complex $K_\bullet(x, A)$ is a free resolution of A/J of length r (loc. cit., p. 159, Remark 3). Moreover, for every A-module N, the A-modules Ext_A^r(A/J, N) and N/JN are isomorphic (loc. cit.); consequently, in order that one have dp_A(A/J) = r, it is necessary and sufficient that J be distinct from A (loc. cit., p. 134, Prop. 1).

One defines analogously the injective dimension of M, denoted by di_A(M), as the greatest lower bound of the set of lengths of injective resolutions of M. One has di_A(0) = -∞, and di_A(M) ≥ 0 if M ≠ 0. In order that M be injective, it is necessary and sufficient that one have di_A(M) ≤ 0.

#### Proposition 1 {#ac-x-s3-prop-1 .statement}

Let A be a ring, M an A-module and n an integer ≥ 0. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) one has di_A(M) ≤ n (in other words, M possesses an injective resolution of length ≤ n) ;
(ii) for every A-module N and every integer i > n, one has Ext_A^i(N, M) = 0 ;
(iii) for every ideal a of A, one has Ext_A^{n+1}(A/a, M) = 0 ;
(iv) for every exact sequence of A-modules

$$
0 \to M \to I^0 \to I^1 \to \ldots \to I^{n-1} \to Q \to 0,
$$

where the $I^i$ are injective, the A-module Q is injective.

(i) ⇒ (ii) : this follows from A, X, p. 100, Th. 1.
(ii) ⇒ (iii) : this is clear.
(iii) ⇒ (iv) : in the situation of (iv), for every A-module N one has an isomorphism of Ext_A^1(N, Q) onto Ext_A^{n+1}(N, M) (A, X, p. 128, Cor. 4) ; under assumption (iii), the A-module Ext_A^1(A/a, Q) is zero for every ideal a of A, and Q is injective (A, X, p. 93, Prop. 11).
(iv) ⇒ (i) : consider the exact sequence (A, X, p. 52)

$$
0 \to M \to I^0(M) \to \ldots \to I^{n-1}(M) \to K^{n-1}(M) \to 0;
$$

if condition (iv) is satisfied, the A-module $K^{n-1}(M)$ is injective, whence (i).

Recall (A, X, p. 138, Def. 2) that the homological dimension of the ring $A$, denoted by $\mathrm{dh}(A)$, is the least upper bound in $\overline{\mathbf{Z}}$ of the set of integers $n$ for which there exist two $A$-modules $M$ and $N$ such that $\mathrm{Ext}^n_A(M, N)$ is nonzero. It is therefore also the least upper bound of the set of projective dimensions (or injective dimensions) of all the $A$-modules; when $A$ is noetherian, it is enough to restrict oneself to finitely generated $A$-modules (A, X, p. 139, Cor.).

### 2. Localization of homological dimension

#### Proposition 2 {#ac-x-s3-prop-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a ring, $M$ and $N$ $\Lambda$-modules, $i$ an integer and $S$ a multiplicative subset of $\Lambda$. One has a canonical isomorphism of $S^{-1}A$-modules

$$
S^{-1} \mathrm{Tor}_i^\Lambda(M, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Tor}_i^{S^{-1}\Lambda}(S^{-1}M, S^{-1}N) .
$$

If the ring $A$ is noetherian and the $A$-module $M$ finitely generated, one has a canonical isomorphism of $S^{-1}\Lambda$-modules

$$
S^{-1} \mathrm{Ext}_\Lambda^i(M, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}_{S^{-1}\Lambda}^i(S^{-1}M, S^{-1}N) .
$$

Since the $A$-module $S^{-1}A$ is flat, this follows from A, X, p. 110, Prop. 9 and p. 111, Prop. 10.

#### Corollary {#ac-x-s3-n2-cor-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a ring, $M$ and $N$ $\Lambda$-modules, $i$ an integer.

a) The support of $\mathrm{Tor}_i^\Lambda(M, N)$ is contained in $\mathrm{Supp}(M) \cap \mathrm{Supp}(N)$, and the same is true of the support of $\mathrm{Ext}_\Lambda^i(M, N)$ if $A$ is noetherian and $M$ finitely generated.

b) Suppose that $A$ is noetherian, and that the modules $M$ and $N$ are finitely generated; if the $\Lambda$-module $M \otimes_A N$ is of finite length, the same is true of $\mathrm{Tor}_i^\Lambda(M, N)$ and of $\mathrm{Ext}_\Lambda^i(M, N)$.

If $p$ is a prime ideal of $A$ not belonging to $\mathrm{Supp}(M) \cap \mathrm{Supp}(N)$, one of the modules $M_p$ or $N_p$ is zero, which implies a) in view of Prop. 2.

In order that a finitely generated module over a noetherian ring be of finite length, it is necessary and sufficient that its support consist of maximal ideals (IV, § 2, No. 5, Prop. 7). Under assumption b), the $A$-modules $\mathrm{Tor}_i^\Lambda(M, N)$ and $\mathrm{Ext}_\Lambda^i(M, N)$ are finitely generated (A, X, p. 108, Cor.) ; the assertion b) therefore follows from a).

#### Proposition 3 {#ac-x-s3-prop-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module, and $N$ an $A$-module.

a) One has

$$
\mathrm{dp}_A(M) = \sup_p \mathrm{dp}_{A_p}(M_p) , \quad \mathrm{di}_A(N) = \sup_p \mathrm{di}_{A_p}(N_p) , \quad \mathrm{dh}(A) = \sup_p \mathrm{dh}(A_p) ,
$$

where $p$ ranges over the set of prime ideals (resp. maximal ideals) of $A$.

b) The mapping $p \mapsto \mathrm{dp}_{A_p}(M_p)$ of $\mathrm{Spec}(A)$ into $\overline{\mathbf{Z}}$ is upper semi-continuous.

Let us prove a). Let $n$ be an integer $\geqslant 0$. Suppose that one has $\mathrm{dp}_A(M) < n$. For every prime ideal $p$ of $A$ and every $A_p$-module $Q$, the $A_p$-module $\mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M_p, Q)$ is isomorphic to $(\mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M, Q))_p$ (Prop. 2), hence is zero; one deduces the inequality

Let us prove b). Let p be a prime ideal of A and n = dp_{A_p}(M_p). Let us show that there exists a neighbourhood U of p in Spec(A) such that one has dp_{A_q}(M_q) \leq n for every q \in U. This is clear if n = +\infty ; if n = -\infty , this follows from the fact that the support of M is closed. Suppose now that n is finite and choose an exact sequence of A-modules
$$
P_{n-1} \xrightarrow{d_{n-1}} P_{n-2} \longrightarrow \ldots \longrightarrow P_0 \xrightarrow{d_0} M \to 0 ,
$$
where the P_i are free of finite type (A, X, p. 53, Prop. 6). Put P = Ker d_{n-1} ; this is a module of finite presentation. The A_p-module P_p is projective (A, X, p. 134, Prop. 1), hence free (II, § 3, No. 2, Cor. 2 of Prop. 5). By II, § 5, No. 1, Cor. of Prop. 2, there exists an element f of A - p such that the A_f-module P_f is free; the A_q-module P_q is then free for every element q of the open set U of Spec(A) formed by the prime ideals not containing f . This proves b).

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-x-s3-prop-3-cor-1 .statement}

For every multiplicative subset S of A , one has
$$
\mathrm{dp}_{S^{-1}A}(S^{-1}M) \leq \mathrm{dp}_A(M) \quad , \quad \mathrm{di}_{S^{-1}A}(S^{-1}N) \leq \mathrm{di}_A(N) \quad , \quad \mathrm{dh}(S^{-1}A) \leq \mathrm{dh}(A) .
$$

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-x-s3-prop-3-cor-2 .statement}

If one has dp_{A_m}(M_m) < +\infty for every maximal ideal m of Supp(M) , one has dp_A(M) < +\infty .

In fact, the subspace X of Supp(M) formed by the maximal ideals is quasi-compact (II, § 4, No. 2, Prop. 8 and 9 and No. 3, Cor. 7 of Prop. 11) ; the mapping m \mapsto dp_{A_m}(M_m) of X into \overline{\mathbf{R}} is upper semi-continuous (Prop. 3), hence bounded (TG, IV, p. 30, Cor. of Th. 3).

#### Remark {#ac-x-s3-n2-rem-1 .statement}

Let A be a regular noetherian ring of infinite dimension (VIII, § 5, Exerc. 6 c)). We shall see below (No. 7, Th. 2 and § 4, No. 1, Prop. 1) that one has di_{A_m}(A_m) = dh(A_m) < +\infty for every maximal ideal m of A ; Prop. 3 therefore implies di_A(A) = dh(A) = +\infty . Consequently, the mappings p \mapsto di_{A_p}(N_p) and p \mapsto dh(A_p) are not in general upper semi-continuous.

### 3. Homological dimension of noetherian rings

Let A be a noetherian local ring, M a finitely generated A-module. Recall (A, X, § 3, No. 6) that a resolution
$$
\ldots \longrightarrow L_n \xrightarrow{d_n} L_{n-1} \longrightarrow \ldots \longrightarrow L_0 \xrightarrow{d_0} M \to 0
$$
of M is a minimal projective resolution if each of the modules L_i is free of finite type, and if the complex $\kappa_A \otimes_A L$ has zero differential. For every integer $i \geq 0$, one then has

(1) $$ \operatorname{Ext}_A^i(M, \kappa_A) : \kappa_A ] = [\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M, \kappa_A) : \kappa_A ] = \operatorname{rg}_A(L_i) $$

(A, X, p. 103, Example 3). Every finitely generated A-module admits such a resolution (A, X, p. 56, Prop. 10).

#### Proposition 4 {#ac-x-s3-prop-4 .statement}

*Let A be a noetherian local ring, M a finitely generated A-module, and n an integer $\geq 0$. The following conditions are equivalent* :

(i) *one has* $\operatorname{dp}_A(M) < n$ ;
(ii) *one has* $\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(M, \kappa_A) = 0$ ;
(iii) *one has* $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(M, \kappa_A) = 0$ ;
(iv) *every minimal projective resolution of M has length* $< n$.

Assertions (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) and (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) are immediate (A, X, p. 100, Th. 1). Let L be a minimal projective resolution of M ; if (ii) or (iii) is satisfied, one has $L_n = 0$ by (1). Since every minimal projective resolution of M is isomorphic to L (A, X, p. 54, Prop. 8), one deduces (iv). Implication (iv) $\Rightarrow$ (i) is trivial.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-x-s3-prop-4-cor-1 .statement}

*Let A be a noetherian local ring and n an integer $\geq 0$. The following conditions are equivalent* :

(i) *one has* $\operatorname{dh}(A) < n$ ;
(ii) *one has* $\operatorname{Ext}_A^i(M, N) = 0$ *and* $\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M, N) = 0$ *for every pair* $(M, N)$ *of A-modules and every integer* $i \geq n$ ;
(iii) *one has* $\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(\kappa_A, \kappa_A) = 0$ ;
(iv) *one has* $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(\kappa_A, \kappa_A) = 0$ ;
(v) *one has* $\operatorname{dp}_A(\kappa_A) < n$.

It is clear that (i) implies (ii) and that (ii) implies (iii) and (iv). By Prop. 4 applied to the A-module $\kappa_A$, each of conditions (iii) and (iv) implies (v). Let us prove that (v) implies (i) : if $\operatorname{dp}_A(\kappa_A) < n$, one has $\operatorname{Tor}_n^A(M, \kappa_A) = 0$ for every A-module M ; hence every finitely generated A-module is of projective dimension $< n$ (Prop. 4), which implies $\operatorname{dh}(A) < n$ (A, X, p. 138, Prop. 4).

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-x-s3-prop-4-cor-2 .statement}

*One has* $\operatorname{dh}(A) = \operatorname{dp}_A(\kappa_A)$.

#### Remark 1 {#ac-x-s3-n3-rem-1 .statement}

Let A be a local ring. The A-module $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(\kappa_A, \kappa_A)$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{m}_A / \mathfrak{m}_A^2$ (A, X, p. 72, Example). Hence when A is noetherian, in order that $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(\kappa_A, \kappa_A)$ be zero, it is necessary and sufficient that $\mathfrak{m}_A$ be zero, that is to say, that A be a field. Cor. 1 therefore implies that a noetherian local ring of homological dimension 0 is a field.

#### Remark 2 {#ac-x-s3-n3-rem-2 .statement}

Let A be a noetherian local ring, M a finitely generated A-module of finite projective dimension $n$, N a nonzero finitely generated A-module. *The A-module* $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(M, N)$ *is nonzero*: let in fact L be a minimal projective resolution of M, and d its differential. We have an exact sequence

$$
\operatorname{Hom}_A(L_{n-1}, N) \xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}(d_n, 1)} \operatorname{Hom}_A(L_n, N) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Ext}_A^n(M, N) \to 0 .
$$

Since $d_n \otimes 1_{\kappa_A}$ is zero, one deduces from this, by tensor product with $\kappa_A$, an isomorphism $\kappa_A \otimes_A \mathrm{Hom}_A(L_n, N) \longrightarrow \kappa_A \otimes_A \mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M, N)$, whence, taking account of formula (1) above,

$$
[\kappa_A \otimes_A \mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M, N) : \kappa_A] = [\mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M, \kappa_A) : \kappa_A][\kappa_A \otimes_A N : \kappa_A],
$$

which is nonzero by Prop. 4 and Nakayama's lemma. Consequently, the projective dimension of M is the largest integer $i$ such that $\mathrm{Ext}_A^i(M, N)$ is nonzero.

#### Remark 3 {#ac-x-s3-n3-rem-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module of finite projective dimension, $N$ a finitely generated $A$-module whose support is equal to $\mathrm{Spec}(A)$. According to the preceding remark and Props. 2 and 3 of No. 2, the projective dimension $n$ of $M$ is the largest integer $i$ such that $\mathrm{Ext}_A^i(M, N)$ is nonzero; the support of the $A$-module $\mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M, N)$ is the set of elements $p$ of $\mathrm{Spec}(A)$ such that $\mathrm{dp}_{A_p}(M_p) = n$.

There may exist finitely generated $A$-modules $M$ of projective dimension $+\infty$, satisfying $\mathrm{Ext}_A^i(M, A) = 0$ for $i$ sufficiently large: *this is the case for example of the $A$-module $\kappa_A$ when $A$ is a Gorenstein local ring which is not regular*.

#### Proposition 5 {#ac-x-s3-prop-5 .statement}

*Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module and $n$ an integer $\geqslant 0$. The following conditions are equivalent :

(i) *we have* $\mathrm{dp}_A(M) < n$ ;
(ii) *for every maximal ideal* $m$ *of* $A$, *we have* $\mathrm{Ext}_A^n(M, A/m) = 0$ (resp. *we have* $\mathrm{Tor}_n^A(M, A/m) = 0$) ;
(iii) *for every maximal ideal* $m$ *of* $A$, *we have* $\mathrm{Ext}_{A_m}^n(M_m, A/m) = 0$ (resp. *we have* $\mathrm{Tor}_n^{A_m}(M_m, A/m) = 0$).

(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) : this is clear.
(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) : this follows from Prop. 2 of No. 2.
(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) : by Prop. 4, condition (iii) implies the inequality $\mathrm{dp}_{A_m}(M_m) < n$ for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$, and one concludes by Prop. 3.

#### Remark 4 {#ac-x-s3-n3-rem-4 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring and $n$ an integer $\geqslant 0$. If $m$ and $m'$ are two distinct maximal ideals of $A$, the $A$-modules $\mathrm{Ext}_A^n(A/m, A/m')$ and $\mathrm{Tor}_n^A(A/m, A/m')$ are annihilated by $m + m'$, hence are zero. By a proof analogous to that of Corollary 1 of Proposition 4, one deduces from Proposition 5 the equivalence of the following conditions:

(i) *one has* $\mathrm{dh}(A) < n$ ;
(ii) *one has* $\mathrm{Ext}_A^i(M, N) = 0$ *and* $\mathrm{Tor}_i^A(M, N) = 0$ *for every pair* $(M, N)$ *of* $A$-*modules and every integer* $i \geqslant n$ ;
(iii) *one has* $\mathrm{Tor}_n^A(A/m, A/m) = 0$ *for every maximal ideal* $m$ *of* $A$ ;
(iv) *one has* $\mathrm{Ext}_A^n(A/m, A/m) = 0$ *for every maximal ideal* $m$ *of* $A$ ;
(v) *one has* $\mathrm{dp}_A(A/m) < n$ *for every maximal ideal* $m$ *of* $A$.

In particular, one has $\mathrm{dh}(A) = \sup_m \mathrm{dp}_A(A/m)$, where $m$ ranges over the set of maximal ideals of $A$.

#### Proposition 6 {#ac-x-s3-prop-6 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $N$ an $A$-module, $n$ an integer $\geqslant 0$. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) one has $\operatorname{di}_A(N) < n$ ;
(ii) for every prime ideal $p$ of $\Lambda$, one has $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(\Lambda/p, N) = 0$ ;
(iii) for every prime ideal $p$ of $A$, one has $\operatorname{Ext}_{A_p}^n(\kappa(p), N_p) = 0$.

If moreover the $A$-module $N$ is of finite type, these conditions are equivalent to:

(iv) for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$, one has $\operatorname{Ext}_A^i(A/m, N) = 0$ for $n \leqslant i \leqslant n + \operatorname{ht}(m)$.

Observe that condition (iii) is equivalent to

(iii') for every prime ideal $p$ of $A$, one has $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(A/p, N) \otimes_A \kappa(p) = 0$.

In fact, since $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(A/p, N)$ is annihilated by $p$, the $A$-module $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(A/p, N) \otimes_A \kappa(p)$ is isomorphic to $\operatorname{Ext}_A^n(A/p, N) \otimes_A A_p$, hence to $\operatorname{Ext}_{A_p}^n(\kappa(p), N_p)$ (No. 2, Proposition 2).

The implications (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) and (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iv) result from Proposition 1 of No. 1, and the implication (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii') is clear.

(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i): for every $A$-module $M$, let us put $T(M) = \operatorname{Ext}_A^n(M, N)$. Suppose that (i) is not satisfied. Then there exists (No. 1, Proposition 1) an ideal $a$ of $A$ such that $T(A/a) \neq 0$. Let $p$ be an ideal of $A$ maximal among the ideals having this property; let us prove that $p$ is prime. By IV, § 1, No. 4, Theorem 1 and Theorem 2, there exists a composition sequence $(M_i)_{0 \leqslant i \leqslant m}$ of $A/p$ such that each quotient $M_i/M_{i+1}$ is isomorphic to a module $A/p_i$, where $p_i$ ($0 \leqslant i \leqslant m-1$) is a prime ideal containing $p$. If $T(A/p_i)$ were zero for every $i$, one would deduce by induction on $i$ from the exact sequences

$$
T(M_0/M_i) \longrightarrow T(M_0/M_{i+1}) \longrightarrow T(M_i/M_{i+1})
$$

that $T(M_0/M_m) = T(A/p)$ is zero, which is not so. There therefore exists an index $i$ such that $T(A/p_i) \neq 0$. In view of the maximal character of $p$, one has $p = p_i$, so that $p$ is prime.

Let $x$ be an element of $A - p$; from the exact sequence

$$
0 \to A/p \xrightarrow{x_{A/p}} A/p \to A/(p + xA) \to 0
$$

one deduces an exact sequence

$$
T(A/(p + xA)) \longrightarrow T(A/p) \xrightarrow{u} T(A/p),
$$

where $u = \operatorname{Ext}_A^n(x_A, 1_N) = x_{T(A/p)}$ (A, X, p. 89, Proposition 6). Because of the maximal character of $p$, one has $T(A/(p + xA)) = 0$, so that the homomorphism $u$ is injective. The nonzero $A/p$-module $T(A/p)$ is therefore torsion-free. This implies that $T(A/p) \otimes_A \kappa(p)$ is nonzero (A, II, p. 117, Corollary 1), which contradicts (iii').

Suppose that the $A$-module $N$ is of finite type, and let us prove that (iv) implies (iii). Let $p$ be a prime ideal of $A$ and $m$ a maximal ideal of $A$ containing $p$. There exists a saturated chain of prime ideals of $A$ with end terms $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{m}$. The length $r$ of this chain is less than $\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak{m})$; under assumption (iv), one therefore has
$$
\mathrm{Ext}_{A_{\mathfrak{m}}}^{n+r}(\kappa(\mathfrak{m}), N_{\mathfrak{m}}) = \mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n+r}(A/\mathfrak{m}, N) \otimes_{A} A_{\mathfrak{m}} = 0 .
$$
It then follows from Lemma 3 of § 1, No. 7 that one has $\mathrm{Ext}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{n}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}), N_{\mathfrak{p}}) = 0$, which proves (iii).

#### Remark 5 {#ac-x-s3-n3-rem-5 .statement}

Let $N$ be a finitely generated $A$-module; the condition $\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n}(A/\mathfrak{m}, N) = 0$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $A$ does not necessarily imply $\mathrm{di}_{A}(N) < n$. If, for example, the ring $A$ is local and is not a Gorenstein ring (No. 7, Def. 1), one has $\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n}(A/\mathfrak{m}, A) = 0$ for $n < \mathrm{prof}(A)$ but $\mathrm{di}_{A}(A) = +\infty$.

### 4. Quotient by a cancellable element

In this No., $A$ denotes a ring and $x$ an element of $A$ *cancellable*.

Let $M$ be an $A$-module and $p : P \to M$ a projective resolution of $M$. By A, X, p. 101, cor. to th. 1, $H_{n}(P/xP)$ is identified with $\mathrm{Tor}_{n}^{A}(M, A/xA)$, and therefore is isomorphic to $M/xM$ if $n = 0$, to $\mathrm{Ker}(x_{M})$ if $n = 1$, and is zero otherwise (*loc. cit.*, p. 102, Example 1). Consider the complexes of $(A/xA)$-modules $R$ and $R'$ such that
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{n} & = P_{n}/xP_{n} & R'_{n} & = 0 & \text{for } n \geqslant 2, \\
R_{1} & = Z_{1}(P/xP) & R'_{1} & = (P_{1}/xP_{1})/R_{1}, \\
R_{n} & = 0 & R'_{n} & = P_{n}/xP_{n} & \text{for } n \leqslant 0,
\end{aligned}
$$
and whose differentials are deduced from that of $P$. The complexes $R(1)$ and $R'$ are left resolutions of $\mathrm{Ker}(x_{M})$ and $M/xM$ respectively, and one has an exact sequence of complexes
$$
0 \to R \to P/xP \to R' \to 0 .
$$

Similarly, let $e : M \to E$ be an injective resolution of $M$; let $K$ denote the complex $\mathrm{Ker}(x_{E})$. By A, X, p. 101, cor. to th. 1, $H_{n}(K)$ is identified with $\mathrm{Ext}_{A}^{n}(A/xA, M)$, and therefore is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Ker}(x_{M})$ if $n = 0$, to $M/xM$ if $n = 1$, and is zero otherwise (*loc. cit.*, p. 102, Example 1). From $E$ one deduces complexes of $(A/xA)$-modules $S$ and $S'$ such that
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{n} & = K^{n} & {S'}^{n} & = 0 & \text{for } n \leqslant 0, \\
S^{1} & = B^{1}(K) & {S'}^{1} & = K^{1}/S^{1}, \\
S^{n} & = 0 & {S'}^{n} & = K^{n} & \text{for } n \geqslant 2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The complexes $S$ and $S'(-1)$ are right resolutions of $\mathrm{Ker}(x_{M})$ and $M/xM$ respectively, and one has an exact sequence of complexes
$$
0 \to S \to \mathrm{Ker}(x_{E}) \to S' \to 0 .
$$

Let $N$ be an $(A/xA)$-module, and $e' : N \to E'$ an injective resolution of $N$. From exact sequence (2) one deduces an exact sequence of complexes of $(A/xA)$-modules
$$
0 \to \mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(R', E') \to \mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(P/xP, E') \to \mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(R, E') \to 0 .
$$

$$
H^n(\mathrm{Homgr}_{\Lambda/xA}(R', E')) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^n_{A/xA}(M/xM, N)
$$
$$
H^n(\mathrm{Homgr}_{\Lambda/xA}(P/xP, E')) \longrightarrow H^n(\mathrm{Homgr}_A(P, E')) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^n_A(M, N)
$$
$$
H^n(\mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(R, E')) = H^{n-1}(\mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(R(1), E')) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{n-1}_{A/xA}(\mathrm{Ker}(x_M), N)
$$

whence a long exact sequence of $(A/xA)$-modules

$$
\ldots \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^n_{A/xA}(M/xM, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^n_A(M, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{n-1}_{A/xA}(\mathrm{Ker}(x_M), N)
$$
$$
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_{A/xA}(M/xM, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_A(M, N) \longrightarrow \ldots
$$

(4)

Analogously, let $p' : P' \to N$ be a projective resolution of the $(\Lambda/xA)$-module $N$. From the exact sequence (3) one deduces an exact sequence of complexes of $(A/xA)$-modules

$0 \to \mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(P', S) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(P', \mathrm{Ker}(x_E)) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(P', S') \to 0$.

In view of the isomorphism $\mathrm{Homgr}_{A/xA}(P', \mathrm{Ker}(x_E)) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Homgr}_A(P', E)$, the associated homology exact sequence may be written

$$
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_{A/xA}(N, \mathrm{Ker}(x_M)) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Ext}^{n+1}_A(N, M) \longrightarrow \ldots
$$

(5)

Finally, consider the exact sequence of complexes of $(A/xA)$-modules

$0 \to R \otimes_{A/xA} P' \longrightarrow (P/xP) \otimes_{A/xA} P' \longrightarrow R' \otimes_{A/xA} P' \to 0$

deduced from the exact sequence (2); in view of the isomorphism $P \otimes_A P' \longrightarrow (P/xP) \otimes_{A/xA} P'$, the associated homology exact sequence may be written

$$
\ldots \longrightarrow \mathrm{Tor}^A_n(M, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Tor}^{A/xA}_n(M/xM, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Tor}^{A/xA}_{n-2}(\mathrm{Ker}\ x_M, N)

\longrightarrow \mathrm{Tor}^A_{n-1}(M, N) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Tor}^{A/xA}_{n-1}(M/xM, N) \longrightarrow \ldots

#### Proposition 7 {#ac-x-s3-prop-7 .statement}

*Let $A$ be a ring, $x$ a cancellable element of $A$, $M$ an $A$-module such that the homothety $x_M$ is injective, $N$ an $A$-module annihilated by $x$, $n$ an integer. The canonical homomorphisms of $(A/xA)$-modules*

$$
$$

deduced from the exact sequences (4), (5) and (6) are isomorphisms.

#### Corollary {#ac-x-s3-n4-cor-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module and $J$ an ideal of $A$ generated by a sequence $(x_1, \ldots, x_r)$ of elements of $m_A$ which is both $A$-regular and $M$-regular. Then $\mathrm{dp}_{A/J}(M/JM) = \mathrm{dp}_A(M)$ and $\mathrm{di}_{A/J}(M/JM) = \mathrm{di}_A(M) - r$.

It is enough to treat the case $r = 1$. Put $x = x_1$. The $A$-modules $\mathrm{Ext}^n_{A/xA}(M/xM, \kappa_A)$ and $\mathrm{Ext}^n_A(M, \kappa_A)$ are isomorphic for every integer $n$ (prop. 7); the equality $\mathrm{dp}_{A/xA}(M/xM) = \mathrm{dp}_A(M)$ follows from prop. 4 of No. 3. Analogously, the $A$-modules $\mathrm{Ext}^{n-1}_{A/xA}(\kappa_A, M/xM)$ and $\mathrm{Ext}^n_A(\kappa_A, M)$ are isomorphic for every integer $n$, and the equality $\mathrm{di}_{A/xA}(M/xM) = \mathrm{di}_A(M) - 1$ follows from prop. 6 of No. 3.

### 5. Depth and projective dimension

#### Theorem 1 (Auslander-Buchsbaum) {#ac-x-s3-thm-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring and $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module of finite projective dimension. Then one has the equality

$$
\mathrm{dp}_A(M) + \mathrm{prof}_A(M) = \mathrm{prof}(A)
$$

Let us reason by induction on $\mathrm{dp}_A(M)$.

a) If $\mathrm{dp}_A(M)$ is zero, $M$ is a nonzero finitely generated free $A$-module; its depth is equal to $\mathrm{prof}(A)$ (§ 1, No. 1, remark 4).

b) Suppose $\mathrm{dp}_A(M) = 1$ and choose a minimal projective resolution

$$
0 \to L_1 \xrightarrow{d_1} L_0 \xrightarrow{d_0} M \to 0
$$

of $M$ (No. 3, prop. 4, (iv)). The $A$-modules $L_0$ and $L_1$ are nonzero finitely generated free modules, hence of depth $\mathrm{prof}(A)$ (§ 1, No. 1, remark 4). The mapping $1_{\kappa_A} \otimes d_1 : \kappa_A \otimes_A L_1 \to \kappa_A \otimes_A L_0$ is zero, which implies that $d_1$ belongs to $m_A \mathrm{Hom}_A(L_1, L_0)$. By remark 5 of § 1, No. 1, one has $\mathrm{prof}_A(M) = \mathrm{prof}(A) - 1$.

c) Suppose $\mathrm{dp}_A(M) > 1$. Choose an exact sequence

$$
0 \to N \to L \to M \to 0
$$

where $L$ is a finitely generated free $A$-module. One then has $\mathrm{prof}_A(L) = \mathrm{prof}(A)$ (§ 1, No. 1, remark 4), $\mathrm{dp}_A(N) = \mathrm{dp}_A(M) - 1$ (A, X, p. 135, cor. 2 c)), whence $\mathrm{prof}_A(N) = \mathrm{prof}(A) - \mathrm{dp}_A(N)$ (induction hypothesis), and in particular $\mathrm{prof}_A(N) < \mathrm{prof}_A(L)$. By prop. 1 of § 1, No. 1, one then has $\mathrm{prof}_A(M) = \mathrm{prof}_A(N) - 1$, which completes the proof.

#### Remark {#ac-x-s3-n5-rem-1 .statement}

In view of cor. 2 of prop. 4 (No. 3), theorem 1 applied to the $A$-module $\kappa_A$ implies that one is in one or the other of the following cases:

(i) one has $\mathrm{dp}_A(\kappa_A) = \mathrm{dh}(A) = +\infty$ ;
(ii) one has $\mathrm{dp}_A(\kappa_A) = \mathrm{dh}(A) = \mathrm{prof}(A) < +\infty$.

We shall see later (§ 4, No. 2) that (ii) characterizes regular local rings.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-x-s3-thm-1-cor-1 .statement}

Retain the hypotheses of theorem 1.

a) One has $dp_A(M) \leq \operatorname{prof}(\Lambda)$. For there to be equality, it is necessary and sufficient that the maximal ideal $m_A$ be associated to $M$.

b) One has $\operatorname{prof}_A(M) \leq \operatorname{prof}(\Lambda)$. For there to be equality, it is necessary and sufficient that $M$ be free.

a) In fact, “ $\operatorname{prof}_A(M) = 0$ ” is equivalent to “ $m_A \in \operatorname{Ass}(A)$ ” ($§ 1$, No. 1, remark 2).

b) In fact, “ $dp_A(M) = 0$ ” is equivalent to “ $M$ is free ”.

COROLLARY 2.: Retain the hypotheses of theorem 1 and suppose moreover that $A$ is a Macaulay ring. Then $dp_A(M)$ is the sum of the two positive integers $\dim(A) - \dim_A(M)$ and $\dim_A(M) - \operatorname{prof}(M)$.

In particular, $dp_A(M)$ is then greater than $\dim(A) - \dim_A(M)$, and there is equality if and only if $M$ is Cohen-Macaulay.

#### Corollary 3 {#ac-x-s3-thm-1-cor-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module of finite projective dimension, $i$ an integer $\geq 0$, $N$ a finitely generated $A$-module, and $F$ the support of the $A$-module $\operatorname{Ext}_A^i(M, N)$ (resp. $\operatorname{Tor}_i^\Lambda(M, N)$). Then $\operatorname{prof}_F(\Lambda) \geq i$.

In fact, let $p \in F$. We have $\operatorname{Ext}_A^i(M_p, N_p) \neq 0$ (resp. $\operatorname{Tor}_i^\Lambda(M_p, N_p) \neq 0$) by prop. 2 of No. 2, hence $i \leq dp_{A_p}(M_p) \leq dp_A(M) < +\infty$ (No. 2, prop. 3). Theorem 1 implies $\operatorname{prof}(A_p) \geq i$. Consequently ($§ 1$, No. 5, prop. 8)

$$
\operatorname{prof}_F(\Lambda) = \inf_{p \in F} \operatorname{prof}(A_p) \geq i .
$$

With the terminology of $§ 1$, No. 5, remark 4, the conclusion of cor. 3 means that the modules $\operatorname{Ext}_\Lambda^i(M, N)$ and $\operatorname{Tor}_i^\Lambda(M, N)$ are of grade $\geq i$. It implies that the codimension of their support in $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is $\geq i$ ($§ 1$, No. 7, prop. 12).

#### Corollary 4 {#ac-x-s3-thm-1-cor-4 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian Macaulay ring and $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module of finite projective dimension.

a) Let $p \in \operatorname{Spec}(\Lambda)$; let us denote by $\mathscr{C}(p)$ the set of irreducible components of $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$ containing $p$. We have

$$
\dim_{A_p}(M_p) - \operatorname{prof}_{A_p}(M_p) = dp_{A_p}(M_p) - \inf_{X \in \mathscr{C}(p)} \operatorname{codim}(X, \operatorname{Spec}(A)) .
$$

b) The mapping $p \mapsto \dim_{A_p}(M_p) - \operatorname{prof}_{A_p}(M_p)$ of $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ into $\overline{\mathbf{Z}}$ is upper semi-continuous.

c) The set of prime ideals $p$ of $\Lambda$ such that the $A_p$-module $M_p$ is Macaulay is open and dense in $\operatorname{Spec}(\Lambda)$. Its intersection with $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$ is dense in $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$.

a) We may suppose that $p \in \operatorname{Supp}(M)$. Put $\varphi(p) = \dim(A_p) - \dim_{A_p}(M_p)$. By cor. 2 above, we have

$$
\dim_{A_p}(M_p) - \operatorname{prof}_{A_p}(M_p) = dp_{A_p}(M_p) - \varphi(p) .
$$

Since $A$ is a Macaulay ring, we have

$$
\dim(A_p) = \operatorname{codim}(V(p), \operatorname{Spec}(A)) = \operatorname{codim}(V(p), X) + \operatorname{codim}(X, \operatorname{Spec}(A))
$$

$$
\dim_{\Lambda_p}(M_p) = \operatorname{codim}(V(p), \operatorname{Supp}(M)) = \sup_{X \in \mathcal{C}(p)} \operatorname{codim}(V(p), X)
$$

and consequently

$$
\varphi(p) = \inf_{X \in \mathcal{C}(p)} \operatorname{codim}(X, \operatorname{Spec}(A)) .
$$

b) Let $p \in \operatorname{Spec}(A)$, and let $F$ be the union of the irreducible components of $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$ which do not contain $p$. For every element $q$ of $\operatorname{Spec}(A) - F$, we have $\mathcal{C}(q) \subset \mathcal{C}(p)$, whence $\varphi(q) \geq \varphi(p)$ by the formula above. Consequently the function $\varphi$ is lower semi-continuous; assertion b) then follows from prop. 3 of No. 2.

c) Let $U$ be the set of elements $p$ of $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ such that $M_p$ is Cohen-Macaulay. The condition $p \in U$ is equivalent to $\dim(M_p) - \operatorname{prof}(M_p) \leq 0$, so that $U$ is open by b). Since $U$ contains $\operatorname{Spec}(A) - \operatorname{Supp}(M)$, it is enough to prove that $U \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M)$ is dense in $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$. For every minimal prime ideal $p$ of $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$, the $A_p$-module $M_p$ is of finite length (IV, § 2, No. 5, Corollary 2 of Proposition 7 and § 1, No. 3, Corollary 1 of Proposition 7), hence Cohen-Macaulay; consequently $U$ meets all the irreducible components of $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$. One concludes by means of Proposition 1 of II, § 4, No. 1.

### 6. Depth and injective dimension

#### Proposition 8 {#ac-x-s3-prop-8 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module. Then $\dim_A(M) \leq \operatorname{di}_A(M)$.

Let $r$ be a positive integer less than $\dim_A(M)$. There exists a saturated chain of prime ideals $p \subset p_1 \subset \ldots \subset p_{r-1} \subset q$ such that $p$ is a minimal element of the support of $M$; then the $A_p$-module $M_p$ is of finite length, so that $\operatorname{Hom}_{A_p}(\kappa(p), M_p) \neq 0$, hence $\operatorname{Ext}^r_{A_q}(\kappa(q), M_q) \neq 0$ (§ 1, No. 7, Lemma 3), which implies $\operatorname{di}_A(M) \geq r$ (No. 3, Proposition 6).

#### Proposition 9 {#ac-x-s3-prop-9 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring and $M$ a nonzero finitely generated $A$-module of finite injective dimension. Then $\operatorname{di}_A(M) = \operatorname{prof}(A)$.

Put $r = \operatorname{di}_A(M)$. We have $\operatorname{Ext}^i_A(\kappa_A, M) = 0$ for $i > r$, hence $\operatorname{Ext}^r_A(\kappa_A, M) \neq 0$ by Proposition 6 of No. 3, (iv) $\Rightarrow$ (i). Let $s$ be the depth of $A$ and let $(x_1, \ldots, x_s)$ be an $A$-regular sequence of elements of $\mathfrak{m}_A$ (§ 1, No. 4, Theorem 2); put $N = A/(x_1A + \ldots + x_sA)$. By the example of No. 1, we have $\operatorname{dp}_A(N) = s$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^s_A(N, M) \neq 0$, hence $s \leq \operatorname{di}_A(M) = r$. But $N$ is of depth 0 (§ 1, No. 4, Proposition 7), hence there exists an exact sequence of $A$-modules

$$
0 \to \kappa_A \to N \to N' \to 0 .
$$

From this one deduces an exact sequence of extension modules

$$
\operatorname{Ext}^r_A(N, M) \to \operatorname{Ext}^r_A(\kappa_A, M) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{r+1}_A(N', M) ;
$$

since $\operatorname{Ext}^{r+1}_A(N', M) = 0$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^r_A(\kappa_A, M) \neq 0$, one obtains $\operatorname{Ext}^r_A(N, M) \neq 0$, whence $r \leq \operatorname{dp}_A(N) = s$. In the end, we have $r = s$, which completes the proof.

### 7. Gorenstein rings

#### Definition 1 {#ac-x-s3-def-1 .statement}

A ring $A$ is said to be a Gorenstein ring if it is noetherian and if the $A_m$-module $A_m$ is of finite injective dimension for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$.

For a noetherian local ring $A$ to be a Gorenstein ring, it is necessary and sufficient that $\mathrm{di}_A(A)$ be finite; for a noetherian ring $A$ to be a Gorenstein ring, it is necessary and sufficient that this be so for $A_m$ for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$.

#### Proposition 10 {#ac-x-s3-prop-10 .statement}

Let $A$ be a Gorenstein ring; then $A$ is a Macaulay ring, and satisfies $\mathrm{di}_A(A) = \dim(A)$.

For every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$, one has
$$
\begin{align*}
\dim(A_m) &\leq \mathrm{di}_{A_m}(A_m) & (\text{No. } 6, \text{Prop. } 8) \\
\mathrm{di}_{A_m}(A_m) &= \mathrm{prof}(A_m) & (\text{No. } 6, \text{Prop. } 9) \\
\mathrm{prof}(A_m) &\leq \dim(A_m) & (§ 1, \text{No. } 4, \text{Cor. } 2 \text{ of Th. } 2) ;
\end{align*}
$$
it follows that $A$ is a Macaulay ring, and that $\mathrm{di}_A(A) = \dim(A)$ by passing to the least upper bound (No. 2, Prop. 3).

Thus the noetherian rings $A$ such that $\mathrm{di}_A(A)$ is finite are the finite-dimensional Gorenstein rings (Prop. 3 of No. 2), and the noetherian rings such that the $A$-module $A$ is injective are the artinian Gorenstein rings.

#### Example 1 {#ac-x-s3-n7-exa-1 .statement}

For every multiplicative subset $S$ of a Gorenstein ring $A$, the ring of fractions $S^{-1}A$ is a Gorenstein ring: in fact, let $q$ be a maximal ideal of $S^{-1}A$; it is of the form $S^{-1}p$, where $p$ is a prime ideal of $A$ not meeting $S$. Let $m$ be a maximal ideal of $A$ containing $p$; then the ring $B = (S^{-1}A)_q$ is isomorphic to $A_p$ (II, § 2, No. 5, Prop. 11), hence to a ring of fractions of $A_m$, and consequently satisfies $\mathrm{di}_B(B) < +\infty$ (No. 2, Cor. 1 of Prop. 3).

#### Example 2 {#ac-x-s3-n7-exa-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a Gorenstein ring and let $J$ be an ideal of $A$, generated by an $A$-regular sequence $x$. The quotient ring $A/J$ is a Gorenstein ring: in fact, for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$ containing $J$, the image in $A_m$ of the sequence $x$ is $A_m$-regular and generates the ideal $J_m$, so that $A_m/J_m$ is a Gorenstein ring by Cor. of Prop. 7 (No. 4).

#### Example 3 {#ac-x-s3-n7-exa-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, $J$ an ideal of $A$ generated by an $A$-regular sequence of elements of $m_A$. If $A/J$ is a Gorenstein ring, then the same is true of $A$ (No. 4, Cor. of Prop. 7).

#### Example 4 {#ac-x-s3-n7-exa-4 .statement}

Let $A$ be a regular local noetherian ring; then $A$ is a Gorenstein ring. In fact, let $x$ be a system of coordinates of $A$ (VIII, § 5, No. 1, Def. 1). The sequence $x$ is $A$-regular (*loc. cit.*, No. 2, Theorem 1) and generates the ideal $m_A$; one can therefore apply Example 3.

#### Example 5 {#ac-x-s3-n7-exa-5 .statement}

Every quotient ring of a principal ideal domain is a Gorenstein ring (Example 2). In particular, every monogenic algebra over a field is a Gorenstein ring.

#### Lemma 1 {#ac-x-s3-lem-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be an artinian local ring. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) $A$ is a Gorenstein ring;
(ii) the $A$-module $A$ is injective;
(iii) the vector space $\mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A)$ over $\kappa_A$ is of dimension 1.

Let us recall (A, VIII, p. 3.5) that $A$ has a minimal nonzero ideal; such an ideal is a simple module, hence isomorphic to $\kappa_A$. The vector space $\mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A)$ over $\kappa_A$ is therefore nonzero; saying that it is of dimension 1 means that $A$ contains only one minimal nonzero ideal, which is therefore the socle of $A$ (A, VIII, § 4, No. 6).

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) was proved after Prop. 10. Suppose that the $A$-module $A$ is injective. Let $x$ and $y$ be two nonzero elements of $A$ annihilated by $m_A$. There exists a unique $A$-linear mapping $\varphi : Ax \to A$ such that $\varphi(x) = y$; since $A$ is injective, it extends to an endomorphism of $A$, which implies that $y$ belongs to $Ax$, whence (iii).

Conversely, suppose that $\mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A)$ is of dimension 1. Let $M$ be a finitely generated $A$-module; it is of finite length, hence has a composition series whose quotients are isomorphic to $\kappa_A$. One deduces by induction on the length of $M$ the inequality $\mathrm{long}_A(\mathrm{Hom}_A(M, A)) \leqslant \mathrm{long}_A(M)$. In the exact sequence of extension modules

$$
0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A) \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(A, A) \xrightarrow{\alpha} \mathrm{Hom}_A(m_A, A) \to \mathrm{Ext}_A^1(\kappa_A, A) \to 0 ,
$$

one therefore has $\mathrm{long}_A(\mathrm{Hom}_A(m_A, A)) \leqslant \mathrm{long}_A(m_A) = \mathrm{long}(A) - 1 = \mathrm{long}_A(\mathrm{Im}\, \alpha)$. Consequently $\alpha$ is surjective, $\mathrm{Ext}_A^1(\kappa_A, A)$ is zero and the $A$-module $A$ is injective (No. 3, Prop. 4).

#### Lemma 2 {#ac-x-s3-lem-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a local noetherian ring such that $\mathrm{di}_A(A) = +\infty$. Then $\mathrm{Ext}_A^i(\kappa_A, A) \neq 0$ for every integer $i \geqslant \dim(A)$.

Let us reason by induction on $\dim(A)$. When $\dim(A) = 0$, $m_A$ is the unique prime ideal of $A$ and the assertion follows from Prop. 6 of No. 3. Suppose therefore that $\dim(A) > 0$ and let $p$ be a prime ideal of $A$ distinct from $m_A$; then $\dim(A_p) < \dim(A)$. If $\mathrm{di}_{A_p}(A_p) = +\infty$, the induction hypothesis implies $\mathrm{Ext}_{A_p}^j(\kappa(p), A_p) \neq 0$ for every integer $j \geqslant \dim(A_p)$; by Lemma 3 of § 1, No. 7, this implies $\mathrm{Ext}_A^i(\kappa_A, A) \neq 0$ for every integer $i \geqslant \dim(A_p) + \dim(A/p)$, and in particular for $i \geqslant \dim(A)$ (VIII, § 1, No. 3, Prop. 8, b)).

It remains for us to treat the case where $\mathrm{di}_A(A)$ is infinite but where the injective dimension of $A_p$ is finite for every prime ideal $p$ of $A$ distinct from $m_A$. For such an ideal, in this case, by Prop. 10, $\mathrm{di}_{A_p}(A_p) = \dim(A_p) < \dim(A)$, hence $\mathrm{Ext}_{A_p}^i(\kappa(p), A_p) = 0$ for $i \geqslant \dim(A)$. Since $\mathrm{di}_A(A)$ is infinite, Prop. 6 of No. 3 then implies $\mathrm{Ext}_A^i(\kappa_A, A) \neq 0$ for every integer $i \geqslant \dim(A)$.

#### Theorem 2 (Bass) {#ac-x-s3-thm-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring; put $d = \dim(A)$. Let $x = (x_1, \ldots, x_d)$ be a maximal secant sequence of elements of $m_A$, and $x$ the ideal which it generates. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) $\Lambda$ is a Gorenstein ring;
(ii) one has $\operatorname{di}_\Lambda(A) = d$;
(iii) there exists an integer $i > d$ such that $\operatorname{Ext}^i_\Lambda(\kappa_A, A) = 0$;
(iv) one has $\operatorname{Ext}^i_\Lambda(\kappa_A, A) = 0$ for $i < d$ and the $\kappa_A$-vector space $\operatorname{Ext}^d_\Lambda(\kappa_A, A)$ is of dimension 1;
(v) the ring $A$ is Macaulay and the $\kappa_A$-vector space $\operatorname{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A/x)$ is of dimension 1;
(vi) the sequence $x$ is $A$-regular and the $\kappa_A$-vector space $\operatorname{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A/x)$ is of dimension 1.

The equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iii) follows from Lemma 2 and Prop. 10. If $\operatorname{Ext}^i_\Lambda(\kappa_A, A)$ is zero for every integer $i < d$, the ring $A$ is Macaulay (§ 2, No. 3, Prop. 3); if the ring $A$ is Macaulay, the sequence $x$ is $A$-regular (*loc. cit.*, Th. 1); if the sequence $x$ is $A$-regular, one has $\operatorname{Ext}^i_\Lambda(\kappa_A, A) = 0$ for $i < d$ and the $\kappa_A$-vector spaces $\operatorname{Ext}^d_\Lambda(\kappa_A, A)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A/x)$ are isomorphic (A, X, p. 166, Prop. 9). This proves the equivalence of conditions (iv), (v) and (vi).

Let us prove that (i) implies (v): if $A$ is a Gorenstein ring, it is a Macaulay ring (prop. 10). The sequence $x$ is then $A$-regular (§ 2, No. 3, prop. 4), hence $A/x$ is an artinian Gorenstein ring (example 2), so that the $\kappa_A$-vector space $\operatorname{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A/x)$ is of dimension 1.

Lastly let us prove that (vi) implies (i): under assumption (vi), the ring $A/x$ is a Gorenstein ring (lemma 1), hence $A$ is a Gorenstein ring (example 3).

#### Corollary {#ac-x-s3-n7-cor-1 .statement}

*Let $\Lambda$ be a noetherian local ring of dimension d. The $\Lambda$-module $\operatorname{Ext}^d_\Lambda(\kappa_A, A)$ is not zero.*

This follows from th. 2 if $\Lambda$ is a Gorenstein ring and from lemma 2 otherwise.

#### Proposition 11 {#ac-x-s3-prop-11 .statement}

*Let $A$ be a noetherian ring. The following conditions are equivalent:*

(i) $A$ is a Gorenstein ring ;
(ii) for every prime ideal $p$ of $A$, the $\kappa(p)$-vector space $\operatorname{Ext}^i_{A_p}(\kappa(p), A_p)$ is zero for $i \neq \operatorname{ht}(p)$ and of dimension 1 for $i = \operatorname{ht}(p)$.
(iii) for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$, there exists an integer $i > \operatorname{ht}(m)$ such that $\operatorname{Ext}^i_{A_m}(A/m, A_m) = 0$.

(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) : this follows from th. 2 applied to the local Gorenstein ring $A_p$ (example 1).
(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) : this is trivial.
(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) : under assumption (iii), $A_m$ is a Gorenstein ring for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$ (th. 2), and $A$ is Gorenstein.

### 8. Gorenstein Rings and Flat Algebras

#### Proposition 12 {#ac-x-s3-prop-12 .statement}

Let $\rho : A \to B$ be a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings, making $B$ into a flat $A$-module. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) $B$ is a Gorenstein ring ;
(ii) $A$ and $\kappa_A \otimes_A B$ are Gorenstein rings.

Let us first treat the case where the rings $A$ and $B$ are artinian. Let $C$ denote the local ring $\kappa_A \otimes_A B$; its residue field $\kappa_C$ identifies with $\kappa_B$. Since $B$ is flat over $A$, the $B$-module $\mathrm{Hom}_B(C, B)$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A) \otimes_A B$ (I, § 2, No. 10, prop. 11), hence to $\mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A) \otimes_{\kappa_A} C$. We deduce a sequence of isomorphisms

$$
\mathrm{Hom}_B(\kappa_B, B) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_C(\kappa_C, \mathrm{Hom}_B(C, B)) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_C(\kappa_C, \mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A) \otimes_{\kappa_A} C)
$$
$$
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A) \otimes_{\kappa_A} \mathrm{Hom}_C(\kappa_C, C) .
$$

In particular, one has $[\mathrm{Hom}_B(\kappa_B, B) : \kappa_B] = [\mathrm{Hom}_A(\kappa_A, A) : \kappa_A] \ [\mathrm{Hom}_C(\kappa_C, C) : \kappa_C]$
and the proposition then follows from lemma 1 of No. 7.

Let us pass to the general case. If in the statement one replaces the word "Gorenstein" by the word "Macaulay", the proposition is a particular case of Prop. 10 of § 2, No. 7. We may therefore suppose that the rings $A$, $B$ and $C = \kappa_A \otimes_A B$ are Macaulay. The $B$-module $C$ is Macaulay (§ 2, No. 1, Example 4). Put $r = \dim(A)$, $s = \dim(C)$. There exists an $A$-regular sequence $(x_1, \ldots, x_r)$ of elements of $m_A$ and a sequence $(y_1, \ldots, y_s)$ of elements of $m_B$ which is regular for the $B$-module $C$ (§ 2, No. 3, Prop. 3); let $x$ denote the ideal of $A$ and $\mathfrak{y}$ the ideal of $B$ which they generate respectively. The sequence $(y_1, \ldots, y_s, \rho(x_1), \ldots, \rho(x_r))$ is $B$-regular (§ 1, No. 6, Prop. 11), and the $A$-module $B/\mathfrak{y}$ is flat (*loc. cit.*, Prop. 10). The homomorphism of $A/x$ into $B/(xB + \mathfrak{y})$ deduced from $\rho$ by passing to quotients therefore makes $B/(xB + \mathfrak{y})$ into a flat $(A/x)$-module, and the ring $\kappa_{A/x} \otimes_{A/x} B/(xB + \mathfrak{y})$ is isomorphic to $C/\mathfrak{y}C$. The proposition thus follows from the first part of the proof, taking account of Example 3 of No. 7.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-x-s3-prop-12-cor-1 .statement}

Let $\rho : A \to B$ be a homomorphism of noetherian rings making $B$ into a flat $A$-module. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) $B$ is a Gorenstein ring;
(ii) (resp. (iii)) for every prime ideal (resp. maximal ideal) $q$ of $B$, the rings $A_{\rho^{-1}(q)}$ and $\kappa(\rho^{-1}(q)) \otimes_A B$ are Gorenstein.

If moreover $B$ is a faithfully flat $A$-module, these conditions imply that $A$ is a Gorenstein ring.

Let $q$ be a prime ideal of $B$; put $p = \rho^{-1}(q)$. The ring $B_q$, isomorphic to a ring of fractions of $B_p$, is flat over $A_p$. In order that $B_q$ be a Gorenstein ring, it is necessary and sufficient that the rings $A_p$ and $\kappa(p) \otimes_{A_p} B_q$ should be so (Prop. 12).

(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii): let $q$ be a prime ideal of $B$ and $p = \rho^{-1}(q)$. If $B$ is a Gorenstein ring, the same is true of $B_q$, hence of $A_p$ and $\kappa(p) \otimes_{A_p} B_q$ by what precedes. By the remark of § 2, No. 6, the local ring of $\kappa(p) \otimes_A B$ at any prime ideal whatever is then a Gorenstein ring, which implies that $\kappa(p) \otimes_A B$ is a Gorenstein ring, whence (ii).

(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii): this is clear.

(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i): for every maximal ideal $n$ of $B$, it follows from the beginning of the proof (applied with $q = n$) that $B_n$ is a Gorenstein ring, whence (i).

If $B$ is a faithfully flat $A$-module, the mapping $^a\rho : \mathrm{Spec}(B) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Spec}(\Lambda)$ is surjective (II, § 2, No. 5, cor. 4 of prop. 11), whence the last assertion.

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-x-s3-prop-12-cor-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $J$ an ideal of $A$, $\hat{A}$ the separated completion of $A$ for the $J$-adic topology. Consider the following conditions:

(i) $A$ is a Gorenstein ring;
(ii) $\hat{A}$ is a Gorenstein ring;
(iii) for every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$ containing $J$, $A_m$ is a Gorenstein ring;
(iv) for every prime ideal $p$ of $A$ such that $p + J \neq A$, $A_p$ and $\kappa(p) \otimes_A \hat{A}$ are Gorenstein rings.

Conditions (ii) to (iv) are equivalent, and are implied by (i). When the ideal $J$ is contained in the radical of $A$, conditions (i) to (iv) are equivalent.

This corollary is deduced from cor. 1 in the same way as corollary 4 of prop. 9 of § 2, No. 7, was deduced: it is enough in the proof to replace the word "Macaulay" by the word "Gorenstein".

#### Corollary 3 {#ac-x-s3-prop-12-cor-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a Gorenstein ring and $(T_i)_{i \in I}$ a finite family of indeterminates. The rings $A[(T_i)_{i \in I}]$ and $A[[T_i]_{i \in I}]$ are Gorenstein rings.

For every field $k$, the ring $k[T]$ is Gorenstein (No. 7, example 5); moreover the ring $A[T]$ is noetherian. It is therefore a Gorenstein ring (cor. 1). It follows by induction on $\mathrm{Card}(I)$ that $A[(T_i)_{i \in I}]$ is a Gorenstein ring; then cor. 2 is applied.
