## § 3. DIMENSION OF NOETHERIAN RINGS

### 1. Dimension of a quotient ring

#### Proposition 1 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian integral domain, $x$ a nonzero element of $A$ and $p$ an element minimal in the set of prime ideals of $A$ containing $x$. Then $p$ is of height 1.

Let $q \subset p$ be a prime ideal distinct from $p$. One has $x \notin q$ in view of the minimal character of $p$. Since $A$ is an integral domain, $A_p$ can be identified with a subring of $A_q$; for every integer $n \geq 0$, let $q_n$ denote the ideal $q^n A_q \cap A_p$ of $A_p$. The minimal character of $p$ means that the local ring $A_p / xA_p$ is of dimension 0; hence it is of finite length ($\S 1$, no 3, example 1), and there exists an integer $n_0 \geq 0$ such that one has

$$
q_n + xA_p = q_{n+1} + xA_p \quad \text{for every } n \geq n_0 .
$$

Fix the integer $n \geq n_0$. Given $y \in q_n$, there exists $a \in A_p$ such that $y - ax \in q_{n+1}$; one then has $ax \in q_n$, whence $a \in q_n$ since $x \notin q$, and finally one has $y \in q_{n+1} + xq_n$. Hence one has

$$
q_n = q_{n+1} + xq_n .
$$

Since $x$ belongs to the maximal ideal of the noetherian local ring $A_p$, Nakayama's lemma shows that one has $q_n = q_{n+1}$. Since one has $(qA_q)^n = q_n A_q$, one concludes

$$
(qA_q)^n = (qA_q)^{n+1} \quad \text{for every } n \geq n_0 .
$$

Since the ring $A_q$ is local and noetherian, one has $\bigcap_{n \geq 0} (qA_q)^n = \{0\}$ (III, § 3, no 2, corollary of the prop. 5) whence $(qA_q)^{n_0} = \{0\}$ and finally the prime ideal $qA_q$ of $A_q$ is reduced to 0. Hence $q = \{0\}$, which proves that $p$ is of height 1.

#### Proposition 2 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $m$ a positive integer and $a$ an ideal contained in the radical of $A$ and generated by $m$ elements. One has

$$
\dim(A/a) \leq \dim(A) \leq \dim(A/a) + m .
$$

The inequality $\dim(A/a) \leq \dim(A)$ follows from prop. 6 of § 1, no 3. An immediate induction on $m$ shows that it is enough to establish the inequality

$$
\dim(A) \leq \dim(A/xA) + 1
$$

for every element $x$ of the radical of $A$, that is to say, to prove that one has $\dim(A/xA) \geq n - 1$ for every chain $p_0 \subset ... \subset p_n$ of prime ideals of $A$, of length $n \geqslant 1$, and such that $x \in p_n$. It suffices to construct a chain $q_1 \subset ... \subset q_n$ of prime ideals of $A$, with $x \in q_1$ and this follows from the following lemma:

#### Lemma 1 {#ac-viii-s3-lem-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $p_0 \subset ... \subset p_n$ a chain of prime ideals of $A$ of length $n \geqslant 1$, and $x$ an element of $p_n$. There exists a chain $p'_0 \subset ... \subset p'_n$ with $p'_0 = p_0$, $p'_n = p_n$ and $x \in p'_1$.

We reason by induction on $n$, the case $n = 1$ being trivial. Suppose therefore that one has $n \geqslant 2$ and that $x$ does not belong to $p_{n-1}$. Let $p'_{n-1}$ be a minimal element of the set of prime ideals of $A$ contained in $p'_n = p_n$ and containing $p_{n-2} + Ax$ (II, § 2, No. 6, lemma 2). By prop. 1, the ideal $p'_{n-1}/p_{n-2}$ of the ring $A/p_{n-2}$ is of height 1, and since $p_{n-2} \subset p_{n-1} \subset p_n$ is a chain of length 2, the same is true of $p_{n-2} \subset p'_{n-1} \subset p'_n$. One has $x \in p'_{n-1}$. The induction hypothesis applied to the chain $p_0 \subset p_1 \subset ... \subset p_{n-2} \subset p'_{n-1}$ shows that there exists a chain $p'_0 \subset p'_1 \subset ... \subset p'_{n-2} \subset p'_{n-1}$ with $x \in p'_1$ and $p'_0 = p_0$. The chain
$$
p'_0 \subset p'_1 \subset ... \subset p'_{n-1} \subset p'_n
$$
satisfies the required conditions.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s3-lem-1-cor-1 .statement}

a) Every noetherian local ring is finite-dimensional. More generally, every nonzero noetherian semi-local ring (II, § 3, No. 5, def. 4) is finite-dimensional.
b) Let $A$ be a noetherian ring. Every ideal of $A$, distinct from $A$, is of finite height.
c) Every decreasing sequence of prime ideals of a noetherian ring $A$ is stationary.

a) Let $A$ be a nonzero semi-local noetherian ring and $a$ its radical; the quotient ring $A/a$ is artinian and nonzero, hence of dimension 0 ($§ 1$, No. 3, Example 1). There exists an integer $m \geqslant 0$ such that the ideal $a$ of $A$ is generated by $m$ elements; hence $0 \leqslant \dim(A) \leqslant m$ by Prop. 2.
b) Let $a \neq A$ be an ideal of $A$, and let $m$ be a maximal ideal of $A$ containing $a$. We have $0 \leqslant \mathrm{ht}(a) \leqslant \dim(A_m)$ by Prop. 7 of $§ 1$, No. 3, and $A_m$ is a local noetherian ring. Hence $\mathrm{ht}(a)$ is finite by a).
c) Every finite strictly decreasing sequence $(p_i)_{0 \leqslant i \leqslant n}$ of prime ideals of $A$ defines a chain $p_n \subset ... \subset p_0$, whence $n \leqslant \dim(A_{p_0}) < + \infty$. Therefore there cannot exist an infinite strictly decreasing sequence of prime ideals of $A$, whence c).

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s3-lem-1-cor-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a local noetherian ring.
a) Let $x \in m_A$. Then $\dim(A/xA)$ is equal to $\dim(A)$ or to $\dim(A) - 1$. In order that one have $\dim(A/xA) = \dim(A) - 1$, it is necessary and sufficient that $x$ belong to none of the minimal prime ideals $p$ of $A$ such that $\dim(A/p) = \dim(A)$, and it suffices that $x$ be not a zero divisor in $A$.
b) Let $a$ be an ideal of $A$ distinct from $A$ such that $\dim(A/a) < \dim(A)$. There exists $x \in a$ such that $\dim(A/xA) = \dim(A) - 1$.
c) If $\dim(A) \geqslant 1$, there exists $x \in m_A$ such that $\dim(A/xA) = \dim(A) - 1$.
By Prop. 2, $\dim(A/xA)$ is equal to $\dim(A)$ or to $\dim(A) - 1$. In order that one have $\dim(A/xA) = \dim(A)$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a chain $p_0 \subset ... \subset p_n$ of prime ideals of $A$ such that $x \in p_0$ and $n = \dim(A)$, that is to say, that there exist a prime ideal $p_0$ of $A$ containing $x$ such that $\dim(A/p_0) = \dim(A)$. But such a prime ideal $p_0$ is necessarily minimal, and every element of $p_0$ is therefore a zero divisor in $A$ (IV, § 1, No. 1, Cor. 3 of Prop. 2 and No. 4, Th. 2). This proves a).

Let $\Phi$ be the set of minimal prime ideals of $A$, and $\Phi'$ the set of $p \in \Phi$ such that $\dim(A/p) = \dim(A)$. We know (II, § 4, No. 3, Corollary 3 of Proposition 14) that $\Phi$ is finite, hence $\Phi'$ is finite. Let $a$ be an ideal of $A$ such that $\dim(A/a) < \dim(A)$. For every $p \in \Phi'$, one has $\dim(A/a) < \dim(A/p)$, hence $a \not\subset p$. By Proposition 2 of II, § 1, No. 1, there exists therefore an element $x$ of $a$ which does not belong to any of the $p \in \Phi'$, and one then has $\dim(A/xA) = \dim(A) - 1$ by a). This proves b).

The assertion c) is the particular case $a = m_A$ of b).

### 2. Dimension and secant sequences

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module, $S$ a subset of the radical of $A$, $\mathfrak{S}$ the ideal of $A$ generated by $S$ and $a$ the annihilator of $M$. One has

(6)
$$
\dim_A(M) = \dim(A/a) \leq \dim(A) ;
$$
hence, if $A$ is local, one has $\dim_A(M) < + \infty$. On the other hand, the support of the $A$-module $M/SM$ is equal to $V(a + \mathfrak{S})$ by the corollary of Proposition 18 of II, § 4, No. 4, whence

(7)
$$
\dim_A(M/SM) = \dim(A/(a + \mathfrak{S})) .
$$

When $S$ is finite, or when $M$ is not reduced to 0, one has the inequality

(8)
$$
\dim_A(M/SM) \leq \dim_A(M) \leq \mathrm{Card}(S) + \dim_A(M/SM) ;
$$
when $S$ is finite, this follows from Proposition 2 of No. 1 and formulas (6) and (7) above, and the case where $S$ is infinite is trivial.

#### Definition 1 {#ac-viii-s3-def-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, $M$ a nonzero finitely generated $A$-module and $S$ a subset of the maximal ideal $m_A$ of $A$. One says that $S$ is secant for $M$ if one has

(9)
$$
\dim_A(M) = \mathrm{Card}(S) + \dim_A(M/SM) .
$$

If $S$ is secant for $M$, one has $\mathrm{Card}(S) \leq \dim_A(M)$, hence $S$ is finite. One says that a family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ of elements of $m_A$ is secant for $M$ if one has

(10)
$$
\dim_A(M) = \mathrm{Card}(I) + \dim_A(M/\sum_{i \in I} x_i M) ,
$$
that is to say if $i \mapsto x_i$ is a bijection of $I$ onto a secant subset of $m_A$ for $M$.

An element x of $m_A$ is said to be secant for M if $\{ x \}$ is a secant subset for M, that is to say if one has
$$
\dim_A(M/xM) = \dim_A(M) - 1 .
$$

#### Remark 1 {#ac-viii-s3-n2-rem-1 .statement}

It follows from formulas (6) and (7) that S is secant for M if and only if it is secant for $A/\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the annihilator of M.
2) Let S and S’ be two disjoint subsets of $m_A$. For $S \cup S'$ to be secant for M, it is necessary and sufficient that S be secant for M and S’ secant for $M' = M/SM$. This follows from inequality (8) and the formula
$$
\text{Card}(S \cup S') + \dim_A(M/(SM + S'M)) - \dim_A(M) =
= (\text{Card}(S) + \dim_A(M/SM) - \dim_A(M)) +
+ (\text{Card}(S') + \dim_A(M'/S'M') - \dim_A(M')) .
$$
3) Let $x \in m_A$ and $n \geq 1$ be an integer. It is immediate that the modules $M/xM$ and $M/x^nM$ have the same support, hence the same dimension. Consequently, x is secant for M if and only if $x^n$ is secant for M. From this and Remark 2, one deduces at once the following result: let $x_1, ..., x_r$ be elements of $m_A$ and $n_1, ..., n_r$ integers $> 0$; then the sequence $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ is secant for M if and only if the sequence $(x_1^{n_1}, ..., x_r^{n_r})$ is secant for M.

#### Proposition 3 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-3 .statement}

Let A be a noetherian local ring and M a nonzero finite a-module. For an element x of $m_A$ to be secant for M, it is necessary and sufficient that it belong to none of the minimal elements $p$ of $\mathrm{Supp}(M)$ such that $\dim(A/p) = \dim_A(M)$, and it is sufficient that the homothety $x_M$ of ratio x in M be injective.
Let $\alpha$ be the annihilator of M. To say that x is secant for M means that x is secant for $A/\alpha$; the support of M consists of the prime ideals $p$ of A such that $\alpha \subset p$; and if $x_M$ is injective, the image of x in $A/\alpha$ is not a divisor of 0 in $A/\alpha$. Proposition 3 then follows from Corollary 2 of Proposition 2 of No. 1 applied to the ring $A/\alpha$.

#### Corollary {#ac-viii-s3-n2-cor-1 .statement}

Every sequence of elements of $m_A$ which is completely secant for M (A, X, p. 157, Definition 2) is secant for M.
Let $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ be a sequence of elements of $m_A$ which is completely secant for M. Put $M_0 = M$ and by induction $M_i = M_{i-1}/x_i M_{i-1}$ for $1 \leq i \leq r$. By Corollary 1 of A, X, p. 160, the homothety of ratio $x_i$ in $M_{i-1}$ is injective, whence $\dim_A(M_i) = \dim_A(M_{i-1}) - 1$ (for $1 \leq i \leq r$) (Proposition 3). Therefore one has
$$
\dim_A(M) = r + \dim_A(M/(x_1M + \cdots + x_rM)) ,
$$
hence the sequence $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ is secant for M.

#### Remark 4 {#ac-viii-s3-n2-rem-4 .statement}

It is not true in general that a sequence secant for M is completely secant for M (p. 87, Exercise 6). We shall study later the modules over a noetherian local ring for which every secant sequence is completely secant.

#### Theorem 1 {#ac-viii-s3-thm-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, $M$ a nonzero finite $A$-module and $S$ a subset of the maximal ideal $m_A$ of $A$.

a) If $M/SM$ is of finite length, one has $\mathrm{Card}(S) \geqslant \dim_A(M)$; if $S$ is secant for $M$, one has $\mathrm{Card}(S) \leqslant \dim_A(M)$.

b) Every secant subset for $M$ is contained in a maximal secant subset for $M$.

c) The following properties are equivalent:
(i) $S$ is a maximal secant subset for $M$;
(ii) $S$ is a secant subset for $M$ and $\mathrm{Card}(S) = \dim_A(M)$;
(iii) $M/SM$ is of finite length and $\mathrm{Card}(S) = \dim_A(M)$;
(iv) $S$ is a secant subset for $M$ and $M/SM$ is of finite length.

Since one has $S \subset m_A$, Nakayama's lemma shows that one has $M/SM \neq \{0\}$, whence $\dim_A(M/SM) \geqslant 0$ with equality if and only if $M/SM$ is of finite length. Assertion a) then results from formulas (8) and (9), as well as the equivalence of properties (ii), (iii) and (iv).

Assertion b) results from the fact that the cardinal of every subset of $m_A$ secant for $M$ is bounded above by the integer $\dim_A(M)$.

By a), every secant subset for $M$, of cardinal equal to $\dim_A(M)$, is maximal. It remains to prove that, if $S$ is secant for $M$ and if $\mathrm{Card}(S) < \dim_A(M)$, then $S$ is not maximal. Let $a$ be the annihilator of $M$, and $B$ the noetherian local ring $A/(a + SA)$. By cor. 2 of prop. 2 of No. 1, there exists an element $x$ of $m_A$ such that $\dim(B/xB) = \dim(B) - 1$ whence $x \notin S$. By remark 2, the subset $S \cup \{x\}$ of $m_A$ is secant for $A/a$, hence for $M$ by remark 1.

#### Corollary {#ac-viii-s3-n2-cor-2 .statement}

The dimension of $M$ is the smallest of the integers $d \geqslant 0$ for which there exists a sequence $(x_1, ..., x_d)$ of elements of $m_A$ such that the $A$-module $M / \sum_{i=1}^d x_i M$ is of finite length.

Since $\varnothing$ is a secant subset for $M$, Theorem 1, b) proves the existence of a maximal secant sequence for $M$, say $(x_1, ..., x_d)$. But then one has $d = \dim_A(M)$ and the $A$-module $M / \sum_{i=1}^d x_i M$ is of finite length by property (iii) of Theorem 1, c). Conversely, if $(x'_1, ..., x'_{d'})$ is a sequence of elements of $m_A$ such that the $A$-module $M / \sum_{j=1}^{d'} x'_j M$ is of finite length, one has $d' \geqslant \dim_A(M)$ by Theorem 1, a).

Recall (III, § 3, No. 2, Definition 1) that an ideal $q$ of a noetherian local ring $A$ is an ideal of definition of $A$ if the $q$-adic and $m_A$-adic topologies of $A$ coincide.

#### Lemma 2 {#ac-viii-s3-lem-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring and $q$ an ideal of $A$. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) $q$ is an ideal of definition of $A$;
(ii) there exists an integer $n \geqslant 0$ such that $m_A^n \subset q \subset m_A$;

(iii) one has q ≠ A and A/q is an A-module of finite length ;
(iv) V(q) is equal to { m_A } (in other words, m_A is the only prime ideal of A containing q).

In fact, the equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iv) was proved in III, § 2, No. 5, and the equivalence of (i) and (iii) follows from IV, § 2, No. 5, Corollary 2 to Proposition 9.

The corollary to Theorem 1 therefore makes it possible to state the following scholium:

#### Scholium {#ac-viii-s3-n2-sch-1 .statement}

The dimension of a noetherian local ring A is the smallest of the integers d ≥ 0 for which there exists an ideal of definition of A generated by d elements.

### 3. First applications

Let A be a noetherian ring, V = Spec(A) its spectrum. In this number, every subset of the form $^1 V(x)$ with $x \in A$ is called a hypersurface in V.

#### Proposition 4 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-4 .statement}

Let X be a closed subset of V, and $H_1, ..., H_m$ hypersurfaces in V. Put $X' = X \cap H_1 \cap ... \cap H_m$.

a) For every closed subset Y of V contained in X', one has

$$
\operatorname{codim}(Y, X') \geq \operatorname{codim}(Y, X) - m .
$$

b) One has $\operatorname{codim}(Z, X) \leq m$ for every irreducible component Z of X'. If X' is nonempty, one has $\operatorname{codim}(X', X) \leq m$.

c) If Z is an irreducible closed subset of V contained in X such that $\operatorname{codim}(Z, X) \leq m$, there exist hypersurfaces $H'_1, ..., H'_m$ such that Z is an irreducible component of $X \cap H'_1 \cap ... \cap H'_m$.

Let α be an ideal of A and $x_1, ..., x_m$ elements of A such that $X = V(\alpha)$ and $H_i = V(x_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq m$. Let Z be an irreducible closed subset of V contained in X; there exists a prime ideal p of A containing α and such that $Z = V(p)$.

Suppose first that Z is contained in X' and denote by $\xi_i$ the image of $x_i$ in the noetherian local ring $B = A_p/\alpha A_p$. By Proposition 7, b) of § 1, No. 3, one has

$$
\operatorname{codim}(Z, X) = \dim(B), \quad \operatorname{codim}(Z, X') = \dim(B/(\xi_1 B + \cdots + \xi_m B)) .
$$

By Proposition 2 of No. 1, one therefore has

(11)
$$
\operatorname{codim}(Z, X') \geq \operatorname{codim}(Z, X) - m .
$$

If Z is an irreducible component of X', one has $\operatorname{codim}(Z, X') = 0$, whence $\operatorname{codim}(Z, X) \leq m$; this proves b). One proves a) by taking in both members of (11) the greatest lower bound over the set of irreducible components Z of Y.

1 Recall that $V(x)$ consists of the prime ideals of A containing x.

Conversely, suppose that one has codim(Z, X) $\leq m$, that is to say dim(B) $\leq m$. Since every element of $A_p$ is the product of an invertible element of $A_p$ by the image of an element of A, the scholium of No. 2 proves the existence of elements $x'_1, ..., x'_m$ of A whose images in B generate an ideal of definition of B. Put $H'_i = V(x'_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq m$. It is clear that Z is an irreducible component of $X \cap H'_1 \cap ... \cap H'_m$.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-4-cor-1 .statement}

Let H be a nonempty hypersurface in V. The codimension of H in V is equal to 0 or 1.

One has codim(H, V) = 1 if and only if H contains no irreducible component of V. If this is so, all the irreducible components of H are of codimension 1 in V.

For every irreducible component Z of H, one has

$$
0 \leq \operatorname{codim}(Z, V) \leq 1
$$

by Proposition 4, b), and one has codim(Z, V) = 0 if and only if Z is an irreducible component of V. One has by definition

$$
\operatorname{codim}(H, V) = \inf_Z \operatorname{codim}(Z, V)
$$

where Z ranges over the set of irreducible components of H. Corollary 1 follows at once from these remarks.

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-4-cor-2 .statement}

Let X be a closed irreducible subset of V and H a hypersurface in V. Only three cases are possible:

1) one has $X \subset H$;
2) the set $X \cap H$ is nonempty and each of its irreducible components Z satisfies $\operatorname{codim}(Z, X) = 1$;
3) the set $X \cap H$ is empty.

Suppose $X' = X \cap H$ nonempty and distinct from X; every irreducible component Z of $X'$ is distinct from X and satisfies $\operatorname{codim}(Z, X) \leq 1$ by Prop. 4, b). Corollary 2 follows at once from this.

#### Corollary 3 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-4-cor-3 .statement}

If A is factorial (VII, § 3, No. 3, Prop. 2), the prime ideals of height 1 of A are the principal ideals generated by the extremal elements of A. If moreover A is local, one has $\dim(A/p) = \dim(A) - 1$ for every prime ideal $p$ of height 1 of A.

Let x be an extremal element of A. Then Ax is a prime ideal because x is extremal, of height 1 because A is an integral domain (No. 1, Prop. 1). Let p be a prime ideal of height 1 of A. Then $V(p)$ is an irreducible component of a hypersurface $V(Ax)$ for a suitable x (Prop. 4, c)). Let $x = \prod_i y_i^{n_i}$ be a decomposition of x into a product of extremal elements such that $y_i$ and $y_j$ are relatively prime if $i \neq j$. The irreducible components of $V(Ax)$ are the $V(Ay_i)$. Hence $p = Ay_i$ for a suitable i. The last assertion follows from Corollary 2 to Prop. 2 of No. 1.

#### Remark 1 {#ac-viii-s3-n3-rem-1 .statement}

Suppose that A is a local noetherian integral domain of dimension d; let x be a nonzero element of m_A and let H = V(x). By Corollary 2 of Prop. 4, every irreducible component of H is of codimension 1 in X, hence of dimension $\leq d - 1$ ($\S 1$, No. 2, Prop. 3). By Corollary 2 of Prop. 2 of No. 1, H is of dimension $d - 1$, and one of these components is therefore of dimension $d - 1$; all are so if A is catenary. However, in general it may happen that there exists an irreducible component of H of dimension < $d - 1$ (cf. p. 87, Exerc. 7).

#### Remark 2 {#ac-viii-s3-n3-rem-2 .statement}

Let $x_1, ..., x_n$ be elements of A, and put $H_i = V(x_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$. Suppose there exists a finite-type A-module M with support $V = \mathrm{Spec}(A)$, such that $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ is a completely secant sequence for M. Then every irreducible component of $H_1 \cap ... \cap H_n$ is of codimension n in V: this follows easily from the corollary to Prop. 3 of No. 2.

#### Remark 3 {#ac-viii-s3-n3-rem-3 .statement}

If the ideal $\alpha$ of the noetherian ring A is generated by m elements, one has $\mathrm{ht}(\alpha) \leq m$. This follows at once from Prop. 4.

#### Proposition 5 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-5 .statement}

Let A be a noetherian ring and $p \subset q$ a non-saturated chain of prime ideals of A. The set E of prime ideals r of A such that $p \subset r \subset q$ is a chain is infinite. One has $\bigcup_{r \in E} r = q$ and $\bigcap_{r \in E} r = p$.

Replacing A by $A/p$ if necessary, one is reduced to the case where $p = \{0\}$.

By Lemma 1 of No. 1, one has $q = \bigcup_{r \in E} r$, and Prop. 2 of II, $\S 1$, No. 1 shows that E is infinite.

Let $y \neq 0$ be an element of $\bigcap_{r \in E} r$. The height of q is finite (No. 1, Cor. 1 of Prop. 2), and one has $\mathrm{ht}(q) \geq 2$ by hypothesis. There therefore exists a prime ideal $q' \subset q$ of height 2. The first part of the proof applied to $q'$ shows that the set E’ of prime ideals of height 1 contained in $q'$ is infinite; each of these ideals contains y by hypothesis. Now the local noetherian ring $B = A_{q'}/yA_{q'}$ is of dimension 1 by Corollary 2 of Prop. 2 of No. 1. For every $r \in E'$, the prime ideal $r/yA_{q'}$ of B is therefore minimal; consequently, the noetherian ring B has an infinity of minimal prime ideals, which is absurd (II, $\S 4$, No. 3, Cor. 3 of Prop. 14). Hence $\bigcap_{r \in E} r = \{0\}$.

#### Proposition 6 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-6 .statement}

Let A be a noetherian ring of dimension $\geq 2$, and h an integer such that $0 < h < \dim(A)$.

a) A possesses an infinity of prime ideals of height h.

b) If A is finite-dimensional, it possesses an infinity of prime ideals p such that $\mathrm{ht}(p) = h$ and $\dim(A/p) = \dim(A) - h$.

Since the dimension of A is the least upper bound of the heights of the prime ideals of A ($\S 1$, no. 3, prop. 8), since every prime ideal of A is of finite height (no. 1, cor. 1 of prop. 2), and since $h < \dim(A)$, there exists an integer $n > h$ and a prime ideal p of height n, hence a chain $p_0 \subset ... \subset p_n = p$ of prime ideals of length n. One has $\mathrm{ht}(p_i) = i$ for $0 \leq i \leq n$, whence $\mathrm{ht}(r) = h$ for every prime ideal r of A such that $p_{h-1} \subset r \subset p_{h+1}$ is a chain. The set E of these ideals is infinite by prop. 5, whence a).

If $A$ is of finite dimension, one may suppose that one has $n = \dim(A)$ in the foregoing. For every ideal $r \in E$, one has $\mathrm{ht}(r) = h$, whence $\dim(A/r) \leq n - h$, and since $r \subset p_{h+1} \subset ... \subset p_n$ is a chain of length $n - h$, one has $\dim(A/r) = n - h$.

There exist non-noetherian integral rings of dimension 2 possessing only one prime ideal of height 1, for example a valuation ring of height 2 (VI, § 4, no. 4, prop. 5).

### 4. Change of rings

#### Proposition 7 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-7 .statement}

Let $\rho : A \to B$ be a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module and $N$ a finitely generated $B$-module. Put $\overline{B} = B \otimes_A \kappa_A = B/\rho(m_A).B$ and $\overline{N} = N \otimes_B \overline{B} = N/\rho(m_A).N$. One has

$$
\dim_B(M \otimes_A N) \leq \dim_A(M) + \dim_B(\overline{N}),
$$

and there is equality if $N$ is flat over $A$.

One may suppose $M$ and $N$ non-zero. By the corollary of th. 1 (no. 2), there exists a subset $S$ (resp. $T$) of $m_A$ (resp. $m_B$) such that $\mathrm{Card}(S) = \dim_A(M)$ (resp. $\mathrm{Card}(T) = \dim_{\overline{B}}(\overline{N})$) and that $M/SM$ is an $A$-module of finite length (resp. $\overline{N}/T\overline{N}$ is a $B$-module of finite length). One has $\rho(S) \subset m_B$. Let $E$ be the $B$-module $M \otimes_A N$; the $B$-module $E/(\rho(S).E + T.E)$ is isomorphic to $M/SM \otimes_A N/TN$, hence is of finite length: by props. 18 and 19 of II, § 4, no. 4, one has

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Supp}(M/SM \otimes_A N/TN) &= \mathrm{Supp}(N/TN) \cap {}^a\rho^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}(M/SM)) \\
&= \mathrm{Supp}(N/TN) \cap {}^a\rho^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}(\kappa_A)) \\
&= \mathrm{Supp}(N/TN \otimes_A \kappa_A) = \mathrm{Supp}(\overline{N}/T\overline{N}) = \{m_B\}.
\end{align*}
$$

It follows, by th. 1, that one has the inequality

$$
\dim_B(E) \leq \mathrm{Card}(S) + \mathrm{Card}(T) = \dim_A(M) + \dim_{\overline{B}}(\overline{N}).
$$

Now suppose $N$ flat over $A$, and let us show that there is equality in formula (12). Let $a$ (resp. $b$) be the annihilator of $M$ (resp. $N$). By props. 18 and 19 of II, § 4, no. 4, one has

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Supp}(E) &= \mathrm{Supp}(N) \cap {}^a\rho^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}(M)) \\
&= V(b) \cap {}^a\rho^{-1}(V(a)) = V(b + \rho(a).B).
\end{align*}
$$

Hence

$$
\dim_B(M \otimes_A N) = \dim(B/(b + \rho(a).B))
$$

and also

$$
\dim_A(M) + \dim_{\overline{B}}(\overline{N}) = \dim(A/a) + \dim(B/(b + \rho(m_A).B)).
$$

$$
\dim(B') \geq \dim(A') + \dim(B'/\rho'(\mathfrak{m}_{A'}).B')
$$

and since the ring $B'/\rho'(\mathfrak{m}_{A'}).B'$ is isomorphic to $B/(b + \rho(\mathfrak{m}_A).B)$, our assertion follows from formulas (13), (14) and (15).

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-7-cor-1 .statement}

*Let $\rho : A \to B$ be a local homomorphism of noetherian local rings.*

*a)* *One has*

$$
\dim(B) \leq \dim(A) + \dim(B \otimes_A \kappa_A)
$$

*with equality if $\rho$ makes $B$ into a flat $A$-module.*

*b)* *Suppose that $\rho$ makes $B$ into a flat $A$-module, and let $S$ be a subset of $\mathfrak{m}_A$. Then $S$ is secant for $A$ if and only if $\rho(S)$ is secant for $B$.*

Assertion *a)* is the particular case $M = A, N = B$ of Prop. 7.
Let us prove *b)*. Under the hypotheses made, we have

$$
\dim(B) = \dim(A) + \dim(\overline{B})
$$

with $\overline{B} = B \otimes_A \kappa_A = B/\rho(\mathfrak{m}_A).B$. Since $\rho$ is injective (I, § 3, No. 5, Prop. 9), we have

$$
\mathrm{Card}(\rho(S)) = \mathrm{Card}(S).
$$

Finally $B' = B/\rho(S).B$ is a flat module over $A' = A/SA$, whence

$$
\dim(B/\rho(S).B) = \dim(A/SA) + \dim(\overline{B})
$$

since $B'/\rho(\mathfrak{m}_{A'}).B'$ is isomorphic to $\overline{B}$. Our assertion follows at once from formulas (17), (18) and (19).

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-7-cor-2 .statement}

*Let $\rho : A \to B$ be a homomorphism of noetherian rings. Then*

$$
\dim(B) \leq \dim(A) + \sup_{p \in \mathrm{Spec}(A)} \dim(B \otimes_A \kappa(p)).
$$

Let $q$ be a prime ideal of $B$ and $p = \rho^{-1}(q)$. By Cor. 1, we have $\dim(B_q) \leq \dim(A_p) + \dim(B_q \otimes_A \kappa(p))$. It follows (Prop. 6, *b*) of § 1, No. 3) that we have the inequality $\dim(B_q) \leq \dim(A) + \dim(B \otimes_A \kappa(p))$, and one concludes by Prop. 8 of § 1, No. 3.

#### Corollary 3 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-7-cor-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring and $n$ an integer $\geqslant 0$. Then we have

$$
\dim(A[X_1, ..., X_n]) = \dim(A) + n .
$$

Put $B = A[X_1, ..., X_n]$. For every prime ideal $p$ of $A$, the ring $B \otimes_A \kappa(p)$ is a polynomial ring in $n$ variables over a field, hence is of dimension $n$ ($\S 2$, No. 4, Cor. 1 to Theorem 3). By Cor. 2, we have $\dim(B) \leqslant \dim(A) + n$; the reverse inequality follows from Example 4 of $\S 1$, No. 3.

#### Corollary 4 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-7-cor-4 .statement}

Let $\rho : A \to B$ be a homomorphism of noetherian rings, and $a$ an ideal of $A$. We have the inequality

$$
\text{ht}(\rho(a).B) \leqslant \text{ht}(a)
$$

if the map $^a\rho : \mathrm{Spec}(B) \to \mathrm{Spec}(A)$ is surjective. If $B$ is a faithfully flat $A$-module, we have $\text{ht}(\rho(a).B) = \text{ht}(a)$.

If $B$ is faithfully flat over $A$, then $^a\rho$ is surjective (II, $\S 2$, No. 5, Cor. 4 to Prop. 11) and we have $\text{ht}(a) \leqslant \text{ht}(\rho(a).B)$ ($\S 2$, No. 1, corollary of Prop. 2).

It remains therefore to prove inequality (20) under the assumption that $^a\rho$ is surjective. Let $p$ be a prime ideal of $A$ such that $a \subset p$ and $\text{ht}(a) = \text{ht}(p)$ ($\S 1$, no 3, prop. 7). Put $\overline{B} = B \otimes_A \kappa(p)$ and denote by $h$ the canonical homomorphism of $B$ into $\overline{B}$. If $X = {}^a\rho^{-1}(p)$ is the nonempty set of prime ideals of $B$ lying over $p$, it is known ($\S 2$, no 1, lemme 1) that the mapping $^ah$ is a homeomorphism of $\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{B})$ onto the subspace $X$ of $\mathrm{Spec}(B)$. Let $q$ be the image under $^ah$ of a minimal prime ideal of $\overline{B}$; one has $\dim(B_q \otimes_A \kappa(p)) = 0$ and Cor. 1 yields the inequality $\dim(B_q) \leqslant \dim(A_p) = \text{ht}(p) = \text{ht}(a)$, finally

$$
\text{ht}(\rho(a).B) \leqslant \text{ht}(q) = \dim(B_q) \leqslant \dim(A_p) = \text{ht}(p) = \text{ht}(a),
$$

whence the corollary.

#### Proposition 8 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-8 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $a$ an ideal of $A$, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module, $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{M}$ the separated completions of $A$ and $M$ respectively for the $a$-adic topology.

a) Let $m$ be a prime ideal of $A$ containing $a$ and $\hat{m} = m\hat{A}$. Then $\hat{m}$ is a prime ideal of $\hat{A}$ and one has $\dim_{\hat{A}_{\hat{m}}}(\hat{M}_{\hat{m}}) = \dim_{A_m}(M_m)$.

b) One has $\dim_{\hat{A}}(\hat{M}) = \sup_m \dim_{A_m}(M_m)$, where $m$ ranges over the set of prime (resp. maximal) ideals of $A$ containing $a$. In particular, one has $\dim_{\hat{A}}(\hat{M}) \leqslant \dim_A(M)$.

a) Since $\hat{A}/\hat{m}$ is identified with $A/m$, $\hat{m}$ is a prime ideal of $\hat{A}$. By Theorem 3 of III, $\S 3$, No. 4, $\hat{A}$ is flat over $A$, hence $\hat{A}_{\hat{m}}$ is flat over $A_m$. Moreover the canonical mapping of $A$ into $\hat{A}$ induces an isomorphism of $A/a$ onto $\hat{A}/a\hat{A}$, hence also an isomorphism of $A_m/mA_m$ onto $\hat{A}_{\hat{m}}/m\hat{A}_{\hat{m}}$. The conclusion follows by applying Proposition 7 to the rings $A_m$ and $\hat{A}_{\hat{m}}$ and to the modules $M_m$ and $\hat{A}_{\hat{m}}$ since $M_m \otimes_{A_m} \hat{A}_{\hat{m}}$ is isomorphic to $\hat{M}_{\hat{m}}$ (III, loc. cit. and Proposition 8).

b) By Proposition 8 of III, $\S 3$, No. 4, the mapping $m \mapsto \hat{m}$ is a bijection of the set of maximal ideals of $A$ containing $a$ onto the set of maximal ideals of $\hat{A}$. Assertion b) follows from this and from Proposition 9 of $\S 1$, No. 4.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-8-cor-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a Zariski ring (III, $\S 3$, No. 3, Definition 2). For every $A$-module $M$ of finite type, one has $\dim_{\hat{A}}(\hat{M}) = \dim_A(M)$.

In fact, the topology of $A$ is the $a$-adic topology, where $a$ is an ideal contained in the radical of $A$ (*loc. cit.*), that is, contained in every maximal ideal $m$ of $A$. It is therefore enough to apply assertion b) of Proposition 8.

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-8-cor-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $a$ an ideal of $A$, and $\hat{A}$ the separated completion of $A$ for the $a$-adic topology. One has $\dim(\hat{A}) \leqslant \dim(A)$, with equality when $A$ is local and $a$ distinct from $A$.

#### Corollary 3 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-8-cor-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring and $n$ an integer $\geqslant 0$. One has

$$
\dim(A[[X_1, ..., X_n]]) = \dim(A) + n .
$$

The ring $A[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ is the separated completion of the polynomial ring $A[X_1, ..., X_n]$ for the $a$-adic topology, where $a$ is the ideal generated by $X_1, ..., X_n$; we therefore have

$$
\dim(A[[X_1, ..., X_n]]) \leqslant \dim(A[X_1, ..., X_n])
$$

by Corollary 2. On the other hand, we have

$$
\dim(A[X_1, ..., X_n]) = \dim(A) + n
$$

by Corollary 3 of Proposition 7. Finally, we have

$$
\dim(A) + n \leqslant \dim(A[[X_1, ..., X_n]])
$$

by Example 4 of $\S 1$, No. 3.

#### Remark 1 {#ac-viii-s3-n4-rem-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring and $a$ an ideal of $A$. Suppose that $A$ is separated and complete for the $a$-adic topology, and consider the ring $R = A \{ X_1, ..., X_n \}$ of restricted formal power series (III, $\S 4$, No. 2, Definition 2). Then $\dim(R) = \dim(A) + n$. In fact, $R$ is the completion of the ring $B = A[X_1, ..., X_n]$ for the $aB$-adic topology, whence $\dim(R) \leqslant \dim(A[X_1, ..., X_n]) = \dim(A) + n$. The inverse inequality is proved as in the case of formal power series.

#### Remark 2 {#ac-viii-s3-n4-rem-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a local noetherian ring, identified with a subring of its completion $\hat{A}$, and $B$ a subring of $\hat{A}$ containing $A$. Suppose that $B$ is local noetherian and that one has $m_A B = m_B$. Then the canonical injection of $A$ into $B$ extends to an isomorphism of $\hat{A}$ onto the completion $\hat{B}$ of $B$ (III, $\S 3$, No. 5, Proposition 11), whence $\dim(B) = \dim(A)$ (Corollary 2 to Proposition 8). This applies in particular to the following situation. \* Let $k$ be a complete non-discrete valued field, $A$ the local ring of the polynomial ring $k[X_1, ..., X_n]$ at the prime ideal generated by $X_1, ..., X_n$, and $B$ the ring of convergent power series in $X_1, ..., X_n$ with coefficients in $k$. Then the preceding hypotheses are satisfied, and consequently one has $\dim(B) = n$.*

### 5. Construction of regular sequences

#### Proposition 9 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-9 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, $M$ a finitely generated $A$-module, $a$ a subset of $A$ stable under addition and multiplication, $q_1, ..., q_m$ prime ideals of $A$ not containing $a$, and $r \geq 1$ an integer such that

$$
\text{codim}(V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M), \operatorname{Supp}(M)) \geq r .
$$

There exists a sequence $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ of elements of $a$, belonging to none of the ideals $q_1, ..., q_m$, and such that the sequence $(x_1/1, ..., x_r/1)$ of elements of $A_p$ is secant for the $A_p$-module $M_p$, for every prime ideal $p \in V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M)$.

We reason by induction on $r$. Suppose first that $r = 1$, and let $\Phi$ be the (finite) set of minimal elements of $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$. We argue by contradiction and suppose that there exists no element $x_1$ of $a$ satisfying the conditions of the statement. Let $x \in a$, not belonging to $q_1 \cup ... \cup q_m$. By hypothesis there exists an element $p$ of $V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M)$ such that the image $x/1$ of $x$ in $A_p$ is not secant for $M_p$. Let $\Psi$ be the set of ideals $q \in \Phi$ contained in $p$; then the minimal elements of $\operatorname{Supp}(M_p)$ are the prime ideals $qA_p$ of $A_p$, where $q$ runs through $\Psi$. By Prop. 3 of No. 2, there therefore exists an element $q$ of $\Psi$ such that $x/1 \in qA_p$, whence $x \in q$. In other words, we have $a \subset q_1 \cup ... \cup q_m \cup \bigcup_{q \in \Phi} q$. Since $a \not\subset q_j$ for $1 \leq j \leq m$, Prop. 2 of II, § 1, No. 1 proves the existence of an element $q$ of $\Phi$ containing $a$, whence

$$
V(q) \subset V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M) .
$$

Since $V(a)$ contains an irreducible component of $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$, this contradicts the hypothesis

$$
\text{codim}(V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M), \operatorname{Supp}(M)) \geq 1 .
$$

Suppose now that $r \geq 2$. By the induction hypothesis, one can find a sequence $(x_1, ..., x_{r-1})$ of elements of $a$, not belonging to $q_1 \cup ... \cup q_m$, and such that, for every $p \in V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M)$, the sequence $(x_1/1, ..., x_{r-1}/1)$ of elements of $A_p$ is secant for $M_p$. Put $N = M / \sum_{i=1}^{r-1} x_i M$. It is enough to construct an element $x_r$ of $a$ not belonging to $q_1 \cup ... \cup q_m$ and such that, for every $p \in V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M)$, the element $x_r/1$ of $A_p$ is secant for $N_p$. By the first part of the proof, it is enough to establish the two relations

$$
V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M) = V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(N) ,
$$
$$
\text{codim}(V(a) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(N), \operatorname{Supp}(N)) \geq 1 .
$$

Now one has

$$
\operatorname{Supp}(N) = V(x_1) \cap ... \cap V(x_{r-1}) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M)
$$

$$
m = r - 1,\quad X = \operatorname{Supp}(M),\quad Y = V(\alpha) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(N),\quad H_i = V(x_i)
$$

whence $X' = \operatorname{Supp}(N)$.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-9-cor-1 .statement}

*Let A be a noetherian ring, M a finitely generated A-module, $p_1, ..., p_n, q_1, ..., q_m$ prime ideals of A and r an integer $\geqslant 1$. Suppose that one has $p_i \not\subset q_j$ for $1 \leqslant i \leqslant n$ and $1 \leqslant j \leqslant m$, and $\dim_{A_{p_i}}(M_{p_i}) \geqslant r$ for $1 \leqslant i \leqslant n$. Then there exists a sequence $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ of elements of A belonging to all the $p_i$ and belonging to none of the $q_j$, such that, for $1 \leqslant i \leqslant n$, the images of $x_1, ..., x_r$ in $A_{p_i}$ form a secant sequence for the module $M_{p_i}$.

Put $\alpha = \bigcap_i p_i$. One has $M_{p_i} \neq \{0\}$, hence $p_i \in \operatorname{Supp}(M)$ for $1 \leqslant i \leqslant n$, whence $V(\alpha) \subset \operatorname{Supp}(M)$. One has

$$
\operatorname{codim}(V(\alpha) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M), \operatorname{Supp}(M)) = \operatorname{codim}(V(\alpha), \operatorname{Supp}(M)) =
= \inf_i (\operatorname{codim}(V(p_i), \operatorname{Supp}(M))) = \inf_i \dim(M_{p_i}) \geqslant r
$$

(§ 1, No. 4, Prop. 9), and Proposition 9 is applied.

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s3-prop-9-cor-2 .statement}

*Let A be a noetherian local ring, M a nonzero finitely generated A-module and $\alpha$ a subset of $m_A$, stable under addition and multiplication and such that $\operatorname{long}(M/\alpha M) < +\infty$. There exists a subset of $\alpha$ which is a maximal secant subset for M.*

In fact, one has $V(\alpha) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M) = \operatorname{Supp}(M/\alpha M) = \{m_A\}$ (§ 1, No. 4, Remark 1), hence $\operatorname{codim}(V(\alpha) \cap \operatorname{Supp}(M), \operatorname{Supp}(M)) = \dim(\operatorname{Supp}(M)) = \dim_A(M)$, and Proposition 9 is applied.
