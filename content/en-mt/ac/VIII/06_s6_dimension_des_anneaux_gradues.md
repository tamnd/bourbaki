## § 6. DIMENSION OF GRADED RINGS

In this paragraph, H denotes a graded ring of type $\mathbf{Z}$, with positive degrees, and $(H_n)_{n \in \mathbf{Z}}$ its graduation; thus one has $H_n = \{0\}$ for $n < 0$.

### 1. Filtered ring associated with a graded ring

For every $n \in \mathbf{Z}$, put $H_{\geq n} = \sum_{i \geq n} H_i$. One has $H = H_{\geq 0}$; the $H_{\geq n}$ are graded ideals of H. Let S denote the multiplicative subset $1 + H_{\geq 1}$ formed by the elements of H whose component of degree 0 is equal to 1, and consider the ring of fractions $S^{-1}H$. Let us identify H with a subring of its completion $\hat{H} = \prod_{n} H_n$ (III, § 2, No. 12, Example 1); since the elements of S are invertible in $\hat{H}$ (III, § 2, No. 13, Lemma 3), $S^{-1}H$ is identified with a subring of $\hat{H}$ containing H. For $s \in S$ and $h \in H_{\geq n}$, the element $s^{-1}h - h$ of $\hat{H}$ belongs to $\prod_{i \geq n} H_i$; consequently one has $S^{-1}H_{\geq n} = (S^{-1}H) \cap \prod_{i \geq n} H_i$.

From this one deduces:

#### Proposition 1 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-1 .statement}

a) The ideals $S^{-1}H_{\geq n}$ form an exhaustive and separated filtration of the ring $S^{-1}H$.

b) The canonical homomorphism of $H$ into $S^{-1}H$ induces for each $n$ an isomorphism $u_n$ of $H_n$ onto $S^{-1}H_{\geq n}/S^{-1}H_{\geq n+1}$; the $u_n$ are the homogeneous components of an isomorphism of graded rings of $H$ onto the graded ring associated with $S^{-1}H$, filtered by the $S^{-1}H_{\geq n}$.

#### Remark 1 {#ac-viii-s6-n1-rem-1 .statement}

An element $h/s$ of $S^{-1}H$ with $h \in H, s \in S$, is invertible if and only if the component of degree 0 of $h$ is invertible in $H_0$. Consequently, if the ring $H_0$ is local, the ring $S^{-1}H$ is local and the canonical injection $H_0 \to S^{-1}H$ induces an isomorphism of the residue fields.

#### Remark 2 {#ac-viii-s6-n1-rem-2 .statement}

Suppose that $H$ is generated by $H_0$ and $H_1$; then for every $n$, one has $H_{n+1} = H_1.H_n$, hence $H_{\geq n+1} = H_1.H_{\geq n}$ and $S^{-1}H_{\geq n+1} = H_1.S^{-1}H_{\geq n}$. It follows that the filtration $(S^{-1}H_{\geq n})$ of $S^{-1}H$ is the $S^{-1}H_{\geq 1}$-adic filtration.

#### Example 1 {#ac-viii-s6-n1-exa-1 .statement}

\* Let $p$ be a graded prime ideal of $\mathbf{C}[X_0, ..., X_n]$ different from the ideal generated by the $X_i$; let $V$ be the algebraic subvariety of $\mathbf{P}^n(\mathbf{C})$ defined by $p$ and $C$ the algebraic subvariety of $\mathbf{C}^{n+1}$ defined by $p$. Then $C$ is the cone with base $V$, $H = \mathbf{C}[X_0, ..., X_n]/p$ is the affine algebra of $C$ and $S^{-1}H$ the local ring of the cone $C$ at its vertex. \*
2) Let $A$ be a local ring and $a$ an ideal of $A$ distinct from $A$. Then $H = \bigoplus_n a^n/a^{n+1}$ is a graded ring such that $H_0 = A/a$ is local; it is generated by $H_0$ and $H_1$. The ring $S^{-1}H$ is therefore local and the filtration $(S^{-1}H_{\geq n})$ is the $S^{-1}H_{\geq 1}$-adic filtration. It should be noted that in general the rings $A$ and $S^{-1}H$ are not isomorphic. \* In particular an algebraic variety is not in general locally isomorphic in a neighbourhood of a point to its tangent cone at that point. \*

### 2. Dimension and chains of graded ideals

In this number, we shall denote by dimgr(H) the least upper bound of the lengths of chains of graded prime ideals of $H$; analogously, if $p$ is a graded prime ideal of $H$, we shall denote by htgr(p) the least upper bound of the lengths of chains of graded prime ideals of $H$ of which $p$ is the greatest element. If $p$ is a graded prime ideal of $H$, we have $p \cap S = \varnothing$; for otherwise indeed $p$ would contain an element whose component of degree 0 would be equal to 1, hence would contain 1 since it is graded. The mapping $p \mapsto S^{-1}p$ from the set of graded prime ideals of $H$ into the set of prime ideals of $S^{-1}H$ is therefore injective and increasing (II, § 2, no. 5, prop. 11); consequently, taking account of § 1, no. 3, prop. 6 and corollary to prop. 7, we have:

#### Proposition 2 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-2 .statement}

a) We have $\dim_{\mathrm{gr}}(H) \leq \dim(S^{-1}H) \leq \dim(H)$.
b) For every graded prime ideal $p$ of $H$, we have $\operatorname{ht}_{\mathrm{gr}}(p) \leq \operatorname{ht}(S^{-1}p) = \operatorname{ht}(p)$.

For every ideal $a$ of $H$, let us denote by $a^{\mathrm{gr}}$ the greatest graded ideal contained in $a$; we have $a^{\mathrm{gr}} = \sum_n (a \cap H_n)$.

#### Lemma 1 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-1 .statement}

a) If $p$ is a prime ideal of $H$, $p^{gr}$ is a prime ideal.

b) Every maximal element of the set of graded ideals of $H$ distinct from $H$ is a maximal ideal of $H$ which contains $H_{\geq 1}$.

c) Every minimal prime ideal of $H$ is graded.

a) This follows from III, § 1, no. 4, prop. 4.

b) Let $m$ be a graded ideal of $H$ distinct from $H$. Then one has
$$
m \subset (m \cap H_0) + H_{\geq 1} \neq H.
$$
If $m$ is maximal, then one has $m = m_0 + H_{\geq 1}$, where $m_0$ is a maximal ideal of $H_0$, whence $b$.

c) Let $p$ be a minimal prime ideal of $H$. Since $p^{gr}$ is prime by $a$ and contained in $p$, one has $p = p^{gr}$, whence $c$.

#### Lemma 2 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-2 .statement}

Let $p$ and $q$ be prime ideals of $H$ such that $q \subset p$ and $q \neq p$. If $q^{gr} = p^{gr}$, then $q$ is graded, $p$ is not, and $ht(p/q) = 1$.

\* Remark 1. --- Let us take up again the notation of example 1 of No. 1. Lemma 2 implies that, if two irreducible subvarieties $Y$ and $Z$ of $\mathbf{C}^{n+1}$ have the same projecting cone and if $Z \subset Y$ and $Z \neq Y$, then $Y$ is the projecting cone of $Z$, and $Z$ is of codimension 1 in $Y$. \*

Replacing $H$ by $H/q^{gr}$, one is reduced to the case where $q^{gr} = \{0\}$. Then $H$ is an integral domain (lemma 1, $a$), $p^{gr} = 0$, and it is a matter of proving that $ht(p) \leq 1$: this will indeed imply that $ht(q) = 0$, hence that $q = \{0\}$. Since $p^{gr} = \{0\}$, one has $p \cap H_n = \{0\}$ for every $n$, and $p$ is disjoint from the multiplicative subset $T = \bigcup (H_n - \{0\})$. The ring $H_p$ is therefore isomorphic to a ring of fractions of $T^{-1}H$, and one has therefore
$$
ht(p) = \dim(H_p) \leq \dim(T^{-1}H)
$$
(§ 1, No. 3, prop. 6 and 7). But, by lemma 4 of V, § 1, No. 8, $T^{-1}H$ is a field or is isomorphic to a ring $K[X, X^{-1}]$, where $K$ is a field; hence one has $\dim(T^{-1}H) \leq 1$ and $ht(p) \leq 1$, which was to be proved.

#### Proposition 3 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-3 .statement}

Let $p$ be a prime ideal of $H$. If $p \neq p^{gr}$, one has $ht(p^{gr}) = ht(p) - 1$.

By Lemma 1, $a$, the ideal $p^{gr}$ is prime and contained in $p$, hence $ht(p^{gr}) \leq ht(p) - 1$. Since the proposition is trivial when $ht(p^{gr}) = + \infty$, one may suppose that $ht(p^{gr}) < + \infty$. Let us prove the inequality $ht(p) \leq ht(p^{gr}) + 1$ by induction on $ht(p^{gr})$. It is enough to prove that, for every prime ideal $q$ contained in $p$ and distinct from $p$, one has $ht(q) \leq ht(p^{gr})$. Let us distinguish two cases according as $q^{gr} \neq p^{gr}$ or $q^{gr} = p^{gr}$. If $q^{gr} \neq p^{gr}$, then one has $ht(q^{gr}) < ht(p^{gr})$; one has
$$
ht(q) \leq ht(q^{gr}) + 1,
$$
by the induction hypothesis if $q \neq q^{gr}$ and trivially if $q = q^{gr}$; consequently, one has $ht(q) \leq ht(q^{gr}) + 1 \leq ht(p^{gr})$, which was to be proved. If $q^{gr} = p^{gr}$, then one has $q = q^{gr}$ by Lemma 2, hence $ht(q) = ht(q^{gr}) \leq ht(p^{gr})$, whence again the desired conclusion.

#### Theorem 1 {#ac-viii-s6-thm-1 .statement}

Suppose that H is noetherian.

a) Every chain of graded prime ideals of H, saturated as a chain of graded prime ideals, is saturated as a chain of prime ideals.

b) For every graded prime ideal p of H, one has htgr(p) = ht(S^{-1}p) = ht(p).

c) One has dimgr(H) = dim(S^{-1}H) = dim(H).

To prove a), it is enough to prove that, if p and q are two distinct graded prime ideals of H such that q ⊂ p and every graded prime ideal contained between q and p is equal either to p or to q, then ht(p/q) = 1. Passing to the quotient by q, one is reduced to the case where q = {0}. It is therefore a matter of proving that, if H is an integral domain, and if p is an ideal of H, minimal among the graded prime ideals ≠ {0}, one has ht(p) = 1. Now let a be a nonzero homogeneous element of p, and let r be a prime ideal of H such that a ∈ r ⊂ p and minimal for these properties (II, § 2, No. 6, Lemma 2). Since r^{gr} is prime (Lemma 1, a)) and nonzero, one has r^{gr} = p, hence p = r. Since H is an integral domain and noetherian, p has height 1 (§ 3, No. 1, Prop. 1), whence a).

Let us prove b). Let p be a graded prime ideal of H. One has

$$
\text{htgr}(p) \leq \text{ht}(S^{-1}p) \leq \text{ht}(p)
$$

(Prop. 2); let us prove the inequality ht(p) ≤ htgr(p) by induction on htgr(p). If htgr(p) = 0, p is minimal among the graded prime ideals, hence minimal (Lemma 1, c)), and one has ht(p) = 0. Suppose that one has htgr(p) > 0 and let us prove the inequality ht(q) ≤ htgr(p) − 1 for every prime ideal q contained in p and distinct from p. Let us distinguish two cases. If q is graded, one concludes by the induction hypothesis. If q is not graded, then one has q^{gr} ≠ p, hence ht(q^{gr}) ≤ htgr(q^{gr}) by the induction hypothesis, whence ht(q) ≤ htgr(q^{gr}) + 1 by Prop. 3; it remains to prove the inequality htgr(q^{gr}) ≤ htgr(p) − 2; but if one had htgr(q^{gr}) = htgr(p) − 1, the chain q^{gr} ⊂ p would be saturated by a), which it is not since q^{gr} ≠ q ≠ p.

Finally let us prove c). One has dimgr(H) ≤ dim(S^{-1}H) ≤ dim(H) (Prop. 2), and it remains to prove dim(H) ≤ dimgr(H), or again ht(p) ≤ dimgr(H) for every prime ideal p of H. Let therefore p be a prime ideal of H. If p is graded, one has ht(p) = htgr(p) ≤ dimgr(H). If p is not graded, one has ht(p) = htgr(p^{gr}) + 1 by Prop. 3; let m be a maximal graded ideal of H containing p^{gr}; by Lemma 1, b), m is maximal, hence distinct from p^{gr}, and one has htgr(p^{gr}) + 1 ≤ htgr(m) ≤ dimgr(H), whence again ht(p) ≤ dimgr(H). This completes the proof.

#### Remark 2 {#ac-viii-s6-n2-rem-2 .statement}

There exist non-noetherian graded rings H such that dimgr(H) < dim(H) (p. 99, Exercise 1).

### 3. Dimension of graded modules

In this number, M denotes a graded H-module (of type $\mathbf{Z}$).
Then S^{-1}M is an S^{-1}H-module, and if one puts $M_{\geq n} = \bigoplus_{i \geq n} M_i$, one sees as in No. 1 that the sequence of sets $S^{-1}M_{\geq n}$ is an exhaustive and separated filtration on

$S^{-1}M$ and that the canonical mapping $M \to S^{-1}M$ induces an isomorphism of $M$ onto the graded module $\bigoplus_n S^{-1}M_{\geq n}/S^{-1}M_{\geq n+1}$.

#### Lemma 3 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-3 .statement}

Suppose that $H$ is generated by $H_0 \cup H_1$ and $M$ generated by $\bigoplus_{i \leq n_0} M_i$ for a suitable integer $n_0$. Then the filtration $(S^{-1}M_{\geq n})$ on $S^{-1}M$ is good for the ideal $S^{-1}H_{\geq 1}$ of $S^{-1}H$.

One has, for $n \geq n_0$, $M_{\geq n+1} = H_1 \cdot M_{\geq n}$, hence $S^{-1}M_{\geq n+1} = H_1 \cdot S^{-1}M_{\geq n} = S^{-1}H_{\geq 1} \cdot S^{-1}M_{\geq n}$.

#### Proposition 4 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-4 .statement}

Suppose that $H$ is noetherian and $M$ finitely generated. Then $\dim_H(M) = \dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M)$.

Let a be the annihilator of the $H$-module $M$; it is a graded ideal of $H$. Since $M$ is a finitely generated $H$-module, the annihilator of the $S^{-1}H$-module $S^{-1}M$ is the ideal $S^{-1}\alpha$ of $S^{-1}H$. One has $\dim_H(M) = \dim(H/\alpha)$ and $\dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M) = \dim(S^{-1}H/S^{-1}\alpha)$. Proposition 4 then follows from Theorem 1, c) of No. 2 applied to the graded ring $H/\alpha$.

#### Proposition 5 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-5 .statement}

Suppose $H_0$ local and artinian, $H$ generated by $H_0 \cup H_1$, $H_1$ a finitely generated $H_0$-module, $M$ nonzero and a finitely generated $H$-module. Then $M_n$ is an $H_0$-module of finite length for each $n$, and there exists $Q(T) \in \mathbf{Z}[T, T^{-1}]$ such that $Q(1) > 0$ and that one has in the ring $\mathbf{Z}((T))$

$$
\sum_{n \in \mathbf{Z}} \operatorname{long}_{H_0}(M_n) \cdot T^n = (1 - T)^{-d} \cdot Q(T),
$$

where $d = \dim_H(M)$.

The ring $S^{-1}H$ is local and noetherian (No. 1, remark 1), the $S^{-1}H$-module $S^{-1}M$ is nonzero and finitely generated, and of dimension $d = \dim_H(M)$ (prop. 4). Moreover, $S^{-1}H_{\geq 1}$ is an ideal of definition of $S^{-1}H$ (§ 3, No. 2, lemma 2), and $S^{-1}M_{\geq n}$ is an $S^{-1}H_{\geq 1}$-good filtration on $S^{-1}M$ (lemma 3). Finally, one has $\operatorname{long}_{S^{-1}H} S^{-1}M_{\geq n}/S^{-1}M_{\geq n+1} = \operatorname{long}_{H_0}(M_n)$ for all $n$. It is enough therefore to apply Theorems 2 and 3 of § 4 (Nos. 3 and 4).

#### Remark {#ac-viii-s6-n3-rem-1 .statement}

With the exception of the determination of the integer $d$, proposition 5 follows directly from Theorem 1 of § 4, No. 2.

#### Corollary {#ac-viii-s6-n3-cor-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a local noetherian ring and $q$ an ideal of definition of $A$. Then one has $\dim(A) = \dim(\operatorname{gr}_q(A))$.

Applying proposition 5 to the case $M = H = \operatorname{gr}_q(A)$, one obtains the relation

$$
\sum_{n \geq 0} \operatorname{long}_{A/q}(q^n/q^{n+1}) \cdot T^n = (1 - T)^{-d} Q(T)
$$

with $d = \dim(\operatorname{gr}_q(A))$ and $Q(1) \neq 0$. One has $d = \dim(A)$ by Theorem 3 of § 4, No. 4, whence the corollary.

#### Proposition 6 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-6 .statement}

Suppose $H_0$ local and artinian, $H$ finitely generated as an $H_0$-algebra and $M$ finitely generated as an $H$-module.

a) Let $a_1, ..., a_n$ be elements of $H$, homogeneous of degrees $> 0$, and let $\varphi$ be the homomorphism (of $H_0$-algebras) of $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$ into $H$ which maps $X_i$ to $a_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$. The $S^{-1}H$-module $S^{-1}M / \sum_{i=1}^n (a_i/1).S^{-1}M$ is of finite length if and only if $\varphi_*(M)$ is a finitely generated module over $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$.

b) There exists a family $(a_1, ..., a_d)$ of elements of $H$, all homogeneous of the same degree $> 0$, with $d = \dim_H(M)$, and such that $(a_1/1, ..., a_d/1)$ is a maximal secant sequence for the $S^{-1}H$-module $S^{-1}M$. If moreover $H$ is generated by $H_1$ as an $H_0$-algebra, and if the residue field of $H_0$ is infinite, one can take the $a_i$ of degree 1.

a) Put $N = M / \sum_{i=1}^n a_i M$. We have $\dim_H(N) = \dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}N)$ by prop. 4. Consequently, the $S^{-1}H$-module $S^{-1}N$ is of finite length if and only if the $H$-module $N$ is of finite length, that is to say if and only if $N$ is a finitely generated module over $H_0$. If $\varphi_*(M)$ is the module over $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$ deduced from $M$ by the homomorphism $\varphi : H_0[X_1, ..., X_n] \to M$, we have $N = \varphi_*(M)/\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \cdot \varphi_*(M)$. Consequently (A, II, p. 171, cor. 3 and remark) $\varphi_*(M)$ is a finitely generated module over $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$ if and only if $N$ is a finitely generated module over $H_0$. This proves a).

To prove b), we shall first establish a lemma.

#### Lemma 4 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-4 .statement}

Let $b$ be an element of $H$, homogeneous of degree $> 0$, and belonging to none of the minimal elements $p$ of $\mathrm{Supp}(M)$ such that $\dim(H/p) = \dim_H(M)$. Then $\dim_H(M/bM) = \dim_H(M) - 1$.

Put $d = \dim_H(M)$. By the definition of $b$, we have $\dim_H(M/bM) < d$. By prop. 4, we have
$$
\dim_H(M/bM) = \dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M/(b/1).S^{-1}M)
$$
and formula (8) of § 3, no. 2 yields the inequality
$$
\dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M/(b/1).S^{-1}M) \geq \dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M) - 1 .
$$
Finally, we have
$$
\dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M) = \dim_H(M) = d
$$
by prop. 4. Hence $\dim_H(M/bM) \geq d - 1$, whence lemma 4.

Let us take up again the proof of Prop. 6, b). We may suppose $\dim_H(M) > 0$. Let us remark that every minimal element of $\mathrm{Supp}(M)$ is graded (apply Lemma 1 of No. 2 to the quotient of $H$ by the annihilator of $M$). By Prop. 8 of III, § 1, No. 4, there therefore exists a homogeneous element $b$ of $H$, of degree $> 0$, not belonging to any of the minimal elements $p$ of $\mathrm{Supp}(M)$ such that $\dim(H/p) = \dim_H(M)$. By Lemma 4, one has $\dim_H(M/bM) = \dim_H(M) - 1$. Suppose further that $H$ is generated by $H_1$ as an $H_0$-algebra and that the residue field $k$ of $H_0$ is infinite. For every minimal element $p$ of $\mathrm{Supp}(M)$ such that $\dim(H/p) = \dim_H(M)$, consider the vector subspace $V_p = (p \cap H_1) \otimes_{H_0} k$ of the vector space $V = H_1 \otimes_{H_0} k$ over $k$. If one had $V_p = V$, one would have $\mathfrak{p} \cap H_1 = H_1$ (II, § 3, No. 2, Prop. 4), whence $H_1 \subset \mathfrak{p}$ and $\dim_H(M) = \dim(H/\mathfrak{p}) \leq \dim(H/H_{\geq 1}) = 0$, which is not the case. Since $k$ is supposed infinite, the union of the $V_p$ is distinct from $V$; if $b \in H_1$ is such that $b \otimes 1$ does not belong to any of the $V_p$, one has $\dim(M/bM) = \dim_H(M) - 1$.

Proceeding by induction on $d = \dim_H(M)$, one then constructs a sequence $(b_1, ..., b_d)$ of elements of $H$, with $b_i$ homogeneous of degree $n_i > 0$ and such that $M / \sum_{i=1}^n b_i M$ is an $H$-module of finite length. If one supposes that $H$ is generated by $H_1$ as an $H_0$-algebra and that the residue field of $H_0$ is infinite, one may suppose $n_i = 1$ for $i = 1, ..., d$. By Prop. 4, one has $\dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M) = d$ and
$$
\dim_{S^{-1}H}(S^{-1}M / \sum_{i=1}^d (b_i/1).S^{-1}M) = 0 .
$$
Then $(b_1/1, ..., b_d/1)$ is a maximal secant sequence for the $S^{-1}H$-module $S^{-1}M$. Put $a_i = b_i^{(n_1 ... n_d)/n_i}$ for $1 \leq i \leq d$; then the $a_i$ are all of the same degree, and $(a_1/1, ..., a_d/1)$ is a maximal secant sequence for $S^{-1}M$ (§ 3, No. 2, Remark 3).

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-4-cor-1 .statement}

*Suppose that $H_0$ is a field and that $H$ is a finitely generated $H_0$-algebra. Put $n = \dim(H)$. There exist homogeneous elements $a_1, ..., a_n$ of $H$, all of the same degree $> 0$, such that the $H_0$-homomorphism $\varphi : H_0[X_1, ..., X_n] \to H$ defined by $\varphi(X_i) = a_i, i = 1, ..., n$, is injective and makes $H$ into a finite $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$-algebra. If $H$ is generated by $H_1$ as an $H_0$-algebra and if $H_0$ is infinite, the $a_i$ may be supposed to be of degree 1.*

By Prop. 6 there exists an $H_0$-homomorphism $\varphi$ of the indicated form which makes $H$ into a finite $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$-algebra. By Theorem 1 of § 2, No. 3, one then has
$$
\dim(H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]/(\mathrm{Ker}\ \varphi)) = \dim(H);
$$
since one has
$$
\dim(H) = n = \dim(H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]),
$$
and since $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$ is an integral domain, this implies $\mathrm{Ker}\ \varphi = \{0\}$.

#### Remark {#ac-viii-s6-n3-rem-2 .statement}

Let $(h_1, ..., h_r)$ be a finite generating system of the vector space $H_1$ over $H_0$. For $\lambda = (\lambda_1, ..., \lambda_r) \in H_0^r$, put $h_\lambda = \lambda_1 h_1 + \cdots + \lambda_r h_r$. The proofs of Prop. 6 and Corollary 1 entail the following result: the set of elements $(\lambda_1, ..., \lambda_n)$ of $(H_0^r)^n$ such that the elements $a_i = h_{\lambda_i} \in H_1$ satisfy the conclusion of Corollary 1 contains the complement of the union of a finite number of vector subspaces of $(H_0^r)^n$ distinct from the whole space.

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-4-cor-2 .statement}

*Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring and $n \in \mathbf{N}$. In order that one may have $\dim(A) \geq n$, it is necessary and sufficient that for every integer $r \geq 0$, one have*
$$
[m_A^r/m_A^{r+1} : \kappa_A] \geq \binom{n+r-1}{n-1}, \quad \left( \text{resp. } \mathrm{long}_A(A/m_A^{r+1}) \geq \binom{n+r}{n} \right);
$$
*one has equality for every $r$ if and only if $A$ is regular of dimension $n$.*

The condition is sufficient (§ 4, No. 4, Theorem 3 and § 5, No. 2, Theorem 1). Let us show that it is necessary. Consider the graded ring gr(A) = gr_{m_A}(A); let k be an infinite extension of the field $\kappa_A$, and put $H = k \otimes_{\kappa_A} \mathrm{gr}(A)$. The ring H is of dimension $\geq n$ (Prop. 5 and its corollary); one therefore deduces from Corollary 1 the existence of an injective graded homomorphism of graded k-algebras $\varphi : H_0[X_1, ..., X_n] \to H$. Consequently one has, for every integer $r \geq 0$,

$$
[\mathrm{gr}_r(A) : \kappa_A] = [H_r : H_0] \geq \binom{n + r - 1}{n - 1},
$$

and equality for every $r$ implies the bijectivity of $\varphi$, hence the regularity of A (§ 5, No. 2, Theorem 1).

The equalities

$$
\mathrm{long}_A(A/m_A^{r+1}) = \sum_{i=0}^r [\mathrm{gr}_i(A) : \kappa_A]
$$

and

$$
\binom{n + r}{n} = \sum_{i=0}^r \binom{n + i - 1}{n - 1}
$$

then imply the analogous assertions for the function $r \mapsto \mathrm{long}_A(A/m_A^{r+1})$.

### 4. Semi-continuity of dimension

#### Lemma 5 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-5 .statement}

Let A be a ring, r its radical, $R = \bigoplus_{i \in \mathbf{Z}} R_i$ a graded A-algebra, $M = \bigoplus_{i \in \mathbf{Z}} M_i$ a graded R-module. Suppose that each $M_i$ is a finitely generated A-module and that $M/rM$ is a finitely generated $R/rR$-module. Then M is a finitely generated R-module.

Let $m_1, ..., m_n$ be homogeneous elements of M whose images in $M/rM$ generate the $R/rR$-module $M/rM$. Let N be the (graded) sub-$R$-module of M generated by $\{ m_1, ..., m_n \}$. For every $i \in \mathbf{Z}$, we have $M_i = N_i + rM_i$, hence $M_i = N_i$ (II, \S 3, No. 2, Prop. 4); consequently we have $M = N$.

#### Lemma 6 {#ac-viii-s6-lem-6 .statement}

Let $\rho : B \to C$ be a ring homomorphism and S a multiplicative subset of B. Suppose that C is a B-algebra of finite type, and that $S^{-1}C$ is a finite $S^{-1}B$-algebra. Then there exists $f \in S$ such that $C_f$ is a finite $B_f$-algebra.

Let X be a finite generating set of the B-algebra C. For every $x \in X$, the image of x in $S^{-1}C$ is integral over $S^{-1}B$, and consequently there exist an integer $n(x) \geq 0$, elements $b_1(x), ..., b_{n(x)}(x) \in B$ and an element $f(x) \in S$ such that

$$
f(x)\ x^{n(x)} + b_1(x)\ x^{n(x)-1} + \cdots + b_{n(x)} = 0.
$$

Let $f = \prod_{x \in X} f(x)$; the image of every element x of X in $C_f$ is integral over $B_f$, hence $C_f$ is a finite $B_f$-algebra (V, \S 1, No. 1, Prop. 4).

#### Proposition 7 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-7 .statement}

Suppose that H is an $H_0$-algebra of finite type. Then the function $p \mapsto \dim(H \otimes_{H_0} \kappa(p))$ is upper semicontinuous on $\mathrm{Spec}(H_0)$.

Since H is a finitely generated $H_0$-algebra, each $H_i$ is a finitely generated $H_0$-module (III, § 1, No. 2, corollary to Prop. 1) and H is generated as an $H_0$-algebra by $H_0 \oplus H_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus H_r$ for a suitable integer $r \geq 0$. Let $p \in \mathrm{Spec}(H_0)$ and put $\dim(H \otimes_{H_0} \kappa(p)) = n \geq 0$. By Corollary 1 to Prop. 6, there exist elements $a_1, ..., a_n$ of H, all homogeneous of the same degree $d > 0$, such that the $\kappa(p)$-homomorphism $\overline{\varphi} : \kappa(p)[X_1, ..., X_n] \to H \otimes_{H_0} \kappa(p)$ which maps $X_i$ to $a_i \otimes 1$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$, makes $H \otimes_{H_0} \kappa(p)$ into a finite $\kappa(p)[X_1, ..., X_n]$-algebra. Let $\varphi$ denote the $H_0$-homomorphism of $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n] = R$ into H which maps $X_i$ to $a_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$. If, for every $m \in \mathbf{Z}$, we put $H'_m = \sum_{(m-1)d < i \leq md} H_i$, we obtain a $\mathbf{Z}$-grading on H, compatible with the structure of R-module given by $\varphi$. Each $H'_m$ is finitely generated over $H_0$. By Lemma 5, $H_p$ is a finitely generated $R_p$-module. By Lemma 6, there therefore exists $f \in H_0 - p$ such that $H_f$ is a finitely generated $R_f$-module. For every $q \in \mathrm{Spec}(H_0)_f$, $H \otimes_{H_0} \kappa(q)$ is a finite $\kappa(q)[X_1, ..., X_n]$-algebra, therefore $\dim(H \otimes_{H_0} \kappa(q)) \leq n$ (§ 2, No. 3, Theorem 1), which completes the proof.

#### Remark 1 {#ac-viii-s6-n4-rem-1 .statement}

\* In algebraic geometry, Prop. 7 implies that the dimension of the fibres of a projective morphism of algebraic varieties is upper semi-continuous. \*
2) We shall see later that if $\rho : A \to B$ is a ring homomorphism which makes B into a finitely generated A-algebra, the function $q \mapsto \dim_q(B \otimes_A \kappa(\rho^{-1}(q)))$ is upper semi-continuous on $\mathrm{Spec}(B)$ (cf. p. 101, Exercise 10).

### 5. Regular graded algebras

In this number, it is assumed that $H_0$ is a field and that H is a finitely generated $H_0$-algebra.

We put $H_+ = H_{\geq 1}$; it is a maximal ideal of H. The ring $S^{-1}H$ is identified with the local ring $H_{H_+}$ of H at the ideal $H_+$; its maximal ideal is $(H_+ )_{H_+} = S^{-1}H_+$, its residue field is identified with $H_0$.

#### Proposition 8 {#ac-viii-s6-prop-8 .statement}

a) One has $\dim(H) \leq [H_+/H_+^2 : H_0]$.
b) The following conditions are equivalent:
(i) $\dim(H) = [H_+/H_+^2 : H_0]$;
(ii) the noetherian local ring $S^{-1}H$ is regular;
(iii) $H$ is generated as an $H_0$-algebra by a family of homogeneous elements of degrees $> 0$, algebraically independent over $H_0$.
c) Assume the conditions of b) satisfied, and let $a_1, ..., a_n \in H$ be homogeneous elements of degrees $> 0$. The following conditions are equivalent:
(i) the classes of the $a_i$ form a basis of the $H_0$-vector space $H_+/H_+^2$;
(ii) the $a_i/1$ form a system of coordinates of the regular noetherian local ring $S^{-1}H$;

(iii) the $a_i$ are algebraically independent over $H_0$ and generate $H$ as an $H_0$-algebra.

One has $\dim(H) = \dim(S^{-1}H)$ (No. 2, th. 1), and $[H_+/H_+^2 : H_0] = [(S^{-1}H_+)/ (S^{-1}H_+)^2 : H_0]$ (II, § 3, No. 3, prop. 9); after § 5, No. 1, this implies $a$ and the equivalences (i) $\Leftrightarrow$ (ii) in $b$ and $c$. The two implications (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) in $b$ and $c$ are trivial. Let us prove the implications (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii). Assume therefore that one has $\dim(H) = [H_+/H_+^2 : H_0]$ and let $a_1, ..., a_n$ be homogeneous elements of $H$, of degrees $> 0$, whose classes form a basis of $H_+/H_+^2$; consider the homomorphism of graded algebras $\varphi : H_0[X_1, ..., X_n] \to H$ which sends $X_i$ to $a_i$. The ideal $H_+$ of $H$ is generated by the $a_i$ (A, II, p. 171, cor. 2 and remark); consequently the homomorphism $\varphi$ is surjective (III, § 1, No. 2, prop. 1). But one has

$$
\dim(H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]) = n = \dim(H) = \dim(H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]/(\mathrm{Ker}\, \varphi))
$$

(§ 2, No. 4, cor. 1 of th. 3), therefore $\mathrm{Ker}\, \varphi = 0$ since $H_0[X_1, ..., X_n]$ is an integral domain. This implies assertions (iii).

Under the hypotheses of $b$, one says that $H$ is a regular graded $H_0$-algebra, or a polynomial graded $H_0$-algebra. Under the hypotheses of $c$, one says that the $a_i$ form a graded system of coordinates of $H$.

#### Remark 1 {#ac-viii-s6-n5-rem-1 .statement}

With the notation of $c$, let $d_i$ be the degree of $a_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq n$). Then the Poincaré series $P_H = \sum_{n \in \mathbf{Z}} [H_n : H_0]. T^n$ is equal to $\prod_i (1 - T^{d_i})^{-1}$ (§ 4, No. 2, example 1).
Consequently, if $H$ is a polynomial graded $H_0$-algebra, one has

$$
P_H = \prod_n (1 - T^n)^{-\delta(n)}, \quad \text{with} \quad \delta(n) = [(H_+/H_+^2)_n : H_0].
$$

#### Remark 2 {#ac-viii-s6-n5-rem-2 .statement}

Conversely, the fact that there exist integers $d_i > 0$ such that $P_H = \prod_i (1 - T^{d_i})^{-1}$ does not imply that $H$ is a graded polynomial algebra; for example, if $H$ is generated by an element $X$ of degree 1 and an element $Y$ of degree 2, subject only to the relation $X^2 = 0$, one has $P_H = (1 - T)^{-1}$.
