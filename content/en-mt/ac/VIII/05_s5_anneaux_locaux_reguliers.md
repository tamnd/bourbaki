## § 5. REGULAR LOCAL RINGS

### 1. Definition of regular local rings

Let A be a noetherian local ring. Let $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of elements of $m_A$. By Cor. 2 to Prop. 4 of II, § 3, No. 2, it amounts to the same to suppose that the family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ generates the ideal $m_A$ of A, or that the classes of the $x_i$ modulo $m_A^2$ generate the $\kappa_A$-vector space $m_A/m_A^2$; if this is so, one has $\dim(A) \leq \mathrm{Card}(I)$ by the scholium of § 3, No. 2. One therefore has the inequality

$$
\dim(A) \leq [m_A/m_A^2 : \kappa_A] \leq \mathrm{Card}(I)
$$

for every family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ generating the ideal $m_A$ of A.

#### Definition 1 {#ac-viii-s5-def-1 .statement}

The noetherian local ring $A$ is said to be regular if one has $\dim(A) = [\mathfrak{m}_A/\mathfrak{m}_A^2 : \kappa_A]$. A **system of coordinates** of $A$ is then any family of elements of $\mathfrak{m}_A$ whose classes modulo $\mathfrak{m}_A^2$ form a basis of the $\kappa_A$-vector space $\mathfrak{m}_A/\mathfrak{m}_A^2$.

A system of coordinates in a regular noetherian local ring $A$ is therefore a finite family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ generating the ideal $\mathfrak{m}_A$ of $A$ and such that $\mathrm{Card}(I) = \dim(A)$. Conversely, if the ideal $\mathfrak{m}_A$ of a noetherian local ring $A$ is generated by $d$ elements with $d \leq \dim(A)$, the ring $A$ is regular.

#### Example 1 {#ac-viii-s5-n1-exa-1 .statement}

Regular noetherian local rings of dimension 0 (resp. 1) are fields (resp. discrete valuation rings) (VI, § 3, No. 6, Prop. 9 and Cor. 1 of Theorem 1 below). Let $A$ be a discrete valuation ring; then an element $t$ of $\mathfrak{m}_A$ is a uniformizer if and only if $\{ t \}$ is a system of coordinates of $A$.

#### Example 2 {#ac-viii-s5-n1-exa-2 .statement}

Let $k$ be a field and let $n$ be a positive integer. The ring of formal power series $k[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ is a regular noetherian local ring of dimension $n$ (§ 3, No. 4, Cor. 3 to Prop. 8). Let $F_1, ..., F_n$ be formal power series without constant term in $k[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$; in order that the sequence $(F_1, ..., F_n)$ be a system of coordinates of $k[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$, it is necessary and sufficient that the matrix $\left( \frac{\partial F_i}{\partial X_j}(0, ..., 0) \right)$ be invertible.

More generally, let $A$ be a regular noetherian local ring of dimension $r$; then $A[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ is a regular noetherian local ring of dimension $r + n$. If $(a_1, ..., a_r)$ is a system of coordinates of $A$, then $(a_1, ..., a_r, X_1, ..., X_n)$ is a system of coordinates of $A[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$.

#### Example 3 {#ac-viii-s5-n1-exa-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a complete regular noetherian local ring of dimension $r$. The ring $A \{ X_1, ..., X_n \}$ of restricted formal power series is a regular noetherian local ring of dimension $r + n$ (§ 3, No. 4, Remark 1). If $(a_1, ..., a_r)$ is a system of coordinates of $A$, then $(a_1, ..., a_r, X_1, ..., X_n)$ is a system of coordinates of $A \{ X_1, ..., X_n \}$.

#### Example 4 {#ac-viii-s5-n1-exa-4 .statement}

Let $k$ be a complete non-discrete valued field. The ring of formal power series in $n$ variables which converge in a neighbourhood of 0 in $k^n$ is a regular local noetherian ring of dimension $n$ (§ 3, No. 4, Remark 2).

#### Example 5 {#ac-viii-s5-n1-exa-5 .statement}

Let $k$ be a field, $A$ an integral $k$-algebra of finite type, and $m$ a maximal ideal of $A$. The local noetherian ring $A_m$ is regular if and only if one has $\dim(A) = [m/m^2 : A/m]$; in fact, one has $\dim(A_m) = \dim(A)$ (§ 2, No. 4, Cor. 2 to Theorem 3) and the vector spaces $m/m^2$ and $mA_m/(mA_m)^2$ over the field $A/m$ are isomorphic (II, § 3, No. 3, Prop. 9). In particular, if $k$ is algebraically closed, the stated condition is equivalent to $\dim(A) = [m/m^2 : k]$ (V, § 3, No. 3, Prop. 1).

#### Example 6 {#ac-viii-s5-n1-exa-6 .statement}

Let $X$ be an algebraic variety over a perfect field $k$. Then $X$ is nonsingular at a point $x$ if and only if the local ring of $X$ at $x$ is regular.

#### Example 7 {#ac-viii-s5-n1-exa-7 .statement}

Let $A$ be a regular local noetherian ring. We shall see later that the local noetherian ring $A_p$ is regular for every prime ideal $p$ of $A$.

#### Proposition 1 {#ac-viii-s5-prop-1 .statement}

Let $A$ and $B$ be local noetherian rings and $\rho : A \to B$ a local homomorphism making $B$ a flat $A$-module. Suppose that one has m_B = B.\rho(m_A). Then dim(A) = dim(B) and B is regular if and only if A is regular.

The first assertion follows from Cor. 1 of Prop. 7 of § 3, No. 4. Since B is flat over A, one can identify m_B^k = B.\rho(m_A^k) with B \otimes_A m_A^k for every integer k \geqslant 0, hence m_B/m_B^2 with B \otimes_A (m_A/m_A^2), or again with \kappa_B \otimes_{\kappa_A} (m_A/m_A^2). One therefore has

(2)
$$
[m_B/m_B^2 : \kappa_B] = [m_A/m_A^2 : \kappa_A],
$$
whence the proposition at once.

#### Corollary {#ac-viii-s5-n1-cor-1 .statement}

A local noetherian ring A is regular if and only if its completion $\hat{A}$ is so.

In fact, $\hat{A}$ is flat over A, and one has $m_{\hat{A}} = \hat{A}.m_A$ (III, § 3, No. 4, Theorem 3 and § 2, No. 12, Cor. 2 to Prop. 16).

### 2. Graded ring associated with a regular local ring

#### Theorem 1 {#ac-viii-s5-thm-1 .statement}

Let A be a local noetherian ring. The following conditions are equivalent:
(i) A is regular.
(ii) The ideal $m_A$ is generated by a secant subset for A (\S 3, No. 2, Def. 1).
(iii) The ideal $m_A$ is generated by a completely secant sequence for A (A, X, p. 157, Def. 2).
(iv) Let S be the symmetric algebra of the $\kappa_A$-vector space $m_A/m_A^2$, and let gr(A) = $\bigoplus_{n \geqslant 0} m_A^n/m_A^{n+1}$ be the graded ring associated with A. The canonical homomorphism $\gamma$ of S onto gr(A) is bijective.
(v) There exists an integer $r \geqslant 0$ such that one has $H_{A,m_A} = (1 - T)^{-r}$, that is to say $m_A = 0$ if $r = 0$ and $[m_A^n/m_A^{n+1} : \kappa_A] = \binom{n + r - 1}{r - 1}$ for every integer $n \geqslant 0$ if $r > 0$.
(vi) One has $H_{A,m_A} = (1 - T)^{-d}$ with $d = \dim(A)$.

If these conditions are fulfilled, every system of coordinates of A is a completely secant sequence for A.

(ii) \Rightarrow (i): for every secant subset has at most $\dim(A)$ elements (§ 3, No. 2, Theorem 1).
(iii) \Rightarrow (ii): for every completely secant sequence is secant (§ 3, No. 2, corollary to Proposition 3).
(iv) \Rightarrow (iii): let $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ be a sequence of elements of $m_A$ whose classes modulo $m_A^2$ form a basis $(\xi_1, ..., \xi_r)$ of $m_A/m_A^2$ over the field $\kappa_A$. If property (iv) is satisfied, $\mathrm{gr}(A)$ is the polynomial algebra $\kappa_A[\xi_1, ..., \xi_r]$ and the sequence $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ is completely secant (A, X, p. 160, Theorem 1). This also proves the last assertion of Theorem 1.
(i) \Rightarrow (iv): put $r = [m_A/m_A^2 : \kappa_A]$. By formula (6) of § 4, No. 2, the Poincaré series of the graded vector space S over the field $\kappa_A$ is equal to

$$
P_S = \sum_{n \geqslant 0} [S^n(m_A/m_A^2) : \kappa_A] T^n = (1 - T)^{-r}.
$$

Suppose that the canonical homomorphism $\gamma : S \to \mathrm{gr}(A)$ is not bijective. Since $\gamma$ is surjective, there exists a homogeneous element $u$ of $S$, of degree $d > 0$, annihilated by $\gamma$. We then have

$$
H_{A,m_A} = P_S - P_{\mathrm{Ker}(\gamma)} \leq P_S - P_{uS} = (1 - T^d)/(1 - T)^r =
$$
$$
= (1 + T + \cdots + T^{d-1})/(1 - T)^{r-1}.
$$

By Theorem 3 of § 4, No. 4 and Lemma 2 of § 4, No. 1, therefore $\dim(A) < r$, and A is not regular.

Finally, let us prove the equivalence of conditions (iv) to (vi). Now (iv) means that one has $H_{A,m_A} = (1 - T)^{-s}$ with $s = [m_A/m_A^2 : \kappa_A]$. Therefore conditions (iv), (v), (vi) mean that one has $H_{A,m_A} = (1 - T)^{-m}$, with respectively $m = [m_A/m_A^2 : \kappa_A]$, $m \geq 0$, $m = \dim(A)$. But if one has $H_{A,m_A} = (1 - T)^{-m}$, then $\dim(A) = m$ by § 4, No. 4, Theorem 3 and $[m_A/m_A^2 : \kappa_A] = m$ (since $(1 - T)^{-m} = 1 + mT + \cdots$). The equivalence of conditions (iv) to (vi) follows immediately from this.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s5-thm-1-cor-1 .statement}

Every regular noetherian local ring is integrally closed, and in particular an integral domain.

Suppose that A is regular. Then $\mathrm{gr}(A)$ is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra in a finite number of indeterminates over a field (Theorem 1, (iv)). Hence $\mathrm{gr}(A)$ is a noetherian integrally closed ring (V, § 1, No. 3, Corollary 3 to Proposition 13), and therefore A is integrally closed (V, § 1, No. 4, Proposition 15).

We shall see in a later chapter that every regular noetherian local ring is factorial.

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s5-thm-1-cor-2 .statement}

Let A and B be noetherian local rings and $\sigma$ a local homomorphism of B into A. Suppose that A is regular and B complete. In order that $\sigma$ be bijective, it is necessary and sufficient that it induce bijections of $\kappa_B$ onto $\kappa_A$ and of $m_B/m_B^2$ onto $m_A/m_A^2$.

The stated condition is obviously necessary.

Suppose conversely that $\sigma$ induces isomorphisms of $\mathrm{gr}_0(B)$ onto $\mathrm{gr}_0(A)$ and of $\mathrm{gr}_1(B)$ onto $\mathrm{gr}_1(A)$. Since the ring $\mathrm{gr}(B)$ is generated by $\mathrm{gr}_0(B) \cup \mathrm{gr}_1(B)$ and $\mathrm{gr}(A)$ is the symmetric algebra of the vector space $\mathrm{gr}_1(A)$ over the field $\mathrm{gr}_0(A)$, the homomorphism $\mathrm{gr}(\sigma)$ is bijective. Hence $\sigma$ is bijective (III, § 2, No. 8, Corollary 3 to Theorem 1).

#### Corollary 3 {#ac-viii-s5-thm-1-cor-3 .statement}

Let k be a field and A a noetherian local k-algebra whose residue field is equal to k. In order that A be regular, it is necessary and sufficient that its completion $\hat{A}$ be isomorphic to a formal power series k-algebra $k[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$.

This follows from the equivalence of (i) and (iv) in Theorem 1, and from Proposition 11 of III, § 2, No. 9.

### 3. Quotients of Regular Local Rings

#### Proposition 2 {#ac-viii-s5-prop-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, $x = (x_1, ..., x_r)$ a sequence of elements of $m_A$ and $x$ the ideal generated by $x$. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) the ring $A$ is regular, and $x$ forms part of a system of coordinates of $A$;
(ii) the ring $A/x$ is regular and $x$ is a secant sequence for $A$;
(iii) the ring $A/x$ is regular and $x$ is a completely secant sequence for $A$.

Moreover, when these conditions are satisfied, $x$ is a prime ideal of $A$.

(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (ii): this follows from the corollary of Prop. 3 of § 3, No. 2.

(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i): suppose that $x$ is a secant sequence for $A$ and that the noetherian local ring $A/x$ is regular. Let $(x_{r+1}, ..., x_d)$ be a sequence of elements of $A$ whose classes modulo $x$ form a system of coordinates of $A/x$. Then the sequence $(x_1, ..., x_d)$ generates the ideal $m_A$ of $A$, and we have

$$
\dim(A) = r + \dim(A/x) = r + (d - r) = d .
$$

It follows that $A$ is regular and that $(x_1, ..., x_d)$ is a system of coordinates of $A$.

(i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii): if condition (i) is satisfied, the sequence $x$ is completely secant (No. 2, Th. 1), hence secant by the corollary of Prop. 3 of § 3, No. 2. We therefore have

(3)
$$
\dim(A/x) = \dim(A) - r ;
$$

moreover, the classes of $x_1, ..., x_r$ modulo $m_A^2$ are linearly independent over the field $\kappa_A$, and therefore we have

(4)
$$
[m_A/(m_A^2 + x) : \kappa_A] = [m_A/m_A^2 : \kappa_A] - r .
$$

Formulas (3) and (4) show that $A/x$ is regular.

Every regular noetherian local ring is an integral domain by Cor. 1 of Th. 1 of No. 2. It follows that $x$ is prime if $A/x$ is regular.

#### Corollary 1 {#ac-viii-s5-prop-2-cor-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring, and $t$ an element of $m_A$. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i) $A$ is regular, and $t$ does not belong to $m_A^2$;
(ii) $A/tA$ is regular and $\dim(A/tA) < \dim(A)$;
(iii) $A/tA$ is regular, and $t$ is not a divisor of 0 in $A$.

#### Corollary 2 {#ac-viii-s5-prop-2-cor-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a regular noetherian local ring, and $q$ an ideal of $A$. Then $A/q$ is regular if and only if $q$ is generated by a subset of a system of coordinates of $A$.

The condition is sufficient by Prop. 2.

Suppose $A/q$ regular, and let $x = (x_1, ..., x_r)$ be a sequence of elements of $q$ whose classes modulo $m_A^2$ form a basis of $(q + m_A^2)/m_A^2$ over the field $\kappa_A$. Let $x$ be the ideal of $A$ generated by $x$. Then $x \subset q$ and $x$ forms part of a system of coordinates of $A$, hence the noetherian local ring $A/x$ is regular (Prop. 2); moreover, the vector spaces $m_A/(q + m_A^2)$ and $m_A/(x + m_A^2)$ have the same dimension over $\kappa_A$. It follows that the regular noetherian local rings $A/q$ and $A/x$ have the same dimension. Since the ideals $q$ and $x$ are prime and one has $x \subset q$, one concludes finally that $q = x$.

#### Example {#ac-viii-s5-n3-exa-1 .statement}

Let $k$ be a field, $A = k[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ and $q$ an ideal of $A$, distinct from $A$. In order that $A/q$ be regular, it is necessary and sufficient that one can find an integer $r \geqslant 0$ and elements $F_1, ..., F_r$ of $A$, generating $q$, and such that the matrix $\left( \frac{\partial F_i}{\partial X_j}(0, ..., 0) \right)$ be of rank $r$ ("jacobian criterion"). One then has $\dim(A/q) = n - r$.

#### Remark {#ac-viii-s5-n3-rem-1 .statement}

Let $A$ be a regular noetherian local ring and $q \subset m_A$ an ideal of $A$ such that $A/q$ is regular. Let $(x_1, ..., x_r)$ be a sequence of elements of $q$ whose classes modulo $m_A^2$ generate the vector space $(q + m_A^2)/m_A^2$ over the field $\kappa_A$. The proof of Cor. 2 shows that the ideal $q$ of $A$ is generated by $(x_1, ..., x_r)$.

### 4. Eisenstein Polynomials

#### Definition 2 {#ac-viii-s5-def-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a ring, $p$ a prime ideal of $A$, and $P$ a polynomial in $A[T]$. One says that $P$ is an Eisenstein polynomial for $p$ if it satisfies the following conditions:
a) $P$ is monic of degree $d \geqslant 1$;
b) one has $P(T) \equiv T^d \mod. pA[T]$;
c) one has $P(0) \notin p^2$.

In other words, a polynomial of Eisenstein for $p$ is a polynomial of the form $P(T) = T^d + \sum_{i=1}^d a_i T^{d-i}$, with $d \geqslant 1$, where $a_1, ..., a_{d-1}$ belong to $p$ and $a_d$ to $p - p^2$.

One says that $P$ is a polynomial of Eisenstein for $pA_p$ if the canonical image of $P$ in the polynomial ring $A_p[T]$ is a polynomial of Eisenstein for the ideal $pA_p$; this also means that $P$ is a polynomial of Eisenstein for $p$ and that it moreover satisfies the following condition, stronger than c):
c’) every element $a$ of $A$ such that $aP(0) \in p^2$ belongs to $p$.

#### Proposition 3 {#ac-viii-s5-prop-3 .statement}

Let $A$ be a ring, $p$ a prime ideal of $A$ and $P \in A[T]$ a polynomial of Eisenstein for $p$.
a) There does not exist a decomposition of the form $P = P_1 P_2$ where $P_1$ and $P_2$ are two monic polynomials of $A[T]$ distinct from 1.
b) Suppose $A$ integrally closed, with field of fractions $K$. Then $P$ is irreducible in $K[T]$.

Let $\varphi$ be the canonical homomorphism of $A$ into the field of fractions $k$ of $A/p$ and let $\varphi': A[T] \to k[T]$ be the extension of $\varphi$ such that $\varphi'(T) = T$. Suppose that one has $P = P_1P_2$ where $P_1$ and $P_2$ are two monic polynomials of $A[T]$ distinct from 1. Then one has $T^d = \varphi'(P_1)\varphi'(P_2)$ in $k[T]$, denoting by $d$ the degree of $P$. If $d_i$ is the degree of $P_i$, one has therefore $\varphi'(P_i) = T^{d_i}$, that is to say $P_i(T) \equiv T^{d_i} \mod pA[T]$, and in particular $P_i(0) \in p$. But then $P(0) = P_1(0).P_2(0)$ belongs to $p^2$, contrary to the hypotheses. This proves $a)$.

Assertion $b)$ results from $a)$ and prop. 11 of V, § 1, no 3.

Let $A$ be a noetherian local ring and $P_1, ..., P_r$ monic polynomials in $A[T]$, of degree $\geq 2$. Let $q$ be the ideal of $A[T_1, ..., T_r]$ generated by $P_1(T_1), ..., P_r(T_r)$ and let $B$ be the quotient $A$-algebra $A[T_1, ..., T_r]/q$. For $1 \leq i \leq r$, let $d_i$ denote the degree of $P_i$, $t_i$ the class of $T_i$ modulo $q$, and $\gamma_i$ the class of $c_i = P_i(0)$ modulo $m_A^2$. Suppose that $P_i(T) \equiv T^{d_i} \mod m_A A[T]$ for $1 \leq i \leq r$.

**Proposition 4. — a)** *The ring $B$ is local and noetherian, with maximal ideal*

$$
m_B = Bm_A + \sum_{i=1}^r Bt_i.
$$

*One has $\dim(A) = \dim(B)$ and $[\kappa_B : \kappa_A] = 1$. The monomials $t_1^{\alpha(1)} ... t_r^{\alpha(r)}$, with $0 \leq \alpha(i) < d_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq r$, form a basis of the $A$-module $B$.*

*b) Let $\lambda$ be the canonical homomorphism of $m_A/m_A^2$ into $m_B/m_B^2$. Then the kernel of $\lambda$ is the $\kappa_A$-vector space generated by $\gamma_1, ..., \gamma_r$. The classes of the elements $t_1, ..., t_r$ form a basis over $\kappa_A$ of the cokernel of $\lambda$.*

*c) For $B$ to be regular, it is necessary and sufficient that $A$ be regular and that $\gamma_1, ..., \gamma_r$ be linearly independent in the $\kappa_A$-vector space $m_A/m_A^2$.*

The $A$-algebra $B$ is isomorphic to the tensor product $B_1 \otimes_A \cdots \otimes_A B_r$ with $B_i = A[T]/(P_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq r$. It follows that the monomials $t_1^{\alpha(1)} ... t_r^{\alpha(r)}$, with $0 \leq \alpha(i) < d_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq r$, form a basis of the $A$-module $B$. In particular, $B$ is integral over $A$, hence $A$ and $B$ have the same dimension by Th. 1 of § 2, No. 3.

According to Cor. 3 of Prop. 9 of IV, § 2, No. 5, the ring $B$ is noetherian, and every maximal ideal of $B$ contains $B.m_A$. Moreover, in view of the assumption made on $P_1, ..., P_r$ and the relation $P_i(t_i) = 0$, one has $t_i^{d_i} \in B.m_A$ for $1 \leq i \leq r$. Hence every maximal ideal of $B$ contains $t_1, ..., t_r$, and therefore also the ideal $q' = B.m_A + Bt_1 + \cdots + Bt_r$. Now one has $m_A = A \cap q'$ and $B = A + q'$, hence $B/q'$ is isomorphic to $A/m_A$ and $q'$ is a maximal ideal of $B$; consequently, $B$ is local and one has $[\kappa_B : \kappa_A] = 1$. This proves $a)$.

Put $r = m_A^2 + \sum_{i=1}^r Ac_i$, and denote by $\varphi$ the canonical homomorphism of $(A/m_A^2)[T_1, ..., T_r]$ onto $B/m_B^2$. Since one has $m_B = B.m_A + \sum_{i=1}^r Bt_i$, the kernel $n$ of $\varphi$ is the ideal generated by the classes $\overline{P}_i(T_i)$ of the polynomials $P_i(T_i)$ modulo $m_A^2.A[T_1, ..., T_r]$ and the monomials $T_iT_j$ and $xT_i$ for $1 \leq i, j \leq r$ and $x$ in $m_A/m_A^2$. According to the assumption made on $P_i$, namely $P_i(T) \equiv T^{d_i} \mod m_A.A[T]$, one can replace $\overline{P}_i(T_i)$ by $\gamma_i$ in this description of $n$; consequently, the ring $B/m_B^2$ is isomorphic to the quotient of $(A/r)[T_1, ..., T_r]$ by the graded ideal generated by the monomials $T_i T_j$ and $x T_i$ for $x$ in $m_A / r$. Let $\tau_i$ denote the class of $t_i$ modulo $m_B^2$; one therefore has

$$
B / m_B^2 = (A/r) \oplus \kappa_A \tau_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \kappa_A \tau_r,
$$

whence

$$
m_B / m_B^2 = (m_A / r) \oplus \kappa_A \tau_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \kappa_A \tau_r.
$$

Assertion $b)$ follows at once from this.

According to formula (6) and the relation $[ \kappa_B : \kappa_A ] = 1$, one has

$$
[m_B / m_B^2 : \kappa_B] = [m_A / m^2 : \kappa_A] + \{ r - [r / m_A^2 : \kappa_A] \}.
$$

Now, the $\kappa_A$-vector space $r / m_A^2$ is generated by $\gamma_1, ..., \gamma_r$ and one has

$$
\dim(B) = \dim(A) \leq [m_A / m_A^2 : \kappa_A].
$$

Assertion $c)$ follows at once from formulas (7) and (8).

#### Corollary {#ac-viii-s5-n4-cor-1 .statement}

*Let A be a regular noetherian local ring and let P $\in A[T]$ be an Eisenstein polynomial for $m_A$. The ring $B = A[T]/(P)$ is a regular noetherian local ring, of the same dimension as A, and one has $[ \kappa_A : \kappa_B ] = 1$. Finally, one has $m_B = B.m_A + Bt$, where t is the class of T modulo (P).*

The case where P has degree $\geq 2$ follows from prop. 4, where one takes $r = 1$; when P has degree 1, that is to say is of the form $T - c$ with $c \in m_A$, the corollary is immediate.

#### Proposition 5 {#ac-viii-s5-prop-5 .statement}

*Let A be an integral domain, with field of fractions K, and let L be an algebraic extension of finite degree of K. We denote by B the integral closure of A in L and by p a prime ideal of A.

Suppose that the local ring $A_p$ is noetherian and regular; let t be an element of L such that $L = K(t)$ and suppose that there exists in $A[T]$ an element P, an Eisenstein polynomial for $pA_p$, having t as a root.

a) There exists in B a unique prime ideal q above p.
b) The local ring $B_q$ is noetherian and regular, of the same dimension as $A_p$.
c) One has $B_q = A_p[t]$.
d) The canonical homomorphism of $A/p$ into $B/q$ induces an isomorphism of the fields of fractions of these rings.*

Put $C = A_p[t]$ and let d denote the degree of P. By prop. 3 applied to the ring $A_p$, the Eisenstein polynomial P is irreducible in $K[T]$ and $(1, t, ..., t^{d-1})$ is a basis of L over K, hence of C over $A_p$. Since P is monic, the kernel of the canonical homomorphism of $A_p[T]$ onto C is equal to (P). By the corollary to prop. 4 above, C is therefore a regular noetherian local ring of the same dimension as $A_p$, the maximal ideal $m_C$ of C is generated by $p \cup \{ t \}$ and the field $\kappa_C$ is a trivial extension of the field of fractions of $A/p$. In order to prove prop. 5, it is therefore enough to show that there exists a unique prime ideal q of B above p, and that one has $C = B_q$.

#### Corollary {#ac-viii-s5-n4-cor-2 .statement}

Suppose that A_p is a discrete valuation ring. Then B_q is a discrete valuation ring, t is a uniformizer of B_q, and one has

$$
f(B_q/A_p) = 1 , \quad e(B_q/A_p) = [L : K]
$$

(VI, § 8, n° 1).

In fact, discrete valuation rings are regular noetherian local rings of dimension 1; putting d = [L : K], one has t^d \in m_A - m_A^2, whence d = e(B_q/A_p). One has [\kappa_B : \kappa_A] = 1, whence f(B_q/A_p) = 1.

#### Example 1 {#ac-viii-s5-n4-exa-1 .statement}

Put A = \mathbf{Z} and L = \mathbf{Q}(p^{1/d}), where p is a prime number and d an integer \geqslant 2. Let B denote the integral closure of \mathbf{Z} in L. Since the polynomial T^d - p of \mathbf{Z}[T] is an Eisenstein polynomial for p\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}, there exists a unique prime ideal q of B above p\mathbf{Z}. There therefore exists a unique normalised discrete valuation v of the field \mathbf{Q}(p^{1/d}) such that v(p) > 0; one has [L : K] = v(p) = d, and B/q is a field with p elements. The ring B_q of the valuation v is equal to \mathbf{Z}_{(p)}[p^{1/d}].

#### Example 2 {#ac-viii-s5-n4-exa-2 .statement}

Put A = \mathbf{Z} and L = R_{pf}(\mathbf{Q}) where p is a prime number and f an integer \geqslant 1 (cf. A, V, p. 78). Thus L = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta) with \zeta = \exp(2\pi i/p^f). Let B denote the integral closure of \mathbf{Z} in L and P the polynomial of \mathbf{Z}[T] such that

$$
P(T - 1) = (T^{p^f} - 1)/(T^{p^{f-1}} - 1) .
$$

Put d = p^f - p^{f-1}. One has P(\zeta - 1) = 0, P(0) = p and

$$
P(T - 1) \equiv (T - 1)^d \mod. p\mathbf{Z}[T] ,
$$

whence P(T) \equiv T^d. It follows that P is an Eisenstein polynomial for p\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}. There is therefore a unique prime ideal q of B above p\mathbf{Z}, and one has B_q = \mathbf{Z}_{(p)}[\zeta] ; moreover, \zeta - 1 is a uniformizer of B_q and one has

$$
[L : K] = d = p^f - p^{f-1} .
$$

If v is the unique normalised valuation of \mathbf{Q}(\zeta) such that v(p) > 0, one has v(p) = d. Moreover, the field B/q has p elements. One can prove (cf. p. 96, exerc. 13) that B is equal to \mathbf{Z}[\zeta].

\* 5. Structure of complete regular noetherian local rings

In this number, we use definitions and results from chapter IX.

Let A be a complete regular noetherian local ring; let $p$ denote the characteristic of its residue field $\kappa_A$, and distinguish two cases.

A) $p = 0$. Then (IX, § 3, no 3, th. 1), there exists a subfield K of A such that the canonical projection of A onto $\kappa_A$ induces an isomorphism of K onto $\kappa_A$. Applying then cor. 3 of th. 1 of no 2 to the K-algebra A, one deduces:

#### Proposition 6 {#ac-viii-s5-prop-6 .statement}

Let A be a complete regular noetherian local ring, whose residue field $\kappa_A$ has characteristic 0. Put $n = \dim(A)$. Then A is isomorphic to the ring of formal power series $\kappa_A[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$.

B) $p \neq 0$. A Cohen subring of A is any subring V of A which is a $p$-ring such that $A = m_A + V$ (IX, § 2, no 2, def. 2). The ring V is local; its maximal ideal $m_V$ is generated by $p.1_V$; hence one has $m_A \cap V = m_V$ and the canonical injection of V into A defines by passage to the quotient an isomorphism of the field $\kappa_V$ onto the field $\kappa_A$. If $p.1_A = 0$, V is a field of characteristic $p$. Otherwise V is a discrete valuation ring whose field of fractions has characteristic zero (IX, § 2, no 1, cor. 1 to prop. 1). One proves (IX, no 2, th. 1) that A possesses Cohen subrings.

#### Example 3 {#ac-viii-s5-n4-exa-3 .statement}

Let k be a field of characteristic $p \neq 0$ and let $n$ be a positive integer. The ring of formal series $k[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ is complete regular local noetherian, of dimension $n$, and k is a Cohen subring of $k[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$.

#### Example 4 {#ac-viii-s5-n4-exa-4 .statement}

Let V be a complete discrete valuation ring and let $n$ be a positive integer. The ring of formal series $V_n = V[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ is complete regular local noetherian, of dimension $n + 1$, and V is a Cohen subring of $V_n$.

#### Example 5 {#ac-viii-s5-n4-exa-5 .statement}

Let us keep the preceding notation. A polynomial P in $V_n[T]$ of degree $d \geq 2$ is said to be special if it is of the form $T^d + \sum_{i=1}^d a_i T^{d-i}$, where $a_1, ..., a_{d-1}$ belong to $m_{V_n}$, $a_d$ belongs to $m_V + m_{V_n}^2$ but not to $m_{V_n}^2$. In particular, P is an Eisenstein polynomial for $m_{V_n}$. Put $A = V_n[T]/(P)$. By the corollary of Prop. 4 of No. 4, the ring A is complete regular local noetherian, of dimension $n + 1$. If t is the class of T modulo (P), the sequence $(1, t, ..., t^{d-1})$ is a basis of the $V_n$-module A, and $(X_1, ..., X_n, t)$ is a system of coordinates of A: in fact, there exists a uniformiser $\pi$ of V such that $a_d \equiv \pi \mod. m_{V_n}^2$; since one also has
$$
a_d = -t(t^{d-1} + a_1 t^{d-2} + \cdots + a_{d-1}),
$$
one has $\pi \in m_A^2$; since $m_A$ is generated by $\{\pi, X_1, ..., X_n, t\}$, this proves our assertion. Moreover, V is a Cohen subring of A, for $\kappa_V$ is identified with $\kappa_{V_n}$, and $\kappa_{V_n}$ with $\kappa_A$ by the corollary to Prop. 4 of No. 4.

#### Theorem 2 {#ac-viii-s5-thm-2 .statement}

Let $A$ be a complete regular local noetherian ring whose residue field is of characteristic $\neq 0$, and let $V$ be a Cohen subring of $A$. Put $n = \dim(A)$.

a) Suppose that $V$ is a field. Then the $V$-algebra $A$ is isomorphic to the algebra of formal series $V[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$.

b) Suppose that $V$ is a complete discrete valuation ring and that one has $m_V \not\subset m_A^2$. Then the $V$-algebra $A$ is isomorphic to the algebra of formal series $V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}]]$.

c) Suppose that $V$ is a complete discrete valuation ring and that one has $m_V \subset m_A^2$. Then there exists a special polynomial $P$ in $V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}]] [T]$ and a $V$-isomorphism of $A$ onto $V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}]] [T]/(P)$.

Assertion a) follows at once from Cor. 3 of Th. 1 of No. 2.

Let us prove b). Let $(x_1, ..., x_m)$ be a sequence of elements of $m_A$, and let $\varphi_0$ be the homomorphism of $V[X_1, ..., X_m]$ into $A$ which coincides with the identity on $V$ and sends $X_i$ to $x_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq m$. If $a$ is the ideal of $V[X_1, ..., X_m]$ generated by $X_1, ..., X_m$, one has $\varphi_0(a) \subset m_A$, hence $\varphi_0$ extends by continuity to a homomorphism $\varphi$ of $V_m = V[[X_1, ..., X_m]]$ into $A$. Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer of $V$. By Corollary 2 of Theorem 1 of No. 2, $\varphi$ is an isomorphism of $V_m$ onto $A$ if and only if $(\pi, x_1, ..., x_m)$ is a system of coordinates of $A$. But the canonical mapping of $m_V/m_V^2$ into $m_A/m_A^2$ is injective, since one has $m_V \not\subset m_A^2$ and $m_V/m_V^2$ is of rank 1 over $\kappa_V$. Hence $m_A/(m_V + m_A^2)$ is of rank $n - 1$ and $(\pi, x_1, ..., x_m)$ is a system of coordinates of $A$ if and only if the classes of the $x_i$ form a basis of $m_A/(m_V + m_A^2)$ over $\kappa_V$. Hence b).

Let us prove c). Let $(y_1, ..., y_n)$ be a system of coordinates of $A$. As above, put $V_n = V[[Y_1, ..., Y_n]]$ and consider the homomorphism $\varphi$ of $V_n$ into $A$ which coincides with the identity on $V$ and sends $Y_i$ to $y_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$. Then $\mathrm{gr}(\varphi)$ is surjective, hence $\varphi$ is surjective (III, § 2, no 8, Corollary 2 of Theorem 1). The kernel $p$ of $\varphi$ is a prime ideal of $V_n$; since one has

$$
\dim(V_n) = n + 1 = \dim(V_n/p) + 1,
$$

the prime ideal $p$ is of height 1 (§ 1, no 3, Proposition 8). But the ring $V_n$ is factorial by Proposition 8 of VII, § 3, no 9; consequently, the ideal $p$ is principal (VII, § 3, no 2, Theorem 1).

Let $R$ be a generator of the ideal $p$ of $V_n$. By Lemma 3 of VII, § 3, No. 7, there exist integers $u(1), ..., u(n-1)$ at least equal to 1, and an isomorphism $\sigma$ of $V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}, T]]$ onto $V_n$ such that

$$
\begin{align*}
\sigma(X_i) &= Y_i + Y_n^{u(i)} \quad \text{for } 1 \leq i < n \\
\sigma(T) &= Y_n
\end{align*}
$$

and such that $\sigma^{-1}(R) = Q$ satisfies $Q(0, ..., 0, T) \neq 0$. Moreover, by the preparation theorem (VII, § 3, No. 8, Prop. 6), there exists a polynomial P in V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}]] [T] of the form

$$
P = T^d + \sum_{i=1}^d a_i(X_1, ..., X_{n-1}) T^{d-i},
$$

generating the same ideal as Q in V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}, T]], and such that $a_i(0, ..., 0) \in m_V$ for $1 \leq i \leq d$. It is deduced that A is V-isomorphic to V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}, T]]/(P). But V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}, T]] is the direct sum of the ideal (P) and of the V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}]]-module with basis 1, T, ..., $T^{d-1}$ (VII, § 3, No. 8, Prop. 5); hence A is V-isomorphic to V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}]] [T]/(P).

Put C = V[[X_1, ..., X_{n-1}]] and denote by α the class of $a_d(X_1, ..., X_{n-1})$ modulo $m_C^2$. We have $\kappa_V = \kappa_C = \kappa_A$. By Prop. 4 of No. 4, the kernel of the canonical homomorphism from $m_C/m_C^2$ into $m_A/m_A^2$ is equal to $\kappa_C \alpha$. Since the image of $m_V/m_V^2$ in $m_A/m_A^2$ is zero and $m_V/m_V^2$ is of rank 1 over $\kappa_C$, it follows that $a_d$ belongs to $m_V + m_C^2$, but not to $m_C^2$. Consequently, the polynomial P is special, as was to be proved.
