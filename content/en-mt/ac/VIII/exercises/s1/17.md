a) Let A be an integral domain and $f \in A[T]$, $f \neq 0$. Now prove that there exists a maximal ideal m of $A[T]$ such that $f \notin m$ (take a maximal ideal containing $T.f - 1$).
b) Let A be a ring. Now prove that $\dim(\mathrm{Specmax}(A[T])) \geq \dim(A) + 1$. (Let $p_0 \subset p_1 \cdots \subset p_n$ be a chain of prime ideals of A. Consider the chain
$$
p_0[T] \subset p_1[T] \subset p_2[T] \cdots \subset p_n[T] \subset (p_n, T)
$$
of prime ideals of $A[T]$ and put
$$
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
U_i = V(p_i[T]) \cap \mathrm{Specmax}(A[T]), \quad 0 \leq i \leq n \\
U_{n+1} = V(p_n, T) \cap \mathrm{Specmax}(A[T]).
\end{array}
\right.
$$
Now prove using a) that $(U_i)_{0 \leq i \leq n+1}$ is a strictly increasing sequence of irreducible closed subsets of $\mathrm{Specmax}(A[T])$.
c) Let $k$ be a field, S the subset of $k[X, Y]$ formed by the elements of the form $1 + Xg(X, Y)$, and $A = S^{-1}k[X, Y]$.
Now prove that $\dim(\mathrm{Specmax}(A)) = 1$ and $\dim(\mathrm{Spec}(A)) = 2$. Generalize to the case of several indeterminates.
d) Now prove that for every pair of integers $0 \leq n < m$, there exists a noetherian ring A such that $\dim(\mathrm{Specmax}(A)) = n$ and $\dim(\mathrm{Specmax}(A[T])) = m$.
e) Let A be the noetherian ring defined in exerc. 13. Now prove that $\dim(\mathrm{Specmax}(A)) = 1$ and $\dim(\mathrm{Specmax}(A[T])) = \infty$.

1 This example is due to M. Nagata, Local Rings, Interscience Publishers, 1960.
