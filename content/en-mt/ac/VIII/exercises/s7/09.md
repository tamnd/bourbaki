(a) Let A and a be as above and let b be an ideal of A containing a. Now prove that the following conditions are equivalent:
(i) Every element of b is integral over a.
(ii) The $\mathcal{A}(a)$-graded algebra $\mathcal{A}(b)$ is integral.
(iii) The P(a)-graded algebra P(b) is integral.
(iv) There exists an integer $k \geqslant 0$ such that $a.b^k = b^{k+1}$ and hence $a^n.b^k = b^{k+n}$ for all positive integers n. We say that b is integral over a.
(b) Now prove that there exists a greatest ideal among those which are integral over a. It is denoted $\overline{a}$ and is called the integral closure of a in A. Verify that if $a \subset b$ then $\overline{a} \subset \overline{b}$ and that $\overline{a} = \overline{a}$. Now prove that if the integral closure $\overline{P(a)}$ is a P(a)-finitely generated module, then $a.a^k = a^{k+1}$ for k sufficiently large.
(c) Now prove that if $a \in A$ satisfies the integral dependence relation
$$
a^k + b_1 a^{k-1} + \cdots + b_k = 0 \quad \text{with} \quad k \geqslant 1 \quad \text{and} \quad b_i \in a^i,
$$
then $a.(a + Aa)^{k-1} = (a + Aa)^k$. Deduce that the integral closure of a in A coincides with the set of elements of A which are integral over a.
