It is said that on an $A$-module $E$ a linear topology $\mathcal{C}$ is *minimal* if it is separated and if there does not exist a separated linear topology strictly less fine than $\mathcal{C}$.

*a*) For a separated linear topology $\mathcal{C}$ on $E$ to be minimal it is necessary and sufficient that every filter base $\mathcal{B}$ on $E$, formed by affine linear varieties, and having a single adherent point, converge to this point. (To see that the condition is necessary, observe that when $M$ runs through $\mathcal{B}$ and $V$ a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $0$ formed by submodules, the $M + V$ form a filter base having the same adherent points as $\mathcal{B}$. To see that the condition is sufficient, remark that a filter base formed by open submodules, whose intersection is reduced to $0$, is a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $0$ for a separated linear topology less fine than $\mathcal{C}$.)

*b*) For the discrete topology on an $A$-module $E$ to be minimal it is necessary and sufficient that $E$ be artinian.

*c*) If $\mathcal{C}$ is minimal, the topology induced by $\mathcal{C}$ on every closed submodule of $E$ is minimal.
