c) Let $f(Y)$ be an invertible formal series of K[[Y]], which is not an element of K(Y). If c is the ideal of B generated by X and Xf(Y), prove that on c the $\mathfrak{n}$-adic topology is strictly finer than the topology induced on c by the $\mathfrak{n}$-adic topology of B. Prove that the canonical mapping $\hat{B} \otimes_B c \to \hat{c}$ (where $\hat{c}$ is the completion of c for the $\mathfrak{n}$-adic topology) is not injective (consider the images of $f(Y) \otimes X$ and of $1 \otimes Xf(Y)$; to prove that these two elements are distinct in $\hat{B} \otimes_B c$, consider this tensor product as a quotient of the tensor product $\hat{B} \otimes_{K(Y)} c$).

d) Let $f_1(Y), f_2(Y)$ be two invertible formal series of K[[Y]] such that 1, $f_1$ and $f_2$ are linearly independent over K(Y). Let $c_1, c_2$ be the principal ideals of B generated respectively by X$f_1(Y)$ and X$f_2(Y)$; the $\mathfrak{n}$-adic topologies on $c_1$ and $c_2$ are respectively identical with the topologies induced by that of B, and the closures of these ideals in $\hat{A} = \hat{B}$ are both identical with the principal ideal $\hat{A}X$ of $\hat{A}$. Deduce that the closure of $c_1 \cap c_2$ in $\hat{A}$ is not equal to $\bar{c}_1 \cap \bar{c}_2$, and that the closure of $c_1 : c_2$ in $\hat{A}$ is not equal to $\bar{c}_1 : \bar{c}_2$.

15) a) Let A be a noetherian integral ring, m an ideal of A, $\hat{A}$ the separated completion of A for the m-adic topology. Prove that if M is a torsion-free A-module, then, for every element b which is not a divisor of 0 in $\hat{A}$, the homothety of ratio b in the $\hat{A}$-module $\hat{A} \otimes_A M$ is injective. (Reduce to the case where M is of finite type; there exists then a maximal free system $(m_j)_{1 \leq j \leq n}$ in M and an $a \in A$ such that for every $m \in M$, one has $am = \sum a_j m_j$ with $a_j \in A$; every $x \in \hat{A} \otimes_A M$ is therefore such that $ax = \sum b_j \otimes m_j$ with $b_j \in \hat{A}$; use then the flatness of the A-module $\hat{A}$.)

b) Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, B the polynomial ring K[X, Y], P(X, Y) = X(X^2 + Y^2) + (X^2 - Y^2). Prove that the ideal BP is prime in B; consider the quotient ring $A = B/BP$ which is integral and noetherian. Let m be the maximal ideal of A, canonical image of the maximal ideal $n = BX + BY$ of B. Prove that the separated completion $\hat{A}$ of A for the m-adic topology is not integral (observe that in the formal series ring $K[[X, Y]]$, P decomposes into a product of two formal series (“double point of an irreducible cubic”)).

16) Let A be a commutative noetherian ring, $m$ an ideal of A, E a finitely generated A-module endowed with the m-adic topology. For every infinite set I, denote by $E_I$ the set of families $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ of elements of E such that $\lim x_i = 0$ according to the filter of complements of finite subsets of I; it is a submodule of the product A-module $E^I$.

a) Prove that if $0 \to E' \to E \to E'' \to 0$ is an exact sequence of finitely generated A-modules, the corresponding sequence $0 \to E'_I \to E_I \to E''_I \to 0$ is exact.

b) Define a canonical A-homomorphism $A_I \otimes_A E \to E_I$ for every A-module E and prove that it is an isomorphism (verify it first when E is free of finite type, then use a)).

c) Deduce from b) that $A_I$ is a faithfully flat A-module.

a) Let K be a commutative field, $A = K[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ the ring of formal series in $n$ indeterminates, with coefficients in K, V a vector space over K. With the notations of § 2, No. 6, Example 1, denote by $V[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ the vector space $V^{N^n}$ endowed with the a-module structure defined by $(\sum c_\alpha X^\alpha)(v_\alpha) = (w_\alpha)$, where $w_\alpha = \sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha} c_\beta v_\gamma$. Prove that if V admits a basis whose set of indices is I, the a-module $V[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ is isomorphic to $A^I$ if I is finite, to the a-module $A_I$ defined in Exercise 16 if I is infinite. Deduce that $V[[X_1, ..., X_n]]$ is a flat A-module, and is faithfully flat if V is not reduced to 0.

In order that $\hat{B} = B$, it is necessary and sufficient that one have $n = 0$ or $[L : K] < +\infty$.

$§ 19)$ Let $A$ be a commutative noetherian ring, $m$ an ideal of $A$, $S$ a multiplicative subset of $A$. Endow $A$ with the $m$-adic topology.

a) Now prove that the ring $A\{ S^{-1} \}$ ($§ 2$, exer. 27) is a flat $A$-module.

b) Let $S'$ be a second multiplicative subset of $A$, contained in $S$. Now prove that $A\{ S^{-1} \}$ is a flat $(A\{ {S'}^{-1} \})$-module (use exer. 27 d) of $§ 2$).

c) Now prove that $A\{ S^{-1} \}$ is a flat $A_{\{ S \}}$-module ($§ 2$, exer. 27 g)).

d) Suppose that $S = A - p$, where $p$ is an open prime ideal of $A$. Now prove that $A\{ S^{-1} \}$ is a faithfully flat $A_{\{ S \}}$-module, and deduce that the ring $A_{\{ S \}}$ is noetherian (cf. $§ 2$, exer. 27 g)).

20) Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $m$ an ideal of $A$; endow $A$ with the $m$-adic topology. Let $B$ be a commutative topological $A$-algebra ($§ 2$, exer. 28); suppose that $B$ is a Zariski ring; let $\mathfrak{n}$ be a defining ideal of $B$. Now prove that if $M$ is a finitely generated $A$-module, endowed with the $m$-adic topology, then, on the $B$-module $B \otimes_A M$, the tensor product of the topology of $B$ and the $m$-adic topology of $M$ is the $\mathfrak{n}$-adic topology of $B \otimes_A M$; consequently the completed tensor product $(B \otimes_A M)^{\sim}$ is isomorphic to $\widehat{B} \otimes_A M$.

21) Let $A$ be a commutative noetherian ring, $m$ an ideal of $A$, $M$ and $N$ two finitely generated $A$-modules.

a) Suppose that $M$ is separated for the $m$-adic topology. Now prove that in $\mathrm{Hom}_A(M, N)$, endowed with the $m$-adic topology, the set of injective homomorphisms is open (use No. 1, prop. 2, and the Artin-Rees lemma).

b) Suppose that $A$ is a complete Zariski ring, $m$ a defining ideal of $A$. For every integer $i$, let $A_i = A/m^{i+1}$, $M_i = M/m^{i+1}M$, $N_i = N/m^{i+1}N$; now prove that the topological $A$-module $\mathrm{Hom}_A(M, N)$ is isomorphic to $\lim \leftarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{A_i}(M_i, N_i)$. Deduce that in $\mathrm{Hom}_A(M, N)$, the set of surjective homomorphisms is open.

¶ 24) An (commutative or noncommutative) A ring, not reduced to 0, is said to be principal if it has no nonzero zero divisor and if every left or right ideal of A is monogenic. Such a ring is left and right noetherian.

a) Show that for every element $c \neq 0$ of A, $A/Ac$ is an A-module of finite length. (Observe that if a decreasing sequence of ideals $Aa_n$ of A contains $Ac$, one has $c = b_n a_n$ for every $n$, and consider the right ideals $b_n A$.)

b) Show that every submodule of a free A-module (on the left or on the right) is free (same reasoning as in Alg., Chapter VII, § 3, Theorem 1).

c) In every A-module M, the set of non-free elements of M is a submodule T of M, called the torsion submodule of M (use Chapter II, § 2, Ex. 14 a)); one says that M is a torsion module if T = M; one says that M is torsion-free if T = 0.

d) Show that every finitely generated torsion-free A-module is free (use Chapter II, § 2, Ex. 23 b)).

e) Show that every finitely generated A-module is a direct sum of a free module and a torsion module.

f) Let a be a two-sided ideal ≠ 0 of A. Show that there exists an element a ∈ a and an automorphism σ of A such that a = Aa = aA and that ax = σ(x)a for every x ∈ A. (If b is a generator of the left ideal a, there exists an endomorphism τ of A such that bx = τ(x)b for every x ∈ A; show that if a = ub is a generator of the right ideal a, u is invertible using Alg., Chapter VIII, § 2, Ex. 8 b).)

¶ 25) Let A be a principal ideal domain (Ex. 24), a a two-sided ideal ≠ 0 of A, a an element of a having the properties stated in Ex. 24 f). We denote by P{M} the following property: M is a left A-module and every finitely generated submodule of M is annihilated by a power of a.

a) Show that the conditions α), β) and γ) of Ex. 22 are satisfied (consider the homothety a_M and reason as in Ex. 23 a), observing that Ker(a_M^r) and Im(a_M^r) are submodules of M).

b) Show that every torsion A-module M (Ex. 24 c)) is a direct sum of a submodule M_a which has the property P, and of a submodule M'_a such that the restriction to M'_a of the homothety a_M is bijective (observe that if N is a torsion A-module and if a_N is injective, then a_N is bijective; one will reduce for this to the case where N is monogenic, and use Ex. 24 a) as well as Alg., Chapter VIII, § 2, No. 2, Lemma 2).

α) I ∩ S ≠ Ø ;   β) I + a = A ;   γ) (A/I)_a = 0 (with the notations of b)) ; δ) for every x ∈ A, there exists s ∈ S such that sx ∈ I.

Deduce that A has a ring of fractions (on the left or on the right) for S (Chapter II, § 2, Ex. 22), which is principal and whose only non-zero two-sided ideals are generated by the canonical images of the ideals a^n; moreover the canonical mapping of A into this ring of fractions is injective.

d) Suppose now that the two-sided ideal a is maximal. Show that for every integer n > 0, the ring A/a^n is isomorphic to a matrix ring M_r(B_n) over a completely primary ring (Alg., Chapter VIII, § 6, Ex. 20). If b_n is the maximal ideal of B_n, show that b_n^n = 0, that every ideal (on the left or on the right) of B_n is of the form b_n^k and is monogenic. (Observe on the one hand that a/a^n = M_r(b_n) (Alg., Chapter VIII, § 6, Ex. 5), and on the other hand that for k < n, b_n^k/b_n^{k+1} is necessarily a simple B_n-module, otherwise M_r(b_n^k) could not be a monogenic (A/a^n)-module.)

Deduce that the completion $\widehat{A}$ of A for the topology $\mathcal{C}_P(A_s)$ is a matrix ring $M_r(B)$ over a ring B without any divisor of zero other than 0, and such that every ideal (on the left or on the right) is a power of the same maximal two-sided ideal.

¶ 27) Let A be a ring, B a commutative A-algebra which is a Zariski ring, N a finitely generated B-module.

a) Suppose that, for an ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ of B contained in the radical of B, the A-modules $N/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}N$ are flat for every $n \geqslant 0$. Show that N is a flat A-module. (If $\varphi : M \to M'$ is an injective homomorphism of A-modules of *type fini*, it is necessary to prove that $u : \varphi \otimes 1 : M \otimes_A N \to M' \otimes_A N$ is injective. Reduce to proving that, if one puts $N_n = N/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}N$ and $u_n = \varphi \otimes 1_{N_n}$, the homomorphism $\lim \leftarrow u_n$ is injective, using the fact that the $\mathfrak{J}$-adic topologies on $M \otimes_A N$ and $M' \otimes_A N$ are separated.)

b) Let b be an element contained in the radical of B and such that the homothety of ratio b in N is injective. Show that if N/bN is a flat A-module, N is a flat A-module (reduce to the case of a)).

\operatorname{gr}(N \otimes_A M) & \xrightarrow{\operatorname{gr}(u \otimes 1)} & \operatorname{gr}(P \otimes_A M)
\end{array}
$$
