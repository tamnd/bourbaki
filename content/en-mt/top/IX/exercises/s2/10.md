¶ 17) Let X be a metric space such that, for every $x \in X$, there exists an open ball with center $x$ which, considered as a subspace of X, admits a countable basis; let $r_x$ be the supremum of the radii of the balls with center $x$ which possess this property.

a) Now prove (with the aid of Zorn’s theorem) that there exists a maximal family $(B_\alpha)$ of open balls of X, pairwise without common point, such that, if $x_\alpha$ is the center of $B_\alpha$, the radius of $B_\alpha$ is $< r_{x_\alpha}$. Now prove that the union of the $B_\alpha$ is everywhere dense in X; deduce that there exists an everywhere dense subset M of X such that every open ball with center an arbitrary point $x \in X$ and radius $< r_x$ contains only a countably infinite number of points of M (remark that such a ball can meet only a countably infinite number of open sets pairwise without common point, and use IX, p. 18, prop. 12).

b) For every point $x \in M$, denote by $S_x$ the open ball with center $x$ and radius $\frac{1}{3} r_x$; now prove that the $S_x$ constitute a covering of X, and that an arbitrary point of X can belong to only a countably infinite number of $S_x$: remark that, if $y \in S_x$, one has
$$
d(x, y) \leq \frac{1}{2} r_y.
$$

c) One defines in X the following equivalence relation R between two points $x, y$: there exists a sequence $(z_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ of points of M, such that $x \in S_{z_1}, y \in S_{z_n}$, and that the intersection of $S_{z_i}$ and $S_{z_{i+1}}$ is not empty for $1 \leq i \leq n - 1$. Now prove that the equivalence classes following R are subspaces both open and closed of X, each of which admits a countable basis; in other words, X is a topological sum of metric spaces admitting a countable basis (I, p. 15). In particular, every locally compact metrizable space is a topological sum of locally compact metrizable spaces of countable type.

¶ 18) In a metric space, every relatively compact set is bounded. Now prove that, in order that a metrizable space X be such that there exists a distance compatible with the topology of X, for which every bounded set is relatively compact, it is necessary and sufficient that X be locally compact and admit a countable basis (to see that the condition is necessary, show that if every bounded set is relatively compact, X is locally compact and countable at infinity; to see that the condition is sufficient, remark that X is metrizable by virtue of IX, p. 19, corollary; if $d$ is a distance compatible with the topology of X, $f$ the function defined in exerc. 12 d) of IX, p. 86, take on X the uniform structure defined by the two deviations $d(x, y)$ and $|f(x) - f(y)|$).

b) In the Stone-Čech compactification $\beta \mathbf{N}$ of the discrete space $\mathbf{N}$, let X be the subspace union of $\mathbf{N}$ and a set $\{\omega\}$ reduced to a single point of $\beta \mathbf{N} - \mathbf{N}$. 21) Let X be a locally compact, metrizable space of countable type, C a compact metrizable and connected space, which is supposed to be embedded in $\mathbf{I}^\mathbf{N}$ (IX, p. 18, prop. 12).

a) If $d$ is a distance defining the topology of C, now prove that there exists in C an infinite everywhere dense sequence $(b_n)$ such that $d(b_n, b_{n+1})$ tends to 0 with $1/n$ (II, p. 32, prop. 5).

b) Let X' be the Alexandroff compactification of X (I, p. 67, th. 4), ω its point at infinity, d’ a distance defining the topology of X’ (IX, p. 21, corollary). Let (x_n) be a sequence of points of X tending to ω in X’ and such that d’(ω, x_n) < d’(ω, x_{n-1}) and d’(ω, x_n) < 1/n. One defines a mapping f from X into I^N by putting f(x) = b_1 if d’(ω, x) > d’(ω, x_1) and

$$
f(x) = tb_k + (1 - t)b_{k+1}
$$

if d’(ω, x) = td’(ω, x_k) + (1 - t)d’(ω, x_{k+1}) with 0 ≤ t ≤ 1. Now prove that f is continuous in X.

c) Let G be the graph of f in X × I^N, which is homeomorphic to X. Now prove that the closure Y = \overline{G} of G in X’ × I^N is a compact space, union of the disjoint sets G and {ω} × C.

d) Suppose that X is connected and locally connected. Now prove that, in order that, for every compact metrizable space Z containing an open everywhere dense subspace X_1 homeomorphic to X, Z − X_1 be connected, it is necessary and sufficient that X have only one end (I, p. 117, exerc. 19).

¶ 27) An topological space $X$ is said to be submetrizable if its topology is finer than a topology of a metrizable space. A submetrizable space is separated, but not necessarily regular (I, p. 103, exerc. 20).
a) In order that a completely regular space $X$ be submetrizable, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist on $X$ a uniform structure compatible with its topology and defined by a family of distances $\Phi$ containing at least one distance $d$ (cf. IX, p. 84, exerc. 3). Let then $\hat{X}$ be the completion of $X$ for this uniform structure; the distance $d$ extends to a distance $\bar{d}$ on $\hat{X}$; now prove that for every $x \in \hat{X}$, there exists at most one point $y \in X$ such that $\bar{d}(x, y) = 0$.
b) Prove that if $X$ is a completely regular submetrizable space, there exists a uniform structure compatible with the topology of $X$ and for which $X$ is complete (use a) and exerc. 25 b of IX, p. 90). Deduce that if moreover $X$ is weierstrassian (IX, p. 88, exerc. 20), it is compact.
c) Prove that a locally compact $X$, paracompact and submetrizable is metrizable (reduce to the case where $X$ is denumerable at infinity with the aid of th. 5 of I, p. 70; use then IX, p. 21, corollary (cf. IX, p. 113, exerc. 14).

28) Let $X, Y$ be two metric spaces, $d, d'$ the distances on $X$ and $Y$, $f$ a mapping of $X$ into $Y$.
a) Suppose that the image by $f$ of every compact subset of $X$ is a compact subset of $Y$. Prove that if at a point $a \in X, f$ is not continuous, there exists an infinite sequence $(x_n)$ of points of $X$, tending to $a$, and such that $x_n \in f^{-1}(b)$, where $b$ is a point of $Y$ distinct from $f(a)$. (Reason by contradiction; prove that there would exist a sequence $(z_n)$ of distinct points of $X$, tending to $a$, such that $f(z_i) \neq f(z_j)$ for $i \neq j$ and that the $f(z_n)$ belong to the complement of a neighbourhood of $f(a)$; the assumption would then imply that the set of the $f(z_n)$ is compact; prove that this leads to a contradiction).
b) Deduce from a) that if $f$ is such that: 1° the image by $f$ of every compact subset of $X$ is a compact subset of $Y$; 2° the inverse image of every point of $Y$ by $f$ is closed in $X$, then $f$ is continuous in $X$.
c) Suppose in addition that $X$ is locally connected. Prove that if $f$ is such that: 1° the image by $f$ of every compact subset of $X$ is a compact subset of $Y$; 2° the image by $f$ of every connected subset of X is a connected subset of Y, then f is continuous in X. (Reason by contradiction using a): with the same notation, let (V_n) be a fundamental system of connected neighbourhoods of a in X. Using the fact that f(V_n) is connected, prove that there would exist in each V_n a point u_n such that 0 < d'(f(u_n), b) < 1/n and deduce a contradiction by considering the set formed by a and the u_n).

30) Let X be a metric space, d the distance on X, (x_i)_{1 \leq i \leq q} a finite sequence of points of X, h a number > 0.

a) For every y ∈ X and every integer k such that k ≥ 1, denote by n_k(y) the number of indices i such that d(y, x_i) ≤ kh/q; we have n_k(y) ≤ q. If there exists an integer k such that k ≤ n_k(y), there exists a greatest integer j ≥ k such that j ≤ n_j(y), and we then have j = n_j(y); this integer is denoted by j(y); if k > n_k(y) for every k, put j(y) = 0. We have j(x_i) ≥ 1 for every i.

b) Let m ≤ q be the greatest value of j(y) when y runs through X, and let z ∈ X be such that j(z) = m ≥ 1. Prove that if x ∈ X is such that d(x, z) > 2mh/q, then there exists an index i such that d(x, x_i) > h. (First observe that there exists at least one index i such that d(x, x_i) > mh/q. If one had d(x, x_i) ≤ h for every i, one would conclude that m = q, whence a contradiction.)

c) Prove that there exists a partition (N_k)_{1 \leq k \leq s} of the interval [1, q] of N into non-empty subsets, and a sequence (z_k)_{1 \leq k \leq s} of points of X having the following properties: 1° if j_k(y) is the function defined as in a) for the partial sequence of (x_i) corresponding to the set of indices N_k ∪ N_{k+1} ∪ ... ∪ N_s, then j_k(z_k) is the greatest possible value of j_k(y) when y runs through X; 2° N_k is the set of the j_k(z_k) indices i such that d(z_k, x_i) ≤ j_k(z_k)h/q. (Define the sequences (z_k) and (N_k) by induction by taking for z_1 a point where j(y) takes its greatest value.)

d) Put $a_k = j_k(z_k)$, $r_k = 2a_kh/q$, so that $a_1 + a_2 + ... + a_s = q$, and
$$
r_1 + r_2 + ... + r_s = 2h.
$$
Let $x \in X$ such that $d(z_k, x) > r_k$ for $1 \leq k \leq s$; let $\sigma$ be a permutation of [1, q] such that
$$
d(x, x_{σ(1)}) \leq d(x, x_{σ(2)}) \leq ... \leq d(x, x_{σ(q)}).
$$
Now prove that for every $p$ such that $1 \leq p \leq q$, one has $d(x, x_{σ(p)}) > ph/q$. (There exists an index $k$ such that the indices σ(1), σ(2), ..., σ(p) belong to the set $N_k \cup N_{k+1} \cup ... \cup N_s$. Apply b) to the sequence formed by the $x_{σ(i)}$ for $1 \leq i \leq p$ using the definition of the $z_k$ given in c).).

e) Deduce from d) that for every point $x \in X$ such that $d(z_k, x) > r_k$ for $1 \leq k \leq s$, one has
$$
\prod_{i=1}^{q} d(x, x_i) > (h/e)^q
$$
where e is the base of natural logarithms (Boutroux-Bloch-H. Cartan theorem).
