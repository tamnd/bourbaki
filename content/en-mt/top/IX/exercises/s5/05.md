a) Show that every open set in $\mathbf{R}_-$ is the union of a countable family of left semi-open or open intervals, pairwise without common point. Deduce that $\mathbf{R}_-$ is a perfectly normal space (IX, p. 111, exerc. 11) and totally inexhaustible (IX, p. 113, exerc. 11).

b) Show that the space $\mathbf{R}_- \times \mathbf{R}_-$ is not normal, and consequently that $\mathbf{R}_-$ is not metrizable, although there exists in $\mathbf{R}_-$ a countable everywhere dense subset. (By considering in $\mathbf{R}_- \times \mathbf{R}_-$ the set of points $(x, y)$ such that $x + y = 1$, show that there exists a closed subset of $\mathbf{R}_- \times \mathbf{R}_-$ which is a subspace homeomorphic to the space $X$ defined in exerc. 14.)

c) Show that every subspace of $\mathbf{R}_-$ is a Lindelöf space (IX, p. 75), and consequently is paracompact (IX, p. 76, prop. 2). (For every $x \in \mathbf{R}_-$, let $U_x$ be a left semi-open interval $]y(x), x]$ with endpoint $x$. Show that the set of $z \in \mathbf{R}_-$ which do not belong to any open interval $]y(x), x[$ is countable; one may use exerc. 1 of IV, p. 47.)

d) Show that every compact subset of $\mathbf{R}_-$ is countable (if $A$ is a compact set in $\mathbf{R}_-$, the topologies induced on $A$ by $T_-(\mathbf{R})$ and $T_0(\mathbf{R})$ are identical; deduce that every point of $A$ is the origin of a contiguous interval to $A$).

16) a) Show that every product $X = \prod_{t \in I} Y_t$ of complete metric spaces is a Baire space. (Reason as in the proof of th. 1 (IX, p. 55), taking for $G_n$ an elementary set (I, p. 24) whose projections which are different from the whole factor space have diameter $\leqslant 1/n$; note then that there is a countable subset $J$ of $I$ such that every $G_n$ is of the form $H_n \times \prod_{t \notin J} Y_t$, where $H_n \subset \prod_{t \in J} Y_t$.

b) Show by an example that if $I$ is not countable, $X$ is not necessarily totally inexhaustible (exerc. 11). (Use IX, p. 90, exerc. 1 and IX, p. 97, 27 b)).

¶ 17) a) Show that, in a complete metric space, every perfect set contains a subset homeomorphic to the triadic Cantor set $K$ (IV, p. 9) (use exerc. 11 of IV, p. 63).

b) Deduce that, in a complete metric space having a countable basis, every perfect set has the power of the continuum; every closed set is countable or has the power of the continuum (use I, p. 108, exerc. 17); every open set is countable or has the power of the continuum.

c) Show that in a non-countable complete metric space having a countable basis, the set of perfect subsets has the power of the continuum. (First prove it for the compact space $\{0, 1\}^\mathbf{N}$, then use $a$ and IX, p. 92, exerc. 11 b)).

d) Let $X$ be a non-countable complete metric space having a countable basis. Show that there exists a subset $Z \subset X$ such that $Z$ and $X - Z$ have the power of the continuum and that neither $Z$, nor $X - Z$ contain a nonempty perfect set. (Use $b$ and $c$ and the method described in E, III, p. 91, exerc. 24a)). If in addition $X$ has no isolated point, show that neither $Z$ nor $X - Z$ are meagre. (If $A$ is a countable intersection of everywhere dense open sets of $X$, show that $A$ contains nonempty perfect sets, using $c$ and IX, p. 58). Deduce that, in this case, $Z$ is not an approachable set in $X$ (IX, p. 91, exerc. 6; note that the preceding reasoning shows that for every nonempty open subset $U$ of $X$, $U \cap Z$ is nonmeagre).

18) Let $A$ be a dense subspace in a topological space $X$, $f$ a continuous mapping from $A$ into a complete metric space $Y$; now prove that the set of points of $X$ where $f$ has no limit (relative to $A$) is a meagre set in $X$ (consider for every $n > 0$ the set of $x \in X$ where the oscillation $\omega(x; f)$ of $f$ is $< 1/n$).

¶ 19) Let $f$ be a homeomorphism of a subset $A$ of a complete metric space $X$ onto a subset $A'$ of a complete metric space $X'$; now prove that there exists an extension $\tilde{f}$ of $f$ to a set $B$, countable intersection of open sets in $X$, which is a homeomorphism of $B$ onto a set $B'$, countable intersection of open sets in $X'$ (apply exerc. 18 to $f$ and to the reciprocal homeomorphism $g$).
