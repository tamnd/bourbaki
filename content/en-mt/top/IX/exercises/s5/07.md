¶ 26) Let $X$ be a locally compact metrizable space, $d$ a distance compatible with the topology of $X$, $R$ a closed equivalence relation in $X$, such that every class following $R$ is compact. Then one knows that $X/R$ is locally compact (I, p. 78, prop. 9) and metrizable (IX, p. 110, exerc. 34).

a) For every pair of elements $u, v$ of $X/R$, put
$$
\rho(u, v) = \sup_{\varphi(x) = u} d(x, \varphi^{-1}(v))
$$
($\varphi$ denoting the canonical mapping of $X$ onto $E/R$), and for every $u \in X/R$,
$$
\lambda(u) = \lim_{v \to u,\ v \neq u} \sup \rho(u, v).
$$
Now prove that $\lambda$ is upper semi-continuous in $X/R$, and that, for every $\alpha > 0$, the set of $u \in X/R$ such that $\lambda(u) \geq \alpha$ is rare. (Otherwise, now prove that there would exist a compact subset $K$ of $X$ and an infinite sequence $(x_n)$ of points of $K$ such that $d(x_m, x_n) > \alpha/2$ for $m \neq n$).

b) Deduce from $a$) that there exists in $X/R$ a dense set $M$, countable intersection of open sets in $X/R$, such that for every $x \in \varphi(M)$, the image by $\varphi$ of every neighbourhood of $x$ in $X$ is a neighbourhood of $\varphi(x)$ in $X/R$ (use IX, p. 55, th. 1).

¶ 27) a) Let $G$ be a topological group, $A$ an approachable set (IX, p. 112, exerc. 6) in $G$, now prove that, if $A$ is not meagre, $AA^{-1}$ is a neighbourhood of the identity element in $G$. (First remark, by virtue of IX, p. 111, exerc. 3, that $G$ is a Baire space. Let $A^*$ be the union of the open sets $U \subset G$ such that $U \cap \complement A$ is meagre. Now prove that $A^*$ is not empty, and that if $x \in G$ is such that $xA^* \cap A^* \neq \emptyset$, then $xA \cap A \neq \emptyset$. Conclude that $AA^{-1} \supset A^*(A^*)^{-1}$.)

b) Deduce from $a$) that an approachable subgroup of a topological group $G$ is either meagre or open (and closed). In particular, a countable topological group which is a separated Baire space is discrete.

c) Now prove that in the topological group $\mathbf{R}$, there exist subgroups $H$ such that $\mathbf{R}/H$ is infinite countable (use a Hamel basis); such a subgroup is everywhere dense, non-meagre and non-approachable.

d) Let $G$ be a metrizable topological group whose right and left uniform structures are identical. Show that if there exists on $G$ a distance compatible with the topology and for which $G$ is a complete metric space, then $G$ is a complete metrizable group. (Use $b$), IX, p. 99, exerc. 2 and IX, p. 58).

¶ 28) Let $G, G'$ be two topological groups. Show that every continuous surjective homomorphism $f : G \to G'$ is a strict morphism when one supposes that $G$ is complete, that the topology of G admits a countable basis¹, and that G' is not exhausted (using the fact that, for every neighbourhood U of the identity element e of G, G is the union of a sequence of sets of the form x_n U, now prove that for every open set A of G, there exists an open set A' of G' containing f(A) and such that f(A) is dense in A'. Consider then a fundamental system (U_n) of symmetric neighbourhoods of e in G, such that U_{n+1}' \subset U_n; show that f(U_p) contains the open set U_{p+1}'; for this, take a point a' \in U_{p+1}', then construct by induction a sequence (b_n) of points of G such that b_n \in U_{p+n}, and that f(b_1 b_2 ... b_n) tends to a'; one will note that, if x' \in U_k', there exists y \in U_k such that f(y) \in x' U_{k+1}'.

29) Let G be a complete metrizable group, whose topology admits a countable basis. Let H be a closed normal subgroup of G, A a closed subgroup of G. In order that the quotient group A/(A \cap H) be canonically isomorphic to AH/H, it is necessary and sufficient that AH be a closed subgroup of G (to see that the condition is necessary, note that A/(A \cap H) is complete according to IX, p. 25, prop. 4; to see that the condition is sufficient, use exerc. 28).

¶ 30) Let G be a group, d a distance on G such that, for the topology $\mathcal{T}$ defined by d on G, all the translations $y \mapsto x_0 y$ and $y \mapsto y x_0$ are continuous ($x_0 \in G$, cf. III, p. 66, exerc. 2).

a) Show that if G is complete for the topology $\mathcal{T}$, the mapping $(x, y) \mapsto xy$ is continuous in $G \times G$ (use IX, p. 115, exerc. 21).

b) We suppose in addition that $\mathcal{T}$ admits a countable basis. Show then that the mapping $x \mapsto x^{-1}$ is continuous in G, in other words that the topology $\mathcal{T}$ is compatible with the group structure of G. (Consider, in the group $G \times G$, endowed with the product topology of $\mathcal{T}$ by itself, the set of points $(x, x^{-1})$, where x runs through G; F is closed in $G \times G$, and the law of composition $(x, x^{-1})(y, y^{-1}) = (xy, y^{-1}x^{-1})$ defines on F a group structure; show that the topology induced on F by that of $G \times G$ is compatible with this group structure by using a). Prove afterwards that the projection $pr_1$ of F onto G is bicontinuous by reasoning as in exerc. 28).

¶ 31) a) We consider the product space $Y = \mathbf{N}^\mathbf{N}$ of mappings from $\mathbf{N}$ into itself, where each factor is endowed with the discrete topology; there exists a distance on Y compatible with the topology of Y and for which Y is complete. Let $X_i$ (resp. $X_s$) be the set of injective (resp. surjective) mappings of $\mathbf{N}$ into itself. Show that $X_i$ is closed in Y and that $X_s$ is a countable intersection of open sets of Y. Deduce that the subspace $X_b = X_i \cap X_s$ of the permutations of $\mathbf{N}$ is a Baire space.

b) Let $(c(n))_{n \in \mathbf{N}}$ be a series of real numbers, convergent but not absolutely convergent. For every permutation $x : n \mapsto x_n$ of $\mathbf{N}$, we denote by B the set of the $x \in X_b$ such that

$$
\lim_{m \to \infty} \sup \sum_{n=0}^m c(x_n) < +\infty.
$$

Show that the set B is meagre in $X_b$. (For every integer $k \geqslant 1$, let $B_k$ be the set of the $x \in X_b$ such that $\sup \sum_{n=0}^m c(x_n) \leqslant k$. Show that each of the sets $B_k$ is nowhere dense in $X_b$.)
