Let $X, Y$ be two metrizable spaces, and let $f$ be an approximable mapping (IX, p. 115, exerc. 22) of $X$ into $Y$. Now prove that for every Borel set $B \subset Y, f^{-1}(B)$ is approximable in $X$ (cf. IX, p. 112, exerc. 6). Deduce that if $g$ is a Borel mapping of $Y$ into a metrizable space $Z$, $g \circ f$ is approximable. Give an example of a continuous mapping $f$ and an approximable mapping $g$ such that $g \circ f$ is not approximable (cf. IX, p. 122, exerc. 14 b)).

Appendix I

1) Let $X$ be an uncountable discrete space, and let $X'$ be its Alexandroff compactification. Show that $X'$ is a Lindelöf space in which there are open subspaces which are not Lindelöf spaces.

3) Let X be the space obtained by endowing $\mathbf{R}^2$ with the topology generated by the open sets of the usual topology of $\mathbf{R}^2$ and the complement in $\mathbf{R}^2$ of the open half-line consisting of the points $(0, y)$ such that $y > 0$ (cf. 5) a) There exists in the interval $I = [0, 1]$ of $\mathbf{R}$ an uncountable subset Y such that every compact subset of I contained in Y is countable (IX, p. 114, exerc. 17 d)).
