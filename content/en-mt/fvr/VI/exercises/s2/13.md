Let $f$ be an integer $\geq 1$, $\chi$ a function defined in $\mathbf{Z}$, with complex values, such that $\chi(x + f) = \chi(x)$ for every $x \in \mathbf{Z}$. If one sets

$$
\hat{\chi}(y) = \frac{1}{f} \sum_{x=0}^{f-1} \chi(x) e^{-2\pi i xy/f} \quad \text{for } y \in \mathbf{Z},
$$

one has

$$
\chi(x) = \sum_{y=0}^{f-1} \hat{\chi}(y) e^{2\pi i xy/f} \quad \text{for } x \in \mathbf{Z}.
$$

Deduce from Exercise 12 that one has, for every integer $m \geq 2$,
$$
\sum_{n \in \mathbf{Z} - \{0\}} \frac{\chi(n)}{n^m} = -\frac{(2\pi i)^m}{m!} \sum_{y=0}^{f-1} \hat{\chi}(y) B_m \left( \frac{y}{f} \right).
$$
For example, one has
$$
1 - \frac{1}{3^3} + \frac{1}{5^3} - \cdots + \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^3} + \cdots = \frac{\pi^3}{32}.
$$
