Let $f$ be a regulated numerical function on a compact interval $[a, b]$. We put
$$
r(n) = \frac{b - a}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} f\left(a + k \frac{b - a}{n}\right) - \int_a^b f(t) \, dt.
$$
a) Now prove that, if $f$ is increasing in $[a, b]$, one has
$$
0 \leq r(n) \leq \frac{b - a}{n} (f(b) - f(a)).
$$
b) If $f$ is continuous and admits in $[a, b]$ a regulated and bounded right derivative, now prove that one has
$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} n r(n) = \frac{b - a}{2} (f(b) - f(a)) \quad \text{(by setting } x_k = a + k \frac{b - a}{n}, \text{ notice that one has}
$$
$$
r(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \int_{x_k}^{x_{k+1}} (f(x_{k+1}) - f(t)) \, dt,
$$
and apply Proposition 5 of II, p. 7).
c) Give an example of an increasing and continuous function $f$ in $[a, b]$ such that $n r(n)$ does not tend to $\frac{b - a}{2} (f(b) - f(a))$ when $n$ increases indefinitely [take for $f$ the limit of a decreasing sequence $(f_n)$ of increasing functions whose representative curves are broken lines, such that
$$
f_n\left(a + k \frac{b - a}{2^n}\right) = f\left(a + k \frac{b - a}{2^n}\right) \quad \text{for } 0 \leq k \leq 2^n
$$
and
$$
(b - a) \sum_{k=1}^{2^n} f_n\left(a + k \frac{b - a}{2^n}\right) - 2^n \int_a^b f_n(t) \, dt \geq \frac{1}{4}(b - a)(f_n(b) - f_n(a))].
$$
