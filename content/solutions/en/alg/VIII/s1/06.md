**(a)** Put $\mathfrak a=A\mathfrak a_1$. Since $\mathfrak a_1$ is a left ideal of $eAe$, every element of $\mathfrak a$ is a finite sum of elements $a x$, with $a\in A$ and $x\in\mathfrak a_1$. Thus, for $x\in\mathfrak a_1$, we have $x=ex$ and hence
$$
ex=x.
$$
It follows that
$$
e\mathfrak a e=e(A\mathfrak a_1)e.
$$
Since $x=exe$ for every $x\in\mathfrak a_1$, we have $\mathfrak a_1\subset e\mathfrak a e$. Conversely, if $a\in A$ and $x\in\mathfrak a_1$, then
$$
eaxe=(eae)x.
$$
Now $eae\in eAe$, and $\mathfrak a_1$ is a left ideal of $eAe$, while $xe=x$; hence $eaxe\in\mathfrak a_1$. Therefore $e\mathfrak a e\subset\mathfrak a_1$, and
$$
e\mathfrak a e=\mathfrak a_1.
$$

If $x\in\mathfrak a\cap eAe$, then $x=exe$, and since $x\in\mathfrak a$ we have $x\in e\mathfrak a e$. Thus $\mathfrak a\cap eAe\subset e\mathfrak a e$. Conversely, if $x\in e\mathfrak a e$, write $x=eae$ with $a\in\mathfrak a$. Since $ea\in\mathfrak a$, it follows from the fact that $\mathfrak a$ is a left ideal that $eae\in\mathfrak a$; and $eae\in eAe$. Hence $e\mathfrak a e\subset\mathfrak a\cap eAe$. Consequently
$$
\mathfrak a_1=e\mathfrak a e=\mathfrak a\cap eAe.
$$

**(b)** Let $\mathfrak a$ be a left ideal of $A$. If $x\in\mathfrak a\cap eAe$, then $x=exe$, and, since $x\in\mathfrak a$, we have $x=exe\in e\mathfrak a e$. Hence
$$
\mathfrak a\cap eAe\subset e\mathfrak a e.
$$

For a strict inclusion, let $A=\mathbf M_2(K)$, where $K$ is a field, and put
$$
e=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Let $\mathfrak a$ be the left ideal generated by
$$
x=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then
$$
\mathfrak a
=A x
=\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\lambda\\ \mu&\mu\end{pmatrix};
\ \lambda,\mu\in K
\right\}.
$$
No nonzero element of this left ideal belongs to $eAe=K e$, since an element of $\mathfrak a\cap eAe$ would have the form
$$
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&\lambda\\0&0\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
which implies $\lambda=\alpha=0$. Thus $\mathfrak a\cap eAe=0$. On the other hand,
$$
exe=e,
$$
so that $e\in e\mathfrak a e$. Hence
$$
0=\mathfrak a\cap eAe\subsetneqq e\mathfrak a e=Ke.
$$

**(d)** Suppose first that $A$ is left Artinian. Let $(\mathfrak a_i)_{i\geqslant0}$ be a decreasing sequence of left ideals of $eAe$. By (a), for each $i$ there is a left ideal $\mathfrak b_i=A\mathfrak a_i$ of $A$ such that
$$
\mathfrak a_i=e\mathfrak b_i e.
$$
Since $\mathfrak a_{i+1}\subset\mathfrak a_i$, we have
$$
\mathfrak b_{i+1}=A\mathfrak a_{i+1}\subset A\mathfrak a_i=\mathfrak b_i.
$$
By Definition 2 of § 1, a ring is left Artinian precisely when its left regular module $A_s$ is Artinian; by Definition 1(ii), this means that every decreasing sequence of left ideals of $A$ is stationary. Thus $(\mathfrak b_i)$ is stationary, and consequently $(\mathfrak a_i)=(e\mathfrak b_i e)$ is stationary. Hence $eAe$ is left Artinian.

If $A$ is left Noetherian, the same argument with increasing sequences shows that $eAe$ is left Noetherian: by Definition 2, the left regular module $A_s$ is Noetherian, and by Definition 1(ii), every increasing sequence of left ideals of $A$ is stationary.

Replacing $e$ by the idempotent $1-e$ gives the same conclusions for $(1-e)A(1-e)$. Thus, if $A$ is left Artinian (resp. left Noetherian), then both $eAe$ and $(1-e)A(1-e)$ are left Artinian (resp. left Noetherian).

**(e)** Let $V$ be an infinite-dimensional vector space over a commutative field $K$, and let
$$
A=K\times V\times K
$$
with multiplication
$$
(\lambda,x,\mu)(\lambda',x',\mu')
=(\lambda\lambda',\,\lambda x'+\mu'x,\,\mu\mu').
$$
Put $e=(1,0,0)$. Then $1-e=(0,0,1)$, and
$$
eAe=\{(\lambda,0,0):\lambda\in K\},
$$
with its induced multiplication, is canonically isomorphic to $K$. Likewise
$$
(1-e)A(1-e)=\{(0,0,\mu):\mu\in K\},
$$
and is canonically isomorphic to $K$. Thus both are commutative fields.

For every $K$-linear subspace $W$ of $V$, put
$$
\mathfrak a_W=\{(0,x,0):x\in W\}.
$$
If $(\lambda,x,\mu)\in A$ and $(0,w,0)\in\mathfrak a_W$, then
$$
(\lambda,x,\mu)(0,w,0)=(0,\lambda w,0)\in\mathfrak a_W
$$
and
$$
(0,w,0)(\lambda,x,\mu)=(0,\mu w,0)\in\mathfrak a_W.
$$
Consequently $\mathfrak a_W$ is a two-sided ideal of $A$ for every subspace $W$ of $V$.
