Suppose first that $M$ is Artinian. Since $A$ is an integral domain which is not a field, and $M$ is Artinian, we show first that $M$ is torsion. If $x\in M$ is not torsion, then the homomorphism $A\to Ax$, $a\mapsto ax$, is injective, so $Ax\simeq A$. By Proposition 3 of §1, $Ax$ is Artinian, and hence $A$ would be Artinian. This contradicts Example 3 of §2. Thus $M$ is a torsion module.

By the standard primary decomposition theorem for torsion modules over a principal ideal domain of VII, §2, No. 2, p. 8, we have
$$
M=\bigoplus_{\pi\in P}M_\pi .
$$
If $M_\pi\neq0$ for infinitely many $\pi\in P$, these nonzero submodules have direct sum in $M$, contrary to Example 2 of §1. Hence there is a finite subset $S\subset P$ such that $M_\pi=0$ for $\pi\in P-S$. This proves (i).

Let $\pi\in S$. By Proposition 3 of §1, the submodule $M_\pi$ is Artinian. The submodule
$$
M_\pi[\pi]=\{x\in M_\pi\mid \pi x=0\}
$$
is therefore Artinian. It is a vector space over the field $A/\pi A$, and hence it is finite-dimensional: indeed, an infinite-dimensional vector space is not Artinian by Example 2 of §1. Moreover,
$$
M[\pi]=M_\pi[\pi].
$$
Indeed, if $x=\sum_{\rho}x_\rho\in M$ is annihilated by $\pi$, with $x_\rho\in M_\rho$, then for $\rho\ne\pi$ some power $\rho^n$ annihilates $x_\rho$, while $\pi$ also annihilates $x_\rho$. Since $\pi$ and $\rho^n$ are relatively prime, there exist $u,v\in A$ such that $u\pi+v\rho^n=1$, and therefore $x_\rho=0$. Thus $x=x_\pi\in M_\pi[\pi]$, while the reverse inclusion is immediate. Condition (ii) follows.

Conversely, suppose that $M$ is a torsion module and that there is a finite subset $S\subset P$ satisfying (i) and (ii). By the standard primary decomposition theorem for torsion modules over a principal ideal domain of VII, §2, No. 2, p. 8, we have
$$
M=\bigoplus_{\pi\in S}M_\pi .
$$
For each $\pi\in S$, condition (ii), together with the equality $M[\pi]=M_\pi[\pi]$ proved above, says that $M_\pi[\pi]$ is finite-dimensional over $A/\pi A$.

Fix $\pi\in S$. We prove that $M_\pi$ is Artinian. Put $K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ and let
$$
E_\pi=\{y\in K/A\mid \pi^ny=0\text{ for some }n\geqslant0\}.
$$
Thus $E_\pi$ is the $\pi$-primary component of $K/A$. It is an injective $A$-module: indeed, it is divisible, since division by every nonzero element of $A$ is possible in $K/A$ within its $\pi$-primary component, and a divisible module over a principal ideal domain is injective (equivalently, this follows directly from Baer's criterion, since every ideal of $A$ is principal).

Moreover, $E_\pi[\pi]$ is one-dimensional over $A/\pi A$, and $E_\pi$ is Artinian. To see the latter directly, every nonzero element of $E_\pi$ has the form $u/\pi^n+A$, with $u$ relatively prime to $\pi$; it generates $\pi^{-n}A/A$. Consequently every submodule of $E_\pi$ is one of
$$
0,\quad \pi^{-1}A/A,\quad \pi^{-2}A/A,\quad\ldots,\quad E_\pi,
$$
and these submodules form a descending chain. Thus $E_\pi$ is Artinian.

Let $r=\dim_{A/\pi A}M_\pi[\pi]$, and choose a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ of $M_\pi[\pi]$. Since $E_\pi[\pi]$ is one-dimensional, there is an injective homomorphism
$$
M_\pi[\pi]\longrightarrow E_\pi^r
$$
whose image is $(E_\pi[\pi])^r$. Since $E_\pi^r$ is injective, this homomorphism extends to a homomorphism
$$
f:M_\pi\longrightarrow E_\pi^r.
$$
The submodule $M_\pi[\pi]$ is essential in $M_\pi$: if $0\ne x\in M_\pi$, choose $n$ minimal with $\pi^nx=0$; then $\pi^{n-1}x$ is a nonzero element of $M_\pi[\pi]$. Hence $\ker f=0$, because $f$ is injective on $M_\pi[\pi]$. Thus $M_\pi$ is isomorphic to a submodule of $E_\pi^r$. Since $E_\pi^r$ is Artinian by Example 5 of §1, $M_\pi$ is Artinian by Proposition 3 of §1.

Since $S$ is finite, $M$ is Artinian by Example 5 of §1.
