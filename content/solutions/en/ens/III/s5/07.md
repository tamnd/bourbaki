**(a)**

The formula for $S_{n,p}$ is derived using the principle of inclusion-exclusion applied to the total number of mappings $p^n$, and relies on the given relation and the binomial inversion formula.

By the note, we have:
$$
p^n = \sum_{k=1}^p \binom{p}{k} S_{n,k}.
$$
To isolate $S_{n,p}$, apply the binomial inversion formula (justified by Exercise 3’s second relation). This inversion yields the desired alternating sum:
$$
S_{n,p} = \sum_{k=0}^p (-1)^k \binom{p}{k} (p - k)^n.
$$
Expanding this gives the stated formula. The alternating nature and coefficients arise directly from inclusion-exclusion and the second proposition of Exercise 3 guarantees the cancellation of cross terms.

**(b)**

For an onto mapping $f: [1,n] \to [1,p]$, consider the image of the $n$th element. Let $S_{n,p}$ be split into two disjoint cases:

1. The first $n-1$ elements already map onto all $p$ elements. The $n$th element can map to any of the $p$ values, contributing $p \cdot S_{n-1,p}$.
2. The first $n-1$ elements map onto exactly $p-1$ elements, and the $n$th element fills the remaining. There are $p \cdot S_{n-1,p-1}$ such mappings.

Combining these cases gives:
$$
S_{n,p} = p (S_{n-1,p} + S_{n-1,p-1}).
$$
This matches the method used in Proposition 13 (no.8), which partitions combinatorial configurations based on an element’s role.

**(c)**

For $S_{n+1,n}$, each onto function maps one codomain element to two pre-images and the rest to single pre-images. Choose the doubled element ( $n$ ways), pair two domain elements ($ \binom{n+1}{2} $ ways), and arrange the rest ($ (n-1)! $):
$$
S_{n+1,n} = n \cdot \frac{(n + 1)!}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{(n - 1)!} \cdot (n - 1)! = \frac{n}{2}(n + 1)!.
$$

For $S_{n+2,n}$, there are two cases:

- Two codomain elements each having two pre-images: Choose two elements ($ \binom{n}{2} $) and partition domain into pairs and singletons:
$$
\binom{n}{2} \cdot \frac{(n + 2)!}{2^2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} \cdot \frac{(n + 2)!}{4}.
$$
- One codomain element with three pre-images: Choose one element ($ \binom{n}{1} $) and partition domain into a triplet and singletons:
$$
n \cdot \frac{(n + 2)!}{3!}.
$$
Summing these gives:
$$
S_{n+2,n} = \left( \frac{n(n-1)}{8} + \frac{n}{6} \right)(n + 2)! = \frac{n(3n + 1)}{24}(n + 2)!.
$$

**(d)**

An onto function $f : [1,n] \to [1,p]$ partitions $[1,n]$ into $p$ labeled non-empty subsets. Each partition count $P_{n,p}$ can be assigned permutations of its subsets, yielding exactly $p!$ ordered versions. Thus:
$$
S_{n,p} = p! \cdot P_{n,p}.
$$
