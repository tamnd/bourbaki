Let $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha, f_{\alpha\beta})$ and $(\mathrm{F}_\alpha, g_{\alpha\beta})$ be two inverse systems of sets relative to the same index set $\mathrm{I}$. For each $\alpha \in \mathrm{I}$, let $u_\alpha : \mathrm{E}_\alpha \to \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ be a mapping such that the family $(u_\alpha)$ is an inverse system of mappings, i.e. $g_{\alpha\beta} \circ u_\beta = u_\alpha \circ f_{\alpha\beta}$ whenever $\alpha \leqslant \beta$. Let $\mathrm{G}_\alpha \subset \mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ be the graph of $u_\alpha$, so $\mathrm{G}_\alpha = \{ (x, u_\alpha(x)) \mid x \in \mathrm{E}_\alpha \}$.

For $\alpha \leqslant \beta$, the mapping $f_{\alpha\beta} \times g_{\alpha\beta} : \mathrm{E}_\beta \times \mathrm{F}_\beta \to \mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ sends $(x,y)$ to $(f_{\alpha\beta}(x), g_{\alpha\beta}(y))$. If $(x,y) \in \mathrm{G}_\beta$, then $y = u_\beta(x)$, and
$$ (f_{\alpha\beta}(x), g_{\alpha\beta}(y)) = (f_{\alpha\beta}(x), g_{\alpha\beta}(u_\beta(x))) = (f_{\alpha\beta}(x), u_\alpha(f_{\alpha\beta}(x))) \in \mathrm{G}_\alpha, $$
where the second equality uses the hypothesis $g_{\alpha\beta} \circ u_\beta = u_\alpha \circ f_{\alpha\beta}$. Hence the restriction $h_{\alpha\beta} = (f_{\alpha\beta} \times g_{\alpha\beta})|_{\mathrm{G}_\beta}$ maps $\mathrm{G}_\beta$ into $\mathrm{G}_\alpha$.
We verify that $(\mathrm{G}_\alpha, h_{\alpha\beta})$ satisfies the inverse system axioms.
- For each $\alpha$, $f_{\alpha\alpha} = \operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{E}_\alpha}$ and $g_{\alpha\alpha} = \operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{F}_\alpha}$, so $f_{\alpha\alpha} \times g_{\alpha\alpha} = \operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha}$. Restricting to $\mathrm{G}_\alpha$ gives $h_{\alpha\alpha} = \operatorname{id}_{\mathrm{G}_\alpha}$.
- If $\alpha \leqslant \beta \leqslant \gamma$, then $f_{\alpha\gamma} = f_{\alpha\beta} \circ f_{\beta\gamma}$ and $g_{\alpha\gamma} = g_{\alpha\beta} \circ g_{\beta\gamma}$, hence $f_{\alpha\gamma} \times g_{\alpha\gamma} = (f_{\alpha\beta} \times g_{\alpha\beta}) \circ (f_{\beta\gamma} \times g_{\beta\gamma})$. Since $h_{\beta\gamma}$ maps $\mathrm{G}_\gamma$ into $\mathrm{G}_\beta$, the restriction of the composition to $\mathrm{G}_\gamma$ equals $h_{\alpha\beta} \circ h_{\beta\gamma}$, while it is also $h_{\alpha\gamma}$. Thus $h_{\alpha\gamma} = h_{\alpha\beta} \circ h_{\beta\gamma}$.
Therefore $(\mathrm{G}_\alpha, h_{\alpha\beta})$ is an inverse system of subsets of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha$.

Let $\mathrm{E} = \varprojlim \mathrm{E}_\alpha$ and $\mathrm{F} = \varprojlim \mathrm{F}_\alpha$, with canonical projections $f_\alpha : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{E}_\alpha$ and $g_\alpha : \mathrm{F} \to \mathrm{F}_\alpha$. By Proposition 1 of § 1 (tag 03OC) applied to the inverse system $(\mathrm{F}_\alpha, g_{\alpha\beta})$ and the family of mappings $v_\alpha = u_\alpha \circ f_\alpha : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{F}_\alpha$, which satisfies $g_{\alpha\beta} \circ v_\beta = v_\alpha$ for $\alpha \leqslant \beta$, there exists a unique mapping $u : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{F}$ such that $v_\alpha = g_\alpha \circ u$ for all $\alpha$, i.e.
$$ u_\alpha \circ f_\alpha = g_\alpha \circ u \qquad (\alpha \in \mathrm{I}). $$
(Although Proposition 1 is stated for an ordered index set, its proof only uses the preorder relations — transitivity and reflexivity — and does not require antisymmetry; hence it remains valid for preordered sets.) This $u$ is the inverse limit $\varprojlim u_\alpha$.

The product inverse system $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha, f_{\alpha\beta} \times g_{\alpha\beta})$ has inverse limit canonically isomorphic to $\mathrm{E} \times \mathrm{F}$ by Corollary 2 of Proposition 4 (tag 03OH). Under this isomorphism, an element $(z_\alpha) \in \varprojlim (\mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha)$ corresponds to $(x,y) \in \mathrm{E} \times \mathrm{F}$ with $x_\alpha = \operatorname{pr}_1(z_\alpha)$, $y_\alpha = \operatorname{pr}_2(z_\alpha)$.

The inverse limit $\varprojlim \mathrm{G}_\alpha$ is the subset of $\varprojlim (\mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha)$ consisting of families $(z_\alpha)$ with $z_\alpha \in \mathrm{G}_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$. Under the canonical isomorphism, this corresponds to the set of $(x,y) \in \mathrm{E} \times \mathrm{F}$ such that for every $\alpha$,
$$ (x_\alpha, y_\alpha) \in \mathrm{G}_\alpha \iff y_\alpha = u_\alpha(x_\alpha). $$
If $(x,y)$ satisfies this condition, then $y_\alpha = u_\alpha(x_\alpha) = u_\alpha(f_\alpha(x)) = g_\alpha(u(x))$ for all $\alpha$, so $y = u(x)$ because the canonical map $\mathrm{F} \to \prod_\alpha \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ is injective. Conversely, if $y = u(x)$, then $g_\alpha(y) = g_\alpha(u(x)) = u_\alpha(f_\alpha(x))$, so $y_\alpha = u_\alpha(x_\alpha)$ for all $\alpha$. Hence the image of $\varprojlim \mathrm{G}_\alpha$ is exactly the graph of $u$. This identification is canonical because it is induced by the canonical projections.
