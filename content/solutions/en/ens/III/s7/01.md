We first show that \((\mathrm{F}_\lambda, g_{\lambda\mu})\) is an inverse system relative to \(L\).
For each \(\lambda \in L\) the set \(\mathrm{F}_\lambda\) is the inverse limit of the system restricted to \(\mathrm{J}_\lambda\).
If \(\lambda \leqslant \mu\) then \(\mathrm{J}_\lambda \subset \mathrm{J}_\mu\) by (ii); the canonical mapping \(g_{\lambda\mu} : \mathrm{F}_\mu \to \mathrm{F}_\lambda\) is defined by restricting a family indexed by \(\mathrm{J}_\mu\) to \(\mathrm{J}_\lambda\) (no. 1).
By property (4) of no. 1, for \(\lambda \leqslant \mu \leqslant \nu\) we have \(g_{\lambda\nu} = g_{\lambda\mu} \circ g_{\mu\nu}\), and \(g_{\lambda\lambda}\) is the identity on \(\mathrm{F}_\lambda\).
Hence the conditions \((\mathrm{LP}_\mathrm{I})\) and \((\mathrm{LP}_\mathrm{II})\) hold for \((\mathrm{F}_\lambda, g_{\lambda\mu})\) on \(L\); it is an inverse system.

Let \(\mathrm{F} = \varprojlim_{\lambda \in L} \mathrm{F}_\lambda\); denote by \(p_\lambda : \mathrm{F} \to \mathrm{F}_\lambda\) the canonical projections.

We now construct a canonical bijection \(\mathrm{F} \to \mathrm{E}\) using the universal property of inverse limits (Proposition 1 of no. 2, tag 03OC).

**Definition of \(u : \mathrm{F} \to \mathrm{E}\).**
For each \(\alpha \in I\) we define a mapping \(u_\alpha : \mathrm{F} \to \mathrm{E}_\alpha\).
Let \(z = (x_\lambda)_{\lambda \in L} \in \mathrm{F}\). By (iii) there exists \(\lambda\) with \(\alpha \in \mathrm{J}_\lambda\); we set \(u_\alpha(z) = x_\lambda(\alpha)\).
If also \(\alpha \in \mathrm{J}_\mu\), choose \(\nu \in L\) with \(\nu \geqslant \lambda,\mu\) (possible because \(L\) is directed). Then \(\mathrm{J}_\lambda, \mathrm{J}_\mu \subset \mathrm{J}_\nu\) by (ii), and the compatibility \(g_{\lambda\nu}(x_\nu) = x_\lambda\), \(g_{\mu\nu}(x_\nu) = x_\mu\) gives \(x_\lambda(\alpha) = x_\nu(\alpha) = x_\mu(\alpha)\). Hence \(u_\alpha\) is well defined.

Let \(\alpha \leqslant \beta\) in \(I\). Choose \(\lambda\) with \(\alpha \in \mathrm{J}_\lambda\) and \(\mu\) with \(\beta \in \mathrm{J}_\mu\). Pick \(\nu \geqslant \lambda,\mu\) in \(L\). Then \(\alpha,\beta \in \mathrm{J}_\nu\). Since \(x_\nu \in \mathrm{F}_\nu = \varprojlim_{\gamma \in \mathrm{J}_\nu} \mathrm{E}_\gamma\), we have \(f_{\alpha\beta}(x_\nu(\beta)) = x_\nu(\alpha)\). By definition \(u_\alpha(z) = x_\nu(\alpha)\) and \(u_\beta(z) = x_\nu(\beta)\), so \(f_{\alpha\beta}(u_\beta(z)) = u_\alpha(z)\). Thus \(f_{\alpha\beta} \circ u_\beta = u_\alpha\) for all \(\alpha \leqslant \beta\).

By Proposition 1 (no. 2) there exists a unique mapping \(u : \mathrm{F} \to \mathrm{E}\) such that \(f_\alpha \circ u = u_\alpha\) for every \(\alpha \in I\), where \(f_\alpha : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{E}_\alpha\) are the canonical projections.

**Definition of \(v : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{F}\).**
For each \(\lambda \in L\) let \(v_\lambda : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{F}_\lambda\) be the canonical mapping obtained by restricting the index set from \(I\) to \(\mathrm{J}_\lambda\) (no. 1): \(v_\lambda(y) = (f_\alpha(y))_{\alpha \in \mathrm{J}_\lambda}\).
If \(\lambda \leqslant \mu\) then \(\mathrm{J}_\lambda \subset \mathrm{J}_\mu\), and by (4) of no. 1 we have \(v_\lambda = g_{\lambda\mu} \circ v_\mu\).
Applying Proposition 1 to the inverse system \((\mathrm{F}_\lambda, g_{\lambda\mu})\) on \(L\), there exists a unique mapping \(v : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{F}\) such that \(p_\lambda \circ v = v_\lambda\) for all \(\lambda \in L\).

**\(u\) and \(v\) are inverses.**
Take \(y \in \mathrm{E}\). For any \(\alpha \in I\) choose \(\lambda\) with \(\alpha \in \mathrm{J}_\lambda\). Then
\(f_\alpha(u(v(y))) = u_\alpha(v(y)) = (v_\lambda(y))_\alpha = f_\alpha(y)\).
Since the projections \(f_\alpha\) separate points in the product \(\prod_{\alpha \in I} \mathrm{E}_\alpha\), we obtain \(u(v(y)) = y\).

Take \(z = (x_\lambda) \in \mathrm{F}\). For any \(\lambda \in L\) and any \(\alpha \in \mathrm{J}_\lambda\),
\((v_\lambda(u(z)))_\alpha = f_\alpha(u(z)) = u_\alpha(z) = x_\lambda(\alpha)\) (using the same \(\lambda\) in the definition of \(u_\alpha\)).
Hence \(v_\lambda(u(z)) = x_\lambda = p_\lambda(z)\). Because the projections \(p_\lambda\) separate points in \(\prod_{\lambda \in L} \mathrm{F}_\lambda\), we get \(v(u(z)) = z\).

Therefore \(u\) is a bijection with inverse \(v\). The construction uses only the given data and the canonical mappings, so \(u\) is a canonical bijection of \(\mathrm{F}\) onto \(\mathrm{E}\).
