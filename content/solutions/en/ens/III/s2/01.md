Let E be a set. Denote by O the set of all order relations (i.e., graphs of orderings) on E. For Γ, Γ' ∈ O we say that Γ is *coarser* than Γ' if Γ' ⊂ Γ; this defines an order relation on O. We shall prove that the minimal elements of (O, ≤) are exactly the total orders on E.

First, suppose Γ is a total order. Let Γ' ∈ O with Γ' ≤ Γ, i.e. Γ ⊂ Γ'. If Γ' ≠ Γ there exists (x, y) ∈ Γ' \ Γ. Since Γ is total, either (x, y) ∈ Γ or (y, x) ∈ Γ. The first is impossible, so (y, x) ∈ Γ ⊂ Γ'. Then both (x, y) and (y, x) belong to Γ' with x ≠ y, contradicting antisymmetry. Hence Γ' = Γ and Γ is minimal.

Conversely, let Γ be a minimal element of O. Assume Γ is not total. Then there exist x, y ∈ E such that (x, y) ∉ Γ and (y, x) ∉ Γ. We construct an order Γ' strictly containing Γ. Define a relation on E by a ≤' b iff (a, b) ∈ Γ or (a ≤_Γ x and y ≤_Γ b) or (a, b) = (x, y). One verifies routinely that ≤' is an order relation (reflexivity and transitivity are clear; antisymmetry follows from the fact that x and y are incomparable in Γ). Since (x, y) ∉ Γ we have Γ ⊂ Γ', so Γ' ∈ O and Γ' ≤ Γ with Γ' ≠ Γ, contradicting minimality. Hence Γ is total.

Now let Γ be an arbitrary ordering on E. Let T be the set of all total orders on E that are coarser than Γ, i.e. Γ ⊂ Γ' for Γ' ∈ T. We prove that Γ = ⋂_{Γ' ∈ T} Γ'. The inclusion Γ ⊂ ⋂_{Γ' ∈ T} Γ' is obvious. For the reverse, let (x, y) ∉ Γ. We shall find a total order Γ' ⊃ Γ with (x, y) ∉ Γ'. Consider the set S of all orders Σ on E such that Γ ⊂ Σ and (x, y) ∉ Σ. Order S by inclusion. The set S is non‑empty because Γ ∈ S. If C is a totally ordered subset of S, its union U = ⋃_{Σ ∈ C} Σ is an order relation on E (the union of a chain of orders is an order), U extends Γ, and (x, y) ∉ U because (x, y) belongs to no member of C. Thus U ∈ S and is an upper bound of C. Hence S is inductive. By Theorem 2 of no. 4 (Zorn's lemma), S has a maximal element Γ_max.

We claim Γ_max is total. Suppose not; then there exist u, v ∈ E incomparable in Γ_max. Form the two orders Σ_1, Σ_2 obtained by adding (u, v) respectively (v, u) to Γ_max and taking the transitive closure. If both Σ_1 and Σ_2 contained (x, y), then from Σ_1 we would have x ≤_max u and v ≤_max y, and from Σ_2 we would have x ≤_max v and u ≤_max y. Then x ≤_max u ≤_max y gives x ≤_max y, contradicting (x, y) ∉ Γ_max. Hence at least one of Σ_1, Σ_2 belongs to S and strictly contains Γ_max, contradicting maximality. Therefore Γ_max is total. It belongs to T and does not contain (x, y). Consequently (x, y) ∉ ⋂_{Γ' ∈ T} Γ', which completes the proof.

Finally, let (E, Γ) be an ordered set. By the preceding result, Γ = ⋂_{i ∈ I} Γ_i where each Γ_i is a total order on E with Γ ⊂ Γ_i. Consider the product ordered set P = ∏_{i ∈ I} (E, Γ_i) endowed with the product order (i.e. (a_i) ≤ (b_i) iff a_i ≤_{Γ_i} b_i for all i). Define φ : E → P by φ(x) = (x)_{i ∈ I}. Then φ is injective, and for x, y ∈ E we have
x ≤_Γ y ⇔ (x, y) ∈ Γ ⇔ ∀i (x, y) ∈ Γ_i ⇔ ∀i x ≤_{Γ_i} y ⇔ φ(x) ≤ φ(y).
Thus φ is an order isomorphism from (E, Γ) onto its image φ(E) ⊂ P. Hence every ordered set is isomorphic to a subset of a product of totally ordered sets.
