Let $A$ and $B$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ be a letter which does not appear in $A$. By definition of implication, the relation $(A \Rightarrow B)$ is the assembly $(\neg A) \vee B$. Since $x$ does not appear in $A$, it does not appear in $\neg A$. The relation $\neg A$ is a relation in $\mathscr{T}$ by CF2 (§1, no. 4). Applying criterion C33 (no. 3) with $R = \neg A$ and $S = B$, we obtain that the equivalence
$$(\forall x)((\neg A) \vee B) \iff (\neg A) \vee (\forall x)B$$
is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. But $(\forall x)((\neg A) \vee B)$ is exactly $(\forall x)(A \Rightarrow B)$, and $(\neg A) \vee (\forall x)B$ is exactly $(A \Rightarrow (\forall x)B)$. Hence $(\forall x)(A \Rightarrow B) \iff (A \Rightarrow (\forall x)B)$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$.
