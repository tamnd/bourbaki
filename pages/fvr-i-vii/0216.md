**9) Let I be an interval in $ \mathbf{R} $, and $ t_0 $ a point of I, let S be a ball with radius r and centre $ x_0 $ in E, let G be the normed space of bounded maps from $ I \times S $ into E, the norm of such a map $ f $ being the supremum $ \|f\| $ of $ \|f(t, x)\| $ over $ I \times S $. For every $ M > 0 $ let $ G_M $ be the ball $ \|f\| \leq M $ in G. Let L be the subset of G formed by the Lipschitz maps of $ I \times S $ into E; for every function $ f \in L \cap G_M $ let $ u = U(f) $ be the integral of $ x' = f(t, x) $ such that $ u(t_0) = x_0 $, with values in S, and defined on the intersection $ J_M $ of I and the interval $ \left[ t_0 - \frac{r}{M}, t_0 + \frac{r}{M} \right] $ (th. 1).

a) Let $ (f_n) $ be a sequence of functions belonging to $ L \cap G_M $; if $ f_n $ converges uniformly on $ I \times S $ to a function $ f $, then every cluster point (for the topology of compact convergence) of the sequence of $ u_n = U(f_n) $ in the space F of bounded maps from $ J_M $ into E, is an integral of $ x' = f(t, x) $ taking the value $ x_0 $ at the point $ t_0 $. Conversely, every integral v of $ x' = f(t, x) $ defined on $ J_M $ and such that $ v(t_0) = x_0 $, is also an integral of an equation $ x' = g(t, x) $, where g is Lipschitz and arbitrarily close to $ f $ in G (consider the equation

$$
x' = f_n(t, x) + v'(t) - f_n(t, v(t)).
$$

b) Suppose further that E is of *finite* dimension. Show that if $ f \in G_M $ satisfies the hypotheses of lemma 1, then for every $ t \in J_M $ the set $ H(t) $ of values at the point t of the integrals of $ x' = f(t, x) $ which take the value $ x_0 $ at the point $ t_0 $ is a *compact* and *connected* set (to see that $ H(t) $ is closed, use Ascoli’s theorem; to see that it is connected, use *a*): if $ x_1, x_2 $ are two points of $ H(t) $ and $ \varepsilon > 0 $ is arbitrary, show that there exists a connected set $ \Phi $ of functions g belonging to $ L \cap G_M $ such that $ \|f - g\| \leq \varepsilon $ for every function $ g \in \Phi $, and that the set of values of the functions $ U(g) $ at the point t is connected and contains $ x_1 $ and $ x_2 $. Conclude by passing to the limit along an ultrafilter finer than the neighbourhood filter of 0 in $ \mathbf{R} $.

10) Let $ f $ be a continuous bounded real function defined on the box $ P : |t - t_0| < a, |x - x_0| < b $ of $ \mathbf{R}^2 $. Let M be the supremum of $ |f(t, x)| $ over P, and let $ I = ]t_0 - \alpha, t_0 + \alpha[ $, where $ \alpha = \inf(a, b/M) $. Show that the upper and lower envelopes of the set $ \Phi $ of integrals of $ x' = f(t, x) $ defined on I and taking the value $ x_0 $ at the point $ t_0 $, are again integrals of $ x' = f(t, x) $, which one calls the *maximal* and *minimal* integral of this equation corresponding to the point $ (t_0, x_0) $ (remark that the set $ \Phi $ is equicontinuous and closed for the topology of uniform convergence on I).

For every $ \tau \in I $ let $ \xi $ be the value of the minimal integral (corresponding to $ (t_0, x_0) $) at the point $ \tau $. Show that the minimal integral corresponding to the point $ (\tau, \xi) $ is identical to the minimal integral corresponding to the point $ (t_0, x_0) $ on an interval of the form $ [\tau, \tau + h[ $ if $ \tau > t_0 $, and of the form $ ]\tau - h, \tau] $ if $ \tau < t_0 $.
