a) If the function $f$ is $n$ times differentiable *at the point* $x$ (and so $n - 1$ times differentiable on a neighbourhood of $x$), one has
$$
\lim_{(h_1, \ldots, h_n) \to (0, \ldots, 0)} \frac{\Delta^n f(x; h_1, \ldots, h_n)}{h_1 h_2 \ldots h_n} = f^{(n)}(x)
$$
(argue by induction on $n$, using the mean value theorem).

b) If $f$ is $n$ times differentiable *on the interval* $I$, one has
$$
\| \Delta^n f(x; h_1, \ldots, h_n) - f^{(n)}(x_0) h_1 h_2 \ldots h_n \| \\
\leq |h_1 h_2 \ldots h_n| \sup \| f^{(n)}(x + t_1 h_1 + \cdots + t_n h_n) - f^{(n)}(x_0) \|
$$
the supremum being taken over the set of $(t_i)$ such that $0 \leq t_i \leq 1$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$ (same method).

c) If $f$ is a real function which is $n$ times differentiable on $I$, one has
$$
\Delta^n f(x; h_1, h_2, \ldots, h_n) = h_1 h_2 \ldots h_n f^{(n)}(x + \theta_1 h_1 + \cdots + \theta_n h_n)
$$
the numbers $\theta_i$ belonging to $[0, 1]$ (same method, using I, p. 22, corollary).

7) Let $f$ be a finite real function $n$ times differentiable at the point $x_0$, and $g$ a vector function which is $n$ times differentiable at the point $y_0 = f(x_0)$. Let
$$
f(x_0 + h) = a_0 + a_1 h + \cdots + a_n h^n + r_n(h)
$$
$$
g(y_0 + k) = b_0 + b_1 k + \cdots + b_n k^n + s_n(k)
$$
be the Taylor expansions of order $n$ of $f$ and $g$ at the points $x_0$ and $y_0$ respectively. Show that the sum of the $n+1$ terms of the Taylor expansion of order $n$ of the composite function $g \circ f$ at the point $x_0$ is equal to the sum of the terms of degree $\leq n$ in the polynomial
$$
b_0 + b_1 (a_1 h + \cdots + a_n h^n) + b_2 (a_1 h + \cdots + a_n h^n)^2 + \cdots + b_n (a_1 h + \cdots + a_n h^n)^n.
$$
Deduce the two following formulae:

a)
$$
D^n(g(f(x))) = \sum \frac{n!}{m_1! m_2! \ldots m_q!} g^{(p)}(f(x)) \left( \frac{f'(x)}{1!} \right)^{m_1} \cdots \left( \frac{f^{(q)}(x)}{q!} \right)^{m_q}
$$
the sum being taken over all systems of positive integers $(m_i)_{1 \leq i \leq q}$ such that
$$
m_1 + 2m_2 + \cdots + qm_q = n
$$
where $p$ denotes the sum $m_1 + m_2 + \cdots + m_q$.

b)
$$
D^n(g(f(x))) = \sum_{p=1}^n \frac{1}{p!} g^{(p)}(f(x)) \left( \sum_{q=1}^p \binom{p}{q} (-f(x))^{p-q} D^n((f(x))^q) \right).
$$

$$
n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_p = n.
