3) Let $f$ be a convex function on an interval $I \subset \mathbf{R}$.

a) Show that if $f$ is not constant it cannot attain its least upper bound at an interior point of $I$.

b) Show that if $I$ is relatively compact in $\mathbf{R}$ then $f$ is bounded below on $I$.

c) Show that if $I = \mathbf{R}$ and $f$ is not constant, then $f$ is not bounded above on $I$.

4) For a function $f$ to be convex on a compact interval $[a,\ b] \subset \mathbf{R}$ it is necessary and sufficient that it be convex on $[a,\ b[$ and that one has $f(a) \geq f(a+)$ and $f(b) \geq f(b-)$.

5) Let $f$ be a convex function on an open interval $]a,\ +\infty[$; if there exists a point $c > a$ such that $f$ is strictly increasing on $]c,\ +\infty[$ then $\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = +\infty$.

6) Let $f$ be a convex function on an interval $]a,\ +\infty[$; show that $f(x)/x$ has a limit (finite or equal to $+\infty$) as $x$ tends to $+\infty$; this limit is also that of $f'_d(x)$ and of $f'_g(x)$; it is $> 0$ if $f(x)$ tends to $+\infty$ as $x$ tends to $+\infty$.

7) Let $f$ be a convex function on the interval $]a,\ b[$ where $a \geq 0$; show that on this interval the function $x \mapsto f(x) - x f'(x)$ (the "ordinate at the origin" of the right semi-tangent at the point $x$ to the graph of $f$) is decreasing (strictly decreasing if $f$ is strictly convex).

Deduce that:

a) If $f$ admits a finite right limit at the point $a$ then $(x-a)f'_d(x)$ has a right limit equal to 0 at this point.

b) On $]a,\ b[$ either $f(x)/x$ is increasing, or $f(x)/x$ is decreasing, or else there exists a $c \in ]a,\ b[$ such that $f(x)/x$ is decreasing on $]a,\ c[$ and increasing on $]c,\ b[$.

c) Suppose that $b = +\infty$: show that if
$$
\beta = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( f(x) - x f'_d(x) \right)
$$
is finite, then so is $\alpha = \lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x)/x$, and that the line $y = \alpha x + \beta$ is *asymptotic* $^5$ to the graph of $f$, and lies *below* this graph (strictly below if $f$ is strictly convex).

8) Let $f$ be a finite real function, upper semi-continuous on an open interval $I \subset \mathbf{R}$.
