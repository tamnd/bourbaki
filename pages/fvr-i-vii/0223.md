little later, Lagrange (III) treated the general theory of linear equations of order $ n $, recognised that the integral of the homogeneous equation is a linear function of the $ n $ constants of integration, introduced the adjoint equation, discovered the reduction of the order of the homogeneous equation when one knows particular solutions, and the method of variation of parameters to solve the inhomogeneous equation. The obscure points in Lagrange’s theory (notably in what concerns the linear independence of the integrals) were clarified by Cauchy, whose exposition (IV *bis*) has remained quasi definitive, apart from the improvements in detail provided by matrix notation and the theory of elementary divisors.
