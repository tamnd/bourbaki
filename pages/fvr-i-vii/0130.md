# EXERCISES

§ 1.

1) Let $f$ be a continuous and differentiable map of $\mathbf{R}$ into a complete normed algebra $E$ over $\mathbf{R}$ having a unit element $e$; suppose that $f(0) = e$ and that, identically, $f'(x) = f(x)c$ where $c$ is an invertible element of $E$. Show that $f$ is a homomorphism of the additive group $\mathbf{R}$ into the multiplicative group of invertible elements of $E$. (Consider the largest open interval $I$ containing 0 such that $f(z)$ is invertible for all $z \in I$, and prove that for $x, y$ and $x + y$ in $I$ one has $f(x + y)(f(x)f(y))^{-1} = e$ keeping $x$ fixed and letting $y$ vary; then deduce that $I = \mathbf{R}$.)

2) a) In order that the function $(1 + 1/x)^{1-p}$ should be decreasing (resp. increasing) for $x > 0$ it is necessary and sufficient that $p \geq \frac{1}{2}$ (resp. $p \leq 0$); for $0 < p < \frac{1}{2}$ the function is decreasing on an interval $]0, x_0[$ and increasing on $]x_0, +\infty[$. In every case the function tends to $e$ as $x$ tends to $+\infty$.

b) Study in the same way the functions $(1 - 1/x)^{1-p}$, $(1 + 1/x)^x(1 + p/x)$ and $(1 + p/x)^{x+1}$ for $x > 0$.

3) a) Show that $(1 + x)^\alpha \geq 1 + \alpha x$ for $\alpha \geq 1$ and $x \geq 0$, and that $(1 + x)^\alpha \leq 1 + \alpha x$ for $0 \leq \alpha \leq 1$ and $x \geq 0$.

b) Show that $(1 - x)^\alpha \leq \frac{1}{1 + \alpha x}$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1$ and $\alpha \geq 0$.

c) For $0 \leq \alpha \leq 1$ show that for $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$ one has $x^\alpha y^{1-\alpha} \leq \alpha x + b y$ for every pair of numbers $> 0$ for which $a^\alpha b^{1-\alpha} = \alpha(1 - \alpha)^{1-\alpha}$.

d) Deduce from c) that if $f, g$ are two regulated $\geq 0$ functions on an interval $I$ such that the integrals $\int_I f(t)\,dt$ and $\int_I g(t)\,dt$ converge, then the integral $\int_I (f(t))^\alpha (g(t))^{1-\alpha}\,dt$ converges, and
$$
\int_I \left( f(t) \right)^\alpha \left( g(t) \right)^{1-\alpha}\,dt \leq a \int_I f(t)\,dt + b \int_I g(t)\,dt.
$$
From this deduce the *Hölder inequality*
$$
\int_I \left( f(t) \right)^\alpha \left( g(t) \right)^{1-\alpha}\,dt \leq \left( \int_I f(t)\,dt \right)^\alpha \left( \int_I g(t)\,dt \right)^{1-\alpha}
$$
(which is called the "Cauchy-Buniakowski-Schwarz inequality" when $\alpha = \frac{1}{2}$).
