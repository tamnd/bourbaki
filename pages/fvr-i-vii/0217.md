11) a) On the box P : $ |t - t_0| < a,\ |x - x_0| < b $ let g and h be two continuous real functions such that $ g(t, x) < h(t, x) $ on P. Let u (resp. v) be an integral of $ x' = g(t, x) $ (resp. $ x' = h(t, x) $) such that $ u(t_0) = x_0 $ (resp. $ v(t_0) = x_0 $) defined on an interval $[t_0, t_0 + c[$; show that for $ t_0 < t < t_0 + c $ one has $ u(t) < v(t) $ (consider the supremum $ \tau $ of the t for which this inequality holds).

b) Let u be the maximal integral of $ x' = g(t, x) $ corresponding to the point $(t_0, x_0)$ (exerc. 10), defined on an interval $[t_0, t_0 + c[$, with values in $ |x - x_0| < b $. Show that in every compact interval $[t_0, t_0 + d]$ contained in $[t_0, t_0 + c[$ the minimal and maximal integrals of the equation $ x' = g(t, x) + \varepsilon $ corresponding to the point $(t_0, x_0)$ are defined once $ \varepsilon > 0 $ is small enough, and converge uniformly to u when $ \varepsilon $ tends to 0 through values > 0.

c) Let g and h be two real continuous functions defined on P and such that $ g(t, x) \leq h(t, x) $ on P. Let $[t_0, t_0 + c[$ be an interval on which are defined an integral u of $ x' = g(t, x) $ such that $ u(t_0) = x_0 $, and the maximal integral v of $ x' = h(t, x) $ corresponding to the point $(t_0, x_0)$. Show that $ u(t) \leq v(t) $ on this interval (reduce to case a) using b)).

$$

¶ 13) a) Let I = $[t_0, t_0 + c]$ and let $ \omega $ be a continuous real function, $ \geq 0 $, defined on $ I \times \mathbf{R} $. Let S be a ball with centre $ x_0 $ in a complete normed space E, and let f be a continuous map from $ I \times S $ into E such that for any $ t \in I $, $ x_1 \in S $ and $ x_2 \in S $ one has $ \|f(t, x_1) - f(t, x_2)\| \leq \omega(t, \|x_1 - x_2\|) $. Let u and v be two integrals of $ x' = f(t, x) $ defined on I, with values in S, such that $ u(t_0) = x_1,\ v(t_0) = x_2 $; let w be the *maximal* integral (exerc. 10) of $ z' = \omega(t, z) $ corresponding to the point $(t_0, \|x_1 - x_2\|)$, assumed to be defined on I; show that, on I, one has $ \|u(t) - v(t)\| \leq w(t) $. (Let $ w(t, \varepsilon) $ be the maximal integral of $ z' = \omega(t, z) + \varepsilon $ corresponding to the point $(t_0, \|x_1 - x_2\|)$, where $ \varepsilon > 0 $ is sufficiently small. Show that $ \|u(t) - v(t)\| \leq w(t, \varepsilon) $ for every $ \varepsilon > 0 $, arguing by contradiction.)

b) In the statement of the hypotheses of a), replace I by the interval $ I' = ]t_0 - c, t_0] $. Show that if w is the *minimal* integral on this interval of $ z' = \omega(t, z) $ corresponding to the point $(t_0, \|x_1 - x_2\|)$, then $ \|u(t) - v(t)\| \geq w(t) $ on I' (same method).
