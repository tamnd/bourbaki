**Lemma 4.** *Suppose that the mapping $a$ is alternating. Then, for all $x \in \mathbf{F}$, the subalgebra of $\mathbf{F}$ generated by $x$ is strongly associative.*

$a(u, v, w) = 0$ whenever two of the three elements $u, v, w$ are equal to $x$ and it suffices to apply Lemma 3.

**Lemma 5.** *Suppose that the mapping $a$ is alternating and let $\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}$ be two strongly associative subalgebras of $\mathbf{F}$. Then the subalgebra of $\mathbf{E}$ generated by $\mathbf{X} \cup \mathbf{Y}$ is associative.*

Let $Z$ be the set of $z \in \mathbf{E}$ such that $a(u, v, z) = 0$ for all $u \in \mathbf{X}$ and $v \in \mathbf{Y}$, this is obviously a sub-A-module containing $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ since $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ are strongly associative; by Lemma 2, it will suffice to verify that, for $u \in \mathbf{X}$ and $v \in \mathbf{Y}$, $uZ \subset Z, vZ \subset Z, Zu \subset Z$ and $Zv \subset Z$. Now, for $u, u'$ in $\mathbf{X}$, $v \in \mathbf{Y}$ and $z \in Z$, by (2)
$$
a(u'u, z, v) - a(u', uz, v) + a(u', u, zv) = a(u', u, z)v + u'a(u, z, v) = 0
$$
by virtue of the fact that $\mathbf{X}$ is strongly associative and the definition of $Z$. But as $\mathbf{X}$ is strongly associative, $a(u', u, zv) = 0$ and since $u'u \in \mathbf{X}$, $a(u'u, z, v) = 0$ by definition of $Z$. Hence $a(u', uz, v) = 0$, which shows that $uZ \subset Z$. Applying (2) now with $(p, q, r, s) = (v, z, u, u')$, we obtain similarly $Zu \subset Z$. Interchanging the roles of $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ and using the fact that $a$ is alternating, we obtain $vZ \subset Z$ and $Zv \subset Z$; whence the lemma.

**Definition 1.** *An algebra $\mathbf{F}$ is called alternative if it satisfies the equivalent conditions of Proposition 1.*

An associative algebra is obviously alternative. In no. 3 we shall give an example of an alternative algebra which is not associative.

If $\mathbf{F}$ is an alternative A-algebra, every A-algebra $\mathbf{F} \otimes_A A'$ obtained from $\mathbf{F}$ by extending the scalars (§ 1, no. 5) is an alternative $A'$-algebra, as follows from condition (b) of Proposition 1.
