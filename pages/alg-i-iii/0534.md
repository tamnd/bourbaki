3. ANTICOMMUTATIVITY OF THE EXTERIOR ALGEBRA

PROPOSITION 5. (i) Let $ (x_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n} $ be a finite sequence of elements of the module $ M $; for every permutation $ \sigma $ in the symmetric group $ S_n $,

$$
x_{\sigma(1)} \wedge x_{\sigma(2)} \wedge \cdots \wedge x_{\sigma(n)} = \varepsilon_\sigma \cdot x_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n
$$

where $ \varepsilon_\sigma $ denotes the signature of the permutation $ \sigma $.

(ii) If there exist two distinct indices $ i, j $ such that $ x_i = x_j $, the product

$$
x_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n
$$

is zero.

(i) First of all, since $ x \wedge x = 0 $ for all $ x \in M $ by definition of the ideal $ \mathfrak{g}' $, also, for $ x, y $ in $ M $,

$$
x \wedge y + y \wedge x = (x + y) \wedge (x + y) - x \wedge x - y \wedge y = 0.
$$

This establishes (6) in the case $ n = 2 $. The general case then follows from § 4, no. 6, Lemma 3.

(ii) Under the hypothesis of (ii), there exists a permutation $ \sigma \in S_n $ such that $ \sigma(1) = i $ and $ \sigma(2) = j $; then the left hand side of (6) is zero for this permutation and hence so is the right hand side.

COROLLARY 1. Let $ H, K $ be two complementary subsets of the interval $ [1, n] $ of $ \mathbf{N} $ and let $ (i_h)_{1 \leq h \leq p}, (j_k)_{1 \leq k \leq n-p} $ be the sequences of elements of $ H $ and $ K $ respectively, arranged in increasing order; we write

$$
x_H = x_{i_1} \wedge x_{i_2} \wedge \cdots \wedge x_{i_p}, \quad x_K = x_{j_1} \wedge x_{j_2} \wedge \cdots \wedge x_{j_{n-p}};
$$

then

$$
x_H \wedge x_K = (-1)^{\nu} x_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n
$$

where $ \nu $ is the number of ordered pairs $ (i, j) \in H \times K $ such that $ i > j $.

By Proposition 5 this reduces to proving

Lemma 1. If $ \sigma \in S_n $ is the permutation such that $ \sigma(h) = i_h $ for $ 1 \leq h \leq p $, $ \sigma(h) = j_{n-p} $ for $ p + 1 \leq h \leq n $, then $ \varepsilon_\sigma = (-1)^\nu $.

For $ 1 \leq h < h' \leq p $ or $ p + 1 \leq h < h' \leq n $, $ \sigma(h') > \sigma(h) $ and the number of ordered pairs $ (h, h') $ such that $ 1 \leq h \leq p < h' \leq n $ and $ \sigma(h) > \sigma(h') $ is equal to $ \nu $.

COROLLARY 2. The graded algebra $ \bigwedge(M) $ is alternating ($ \S 4 $, no. 9).

It suffices to apply Proposition 13 of $ \S 4 $, no. 9 to $ \bigwedge(M) $, taking as generating system the set $ M $ and using Proposition 5.
