**Proposition 13.** *Let $M$ be an $A$-module, $N$ a direct factor submodule of $M$ which is free of dimension $p$ and $\{u\}$ a basis of $\Lambda^p(N)$. For an element $x \in M$ to belong to $N$, it is necessary and sufficient that $u \wedge x = 0$.*

Let $P$ be a submodule of $M$ complementary to $N$ and let $y \in N,\ z \in P$ be such that $x = y + z$. Then $u \wedge x = u \wedge z$. As $\Lambda^p(N)$ is free of dimension 1, the mapping $\phi: P \to P \otimes \Lambda^p(N)$ such that $\phi(p) = p \otimes u$ is bijective (II, § 3, no. 4, Proposition 4). On the other hand (no. 7, Proposition 10), the composition of the canonical homomorphisms

$$
\psi: P \otimes \Lambda^p(N) \to \Lambda(P) \otimes \Lambda(N) \to \Lambda(M)
$$

is injective. The mapping $\psi \circ \phi$ is therefore injective, whence the proposition.

**Theorem 2.** *Let $M$ be an $A$-module with a finite basis $(e_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$. For a sequence $(x_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ of $n$ elements of $M$ to form a basis of $M$, it is necessary and sufficient that the element $\lambda \in A$ such that

$$
x_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge \ldots \wedge x_n = \lambda \cdot e_1 \wedge e_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n
$$

be invertible in $A$.*

Recall that $e_1 \wedge e_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n$ is the unique element of a basis of $\Lambda^n(M)$ (no. 8, Corollary 1 to Theorem 1) so that the element $\lambda \in A$ satisfying (23) is determined uniquely. If $(x_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ is a basis of $M$, $x_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n$ is the unique element in a basis of $\Lambda^n(M)$ (no. 8), then $\lambda$ is invertible. Conversely, suppose $\lambda$ is invertible; then the alternating $n$-linear form $f$ corresponding to the linear mapping $g: \Lambda^n(M) \to A$ such that

$$
g(e_1 \wedge e_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n) = \lambda^{-1}
$$

is such that $f(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n) = 1$. For all $x \in M$, obviously

$$
x \wedge x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n = 0
$$
