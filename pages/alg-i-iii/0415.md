16. A submodule E of an A-module F is called irreducible with respect to F (or in F) if E ≠ F and E is not the intersection of two submodules of F distinct from E.

(a) For an A-module F to be an essential extension of each of its submodules ≠ {0} it is necessary and sufficient that {0} be irreducible with respect to F.

(b) Let (E_λ)_{λ ∈ L} be a family of submodules of an A-module F. (E_λ)_{λ ∈ L} is called a reduced irreducible decomposition of {0} in F if each of the F_λ is irreducible in F, if the intersection of the E_λ reduces to 0 and if none of the E_λ contains the intersection of the E_μ for μ ≠ λ. When this is so, show that the canonical homomorphism of F into $ \bigoplus_{λ ∈ L} (F/E_λ) $ is essential (if $ E'_λ = \bigcap_{μ ≠ λ} E_μ $, observe that the image F'_λ of E'_λ in F/E_λ is non-zero and that the image of F in $ \bigoplus_{λ ∈ L} (F/E_λ) $ contains $ \bigoplus_{λ ∈ L} F'_λ $; then apply Exercise 15(c)). Conversely, if (E_λ)_{λ ∈ L} is a family of submodules of F irreducible in F and the canonical homomorphism F → $ \bigoplus_{λ ∈ L} (F/E_λ) $ is essential, the family (E_λ) is a reduced irreducible decomposition of {0} in F.

¶ 17. (a) Let M, N, M', N' be four submodules of a module E such that M ∩ N = M' ∩ N' = P. Show that

$$
M = (M + (N ∩ M')) ∩ (M + (N ∩ N'))
$$

Deduce that if M is irreducible in E, then necessarily P = M' ∩ N or P = N' ∩ N.

(b) Let (N_i)_{1 ≤ i ≤ n} be a finite family of submodules of E irreducible in E and M its intersection. Show that if (P_j)_{1 ≤ j ≤ m} is a finite family of submodules of E such that M = $ \bigcap_j P_j $, then for every index i such that 1 ≤ i ≤ n there exists an index φ(i) such that 1 ≤ φ(i) ≤ m and such that, if we write $ N'_i = \bigcap_{k ≠ i} N_k $, then M = P_{φ(i)} ∩ N'_i (use (a)). Deduce that if none of the P_j contains the intersection of the P_k of index ≠ j, then m ≤ n (successively replace the N_i by suitable P_j, using the above result). Conclude that two reduced irreducible decompositions of M in E, one of which is finite, necessarily have the same number of terms (cf. Exercise 23).
