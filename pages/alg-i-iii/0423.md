6. With the rings A and B defined as in § 1, Exercise 7, let M be an A-module; show that for M to be finitely generated (resp. free, projective), it is necessary and sufficient that $M \otimes_A B$ be finitely generated (resp. free, projective).

7. Let $\rho : A \to B$ be a ring homomorphism. For every left B-module F, define a canonical B-module homomorphism
$$
F \to \tilde{\rho}(\rho_*(F))
$$
and show that if E is a left A-module, the composite mappings
$$
\tilde{\rho}(E) \to \tilde{\rho}(\rho_*(\tilde{\rho}(E))) \to \tilde{\rho}(E)
$$
$$
\rho_*(F) \to \rho_*(\tilde{\rho}(\rho_*(F))) \to \rho_*(F)
$$
are the identity mappings.

§ 6

1. Let $(E_n, f_{nm})$ be the inverse system of $\mathbf{Z}$-modules whose indexing set is $\mathbf{N}$, such that $E_n = \mathbf{Z}$ for all $n$ and, for $n \leq m$, $f_{nm}$ is the mapping $x \mapsto 3^{m-n}x$. For all $n$, let $u_n$ be the canonical mapping $E_n \to \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}$. Show that $(u_n)$ is an inverse system of surjective linear mappings but that $\lim \leftarrow u_n$ is not surjective.

2. Let $(E_\alpha, f_{\beta\alpha})$ be a direct system of A-modules. Show that the A-module $\lim \rightarrow E_\alpha$ is canonically isomorphic to the quotient of the direct sum $\bigoplus_\alpha E_\alpha = F$ by the submodule N generated by the elements of the form $j_\beta(f_{\beta\alpha}(x_\alpha)) - j_\alpha(x_\alpha)$, for every ordered pair $(\alpha, \beta)$ such that $\alpha \leq \beta$ and all $x_\alpha \in E_\alpha$, the $j_\alpha : E_\alpha \to F$ being the canonical injections.

3. Let $(F_n, f_{mn})$ be the direct system of $\mathbf{Z}$-modules such that $F_n$ is equal to $U_p$ (\S 1, Exercise 12 (d)) for all $n \geq 0$ and $f_{mn}$ is the endomorphism $x \mapsto p^{m-n}x$ of $U_p$ for $n \leq m$. Show that $\lim \rightarrow F_n = \{0\}$ although the $f_{mn}$ are surjective.

4. Let $(F_\alpha, f_{\beta\alpha})$ be a direct system of A-modules.
(a) For every A-module E, define a canonical $\mathbf{Z}$-module homomorphism
$$
\varepsilon : \lim \rightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_A(E, F_\alpha) \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(E, \lim \rightarrow F_\alpha).
$$
(b) Show that if E is finitely generated, $\varepsilon$ is injective. Give an example where $\varepsilon$ is not injective (cf. Exercise 3, taking $E = \lim \rightarrow F_\alpha$).
