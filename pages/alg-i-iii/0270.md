**3. CHANGE OF RING**

**Proposition 2.** *Let $ A, B $ be two rings, $ \rho : B \to A $ a ring homomorphism and $ E $ (resp. $ F $) a right (resp. left) $ A $-module. Then there exists one and only one $ \mathbf{Z} $-linear mapping*
(6)
$$
\phi : \rho_*(E) \otimes_B \rho_*(F) \to E \otimes_A F
$$
*such that, for all $ x \in E $ and $ y \in F $, the image under $ \phi $ of the element $ x \otimes y $ of $ \rho_*(E) \otimes_B \rho_*(F) $ is the element $ x \otimes y $ of $ E \otimes_A F $; this $ \mathbf{Z} $-linear mapping is surjective.*

We consider the mapping $ (x, y) \mapsto x \otimes y $ of $ \rho_*(E) \times \rho_*(F) $ into $ E \otimes_A F $; it is $ \mathbf{Z} $-bilinear and, for all $ \beta \in B $, by definition $ (x \rho(\beta)) \otimes y = x \otimes (\rho(\beta)y) $, hence conditions (1) of no. 1 hold, whence the existence and uniqueness of $ \phi $ (no. 1, Proposition 1). The latter assertion follows from the fact that the elements $ x \otimes y $ generate the $ \mathbf{Z} $-module $ E \otimes_A F $.

The mapping (6) is called *canonical*.

**Corollary.** *Let $ \mathfrak{J} $ be a two-sided ideal of $ A $ such that $ \mathfrak{J} $ is contained in the annihilator of $ E $ and in the annihilator of $ F $, so that $ E $ (resp. $ F $) has a canonical left (resp. right) $ (A/\mathfrak{J}) $-module structure* ($ \S 1 $, no. 12). *Then the canonical homomorphism* (6)
$$
\phi : E \otimes_A F \to E \otimes_{A/\mathfrak{J}} F
$$
*corresponding to the canonical homomorphism $ \rho : A \to A/\mathfrak{J} $ is the identity.*

For all $ \bar{\alpha} \in A/\mathfrak{J} $, all $ x \in E $ and all $ y \in F $, $ x \bar{\alpha} = x \alpha $ (resp. $ \bar{\alpha} y = \alpha y $) for all $ \alpha $ such that $ \rho(\alpha) = \bar{\alpha} $.
