**7. APPLICATIONS: III. GENERALIZED POWERS**

Let $E$ be a monoid with identity element $e$ and law of composition denoted by $\mathsf{T}$. If $x$ is invertible in $E$, let $g_x$ be the homomorphism of $\mathbf{Z}$ into $E$ mapping $1$ to $x$. Let $g_x(n)=\frac{n}{\mathsf{T}}x$ for all $n\in\mathbf{Z}$; by Lemma 2 this notation is compatible for $n\in\mathbf{N}$ with the notation introduced earlier. Then

(10)

(11)

T 1  x=x

for $x$ invertible in $E$ and $m,n$ in $\mathbf{Z}$. Further, if $y=\frac{m}{\mathsf{T}}x$, the mapping $n\mapsto g_x(mn)$ of $\mathbf{Z}$ into $E$ is a homomorphism mapping $1$ to $y$, whence $g_x(mn)=g_y(n)$, that is

(12)

T mn  x= T n  ( T m  x).

As $-1$ is the negative of $1$ in $\mathbf{Z}$, $\frac{-1}{\mathsf{T}}x$ is the inverse of $x=\frac{1}{\mathsf{T}}x$ in $E$. If we write $n=-m$ in (9), it is seen that $\frac{-m}{\mathsf{T}}x$ is the inverse of $\frac{m}{\mathsf{T}}x$.

**8. NOTATION**

(a) As a general rule the law of a commutative monoid is written additively. It is then a convention that $-x$ denotes the negative of $x$. The notation $x+(-y)$ is abbreviated to $x-y$ and similarly

x+y−z,x−y−z,x−y+z−t,etc.…
