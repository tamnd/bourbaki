5. DISTRIBUTIVITY OF ONE INTERNAL LAW WITH RESPECT TO ANOTHER

**Definition 7.** — Let $\mathrm T$ and $\perp$ be two internal laws on a set $E$. The law $\perp$ is said to be distributive with respect to the law $\mathrm T$ if

(10)
$$
x\perp(y\mathbin{\mathrm T}z)=(x\perp y)\mathbin{\mathrm T}(x\perp z)
$$

(11)
$$
(x\mathbin{\mathrm T}y)\perp z=(x\perp z)\mathbin{\mathrm T}(y\perp z)
$$

for all $x,y,z$ in $E$.

Note that (10) and (11) are equivalent if the law $\perp$ is commutative. In general, one of the laws is written additively and the other multiplicatively; if multiplication is distributive with respect to addition, then:

(12)
$$
x.(y+z)=x.y+x.z
$$

(13)
$$
(x+y).z=x.z+y.z
$$

**Examples.** (1) In the set $\mathcal P(E)$ of subsets of a set $E$, each of the internal laws $\cap$ and $\cup$ is distributive with respect to itself and the other. This follows from formulae of the form

$$
A\cap(B\cup C)=(A\cap B)\cup(A\cap C)
$$

$$
A\cup(B\cap C)=(A\cup B)\cap(A\cup C).
$$

(2) In $\mathbf Z$ (and more generally, in any totally ordered set) each of laws $\sup$ and $\inf$ is distributive with respect to the other and with respect to itself.

(3) In $\mathbf Z$ ($*$ and more generally in any ring$_*$) multiplication is distributive with respect to addition.

(4) In $\mathbf N$ addition and multiplication are distributive with respect to the laws $\sup$ and $\inf.
