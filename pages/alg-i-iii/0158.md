12. Let G be a group such that, for a fixed integer $n > 1$, $(xy)^n = x^n y^n$ for all $x \in G, y \in G$. If $G^{(n)}$ denotes the set of $x^n$, where $x$ runs through $G$, and $G_{(n)}$ the set of $x \in G$ such that $x^n = e$, show that $G^{(n)}$ and $G_{(n)}$ are normal subgroups of $G$; if $G$ is finite, the order of $G^{(n)}$ is equal to the index of $G_{(n)}$. Show that, for all $x, y$ in $G$, also $x^{1-n} y^{1-n} = (xy)^{1-n}$ and deduce that $x^{n-1} y^n = y^n x^{n-1}$; conclude from this that the set of elements of $G$ of the form $x^{n(n-1)}$ generates a commutative subgroup of $G$.

13. Let $G$ be a group. If $S$ is a simple group, $S$ is said to occur in $G$ if there exist two subgroups $H, H'$ of $G$ with $H$ a normal subgroup of $H'$ such that $H'/H$ is isomorphic to $S$. Let $\operatorname{in}(G)$ denote the set of isomorphism classes of simple groups occurring in $G$.
(a) Show that $\operatorname{in}(G) = \varnothing \Leftrightarrow G = \{e\}$.
(b) If $H$ is a subgroup of $G$, show that $\operatorname{in}(H) \subset \operatorname{in}(G)$; if moreover $H$ is normal, then
$$
\operatorname{in}(G) = \operatorname{in}(H) \cup \operatorname{in}(G/H).
$$
(c) Let $G_1$ and $G_2$ be two groups. Show the equivalence of the two following properties:
(i) $\operatorname{in}(G_1) \cap \operatorname{in}(G_2) = \varnothing$;
(ii) Every subgroup of $G_1 \times G_2$ is of the form $H_1 \times H_2$ with $H_1 \subset G_1$ and $H_2 \subset G_2$.
(Use Exercise 8.)
*When $G_1$ and $G_2$ are finite groups, show that (i) and (ii) are equivalent to:
(iii) Card($G_1$) and Card($G_2$) are relatively prime.*

14. Let $G$ be a group and $H$ a subgroup of $G$. Any subset $T$ of $G$ which meets every left coset mod. $H$ in one and only one point is called a representative system of the left cosets mod. $H$ (cf. Set Theory, II, § 6, no. 2); this condition is equivalent to the following:
The mapping $(x, y) \mapsto xy$ is a bijection of $T \times H$ onto $G$.
(a) Let $T$ be such a representative system; for all $g \in G$ and all $t \in T$, let $x(g, t) \in T$ and $y(g, t) \in H$ be such that $gt = x(g, t)y(g, t)$. Let $S$ be a subset of $G$ generating $G$. Show that the elements $y(g, t), g \in S, t \in T$ generate $H$. (If $H'$ denotes the subgroup of $H$ generated by these elements, show that $T.H'$ is stable under the $\gamma_g, g \in S$, hence also under the $\gamma_g, g \in G$, and deduce that $T.H' = G$, whence $H' = H$.)
(b) Suppose that $(G:H)$ is finite. Show that $G$ can be generated by a finite subset if and only if $H$ can.
