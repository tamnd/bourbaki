5. Show that the only triples $(m, n, p)$ of natural numbers $\neq 0$ such that $(m^n)^p = m^{np}$ are: $(1, n, p)$, $n$ and $p$ being arbitrary; $(m, n, 1)$, $m$ and $n$ arbitrary and $(m, 2, 2)$ where $m$ is arbitrary.

6. Let $\tau$ be a law of composition on a set E; let A be the subset of E consisting of the elements $x$ such that $x \tau (y \tau z) = (x \tau y) \tau z$ for all $y \in E, z \in E$; show that A is a stable subset of E and that the law induced on A by $\tau$ is associative.

7. If $\tau$ is an associative law on E and $a$ and $b$ two elements of E, the sets $\{a\} \tau E, E \tau \{b\}, \{a\} \tau E \tau \{b\}, E \tau \{a\} \tau E$ are stable subsets of E under the law $\tau$.

8. Let $\tau$ be an associative law on E and $a$ an element of E; for all $x \in E, y \in E$, we write $x \perp y = x \tau a \tau y$: show that the law $\perp$ is associative.

9. On a set E the mappings $(x, y) \mapsto x$ and $(x, y) \mapsto y$ are opposite associative laws of composition.

10. Let X and Y be any two subsets of a set E and let $X \tau Y = X \cup Y$ if $X \cap Y = \varnothing, X \tau Y = E$ if $X \cap Y \neq \varnothing$; show that the law of composition thus defined on $\mathcal{P}(E)$ is associative and commutative.

11. Let $\tau$ be an associative law on E and A and B two subsets of E which are stable under this law; show that, if $B \tau A \subset A \tau B, A \tau B$ is a stable subset of E.

12. The only distinct natural numbers $\neq 0$ which are permutable under the law $(x, y) \mapsto x^y$ are 2 and 4.

13. Show that under the law $(X, Y) \mapsto X \circ Y$ between subsets of $E \times E$ the centre is the set consisting of $\varnothing$ and the diagonal.

14. Show that under the law of composition $f \circ g$ between mappings of E into E the centre consists of the identity mapping.

15. A law $\tau$ on a set E is called idempotent if all the elements of E are idempotent (no. 4) under this law, that is if $x \tau x = x$ for all $x \in E$. Show that, if a law $\tau$ on E is associative, commutative and idempotent, the relation $x \tau y = y$ is an order relation on E; if we write $x \leq y$, any two elements $x, y$ of E admit a least upper bound (under this order relation) equal to $x \tau y$. Obtain the converse.
