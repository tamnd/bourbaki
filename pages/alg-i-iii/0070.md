**Definition 13.** Let $G$ be a group with operators and $(H_i)_{i\in I}$ a family of normal stable subgroups of $G$. Let $p_i:G\longrightarrow G/H_i$ be the canonical homomorphism. $G$ is called the internal product (or product) of the family of quotient groups $(G/H_i)$ if the homomorphism $g\longmapsto (p_i(g))_{i\in I}$ is an isomorphism of $G$ onto $\displaystyle\prod_{i\in I}G/H_i$.

Let $G$ and $H$ be groups with operators and let $\phi$ and $\psi$ be two homomorphisms of $G$ into $H$. The set of elements $x$ in $G$ such that $\phi(x)=\psi(x)$ is a stable subgroup of $G$, called the coincidence group of $\phi$ and $\psi$. In particular, let $\phi_1:G_1\longrightarrow H$ and $\phi_2:G_2\longrightarrow H$ be homomorphisms of groups with operators; the coincidence group of the homomorphisms $\phi_1\circ\operatorname{pr}_1$ and $\phi_2\circ\operatorname{pr}_2$ of $G_1\times G_2$ into $H$ is called the fibre product of $G_1$ and $G_2$ over $H$ relative to $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$. It is denoted by $G_1\times_HG_2$ when there is no ambiguity about $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ and the restrictions $p_1$ and $p_2$ of $\operatorname{pr}_1$ and $\operatorname{pr}_2$ to $G_1\times_HG_2$ are also called projection homomorphisms. Then $\phi_1\circ p_1=\phi_2\circ p_2$. The elements of $G_1\times_HG_2$ are the ordered pairs $(g_1,g_2)\in G_1\times G_2$ such that $\phi_1(g_1)=\phi_2(g_2)$. If $f_i$ is a homomorphism of a group with operators $K$ into $G_i$ ($i=1,2$) and $\phi_1\circ f_1=\phi_2\circ f_2$, there exist one and only one homomorphism $f$ of $K$ into $G_1\times_HG_2$ such that $f_i=p_i\circ f$ for $i=1,2$.
