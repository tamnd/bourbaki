34. Let p be a prime number. Show that every group of order $p^2$ is commutative.

35. Let G be a group such that the derived group D = D(G) is contained in the centre C of G.

(a) Show that the mapping $(x, y) \mapsto (x, y)$ induces a mapping
$$
\phi : (G/C) \times (G/C) \to D
$$
such that, writing these groups additively:
$$
\begin{align*}
\phi(\alpha + \beta, \gamma) &= \phi(\alpha, \gamma) + \phi(\beta, \gamma) & \phi(\alpha, \beta) &= -\phi(\beta, \alpha) \\
\phi(\alpha, \beta + \gamma) &= \phi(\alpha, \beta) + \phi(\alpha, \gamma) & \phi(\alpha, \alpha) &= 0
\end{align*}
$$
for all $\alpha, \beta, \gamma \in G/C$.

(b) Show that, for every integer n,
$$
(yx)^n = y^n x^n (x, y)^{n(n-1)/2}
$$
for $x, y \in G$. Deduce that if n is odd and $d^n = 1$ for all $d \in D$, the mapping $x \mapsto x^n$ induces a homomorphism $\theta : G/D \to C$.

¶ 36. Let p be a prime number and G a non-commutative group of order $p^3$.

(a) Show that, with the notation of the preceding exercise, C = D and that G/D is isomorphic to the product of two groups of order p.

(b) Suppose that p is odd and that the homomorphism $\theta$ of the preceding exercise is non-zero. Show that G is the semi-direct product of a group of order p by a cyclic group of order $p^2$. Show that there exist elements x, y generating G such that
$$
x^{p^2} = 1, \quad y^p = 1, \quad (x, y) = x^p,
$$
that G is characterized up to isomorphism by this property (and by the fact that it is of order $p^3$) and that such a group G exists.

(c) For $p = 2$, consider the same question as in (b) with the hypothesis $\theta \neq 0$ replaced by the hypothesis that G contains a non-central element of order 2. Show that such a group is isomorphic to the dihedral group $\mathbf{D}_4$ (cf. Exercise 4) and also to a Sylow 2-subgroup of $\mathfrak{S}_4$.
