If $\epsilon$ is bijective every element of $S$ is invertible, for $\epsilon(S)$ is composed of invertible elements of $E_S$. Conversely, suppose every element of $S$ is invertible; then every element of $S'$ is invertible (no. 3, Corollary 2 to Proposition 4) and hence cancellable. Then $\epsilon$ is injective by (ii) and $a/p=\epsilon(a\mathbin{\cdot}p^*)$ by (1), hence $\epsilon$ is surjective.

**Theorem 1.** — Let $E$ be a commutative monoid, $S$ a subset of $E$, $E_S$ the monoid of fractions associated with $S$ and $\epsilon:E\longrightarrow E_S$ the canonical homomorphism. Further let $f$ be a homomorphism of $E$ into a monoid $F$ (not necessarily commutative) such that every element of $f(S)$ is invertible in $F$. There exists one and only one homomorphism $\bar f$ of $E_S$ into $F$ such that $f=\bar f\circ\epsilon$.

If $\bar f$ is a homomorphism of $E_S$ into $F$ such that $f=\bar f\circ\epsilon$, then

$$
\bar f(a/p)=\bar f(\epsilon(a)\epsilon(p)^*)=\bar f(\epsilon(a))\bar f(\epsilon(p))^*=f(a)f(p)^*
$$

for $a\in E$ and $p\in S'$, whence the uniqueness of $\bar f$.

Let $g$ be the mapping of $E\times S'$ into $F$ defined by $g(a,p)=f(a)\mathbin{\cdot}f(p)^*$. We show that $g$ is a homomorphism of $E\times S'$ into $F$. First of all,

$$
g(e,e)=f(e)f(e)^*=e.
$$

Let $(a,p)$ and $(a',p')$ be two elements of $E\times S'$; as $a'$ and $p$ commute in $E$, $f(a')$ and $f(p)$ commute in $F$, whence $f(a')f(p)=f(p)f(a')$ by no. 3, Proposition 5. Moreover $f(pp')^*=f(p'p)^*=(f(p')f(p))^*=f(p)^*f(p')^*$ by no. 3, Proposition 4, whence

$$
g(aa',pp')=f(aa')f(pp')^*=f(a)f(a')f(p)^*f(p')^*=f(a)f(p)^*f(a')f(p')^*
=g(a,p)g(a',p').
$$

We show that $g$ is compatible with the equivalence relation R on $E\times S'$: if $(a,p)$ and $(a',p')$ are congruent mod. R, there exists $s\in S'$ with $spa'=sap'$, whence $f(s)f(p)f(a')=f(s)f(a)f(p')$. As $f(s)$ is invertible, it follows that $f(p)f(a')=f(a)f(p')$ and then by left multiplication by $f(p)^*$ and right multiplication by $f(p')^*$

$$
g(a',p')=f(a')f(p')^*=f(p)^*f(a)=f(a)f(p)^*=g(a,p).
$$

Hence there exists a homomorphism $\bar f$ of $E_S$ into $F$ such that $\bar f(a/p)=g(a,p)$, whence $\bar f(\epsilon(a))=\bar f(a/e)=f(a)f(e)^*=f(a)$. Hence $\bar f\circ\epsilon=f$.

**Corollary.** — Let $E$ and $F$ be two commutative monoids, $S$ and $T$ subsets of $E$ and $F$ respectively, $f$ a homomorphism of $E$ into $F$ such that $f(S)\subset T$ and $\epsilon:E\longrightarrow E_S$, $\eta:F\longrightarrow F_T$ the canonical homomorphisms. There exists one and only one homomorphism $g:E_S\longrightarrow F_T$ such that $g\circ\epsilon=\eta\circ f$.

The homomorphism $\eta\circ f$ of $E$ into $F_T$ maps every element of $S$ to an invertible element of $F_T$.
