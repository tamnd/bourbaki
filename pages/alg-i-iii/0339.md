Remark. Let α^{-1}φ(x), β^{-1}φ(y) be two elements of E_{(K)}, with α ∈ A, β ∈ A, x ∈ E, y ∈ E, αβ ≠ 0. In order that α^{-1}φ(x) = β^{-1}φ(y), it is necessary and sufficient that βx − αy be a torsion element of E, for this relation is equivalent to βφ(x) = αφ(y), which may also be written φ(βx − αy) = 0.

COROLLARY 1. *If E is a torsion-free A-module, the canonical mapping* φ : E → E_{(K)} *is injective*.

Recall (§ 5, no. 1) that for every A-linear mapping of E into a vector space F over K, there exists one and only one K-linear mapping $ \vec{f} : E_{(K)} \to F $ such that $ f = \vec{f} \circ \phi $; we shall say that $ \vec{f} $ is *associated* with $ f $.

COROLLARY 2. *Let f be an A-linear mapping of E into a vector space F over K; if Ker(f) ⊂ T(E), the K-linear mapping* $ \vec{f} $ *associated with f is injective*.

We write an element of Ker($ \vec{f} $) in the form $ \lambda^{-1}\phi(x) $, where $ \lambda \in A, \lambda \neq 0, x \in E $; the relation $ \vec{f}(\lambda^{-1}\phi(x)) = 0 $ is equivalent to $ \lambda^{-1}\vec{f}(\phi(x)) = 0 $ in F and hence to $ f(x) = \vec{f}(\phi(x)) = 0 $. By hypothesis, this implies $ x \in T(E) $ and hence $ \phi(x) = 0 $, which proves the corollary.

COROLLARY 3. *Let E be an A-module and g an A-linear mapping of E into a vector space F over K such that g(E) generates F and Ker(g) ⊂ T(E). Then the K-linear mapping* $ \bar{g} $ *associated with g is an isomorphism of* E_{(K)} *onto F*.

$ \bar{g} $ is injective by Corollary 2 and the hypothesis that $ g(E) $ generates F implies that $ \bar{g} $ is surjective.

For every A-module E, the vector space E_{(K)} is said to be *associated* with E. For every subset S of E the *rank* of S over K (or by an abuse of language, the *rank* of S) is the rank of the canonical image $ \phi(S) $ of S in E_{(K)}, in other words (no. 2, Definition 1) the dimension over K of the vector subspace of E_{(K)} generated by $ \phi(S) $.

When E is a *torsion-free* A-module, it is usually identified with its canonical image $ \phi(E) $ in E_{(K)}. With this convention, every generating system of E contains a *basis* of E_{(K)} (no. 1, Theorem 2). In particular:

COROLLARY 4. *Every finitely generated A-module is of finite rank*.
