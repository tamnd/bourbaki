Let X be a set and $(u_\alpha, v_\alpha)_{\alpha \in I}$ be a family of ordered pairs of elements of Mo(X). Let R be the equivalence relation on Mo(X) compatible with the law on Mo(X) and generated by the $(u_\alpha, v_\alpha)$ (§ 1, no. 6). The monoid Mo(X)/R is called the *monoid defined by X and the relators* $(u_\alpha, v_\alpha)_{\alpha \in I}$. Let h be the canonical morphism of Mo(X) onto Mo(X)/R. Then Mo(X) is generated by h(X).

Let N be a monoid and $(n_x)_{x \in X}$ a family of elements of N. Let k be the morphism of Mo(X) into N such that $k(x) = n_x$ for all $x \in X$ (Proposition 3). ### 3. AMALGAMATED SUM OF MONOIDS

*Let $(M_i)_{i \in I}$ denote a family of monoids and $e_i$ the identity element of $M_i$. We are given a monoid A and a family of homomorphisms $h_i : A \to M_i$ (for $i \in I$).*

The set S the sum of the family $(M_i)_{i \in I}$ has elements the ordered pairs $(i, x)$ with $i \in I$ and $x \in M_i$. For every triple $\alpha = (i, x, x')$ with $i \in I$, $x, x'$ in $M_i$, write $u_\alpha = (i, xx')$ and $v_\alpha = (i, x).(i, x')$; for every triple $\lambda = (i, j, a)$ in $I \times I \times A$, write $p_\lambda = (i, h_i(a))$ and $q_\lambda = (j, h_j(a))$; for all $i \in I$, write $\varepsilon_i = (i, e_i)$. The monoid M defined by S and the relators $(u_\alpha, v_\alpha)$, $(p_\lambda, q_\lambda)$ and $(\varepsilon_i, e)$ is called the *sum of the family* $(M_i)_{i \in I}$ *amalgamated by* A. Let $\phi$ denote the canonical homomorphism of Mo(S) onto M and write $\phi_i(x) = \phi(i, x)$ for $(i, x) \in S$. It is said that $\phi_i$ is the *canonical mapping* of $M_i$ into M. For all $a \in A$, the element $\phi(i, h_i(a))$ is independent of i and denoted by $h(a)$.\footnote{When I is empty, $M = \{e\}$ and $h(a) = e$ for all $a \in A$.}

The universal property of monoids defined by generators and relators (no. 2) implies the following result:

**Proposition 4.** (a) *For all $i \in I$, the mapping $\phi_i$ is a homomorphism of $M_i$ into M and $\phi_i \circ h_i = h$ for all $i \in I$. Further, M is generated by $\bigcup_{i \in I} \phi_i(M_i)$.*

(b) *Let $M'$ be a monoid and $f' : M_i \to M'$ (for $i \in I$) homomorphisms such that $f_i \circ h_i$ is independent of $i \in I$. There exists one and only one homomorphism $f : M \to M'$ such that $f_i = f \circ \phi_i$ for all $i \in I$.*

In what follows we shall make the following hypothesis:

(A) *For all $i \in I$, there exists a subset $P_i$ of $M_i$ containing $e_i$ such that the mapping $(a, p) \mapsto h_i(a).p$ of $A \times P_i$ into $M_i$ is bijective.*
