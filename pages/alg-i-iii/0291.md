## § 4. RELATIONS BETWEEN TENSOR PRODUCTS AND HOMOMORPHISM MODULES

1. THE ISOMORPHISMS $ \mathrm{Hom}_B(E \otimes_A F, G) \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(F, \mathrm{Hom}_B(E, G)) $ AND $ \mathrm{Hom}_c(E \otimes_A F, G) \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(E, \mathrm{Hom}_c(F, G)) $

Let E be a right A-module, F a left A-module, G a $ \mathbf{Z} $-module and H the $ \mathbf{Z} $-module of mappings $ f : E \times F \to G $ which are $ \mathbf{Z} $-bilinear and satisfy

(1)
$$
f(x \lambda, y) = f(x, \lambda y) \quad \text{for } x \in E, y \in F, \lambda \in A.
$$

It has been seen (\S 3, no. 1, Proposition 1) that there exists a canonical $ \mathbf{Z} $-module homomorphism

(2)
$$
H \to \mathrm{Hom}_Z(E \otimes_A F, G).
$$

On the other hand, a left A-module structure has been defined on $ \mathrm{Hom}_Z(E, G) $ and a right A-module structure on $ \mathrm{Hom}_Z(F, G) $ (\S 3, no. 3); we may therefore consider the $ \mathbf{Z} $-modules $ \mathrm{Hom}_A(E, \mathrm{Hom}_Z(F, G)) $ and $ \mathrm{Hom}_A(F, \mathrm{Hom}_Z(E, G)) $. A mapping $ f $ of $ E \times F $ into $ G $ is canonically identified with a mapping of $ E $ into the set $ G^F $ of mappings of $ F $ into $ G $ (Set Theory, II, \S 5, no. 2); by expressing the fact that the latter mapping belongs to $ \mathrm{Hom}_A(E, \mathrm{Hom}_Z(F, G)) $, we obtain precisely the fact that $ f $ is biadditive and conditions (1); whence there is a canonical isomorphism

(3)
$$
H \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(E, \mathrm{Hom}_Z(F, G))
$$
and similarly there is defined a canonical isomorphism

(4)
$$
H \to \mathrm{Hom}_A(F, \mathrm{Hom}_Z(E, G)).
$$

Suppose now that E and G also have left (resp. right) B-module structures and that the A-module and B-module structures on E are compatible. Then $ E \otimes_A F $ has canonically a left (resp. right) B-module structure (\S 3, no. 4) and on the other hand $ \mathrm{Hom}_B(E, G) $ has canonically a left A-module structure (\S 1, no. 14). We may therefore consider the $ \mathbf{Z} $-modules $ \mathrm{Hom}_B(E \otimes_A F, G) $ and $ \mathrm{Hom}_A(F, \mathrm{Hom}_B(E, G)) $, which are submodules of $ \mathrm{Hom}_Z(E \otimes_A F, G) $ and $ \mathrm{Hom}_A(F, \mathrm{Hom}_Z(E, G)) $ respectively (\S 2, no. 1, Theorem 2). We examine under what condition a mapping $ f \in H $ has as image under the isomorphisms (2) and (4) an element of $ \mathrm{Hom}_B(E \otimes_A F, G) $ and an element of $ \mathrm{Hom}_A(E, \mathrm{Hom}_B(F, G)) $ respectively; in each of the two cases we find the *same* condition

$$
f(\beta x, y) = \beta f(x, y)
$$
(resp. $ f(x \beta, y) = f(x, y) \beta $)

for $ x \in E, y \in F, \beta \in B $.
