c) Show that for a function $g \in \mathscr{C}(X; \mathbf{R})$, the following conditions are equivalent:
$\alpha$ For every $x \in X$ and every measure $\mu \in \mathscr{M}_x^F$, one has $g(x) = \int g d\mu$.
$\beta$ For every $x \in X$, $\underline{H}_x^F(g|F) = \overline{H}_x^F(g|F) = g(x)$.
$\gamma$ For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exist two finite sequences $(h_i'), (h_j'')$ of functions in $\mathscr{H}$ such that, setting $h' = \sup(h_i')$, $h'' = \inf(h_j'')$, one has $h' \leq g \leq h''$ and $h'' - h' \leq \varepsilon$.
(To see that $\beta$ implies $\gamma$), argue as in GT, X, §4, No. 1, Prop. 2.)

When a function $g \in \mathscr{C}(X; \mathbf{R})$ has the preceding equivalent properties, it is said to be $\mathscr{H}$-harmonic; the set $\mathscr{H}^c$ of $\mathscr{H}$-harmonic functions is a closed linear subspace of $\mathscr{C}(X; \mathbf{R})$ containing $\mathscr{H}$; it is independent of the closed set $F \supset \mathrm{Ch}_{\mathscr{H}}(X)$ under consideration. One has $(\mathscr{H}^c)^c = \mathscr{H}^c$, and the equivalence relation in $\mathscr{M}_+(X)$ defined by $\mathscr{H}^c$ is identical to the relation $\lambda \prec \mu$ defined by $\mathscr{H}$; consequently $\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathscr{H}^c}(X) = \mathrm{Ch}_{\mathscr{H}}(X)$. The mapping $g \mapsto g|F$ is a strictly increasing *isometry* of $\mathscr{H}^c$ onto its image in $\mathscr{C}(F; \mathbf{R})$ (which is therefore a closed subspace of $\mathscr{C}(F; \mathbf{R})$).
