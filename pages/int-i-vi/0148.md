### 5. Integrable sets

**Definition 2.** — *A subset* $ A $ *of a locally compact space* $ X $ *is said to be integrable with respect to a measure* $ \mu $ *on* $ X $ *(or, that it is* $ \mu $*-integrable)* *if the characteristic function* $ \varphi_A $ *of* $ A $ *is integrable.* *The finite number* $ \mu(A) = \int \varphi_A\, d\mu $ *is called the measure of* $ A $.

For every integrable set $ A $, $ |\mu|(A) = |\mu|^*(A) $ (No. 2, Prop. 1); for a set to be *negligible*, it is necessary and sufficient that it be of *measure zero with respect to* $ |\mu| $.

**Proposition 6.** — *The union of a finite family* $ (A_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n} $ *of integrable sets is integrable,* and

$$
|\mu| \left( \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i \right) \leq \sum_{i=1}^n |\mu|(A_i).
$$

*If, moreover, the* $ A_i $ *are pairwise disjoint,* then

$$
\mu \left( \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i \right) = \sum_{i=1}^n \mu(A_i).
$$
