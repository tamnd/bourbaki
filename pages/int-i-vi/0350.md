4) Let T and $\mu$ be the locally compact space and measure defined in Exer. 5 of Ch. IV, §1.

a) Show that $\mu$ is the supremum of a sequence of measures with finite support, but that $\mu^* \neq \mu^*$, and that $\mu$ is not moderated. From this, deduce that Prop. 11 becomes false when the symbol $\int^*$ therein is replaced by $\int^*$, even when the set A is countable.

b) For every real number $y$, let $f_y$ be the characteristic function of the set reduced to the point $(0, y)$ of T. Show that the mapping $t \mapsto (f_y(t))_{y \in \mathbf{R}}$ is $\mu$-measurable, but that if $f = \sup_{y \in \mathbf{R}} f_y$ then $\mu^*(f) = +\infty$ and $\sup_{y \in \mathbf{R}} \mu^*(f_y) = 0$.

5) Let $(\mu_n)$ be a sequence of positive measures on T that converges quasi-strongly to $\mu$ in $\mathcal{M}(T)$ (Ch. III, §1, Exer. 8).

a) Show that if a mapping $f$ of T into a topological space G is $\mu_n$-measurable for every $n$, then it is $\mu$-measurable (make use of Exercise 6 b) of Ch. IV, §1).

b) Let $f$ be a mapping of T into a Banach space F, that is essentially $\mu_n$-integrable for every $n$. Show that if the sequence $(\mu_n^* (|f|))$ is bounded, then $f$ is essentially $\mu$-integrable (Exer. 2). Show by means of an example that one does not necessarily have $\int f \, d\mu = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int f \, d\mu_n$ (take for $\mu_n$ a point measure on a compact space, such that $\lim \| \mu_n \| = 0$); however, this relation holds when $f$ is bounded and has compact support.

6) Let $\mathcal{K}$ be a $\mu$-dense set of compact subsets of T. Let $f$ be a function defined on T, with values in a Banach space F, such that $f \varphi_K$ is $\mu$-integrable for every $K \in \mathcal{K}$. If the integrals $\int f \varphi_K \, d\mu$ have a limit in F with respect to the directed set $\mathcal{K}$ (for $\subset$), then $f$ is essentially $\mu$-integrable.

1) Let $(\lambda_\alpha)$ be a family of complex point measures on T. For the family $(\lambda_\alpha)$ to be summable in the vector space $\mathcal{M}(T; \mathbf{C})$ equipped with the vague topology, it is necessary and sufficient that the family $(|\lambda_\alpha|)$ be so.

2) Let $(\lambda_n)$ be a sequence of complex measures on T. For the sequence $(\lambda_n)$ to be summable in $\mathcal{M}(T; \mathbf{C})$ equipped with the vague topology, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every function $f \in \mathcal{H}(T)$, the series with general term $\lambda_n(f)$ be absolutely convergent (make use of the Banach-Steinhaus theorem; cf. TVS, III, §4, No. 2, Cor. 2 of Th. 1).

3) Let T be the compact interval $[0, 1]$ of $\mathbf{R}$; for every integer $n > 0$, let $\lambda_n$ be the measure
$$
f \mapsto \frac{1}{n} \int_0^1 f(x) \sin 2n \pi x \, dx
$$
on T. Show that, in the space $\mathcal{M}(T)$ equipped with the vague topology (or with the quasi-strong topology (Ch. III, §1, Exer. 8)), the sequence of measures $(\lambda_n)$ is summable, but the sequence $(|\lambda_n|)$ is not. (Observe that if $\alpha_n = n \cdot \lambda_n(f)$ then $\sum_n \alpha_n^2 < +\infty$, by making use of Bessel’s inequality, and deduce from this that $\sum_n |\lambda_n(f)| < +\infty$ by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.)
