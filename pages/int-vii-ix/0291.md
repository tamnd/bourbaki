3) Let E be a set and $\mathscr{T}$ a tribe of subsets of E. Denote by $\mathscr{M}$ the set of mappings f of E into $\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$ such that the set of $x \in E$ for which $f(x) \geq c$ belongs to $\mathscr{T}$ for every $c \in \overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$.

a) Show that the limit superior and limit inferior of every sequence of elements of $\mathscr{M}$ belong to $\mathscr{M}$ (first study sequences that are increasing or decreasing).

b) Show that $\mathscr{T}$ is the set of subsets of E whose characteristic function belongs to $\mathscr{M}$.

c) Let $f \in \mathscr{M}$. For every Borel subset B of $\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$, the set $f^{-1}(B)$ belongs to $\mathscr{T}$ (the set of subsets B of $\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$ such that $f^{-1}(B) \in \mathscr{T}$ is a tribe, containing the intervals $[c, +\infty])$.

d) Let $f_1, \ldots, f_n$ be in $\mathscr{M}$ and let $\varphi$ be a Borel mapping of $(\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+)^n$ into $\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$; show that the mapping $x \mapsto \varphi(f_1(x), \ldots, f_n(x))$ of E into $\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$ belongs to $\mathscr{M}$. Deduce from this that $\mathscr{M}$ contains the sum of every series of elements of $\mathscr{M}$.

e) Show that $\mathscr{M}$ is the set of limits of increasing sequences of finite positive $\mathscr{T}$-step functions.

4) Notations and hypotheses are those of the preceding exercise. One calls abstract measure on $(E, \mathscr{T})$ every mapping m of $\mathscr{T}$ into $\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$ having the following property: for every sequence $(A_n)_{n \in \mathbf{N}}$ of sets belonging to $\mathscr{T}$, pairwise disjoint, one has $m(\bigcup_{n \in \mathbf{N}} A_n) = \sum_{n \in \mathbf{N}} m(A_n)$. One calls integral on $(E, \mathscr{T})$ every mapping J of $\mathscr{M}$ into $\overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$ satisfying the following conditions: $\alpha)$ $J(\lambda \cdot f) = \lambda \cdot J(f)$ for $\lambda \in \overline{\mathbf{R}}_+$ and $f \in \mathscr{M}$ (with the usual convention $0 \cdot (+\infty) = 0$); $\beta)$ $J\left( \sum_{n \in \mathbf{N}} f_n \right) = \sum_{n \in \mathbf{N}} J(f_n)$ for every sequence $(f_n)_{n \in \mathbf{N}}$ of elements of $\mathscr{M}$.

a) Let J be an integral on $(E, \mathscr{T})$; for every subset $A \in \mathscr{T}$, set $m_J(A) = J(\varphi_A)$; show that $m_J$ is an abstract measure and that the mapping $J \mapsto m_J$ is a bijection of the set of integrals on $(E, \mathscr{T})$ onto the set of abstract measures on $(E, \mathscr{T})$ (if m is an abstract measure, first define $J(f)$ by linearity for the finite positive $\mathscr{T}$-step functions and use Exer. 3 e)). If m is an abstract measure, we will denote by $m^*$ the corresponding integral.

b) Let m be an abstract measure on $(E, \mathscr{T})$. For every positive function f on E, finite or not, set $m^*(f) = \inf_{g \in \mathscr{M}, g \geq f} m^*(g)$. Show that $m^*$ is an encumbrance on E (imitate the proof of Th. 3 of Ch. IV, §1, No. 3). One sets $m^*(A) = m^*(\varphi_A)$ for every subset A of E.

One equips the vector space $\mathscr{F}^p$ with the semi-norm $N_p$, for which it is complete.

a) Let $\mathscr{N}$ be the set of functions f such that $m^*(|f|) = 0$, $\mathscr{V}$ the vector space generated by the finite functions belonging to $\mathscr{M}$, and $\mathscr{L}^p$ the smallest closed linear subspace of $\mathscr{F}^p$ containing the characteristic functions of the sets $A \in \mathscr{T}$ such that $m^*(A)$ is finite.
