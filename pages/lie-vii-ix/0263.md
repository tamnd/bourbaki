Prove that

$\Delta (J(e^\mu)) =\langle \mu, \mu\rangle J(e^\mu)$ for $\mu\in P$,

$\Delta '(e^{\lambda}, e^\mu) = 2\langle \lambda , \mu\rangle e^{\lambda+\mu}$ for $\lambda , \mu\in P$,

$\Delta '(ab, c) =a\Delta '(b, c) +b\Delta '(a, c)$ for $a, b, c\in k[P]$.

$c)$ Let $\lambda \in P_{++},c_{\lambda}=$ ch(E($\lambda$ )) and $d= J(e^{\rho})$. Prove that

$$
\Delta (c_{\lambda}d) =\langle \lambda +\rho , \lambda +\rho \rangle c_{\lambda}d
$$

(Use $a),b)$, §6, Cor. of Prop. 7, and Chap. VI, §3, no. 3, formula (3).)

$d)$ Deduce another proof of the formula of H. Weyl from the above and §7, no. 2, Prop. 5 (iii).

$e)$ For all $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, put dim $E^{\lambda}=m(\lambda )$. Deduce from $a)$ that

Tr(($xy$)$_E|E^{\lambda}) =\sum^{+\infty}_{i=0}(\lambda +i\alpha )([x, y])m(\lambda +i\alpha )$

$$
\sum_{i=-\infty}^{+\infty}(\lambda +i\alpha )([x, y])m(\lambda +i\alpha ) = 0
$$

$f)$ Let $\langle  \cdot ,\cdot  \rangle$ be a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form on $\mathfrak{g}$ whose restriction to $\mathfrak{h}$ is the inverse of the form chosen above. Let $\Gamma$ be the corresponding Casimir element. Assume that E is simple; put $\Gamma_E=\gamma .1$, where $\gamma \in k$. By using $e)$ and §2, no. 3, Prop. 6, show that

$$
\gamma m(\lambda ) =\langle \lambda , \lambda \rangle m(\lambda ) +\sum_{\alpha\in R}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=0}\langle \lambda +i\alpha , \alpha \rangle m(\lambda +i\alpha )
$$

for all $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, and then that

$$
\gamma m(\lambda ) =\langle \lambda , \lambda \rangle m(\lambda ) +\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}m(\lambda )\langle \lambda , \alpha \rangle + 2\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}m(\lambda +i\alpha )\langle \lambda +i\alpha , \alpha \rangle
$$

$$
=\langle \lambda , \lambda + 2\rho \rangle m(\lambda ) + 2\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}m(\lambda +i\alpha )\langle \lambda +i\alpha , \alpha \rangle
$$

$g)$ Continue to assume that E is simple; let $\omega$ be its highest weight. Deduce from $f)$ that, for all $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$,

$$
(\langle \omega +\rho , \omega +\rho \rangle  - \langle \lambda +\rho , \lambda +\rho \rangle )m(\lambda ) = 2\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}\sum^{+\infty}_{i=1}m(\lambda +i\alpha )\langle \lambda +i\alpha , \alpha \rangle
$$
