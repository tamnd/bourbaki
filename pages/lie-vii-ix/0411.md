19) We retain the notations of Exerc. 16 to 18; assume that the subgroup H is principal. Give $X(T)\otimes \mathbf{R}$ (resp. $X(S)\otimes \mathbf{R})$ a scalar product invariant under $W_G(T)$ (resp. under $W_H(S))$. Let $\lambda , \mu$ be two roots in $B_H$.

$a)$ Show that, if $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are orthogonal, the sets $R(\lambda )$ and $R(\mu)$ are orthogonal. Deduce that if G is almost simple then so is H.

$b)$ Assume from now on that $n(\lambda , \mu) =-1$. Show that there exists a surjective map $u: R(\lambda )\rightarrow R(\mu)$ such that, for $\alpha \in R(\lambda ),u(\alpha )$ is the unique root of $R(\mu)$ linked (that is, not orthogonal) to $\alpha$; we have $K_\mu=\sum_{\beta\in R(\mu)}K_{\beta}$, and the roots

in $R(\mu)$ are pairwise orthogonal (write $K_\mu$ and $K_{\lambda}$ as linear combinations of the $K_{\alpha}$, then identify the coefficients).

$c)$ If $n(\mu, \lambda ) =-1$, the map $u$ is bijective, and the only pairs of linked roots in $R(\lambda )\cup R(\mu)$ are the pairs $(\alpha , u(\alpha ))$ for $\alpha \in R(\lambda )$.

$d)$ Assume that $n(\mu, \lambda ) =-2$; let $\beta \in R(\mu)$. Show that $u^{-1}(\beta )$ contains either one or two elements; if $u^{-1}(\beta ) =\{\alpha_1, \alpha_2\}$, the root $\alpha_i(i= 1,2)$ is orthogonal to the other roots in $R(\lambda )$, and has the same length as $\beta$; if $u^{-1}(\beta ) =\{\alpha \}$ and $\|\alpha \|=\|\beta \|,\alpha$ is linked to a unique root $\alpha '\in R(\lambda )$, of the same length as $\alpha$, and $\{\alpha , \alpha '\}$ is orthogonal to the remainder of $R(\lambda )$; if $u^{-1}(\beta ) =\{\alpha \}$ and $\|\alpha \| \not=\|\beta \|,\alpha$ is orthogonal to the other roots in $R(\lambda )$.

$e)$ Study similarly the case $n(\mu, \lambda ) =-3$.

20) Assume that the group G is almost simple; let H be a principal subgroup of G, of rank $\geq 2$.

$a)$ Show that H is semi-simple of type $B_h,C_h,F_4$ or $G_2$ (use Exerc. 19).

$b)$ Assume that H is of rank $\geq 3$. Show that G is of type $A_l,D_l,E_6,E_7$ or $E_8$ (consider the terminal vertices of the Dynkin graph of $R(G,T)$, and apply Exerc. 19).

$c)$ Show that, if rk $H\geq 3$, we are in one of the following situations:

G is of type $A_{2l}(l\geq 3)$ and H of type $B_l$;

G is of type $A_{2l-1}(l\geq 3)$ and H of type $C_l$;

G is of type $D_l(l\geq 4)$ and H of type $B_{l-1}$;

G is of type $E_6$ and H of type $F_4$.

$d)$ Show that, if H is of type $B_2$, then G is of type $A_3$ or $A_4$.

$e)$ Show that, if H is of type $G_2$, then G is of type $B_3,D_4$ or $A_6$.

(For a more explicit description of these situations, see §5, Exerc. 5.)

$f)$ Let K be a connected closed subgroup of G containing H; show that either K = G or K = H, or H is of type $G_2, K$ of type $B_3$ and G of type $D_4$ or $A_6$.
