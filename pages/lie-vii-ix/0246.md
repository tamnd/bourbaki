§**6**

1) Show that $Z(\lambda )$ can be defined as a quotient of the representation $\rho_{\lambda}$ of §4, Exerc. 1.

2) Let $\mu$ be a weight of $Z(\lambda )$ (resp. $E(\lambda ))$. Show that there exists a sequence of weights $\mu_0, . . . , \mu_n$ of $Z(\lambda )$ (resp. of $E(\lambda ))$ such that $\mu_0=\lambda , \mu_n=\mu$, and $\mu_{i-1}-\mu_i\in B$ for $1\leq i\leq n$.

3) Assume that $\mathfrak{g}$ is simple, and denote by $\widetilde{\alpha}$ the highest root of R. The module $E( \widetilde{\alpha})$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{g}$, equipped with the adjoint representation. If C is the Casimir element associated to the Killing form of $\mathfrak{g}$, the image of C in End $E( \widetilde{\alpha})$ is the identity (cf. Chap. I, §3, no. 7, Prop. 12). Deduce, by using the Cor. of Prop. 7, that

$$
\Phi_R( \widetilde{\alpha ,}\widetilde{\alpha}+ 2\rho ) = 1
$$

where $\Phi_R$ is the canonical bilinear form on $\mathfrak{h}^*$ (Chap. VI, §1, no. 12).

4) We use the notations of no. 4.

$a)$ Let $m\in \mathbf{N}$. Give $\mathbf{N}^m$ the product order. Show that, for any subset S of $\mathbf{N}^m$, the set of minimal elements of S is finite.

$b)$ Let $\alpha_1, . . . , \alpha_m$ be distinct elements of $R,\sum X_i\in \mathfrak{g}^{\alpha_i}$ **--** $\{0\}, S$ the set of non-zero sequences $(p_i)\in \mathbf{N}^m$ such that $p_i\alpha_i= 0, M$ the set of minimal elements of S. Then $\mathfrak{h}$ and the $X_1^{p_1}. . . X_m^{p_m}$, where $(p_1, . . . , p_m)\in M$, generate the algebra $U^0$.

$c)$ Show that $U^0$ is both a left- and right-noetherian algebra. (Give $U^0$ the filtration induced by that of $U(\mathfrak{g})$, and show, by using $a)$ and $b)$, that gr $U^0$ is commutative of finite type.)

$d)$ Show that, for all $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*, U^{\lambda}$ is a left (resp. right) $U^0$-module of finite type. $\bigoplus$

$e)$ Let V be a simple $\mathfrak{g}$-module such that $V =_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^*}V_{\lambda}$. If one of the $V_{\lambda}\not= 0$

is finite dimensional, then all of the $V_{\lambda}$ are finite dimensional. (Use $d).)$

5) Show that, if $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k)$, the modules $Z(\lambda )$ of this paragraph are isomorphic to the modules $Z(\lambda )$ of §1, Exerc. 2.
