$a)$ Show that there exists a framing of G stable under $\Phi$.

$b)$ Show that we must be in one of the following situations:

(i) $H = F_0$;

(ii) G is of type $B_3, H$ of type $G_2$ and $\Phi$ reduces to the identity;

(iii) G is of type $A_6, H$ of type $G_2$ and $\Phi$ of order 2.

(Use Exerc. 4 and Exerc. 20 of §4.)

$\P 6)$ Assume that G is simply-connected. Let $p$ be a prime number and $g$ an element of C(G) such that $g^p=e$.

$a)$ Show that there exist elements $u\in T$ and $w\in W$ such that

(i) $w(u)u^{-1}=g$;

(ii) $w^p= 1$;

(iii) $u^p=e$ if $p\not= 2,u^p=e$ or $g$ if $p= 2$.

(Let A be an alcove in $\mathfrak{t}$; for $i= 0,1, . . . , p-1$, let $x_i\in \overline{A}$ be such that exp $x_i=g^i$. Take $u$ to be the element exp $x$, where $x$ is the barycentre of the facet of $\overline{A}$ whose vertices are the $x_i$, and $w$ to be the element of W such that $w(A) = A-x.$)

$b)$ Prove that there exist $u\in T$ and $v\in N_G(T)$ such that

(i) $vuv^{-1}u^{-1}=g$;

(ii) $v^p=e$ if $p\not= 2,v^p=e$ or $g$ if $p= 2$;

(iii) $u^p=e$ if $p\not= 2,u^p=e$ or $g$ if $p= 2$.

(Use the construction in $a$), lifting $w$ to $n_w$ as in Exerc. 12 of §4; take $v=n^{p+1}_w$ if $p\not= 2,v=n_w$ if $p= 2.)$

7) Let $p$ be a prime number. Show that the following conditions are equivalent: (i) $p$ does not divide the order of the torsion subgroup of $\pi_1(G)$;

(ii) for every element $g$ of G of order $p$, the centralizer of $g$ in G is connected; (iii) every subgroup of G isomorphic to $(\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z})^2$ is contained in a maximal torus.

(To prove that (i) $=\Rightarrow$ (ii), use Exerc. $3c$); to prove that (iii) $=\Rightarrow$ (i), use Exerc. 6.)

8) Take $G =\mathbf{S}\mathbf{O}(8,\mathbf{R})/\{\pm I_8\}$; denote by $(\alpha_i)_{1\leq i\leq 4}$ a basis of $R(G,T)$ such that $\alpha_1, \alpha_3$ and $\alpha_4$ are not orthogonal to $\alpha_2$ (Chap. VI, Plate IV). Let A be the subgroup of T consisting of the $t\in T$ such that

$$
\alpha_1(t) =\alpha_3(t) =\alpha_4(t), \alpha_1(t)^2=\alpha_2(t)^2= 1
$$

Prove that A is isomorphic to $(\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})^2$ and that its centralizer in G is a finite non-commutative group.

9) Let R be an irreducible root system, $R^{\vee}$ the inverse system, B a basis of R and $\alpha$ the highest root of R (relative to B). Put

$\alpha =\sum_{\beta\in B}n_{\beta}\beta$ and $\alpha^{\vee}=\sum_{\beta\in B}n^{\vee}_{\beta}\beta^{\vee}$;

let $\nu (R) =$ sup$_{\beta\in B}n^{\vee}_{\beta}$.
