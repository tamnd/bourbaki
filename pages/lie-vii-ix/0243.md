6) Assume that $k=\mathbf{R}$ or $\mathbf{C}$. Let G be the Lie group Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$. Show that an element $a\in G$ is regular (in the sense of Chap. VII, §4, no. 2) if and only if it satisfies the conditions of Exerc. $5a)$.

7) Assume that $\mathfrak{g}$ is splittable. Let $B(\mathfrak{g})$ be the canonical basis of the canonical Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ (no. 3, Remark 2). If $s\in$ Aut($\mathfrak{g}$)$,s$ induces a permutation of $B(\mathfrak{g})$; denote by sgn($s$) the sign of this permutation. Show that $s$ operates on $\bigwedge^n\mathfrak{g}$ (with $n=$ dim $\mathfrak{g})$ by

$x \rightarrow$ sgn($s$)$.x$.

$\P 8)$ Let $\overline{k}$ be an algebraic closure of $k$, and $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}= \overline{k}\otimes_k\mathfrak{g}$. The Galois group Gal($\overline{k}/k$) operates naturally on $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}$, on the canonical Cartan subalgebra of $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}$ (no. 3, Remark 2), as well as on its root system $\overline{R}$ and its canonical basis $\overline{B}$. We thus obtain a continuous homomorphism (i.e. with open kernel)

$\pi :$ Gal($\overline{k}/k$)$\rightarrow$ Aut( $\overline{R},\overline{B})$.

Show that $\mathfrak{g}$ is splittable if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i) The homomorphism $\pi$ is trivial.

(ii) $\mathfrak{g}$ has a Borel subalgebra $\mathfrak{b}$.

(Show that a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ contained in $\mathfrak{b}$ is splitting if and only if $\pi$ is trivial.)

9) Let R be a reduced root system, B a basis of R, and $(\mathfrak{g}_0,\mathfrak{h}_0,B,(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in B})$ a corresponding framed semi-simple Lie algebra (§4). Let $\overline{k}$ be an algebraic closure of $k$, and $\rho :$ Gal($\overline{k}/k$)$\rightarrow$ Aut(R$,B)$ a continuous homomorphism (cf. Exerc. 8); if $\sigma \in$ Gal($\overline{k}/k$), denote by $\rho_{\sigma}$ the $k$-linear automorphism of $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_0= \overline{k}\otimes_k\mathfrak{g}_0$ such that $\rho_{\sigma}(X_{\alpha}) =X_{\rho(\sigma)\alpha}$. On the other hand, the natural operation of Gal($\overline{k}/k$) on $\overline{k}$ can be extended to an operation on $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_0$. Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be the subset of $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_0$ consisting of the elements $x$ such that $\rho_{\sigma}(x) =\sigma^{-1}.x$ for all $\sigma \in$ Gal($\overline{k}/k$).

$a)$ Show that $\mathfrak{g}$ is a $k$-Lie subalgebra of $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_0$, and that the injection of $\mathfrak{g}$ into $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_0$ extends to an isomorphism from $\overline{k}\otimes_k\mathfrak{g}$ to $\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_0$. In particular, $\mathfrak{g}$ is semi-simple.

$b)$ Let $\mathfrak{b}_0$ be the subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}_0$ generated by $\mathfrak{h}_0$ and the $X_{\alpha}$. Put

$$
\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_0= \overline{k}\otimes_k\mathfrak{b}_0,\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}\cap \overline{\mathfrak{b}}_0,\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_0= \overline{k}\otimes_k\mathfrak{h}_0,\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}\cap \overline{\mathfrak{h}}_0
$$

Show that $\overline{k}\otimes_k\mathfrak{b}= \overline{\mathfrak{b}}_0$ and that $\overline{k}\otimes_k\mathfrak{h}= \overline{\mathfrak{h}}_0$, so that $\mathfrak{b}$ is a Borel subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $\mathfrak{h}$ is a Cartan subalgebra contained in it.
