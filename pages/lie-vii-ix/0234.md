$\P 18)$ Assume that $k=\mathbf{R},\mathbf{C}$ or a field complete for a discrete valuation and with residue field of characteristic $p\not= 0$ (for example a finite extension of the $p$-adic field $\mathbf{Q}_p)$.

$a)$ Let $\mathfrak{n}$ be a nilpotent Lie algebra, N the Lie group obtained by giving $\mathfrak{n}$ the Hausdorff law (Chap. III, §9, no. 5), and $\rho :\mathfrak{n}\rightarrow \mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(E)$ a linear representation of $\mathfrak{n}$ on a finite dimensional vector space E. Assume that $\rho (x)$ is nilpotent for all $x\in \mathfrak{n}$, and put $\pi (x) =$ exp($\rho (x)$). Show that $\pi$ is the only homomorphism of Lie groups $\varphi : N\rightarrow \mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}(E)$ such that $L(\varphi ) =\rho$.

(When $k=\mathbf{R}$ or $\mathbf{C}$, use the fact that N is connected. When $k$ is ultrametric, show that the eigenvalues of $\varphi (x),x\in N$, are equal to 1; for this, prove first of all that, if $k'$ is a finite extension of $k$, and if $(\lambda_1, . . . , \lambda_n, . . .)$ is a sequence of elements of $k'$ such that $\lambda_n=\lambda^p_{n+1}$ for all $n$ and $\lambda_1= 1$, then $\lambda_n= 1$ for all $n.)$

$b)$ Let $\rho :\mathfrak{s}\rightarrow \mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(E)$ be a finite dimensional linear representation of $\mathfrak{s}$, and let $\pi$ be the homomorphism from $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{L}(2, k)$ to $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}(E)$ compatible with $\rho$ (no. 4). Show that $\pi$ is the unique homomorphism of Lie groups $\varphi :\mathbf{S}\mathbf{L}(2, k)\rightarrow$ $\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}(E)$ such that $L(\varphi ) =\rho$. (Use $a)$ to prove that $\pi$ and $\rho$ coincide on the exp($n$), with $n$ nilpotent in $\mathfrak{s}$, and remark that $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{L}(2, k)$ is generated by the exp($n$)$.)$

§**2**

1) Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a simple Lie algebra of dimension 3, and $\Phi$ its Killing form (cf. §1, Exerc. 15, 16, 17).

$a)$ An element $x\in \mathfrak{g}$ is regular if and only if $\Phi (x, x)\not= 0$. Let $\mathfrak{h}_x=kx$ be the Cartan subalgebra generated by such an element. Show that $\mathfrak{h}_x$ is splitting if and only if $2\Phi (x, x)$ is a square in $k$.

$b)$ Show that

$\mathfrak{g}$ is splittable $\Leftarrow \Rightarrow \mathfrak{g}$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k)$.

2) Let $k_1$ be an extension of $k$ of finite degree $n\geq 2$.

$a)$ Show that the semi-simple $k$-algebra $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k_1)$ is not splittable.

$b)$ Show that the splittable simple $k$-algebra $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(n, k)$ contains a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}_1$ that is not splitting. (Choose an embedding of the algebra $k_1$ into $\mathbf{M}_n(k)$, and take $\mathfrak{h}_1=k_1\cap \mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(n, k).)$

3) Let $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})$ be a split semi-simple Lie algebra, R its root system, and K the restriction of the Killing form of $\mathfrak{g}$ to $\mathfrak{h}$. With the notations of Chap. VI, §1, no. 12, we have $K = B_{R^{\vee}}$ and $K = 4\gamma (R)\Phi_{R^{\vee}}$ if R is irreducible; moreover, if all the roots of R have the same length, then

$K(H_{\alpha}, H_{\alpha}) = 4h$ for all $\alpha \in R$,

where $h$ is the Coxeter number of W(R).
