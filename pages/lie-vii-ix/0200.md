(IV) For $r= 1,2, . . . , l$, let $_r=\varepsilon_1+\cdots +\varepsilon_r$. We have $_i(H_{\alpha_j}) =\delta_{ij}$, so $_r$ is the fundamental weight corresponding to $\alpha_r.\bigwedge$

Let $\sigma$ be the identity representation of$\bigwedge\mathfrak{g}$ on V. The exterior power $^r\sigma$ of $\sigma$ is a representation on E = $^r(V)$. Let $(e_1, . . . , e_{l+1})$ be the chosen basis of V. The $e_{i_1}\wedge  \cdots  \wedge e_{i_r}$, where $i_1<\cdots < i_r$, form a basis of E. If $h\in \mathfrak{h}$,

$$
(\bigwedge^r\sigma )(h).e_{i_1}\wedge  \cdots  \wedge e_{i_r}= (\varepsilon_{i_1}+\cdots +\varepsilon_{i_r})(h)e_{i_1}\wedge  \cdots  \wedge e_{i_r}
$$

Thus, every weight is of multiplicity 1, $_r$ is a weight of $\bigwedge^r\sigma$, and every other weight is of the form $_r-\mu$, where$\bigwedge\mu$ is a positive radical weight. Consequently, $_r$ is the highest weight of $^r\sigma$, and $e_1\wedge \cdots \wedge e_r$ is a primitive element. By Chap. VI, $§4$, no. 7.IX, the Weyl group can be identified with the symmetric group of

$$
\{\varepsilon_1, . . . , \varepsilon_{l+1}\}
$$

The orbit of $_r$ under the Weyl group thus contains all the $\varepsilon_{i_1}+\cdots +\varepsilon_{i_r}$ with $i_1<\cdots < i_r$. The simple submodule generated by the primitive element $e_1\wedge  \cdots  \wedge e_r$ thus admits all the$\bigwedge\varepsilon_{i_1}+\cdots +\varepsilon_{i_r}$ as weights and consequently is equal to E. Thus, $^r\sigma$ is irreducible with highest weight$\bigwedge_r$.

Thus, the representations $(^r\sigma (1)\leq r\leq l)$ are the fundamental representations. We have dim($\bigwedge^r\sigma$ ) $=^{l+1}_r$ .
