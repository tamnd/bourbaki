Recall now the notations used at the beginning of this number. Put $\widetilde{V} = F + F'$ and let $\widetilde{Q}$ (resp. $\widetilde{\Psi})$ be the restriction of Q (resp. $\Psi )$ to $\widetilde{V}$. Then $\widetilde{Q}$ is a non-degenerate quadratic form of maximum index $l$ on the space $\widetilde{V}$ of dimension $2l$ and the Clifford algebra $C( \widetilde{Q})$ is a central simple algebra of dimension $2^{2l}($Algebra, Chap. IX, §9, no. 4, Th. 2). Let N be the exterior algebra of the maximal isotropic subspace $F'$ generated by $e_{-1}, . . . , e_{-l}$. Identify F with the dual of $F'$ by means of $\Psi$ and for $x\in F'$ (resp. $y\in F)$ denote by $\lambda (x)$ (resp. $\lambda (y))$ the left exterior product with $x$ (resp. the left interior product with $y)$ in N; if $a_1, . . . , a_k\in F'$, then

$$
\lambda (x).(a_1\wedge  \cdots  \wedge a_k) =x\wedge a_1\wedge  \cdots  \wedge a_k
$$

$$
\lambda (y).(a_1\wedge  \cdots  \wedge a_k) =\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}\Psi (a_i, y)a_1\wedge  \cdots  \wedge a_{i-1}\wedge a_{i+1}\wedge  \cdots  \wedge a_k
$$

It is easily verified that $\lambda (x)^2=\lambda (y)^2= 0$ and that

$$
\lambda (x)\lambda (y) +\lambda (y)\lambda (x) =\Psi (x, y).1
$$

It follows (Algebra, Chap. IX, §9, no. 1) that there exists a unique homomorphism (again denoted by $\lambda )$ from $C( \widetilde{Q})$ to End(N) extending the map $\lambda : F\cup F'\rightarrow$ End(N). Since dim $N = 2^l$ and since $C( \widetilde{Q})$ has a unique class of simple modules, of dimension $2^l($Algebra, Chap. IX, §9, no. 4, Th. 2), the representation of $C( \widetilde{Q})$ on N defined by $\lambda$ is irreducible and is a spinor representation of $C( \widetilde{Q}) ($loc. cit.).

$$
(e_0v)^2=-e^2_0v^2=-Q(e_0)Q(v) = Q(v) = \widetilde{Q}(v)
$$

and $\mu$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism, again denoted by $\mu$, from $C( \widetilde{Q})$ to $C^+(Q)$. Since $C( \widetilde{Q})$ is simple and since

dim $C^+(Q) =$ dim $C( \widetilde{Q}) = 2^{2l}$,
