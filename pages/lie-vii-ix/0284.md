### FINITE DIMENSIONAL MODULES

19) Let V be a finite dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-module. For all $\mu\in \mathfrak{h}^*$, let $V^\mu$ be the set of $v\in V$ such that $h.v=\mu(h)v$ for all $h\in \mathfrak{h}$. The dimension of $V^\mu$ is called the multiplicity of $\mu$ in V; if it is$\bigoplus\geq 1$, i.e. if $V^\mu\not= 0,\mu$ is said to be a weight of V. We have $V =_{\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^*}V^\mu$. Every weight of V belongs to P(R). If $\mu$ is a weight of V, and if $w\in W,w\mu$ is a weight of V of the same multiplicity as $\mu$. If $v\in V^\mu$ and $x\in \mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}$, then $x.v\in V^{\mu+\alpha}$.

20) Let B be a basis of R. Giving B determines an order relation on $\mathfrak{h}^*_{\mathbf{Q}}:$ the elements of $\mathfrak{h}^*_{\mathbf{Q}}$ that are $\geq 0$ are the linear combinations of elements of B with rational coefficients $\geq 0$. Denote by $Q_+(R)$ (resp. $R_+)$ the set of positive elements of Q(R) (resp. of R).

Let V be a finite dimensional simple $\mathfrak{g}$-module. Then V has a highest weight $\lambda$. This weight is of multiplicity 1, and it is a dominant weight: if $\alpha \in R_+,\lambda (H_{\alpha})$ is an integer $\geq 0$. We have $\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}V^{\lambda}= 0$ if $\alpha \in R_+$. Every weight of V is of the form $\lambda -\nu$ with $\nu \in Q_+(R)$; conversely, if a weight is dominant and is of the form $\lambda -\nu$ with $\nu \in Q_+$(R), then it is a weight of V.

21) Two finite dimensional simple $\mathfrak{g}$-modules with the same highest weight are isomorphic. Every dominant weight is the highest weight of a finite dimensional simple $\mathfrak{g}$-module.

Every finite dimensional simple $\mathfrak{g}$-module is absolutely simple.
