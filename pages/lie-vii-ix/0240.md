$a)$ Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be two distinct elements of B. Assume that (ad $x_{\alpha})^Nx_{\beta}$ belongs to $\mathfrak{m}$ for N sufficiently large. Show that $x_{\alpha \beta}\in \mathfrak{m}$. (Apply the results of $§1$, no. 2 to $\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{m}$, provided with a suitable $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k$)-module structure.)

$b)$ Show that $\mathfrak{n}+\theta \mathfrak{n}$ is the smallest finite codimensional ideal of $\mathfrak{a}$. Show that this is also the smallest ideal containing the (ad $x_{\alpha})^4x_{\beta}$ and the (ad $x_{-\alpha})^4x_{-\beta}$.

3) Let $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})$ be a split semi-simple Lie algebra, R the corresponding root system, and B a basis of R. For any $\alpha \in B$ (resp. for any pair $(\alpha , \beta )\in B^2)$, let $R(\alpha )$ (resp. $R(\alpha , \beta ))$ be the closed subset of R formed by the $\pm \alpha$ (resp. the smallest closed subset of R containing $\pm \alpha$ and $\pm \beta )$. Let $\mathfrak{g}(\alpha )$ (resp. $\mathfrak{g}(\alpha , \beta ))$ be the derived algebra of the algebra $\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{g}^{R(\alpha)}$ (resp. $\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{g}^{R(\alpha ,\beta)})$, cf. $§3$.

$a)$ Show that $\mathfrak{g}(\alpha ) =kH_{\alpha}\oplus \mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}\oplus \mathfrak{g}^{-\alpha}$; it is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k)$.

$b)$ Show that $\mathfrak{g}(\alpha , \beta )$ is semi-simple, and that it is generated by $\mathfrak{g}(\alpha )$ and $\mathfrak{g}(\beta )$. Its root system can be identified with $R(\alpha , \beta )$.

$c)$ Let $\mathfrak{s}$ be a Lie algebra (not necessarily finite dimensional). For all $\alpha \in B$, let $f_{\alpha}$ be a homomorphism from $\mathfrak{g}(\alpha )$ to $\mathfrak{s}$. Assume that, for any pair $(\alpha , \beta )$, there exists a homomorphism $f_{\alpha \beta}:\mathfrak{g}(\alpha , \beta )\rightarrow \mathfrak{s}$ that extends both $f_{\alpha}$ and $f_{\beta}$. Show that, in that case, there exists a unique homomorphism $f:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow \mathfrak{s}$ that extends the $f_{\alpha}$. (Use Prop. 4 (i).)

4) Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a splittable semi-simple Lie algebra, and $\sigma$ an automorphism of $k$. Let $\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma}$ be the Lie algebra obtained from $\mathfrak{g}$ by extending scalars by means of $\sigma$. Show that $\mathfrak{g}_{\sigma}$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{g}$. (Use the Cor. of Prop. 4.)

$5)a)$ Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a simple Lie algebra, and $k_1$ the commutant of the adjoint representation of $\mathfrak{g}$. Show that $k_1$ is a commutative field, a finite extension of $k$, and that $\mathfrak{g}$ is an absolutely simple $k_1$-Lie algebra.

Conversely, if $k_1$ is a finite extension of $k$, and $\mathfrak{g}$ an absolutely simple $k_1$-Lie algebra, then $\mathfrak{g}$ is a simple $k$-Lie algebra, and the commutant of its adjoint representation can be identified with $k_1$.

$b)$ Let $k'$ be a Galois extension of $k$ containing $k_1$. Show that $\mathfrak{g}_{(k')}$ is a product of $[k_1:k]$ absolutely simple algebras. When $\mathfrak{g}_{(k')}$ is split, these algebras are mutually isomorphic. (Use Exerc. 4.)
