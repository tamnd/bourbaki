### AUTOMORPHISMS

16) The subgroup of Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) generated by the $e^{adx}$, with $x$ nilpotent, is the group Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})$ of elementary automorphisms of $\mathfrak{g}$; it is a normal subgroup of Aut($\mathfrak{g}$); it is equal to its derived group.

If $\overline{k}$ is an algebraic closure of $k$, the group Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) embeds naturally in Aut($\mathfrak{g}\otimes_k\overline{k}$). Put

Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g}) =$ Aut($\mathfrak{g}$)$\cap$ Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g}\otimes_k\overline{k})$;

this is a normal subgroup of Aut($\mathfrak{g}$), independent of the choice of $\overline{k}$. We have

Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})\subset$ Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})\subset$ Aut($\mathfrak{g}$).

The derived group of Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$ is Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})$. In the Zariski topology, Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) and Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$ are closed in End$_k(\mathfrak{g})$, Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$ is the connected component of the identity in Aut($\mathfrak{g}$), and Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})$ is dense in Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$.

Let B be a basis of R, and Aut(R$,B)$ the group of automorphisms of R that leave B stable. Then Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) is the semi-direct product of a subgroup isomorphic to Aut(R$,B)$ and Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$; in particular, Aut($\mathfrak{g}$)$/$Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$ is isomorphic to Aut(R$,B)$, which is itself isomorphic to a group of automorphisms of the Dynkin graph of $\mathfrak{g}$.

17) A framing of $\mathfrak{g}$ is a triplet $(\mathfrak{h}',B,(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in B})$, where $\mathfrak{h}'$ is a splitting Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}, B$ is a basis of $R(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}')$, and where, for all $\alpha \in B,X_{\alpha}$ is a non-zero element of $\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}$. The group Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$ operates simply-transitively on the set of framings of $\mathfrak{g}$.

The group Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})$ operates transitively on the set of pairs $(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{b})$, where $\mathfrak{k}$ is a splitting Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ is a Borel subalgebra of $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})$. 18) Denote by Aut($\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}$) the set of $s\in$ Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) such that $s(\mathfrak{h}) =\mathfrak{h}$. Put

Aut($\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}$) $=$ Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})\cap$ Aut($\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}$), Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}) =$ Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})\cap$ Aut($\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}$).

If $s\in$ Aut($\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}$), the contragredient map of $s|\mathfrak{h}$ is an element of the group A(R) of automorphisms of R; denote this element by $\varepsilon (s)$; the map $\varepsilon$ is a homomorphism from Aut($\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}$) to A(R). We have Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g}) =$ Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})$.Ker$\varepsilon$, and
