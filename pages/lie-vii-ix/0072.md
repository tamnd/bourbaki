§ **4**

The notations and assumptions are those of nos. 1, 2, 3 of §4.

1) Take $G =\mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}_n(k),n\geq 0$.

a) Show that $r_{Ad}^0(g) =n$ for all $g\in G$.

b) Show that an element $g\in G$ is regular if and only if its characteristic polynomial $P_g(T) =$ det(T$-g$) is separable; this is equivalent to saying that the discriminant (Algebra, Chap. IV, §1, no. 10) of $P_g(T)$ is $\not= 0$.

2) Construct a Lie group G such that the function $r^0_{Ad}$ is not constant. (Take $\mathfrak{g}$ abelian $\not= 0$ and Ad non-trivial.)

3) Let $(\rho_i)_{i\in I}$ be a countable family of analytic linear representations of G. Prove that the elements of G which are regular for all the $\rho_i$ constitute a dense subset of G. Construct an example showing that the countability assumption cannot be omitted.

4) Assume that $k=\mathbf{C}$ and that G is connected. Prove the equivalence of the following properties:

(i) G is nilpotent.

(ii) Every element $\not= 1$ of G is regular.

(Show first that (ii) implies

(ii)$'$ Every element $\not= 0$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ is regular.

Remark next that, if $\mathfrak{g}\not= 0$, then $\mathfrak{g}$ contains elements $x\not= 0$ such that ad $x$ is nilpotent, cf. §3, Exerc. 10. Deduce that $\mathfrak{g}$ is nilpotent, hence (i).)

§ **5**

1) Show that the solvable Lie algebra considered in Chap. I, §5, Exerc. 6 is not isomorphic to any decomposable Lie algebra.

2) Let $u$ (resp. $v$) be a non-zero semi-simple (resp. nilpotent) endomorphism of V. Then the map $\lambda u \rightarrow \lambda v(\lambda \in k)$ is an isomorphism from $\mathfrak{g}=ku$ to $\mathfrak{g}'=kv$ which does not take semi-simple elements to semi-simple elements. 3) Let $u$ be an endomorphism of V that is neither semi-simple nor nilpotent. Then $\mathfrak{g}=ku$ is non-decomposable, but ad$_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathfrak{g}$ is decomposable.

$\P$ 4) Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a decomposable Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)$. Let $\mathfrak{q}$ be a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ whose identity representation is semi-simple. There exists $a\in$ Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})$ such that $a(\mathfrak{q})$ is contained in the subalgebra $\mathfrak{m}$ of Prop. 7. (Imitate the proof of Prop. 7 (ii).)

$\P 5)$ Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)$. Then $\mathfrak{g}$ is said to be algebraic if, for all $x\in \mathfrak{g}$, the replicas of $x$ (Chap. I, §5, Exerc. 14) belong to $\mathfrak{g}$. Such an algebra is decomposable.
