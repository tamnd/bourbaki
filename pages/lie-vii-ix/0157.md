$\varphi$ the Harish-Chandra homomorphism $(§6$, no. 4) from $U^0$ to V relative to B. Let $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{h})^W$ be the set of elements of $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{h})$ invariant under the action of W. Then $(\delta \circ \varphi )|Z$ is an isomorphism from Z to $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{h})^W$, independent of the choice of B.

$a)$ Let $P_{++}$ be the set of dominant weights of $R,w\in W,\lambda \in P_{++}$, $\mu=w\lambda$. Then $Z(\mu-\rho )$ is isomorphic to a submodule of $Z(\lambda -\rho ) (§6$, no. 3, Cor. 2 of Prop. 6), and $\varphi (u)(\lambda -\rho ) =\varphi (u)(\mu-\rho )$ for all $u\in Z (§6$, no. 4, Prop. 7). Thus, the polynomial functions $(\delta \circ \varphi )(u)$ and $(\delta \circ \varphi )(u)\circ w$ on $\mathfrak{h}^*$ coincide on $P_{++}$. But $P_{++}$ is dense in $\mathfrak{h}^*$ in the Zariski topology: this can be seen by identifying $\mathfrak{h}^*$ with $k^B$ by means of the basis consisting of the fundamental weights $_{\alpha}$, and by applying Prop. 9 of Algebra, Chap. IV, $§2$, no. 3. Hence

$$
(\delta \circ \varphi )(u) = (\delta \circ \varphi )(u)\circ w
$$

which proves that $(\delta \circ \varphi )(Z)\subset \mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{h})^W$.

$b)$ Let $\eta$ be the isomorphism from $I(\mathfrak{g})$ to $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{h})^W$ defined in no. 3, Cor. 2 of Th. 1. Consider the canonical isomorphism from the $\mathfrak{g}$-module U to the $\mathfrak{g}$-module $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{g})$ (Chap. $I,§2$, no. 8), and let $\theta$ be its restriction to Z. Then $\theta (Z) = I(\mathfrak{g})$. Let $z$ be an element of Z with filtration $\leq f$ in U.

Z $\longrightarrow^{\theta}I(\mathfrak{g})$

$$
\mathbf{S}^{\varphi}()\mathfrak{h})\longrightarrow^{\delta}\mathbf{S}()\mathfrak{h}^{\eta})
$$

Introduce the notations of $§6$, no. 4, and put

$$
z=\sum\sum\sum\sum\lambda_{(q_i),(m_i),(p_i)}u((q_i),(m_i),(p_i))
$$

$q_i+m_i+p_i\leq f$

Let $v((q_i),(m_i),(p_i))$ be the monomial $X_{-\alpha_1}^{q_1}. . . X_{-\alpha_n}^{q_n}H_1^{m_1}. . . H_l^{m_l}X_{\alpha}^{p_{11}}. . . X_{\alpha}^{p_{nn}}$ calculated in $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{g})$. Denoting by $\mathbf{S}_d(\mathfrak{g})$ the sum of the homogeneous components of $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{g})$ of degrees $0,1, . . . , d$, we have

$\theta (z)\equiv \sum\sum\sum\sum\lambda_{(q_i),(m_i),(p_i)}v((q_i),(m_i),(p_i))$ (mod$.\mathbf{S}_{f-1}(\mathfrak{g}))$

$q_i+m_i+p_i=f$

so

$(\eta \circ \theta )(z)\equiv \sum\sum_{m_i=f}\lambda_{(0),(m_i),(0)}v((0),(m_i),(0))$ (mod$.\mathbf{S}_{f-1}(\mathfrak{h}))$

and consequently

$(\eta \circ \theta )(z)\equiv \varphi (z)$ (mod$.\mathbf{S}_{f-1}(\mathfrak{h}))$. (3)
