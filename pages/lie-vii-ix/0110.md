Since $\mathfrak{a}^0\cap (\mathfrak{n}+\theta (\mathfrak{n})) = 0$, the canonical map from $\mathfrak{a}^0$ to $\mathfrak{g}^0$ is an isomorphism. Consequently, $(H_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in B}$ is a basis of the vector space $\mathfrak{g}^0$. The commutative subalgebra $\mathfrak{g}^0$ of $\mathfrak{g}_B$ will be denoted by $\mathfrak{h}_B$ or simply by $\mathfrak{h}$. There exists a unique isomorphism $\mu \rightarrow \mu_B$ from V to $\mathfrak{h}^*$ such that $\langle \mu_B, H_{\alpha}\rangle =\langle \mu, \alpha^{\vee}\rangle$ for all $\mu\in V$ and all $\alpha \in B$.

The involutive automorphism $\theta$ of $\mathfrak{a}$ defines by passage to the quotient an involutive automorphism of $\mathfrak{g}$ that will also be denoted by $\theta$. We have $\theta (X_{\alpha}) =X_{-\alpha}$ for $\alpha \in B\cup (-B)$, and $\theta (H_{\alpha}) =-H_{\alpha}$.

THEOREM 1. Let R be a reduced root system, B a basis of R. Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be the Lie algebra defined by the generating family $((X_{\alpha}, H_{\alpha}, X_{-\alpha}))_{\alpha\in B}$ and the relations (16) to (22). Let $\mathfrak{h}=\sum_{\alpha\in B}kH_{\alpha}$. Then $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})$ is a split semi-simple Lie algebra. The isomorphism $\mu \rightarrow \mu_B$ from V to $\mathfrak{h}^*$ maps R to the root system of $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})$. For all $\mu\in R,\mathfrak{g}^\mu$ is the eigenspace relative to the root $\mu$.

The proof follows that of Lemmas 8, 9, 10, 11.

Lemma 8. Let $\alpha \in B\cup (-B)$. Then ad $X_{\alpha}$ is locally nilpotent.$^1$

Assume that $\alpha \in B$. Let $\mathfrak{g}'$ be the set of $z\in \mathfrak{g}$ such that (ad $X_{\alpha})^pz= 0$ for sufficiently large $p$. Since ad $X_{\alpha}$ is a derivation of $\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}'$ is a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{g}$. By (21), $X_{\beta}\in \mathfrak{g}'$ for all $\beta \in B$. By (17), (19), (20), $H_{\beta}\in \mathfrak{g}'$ and $X_{-\beta}\in \mathfrak{g}'$ for all $\beta \in B$. Hence $\mathfrak{g}'=\mathfrak{g}$ and ad $X_{\alpha}$ is locally nilpotent. Since ad $X_{-\alpha}=\theta$(ad $X_{\alpha})\theta^{-1}$, we see that ad $X_{-\alpha}$ is locally nilpotent.

We shall see that $\mathfrak{g}$ is finite dimensional, so that ad $X_{\alpha}$ is actually nilpotent.
