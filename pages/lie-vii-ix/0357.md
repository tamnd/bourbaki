PROPOSITION 1. Let $\tau : G\rightarrow \mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}(V)$ be an irreducible representation of G of highest weight $\lambda \in X_{++}$. Let $m$ be the integer $\sum_{\alpha\in R_+}\langle \lambda , K_{\alpha}\rangle$, and let $w_0$

be the element of the Weyl group such that $w_0(R_+) = R_-$ (Chap. VI, § 6, Cor. 3 of Prop. 17). There are three possible cases:

a$)w_0(\lambda ) =-\lambda$ and $m$ is even. Then there exists a G-invariant separating symmetric bilinear form on V; the representation $\tau$ is of real type (Appendix II).

b$)w_0(\lambda )\not=-\lambda$. Every G-invariant bilinear form on V is zero; the representation $\tau$ is of complex type (loc. cit.).

c$)w_0(\lambda ) =-\lambda$ and $m$ is odd. Then there exists a G-invariant separating alternating bilinear form on V; the representation $\tau$ is of quaternionic type (loc. cit.).

If the restriction of $\tau$ to $C(G)_0$ is not trivial, we are in case b).

A bilinear form B on V is invariant under G if and only if it is invariant under $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbf{C}}$ (Chap. III, §6, no. 5, Cor. 3). Thus, if G is semi-simple, the proposition follows from Chap. VIII, §7, no. 5, Prop. 12 and Prop. 3 of Appendix II.
