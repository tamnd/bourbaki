(VI) For $x\in \mathfrak{g}$, the characteristic polynomial of $\sigma (x)$ takes the form

$$
T^{2l+1}+f_1(x)T^{2l}+f_2(x)T^{2l-1}+\cdots +f_{2l+1}(x)
$$

where $f_1, . . . , f_{2l+1}$ are invariant polynomial functions on $\mathfrak{g}$.

If $x=\xi_1H_1+\cdots +\xi_lH_l\in \mathfrak{h}$, the $f_i(x)$ are, up to sign, the elementary symmetric functions of $\xi_1, . . . , \xi_l,-\xi_1, . . . ,-\xi_l$; these symmetric functions are zero in odd degrees, and

$$
T^{2l+1}+f_2(x)T^{2l-1}+f_4(x)T^{2l-3}+\cdots +f_{2l}(x)T = T(T^2-\xi_1^2). . .(T^2-\xi^2_l)
$$

so that $f_2, . . . , f_{2l}$ are, up to sign, the elementary symmetric functions of $\xi_1^2, . . . , \xi_l^2$, which are algebraically independent generators of $\mathbf{S}(\mathfrak{h}^*)^W$ (Chap. VI, §4, no. 5.IX). In view of §8, no. 3, Th. 1 (i), we see that $f_1=f_3=f_5=\cdots = 0$ and that $(f_2, f_4, . . . , f_{2l})$ is an algebraically free family generating the algebra of invariant polynomial functions on $\mathfrak{g}$.

(VII) Since the only automorphism of the Dynkin graph is the identity, we have Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) $=$ Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$.

Let $\Sigma$ be the group of similarities of V relative to $\Psi$. For all $g\in \Sigma$, let $\varphi (g)$ be the automorphism $x \rightarrow gxg^{-1}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$. Then $\varphi$ is a homomorphism from $\Sigma$ to Aut($\mathfrak{g}$). We show that it is surjective. Let $\alpha \in$ Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) $=$ Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$. By Prop. 2 of §7, no. 1, there exists $s\in \mathbf{G}\mathbf{L}(V)$ such that $\alpha (x) =sxs^{-1}$ for all $x\in \mathfrak{g}$. Then $s$ transforms $\Psi$ into a bilinear form $\Psi '$ on V that is invariant under $\mathfrak{g}$, and hence proportional to $\Psi ($§7, no. 5, Prop. 12). This proves that $s\in \Sigma$.

Since the identity representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ is irreducible, its commutant reduces to the scalars (§6, no. 1, Prop. 1), so the kernel of $\varphi$ is $k^*$. Thus, the group Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) $=$ Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$ can be identified with $\Sigma /k^*$. But, it follows from Algebra, Chap. IX, §6, no. 5, that the group $\Sigma$ is the product of the groups $k^*$ and $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{O}(\Psi )$; hence Aut($\mathfrak{g}$) $=$ Aut$_0(\mathfrak{g})$ can be identified with $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{O}(\Psi )$.

Let $\mathbf{O}^+_0(\Psi )$ be the reduced orthogonal group of $\Psi ($Algebra, Chap. IX, §9, no. 5). Since $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{O}(\Psi )/\mathbf{O}^+_0(\Psi )$ is commutative (loc. cit.), the group Aut$_e(\mathfrak{g})$ is contained in $\mathbf{O}^+_0(\Psi ) ($§11, no. 2, Prop. 3); in fact, it is equal to it (Exerc. 7).

(VIII) The canonical bilinear form $\Phi_R$ on $\mathfrak{h}^*$ is given by

$\Phi_R(\xi_1\varepsilon_1+\cdots +\xi_l\varepsilon_l, \xi '\varepsilon_1+\cdots +\xi '\varepsilon_l) =$ 1 $(\xi_1\xi '+\cdots +\xi_l\xi ')$

$$
^1l\overline{4l- 2}^1l
$$

(Chap. VI, §4, no. 5.V). The isomorphism from $\mathfrak{h}$ to $\mathfrak{h}^*$ defined by $\Phi_R$ takes $H_i$ to $(4l-2).\varepsilon_i$. Thus, the inverse form of $\Phi_R$, that is the restriction to $\mathfrak{h}$ of the Killing form, is

$$
\Phi (\xi_1H_1+\cdots +\xi_lH_l, \xi '_1H_1+\cdots +\xi '_lH_l) = (4l-2)(\xi_1\xi '_1+\cdots +\xi_l\xi '_l)
$$
