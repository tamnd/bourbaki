2) Let $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})$ be a split simple Lie algebra, and B a basis of $R(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})$. The simple $\mathfrak{g}$-modules $E(\lambda ),\lambda \in P_{++}$ **--** $\{0\}$, of minimum dimension are those for which $\lambda$ is one of the following weights:

$_1(A_1);_1$ and $_l(A_l,l\geq 2);_1(B_l$ and $C_l,l\geq 2);_1,_3$ and $_4$ $(D_4);_1(D_l,l\geq 5);_1$ and $_6(E_6);_7(E_7);_8(E_8);_4(F_4);_1(G_2)$.

Any two such modules can be transformed into each other by an automorphism of $\mathfrak{g}$.

Type $E_8$ is the only one for which the adjoint representation is of minimum dimension.

$3)a)$ Define an isomorphism from $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(4, k)$ to the orthogonal algebra $\mathfrak{o}_S(6, k)$. (Use the fact that the representation $\bigwedge^2\sigma$ of no. 1.V is orthogonal and of dimension 6.) The two types of irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(4, k)$ of degree 4 correspond to the two semi-spinor representations of $\mathfrak{o}_S(6, k)$.

$b)$ Define an isomorphism from $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(4, k)$ to the orthogonal algebra $\mathfrak{o}_S(5, k)$. (Use the fact that the representation $\sigma_2$ of no. 3.V is orthogonal and of dimension 5.) The irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(4, k)$ of degree 4 corresponds to the spinor representation of $\mathfrak{o}_S(5, k)$.

$c)$ Define an isomorphism $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k)\times \mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k)\rightarrow \mathfrak{o}_S(4, k)$ by using the tensor product of the identity representations of the two $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2, k)$ factors. Recover this result by means of Chap. I, §6, Exerc. 26.

4) Let $S$ be the square matrix of order $n$

0 0 $\cdots$ 0 1

0 0 $\cdots$ 1 0

$(\delta_{i,n+1-i}) =$ ... ... . .. ... ....

0 1 $\cdots$ 0 0

1 0 $\cdots$ 0 0

The elements of $\mathfrak{o}_S(n, k)$ are the matrices $(a_{ij})$ that are anti-symmetric with respect to the second diagonal:

$a_{i,j}=-a_{n+1-j,n+1-i}$ for every pair $(i, j)$.

The algebra $\mathfrak{o}_S(n, k)$ is splittable simple of type $D_{n/2}$ if $n$ is even and $\geq 6$, and of type $B_{(n-1)/2}$ if $n$ is odd and $\geq 5$. The diagonal (resp. upper triangular) elements of $\mathfrak{o}_S(n, k)$ form a splitting Cartan (resp. Borel) subalgebra.
