(IX) Recall the $X_{\alpha}$ defined by formulas $(6) (\alpha \in R)$. It is easily verified that $[X_{\alpha}, X_{-\alpha}] =-H_{\alpha}$ for $\alpha \in R$. On the other hand, the map $\theta :a \rightarrow  -^ta$ is an automorphism of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $\theta (X_{\alpha}) =X_{-\alpha}$ for all $\alpha \in R$. Consequently, $(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in R}$ is a Chevalley system in $(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})$.

Assume that $k=\mathbf{Q}$. The Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ has two permissible lattices $Q(R^{\vee}) =_i\sum_{=1}^l\mathbf{Z}.H_i$ and $P(R^{\vee}) = Q(R^{\vee}) +^1_2\mathbf{Z}._i\sum_{=1}^lH_i$ (Chap. VI, §4,

no. 5.VIII). We see that $Q(R^{\vee})$ is the set of matrices with integer entries$\sum$ belonging to $\mathfrak{h}$. It follows that the Chevalley order $Q(R^{\vee}) +_{\alpha\in R}\mathbf{Z}.X_{\alpha}$ is the set $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2l,\mathbf{Z})$ of matrices in $\mathfrak{g}$ with integer entries.

Consider the reductive Lie algebra $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(\Psi ) +\mathbf{Q}.1$. It is easy to see that the set of its elements with integer entries is a Chevalley order, whose projection$\sum$ onto $\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(\Psi )$ parallel to $\mathbf{Q}.1$ is the Chevalley order $P(R^{\vee}) +\mathbf{Z}.X_{\alpha}$.

Finally, $X_{\alpha}^2= 0$ for all $\alpha \in R$. It follows that the lattice $\mathscr{V}$ in V generated by the $e_i$ is admissible for the Chevalley order$\bigwedge\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(2l,\mathbf{Z})$. The same holds for the lattice $E_r\cap^r\mathscr{V}$ in $E_r$.
