### Exercises

1) Prove that a left Artinian and right Noetherian ring A is also right Artinian (view the right A-modules $\mathfrak{R}(A)^k/\mathfrak{R}(A)^{k+1}$ as $(A/\mathfrak{R}$(A))-modules).

2) Let A be a (left) Artinian ring and $\mathfrak{r}$ its radical.

a) Prove that every right ideal $\mathfrak{b}$ of A contains a minimal right ideal (consider the ideals $\mathfrak{b}\cap \mathfrak{r}^p$, and observe that if $\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{r}= 0$, then $\mathfrak{b}$ is a right $A/\mathfrak{r}$-module).

b) Prove that the left (resp. right) socle of A is the right (resp. left) annihilator of $\mathfrak{r}$. Deduce that every minimal two-sided ideal of A is contained in the intersection of the left and right socles of A.

3) Let A be an Artinian ring such that the ring $A/\mathfrak{R}(A)$ is simple. Prove that A is isomorphic to a matrix ring $\mathbf{M}_r(B)$ over an Artinian local ring B (use Exercises 19 and 20 of VIII, p. 169, as well as Exercise 19 of VIII, p. 94).

$\P 4) A$ ring A is called pseudoregular if for every element $a$ of A, there exist an integer $n$ and an element $x$ of A such that $a^nxa^n=a^n$. An absolutely flat ring (VIII, p. 171, Exercise 27) is pseudoregular.

a) Prove that a pseudoregular ring is a Zorn ring (VIII, p. 171, Exercise 26) in which every noninvertible element is a left and right zero divisor.

b) Prove that every quotient ring of a pseudoregular ring is pseudoregular (cf. VIII, p. 171, Exercise 27, d)).

c) Prove that the center Z of a pseudoregular ring A is pseudoregular (prove that if $a\in Z$ and $x\in A$ satisfy $a^nxa^n=a^n$, then $a^{2n}x^k$ belongs to Z for every $k\geqslant 1)$.

d) Let $p$ be a prime number, $U_p$ the $p$-primary component of the group $\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}$, and A the (commutative) algebra of the group $U_p$ over the field $\mathbf{F}_p$. Prove that the ring A is pseudoregular but $A^{\mathbf{N}}$ is not.

$\P 5)$ Let $n$ be an integer. A ring A is called $n$-regular if for every element $a$ of A, there exists an $x\in A$ such that $xa^{n+1}=a^n$.

a) Suppose that A is $n$-regular. Prove that we have $r^n= 0$ for every element $r$ of $\mathfrak{R}(A)$.

b) Prove that an $n$-regular primitive ring is isomorphic to a matrix ring $\mathbf{M}_r(D)$ over a field, with $r\leqslant n$. (Observe that if V is a D-vector space of dimension $> n$ and $e$ a nonzero vector in V, then every dense subring of End$_D(V)$ contains an element $u$ such that $u^n(e)\not= 0$ and $u^{n+1}(e) = 0.)$

c) Prove that an $n$-regular ring is a Zorn ring (VIII, p. 171, Exercise 26). (First, observe that the relation $xa^{n+1}=a^n$ implies $x^na^{2n}=a^n$. Using b), Exercise 12, a) of VIII, p. 168 and Proposition 2 of VIII, p. 27, deduce that we have $a^nx^na^n-a^n\in$ $\mathfrak{R}(A)$. Finally, using Lemma 1 of VIII, p. 159, prove that if two elements $y$ and $z$ of A satisfy $y=zy^2$ and $yzy-y\in \mathfrak{R}$(A), then there exists a $t\in A$ such that $y=ty^2$
