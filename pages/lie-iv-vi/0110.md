This is simply the content of properties b) and c), together with the definition of W.

*Remark.* 2) We have thus shown that *every Coxeter matrix corresponds to a Coxeter group*.

4. CONTRAGREDIENT REPRESENTATION

Let E* be the dual of E. Since W acts on E via σ, it also acts, by transport of structure, on E*. The corresponding representation

$$
\sigma^*: W \to \mathbf{GL}(E^*)
$$

is called the *contragredient representation* of σ. We have

$$
\sigma^*(w) = {}^t\sigma(w^{-1}) \quad \text{for all } w \in W.
$$

If x* ∈ E* and w ∈ W, we denote by w(x*) the transform of x* by σ*(w).

If s ∈ S, denote by A_s the set of x* ∈ E* such that x*(e_s) > 0. Let C be the intersection of the A_s, s ∈ S. When S is *finite*, C is an *open simplicial cone* in E* (§ 1, no. 6).

THEOREM 1 (Tits). *If w ∈ W and C ∩ w(C) ≠ ∅, then w = 1*.

We indicate immediately several consequences of this theorem:

COROLLARY 1. *The group* W *acts simply-transitively on the set of* w(C) *for* w ∈ W.

This is clear.

COROLLARY 2. *The representations* σ *and* σ* *are injective*.

Indeed, if σ*(w) = 1, then w(C) = C, so w = 1 by the theorem. The injectivity of σ follows from that of σ*.

COROLLARY 3. *If S is finite, σ(W) is a discrete subgroup of* \mathbf{GL}(E) *provided with its canonical Lie group structure; similarly, σ*(W) is a discrete subgroup of* \mathbf{GL}(E^*) *.*

Let x* ∈ C. The set U of g ∈ \mathbf{GL}(E^*) such that g(x*) ∈ C is a neighbourhood of the identity element in \mathbf{GL}(E^*); by the theorem,

$$
\sigma^*(W) \cap U = \{1\};
$$

thus, σ*(W) is a discrete subgroup of \mathbf{GL}(E^*). By transport of structure, it follows that σ(W) is discrete in \mathbf{GL}(E).
