$$
I = \{1, 2, \ldots, s\}
$$
such that $ (\beta_i)_{i \in J} $ is a minimal system of generators of $ b $. Then $ J \neq \varnothing $ since $ b \neq 0 $. We shall deduce from (9) and (10) that, if $ i \in J $, $ \alpha_i $ is an $ R $-linear combination of the $ \alpha_j $ for $ j \neq i $, which will contradict the minimality of $ (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_s) $ and will complete the proof.

There exist homogeneous elements $ \gamma_{ji} $ of $ R $ ($ i \in J, j \in I - J $) such that
$$
\beta_j = \sum_{i \in J} \gamma_{ji} \beta_i \quad (j \in I - J).
$$
Taking (11) into account, formula (10) gives
$$
\sum_{i \in J} \beta_i \left( d_{ik} + \sum_{j \in I - J} \gamma_{ji} d_{jk} \right) = 0.
$$
Put
$$
u_{ik} = d_{ik} + \sum_{j \in I - J} \gamma_{ji} d_{jk}.
$$
Thus
$$
\sum_{i \in J} \beta_i u_{ik} = 0.
$$
Write $ u_{ik} = \sum_{\lambda \in \Lambda} \delta_{ik\lambda} z_\lambda $, where the $ \delta_{ik\lambda} $ belong to $ R $. Relation (14) implies that
$$
\sum_{i \in J} \beta_i \delta_{ik\lambda} = 0 \text{ for all } k \text{ and } \lambda. $$
If one of the $ \delta_{ik\lambda} $ had a non-zero homogeneous component of degree 0, the preceding equality would imply that one of the $ \beta_i $ ($ i \in J $) is an $ R $-linear combination of the others, contradicting the minimality of $ (\beta_i)_{i \in J} $. Thus $ \delta_{ik\lambda} \in R_+ $ and consequently $ u_{ik} \in R_+ S $ for all $ i $ and $ k $. Thus, there exist $ u_{ikh} \in S $ such that $ u_{ik} = \sum_{h=1}^s u_{ikh} \alpha_h $, in other words, by (13),
$$
d_{ik} + \sum_{j \in I - J} \gamma_{ji} d_{jk} = \sum_{h=1}^s u_{ikh} \alpha_h.
