$$
L_e = \det(X_i^{q^{e_j}}).
$$
This is an element of S.
Show that
$$
g.L_e = \det(g)L_e \quad \text{for all } g \in G.
$$
(Remark that, if $g.X_i = \sum_j a_{ij} X_j$, then $g.X_i^{q^e} = \sum_j a_{ij} X_j^{q^{e_j}}$.) In particular, $L_e$ belongs to $R_1$.

b) Let $j \in [1, n]$. Put
$$
Z_j = \prod_{(a_{ij})} (X_j + \sum_{i > j} a_{ij} X_i),
$$
the product being over all families $(a_{ij})_{j < i \leq n}$ of elements of K. Let
$$
T = \prod_{j=1}^n Z_j.
$$
Show that T divides all the $L_e$. Deduce that $T = L_{e_n}$, where $e_n = (0, 1, \ldots, n-1)$, and that T is invariant under $G_1$.

c) Denote by $e_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq n-1$) the sequence $(0, 1, \ldots, i-1, i+1, \ldots, n)$, and put $Y_i = L_{e_i}/T$, cf. b). Show that the $Y_i$ belong to R and that $\deg(Y_i) = q^n - q^i$.

d) Let $S' = K[X_1, \ldots, X_{n-1}]$, and let $T', Y'_1, \ldots, Y'_{n-2}$ be the elements of $S'$ defined in the same way as T, $Y_1, \ldots, Y_{n-1}$ (but replacing n by $n-1$). Let $f : S \to S'$ be the homomorphism defined by
$$
f(X_i) = X_i \quad (1 \leq i \leq n-1), \quad f(X_n) = 0.
$$

f(T) = 0, \quad f(Y_1) = T'^{q(q-1)}, \quad f(Y_i) = Y'_{i-1}^q \quad \text{for } 2 \leq i \leq n-1.
