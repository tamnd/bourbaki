Lemma 4. Let $ w \in W $ have length $ q \geq 1 $, let D be the set of reduced decompositions of $ w $, and let F be a map from D to a set E. Assume that $ F(s) = F(s') $ if the elements $ s = (s_1, \ldots, s_q); s' = (s'_1, \ldots, s'_q) $ of D satisfy one of the following hypotheses:

a) $ s_1 = s'_1 $ or $ s_q = s'_q $.
b) There exist s and $ s' $ in S such that $ s_j = s'_k = s $ and $ s_k = s'_j = s' $ for $ j $ odd and $ k $ even.

Then F is constant.

A) Let $ s, s' \in D $ and put $ t = (s'_1, s_1, \ldots, s_{q-1}) $. We are going to show that if $ F(s) \neq F(s') $ then $ t \in D $ and $ F(t) \neq F(s) $. Indeed, $ w = s'_1 \ldots s'_q $ and hence $ s'_1 w = s'_2 \ldots s'_q $ is of length $ < q $. By Prop. 4, there exists an integer $ j $ such that $ 1 \leq j \leq q $ and the sequence $ u = (s'_1, s_1, \ldots, s_{j-1}, s_{j+1}, \ldots, s_q) $ belongs to D. We have $ F(u) = F(s') $ by condition a); if $ j \neq q $ we would have $ F(s) = F(u) $ for the same reason, and hence $ F(s) = F(s') $ contrary to our hypothesis. Thus $ j = q $ and hence $ t = u \in D $ and $ F(t) = F(s') \neq F(s) $.
