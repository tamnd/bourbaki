In particular:

PROPOSITION 8. (i) *If two roots are proportional, the factor of proportionality can only be $ \pm 1, \pm \frac{1}{2}, \pm 2 $.*

(ii) *If $ \alpha $ and $ \beta $ are two non-proportional roots, and if $ \| \alpha \| \leq \| \beta \| $, then $ n(\alpha, \beta) $ takes one of the values 0, 1, -1.*

If a root $ \alpha \in \mathbf{R} $ is such that $ \frac{1}{2}\alpha \notin \mathbf{R} $, then $ \alpha $ is called an *indivisible* root.

THEOREM 1. *Let $ \alpha, \beta $ be two roots.*

(i) *If $ n(\alpha, \beta) > 0 $, $ \alpha - \beta $ is a root unless $ \alpha = \beta $.*
(ii) *If $ n(\alpha, \beta) < 0 $, $ \alpha + \beta $ is a root unless $ \alpha = -\beta $.*

If $ n(\alpha, \beta) > 0 $, the possibilities, by the list above, are the following:

1) $ n(\alpha, \beta) = 1 $; then $ \alpha - \beta - s_{\beta}(\alpha) \in \mathbf{R} $;
2) $ n(\beta, \alpha) = 1 $; then $ \beta - \alpha = s_{\alpha}(\beta) \in \mathbf{R} $, so $ \alpha - \beta \in \mathbf{R} $;
3) $ \beta = \alpha $.

This proves (i), and (ii) follows by changing $ \beta $ to $ -\beta $.
