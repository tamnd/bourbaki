(V) The formula $\alpha^- = \frac{2\alpha}{(\alpha|\alpha)}$ gives for $R^-$ the set of vectors $\pm 2\varepsilon_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq l$), $\pm \varepsilon_i \pm \varepsilon_j$ ($1 \leq i < j \leq l$). The Dynkin graph of $R^-$ is obtained from that of $R$ by the procedure explained in no. 2, and we see that $R^-$ is of type $C_l$.

There are $4l - 2$ roots not orthogonal to $\beta = \varepsilon_1$, namely $\pm \varepsilon_1$ and $\pm \varepsilon_1 \pm \varepsilon_j$ for $2 \leq j \leq l$; for each such root $\alpha$, $n(\alpha, \beta) = \pm 2$. Formula (17) of § 1, no. 12 shows that, for $\Phi_R$, the square of the length of $\beta$ is $(4l - 2)^{-1}$: hence $\Phi_R(x, y) = (x|y)/(4l - 2)$. Apply formula (18) of § 1, no. 12 with $x = y = \beta$. This gives
$$
2 + \frac{1}{4}(4l - 4) = \gamma(R) \frac{1}{4l - 2}
$$
and so $\gamma(R) = (l + 1)(4l - 2)$.

(VI) The fundamental weights $\omega_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq l$) such that $(\omega_i|\alpha_j) = \delta_{ij}$ are easily calculated, and we find that
$$
\begin{align*}
\omega_i &= \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 + \cdots + \varepsilon_i \\
&= \alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2 + \cdots + (i - 1)\alpha_{i-1} + i(\alpha_1 + \alpha_{i+1} + \cdots + \alpha_l) \quad (i < l) \\
\omega_l &= \frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 + \cdots + \varepsilon_l) = \frac{1}{2}(\alpha_1 + 2\alpha_2 + \cdots + l\alpha_l).
\end{align*}
$$

(VII) The sum of the positive roots is
$$
\begin{align*}
2\rho &= (2l - 1)\varepsilon_1 + (2l - 3)\varepsilon_2 + \cdots + 3\varepsilon_{l-1} + \varepsilon_l \\
&= (2l - 1)\alpha_1 + 2(2l - 2)\alpha_2 + \cdots + i(2l - i)\alpha_i + \cdots + l^2\alpha_l.
\end{align*}
$$

(VIII) We have $Q(R) = L_0$ (no. 4), and $P(R)$ is generated by $Q(R)$ and $\omega_l$, hence is equal to $L_2$ (no. 4). Hence, $P(R)/Q(R)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}$, and the connection index is equal to 2.

(IX) and (X) In $\mathbf{R}^l$, the orthogonal reflection $s_{\varepsilon_i, -\varepsilon_j}$ ($i \neq j$) interchanges $\varepsilon_i$ and $\varepsilon_j$ and leaves $\varepsilon_k$ invariant when the index $k$ is distinct from $i$ and $j$. The $s_{\varepsilon_i, -\varepsilon_i}$ generate a group $G_1$ isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_l$. The orthogonal reflection $s_{\varepsilon_i}$ transforms $\varepsilon_i$ to $-\varepsilon_i$ and leaves invariant the $\varepsilon_k$ when the index $k$ is distinct from $i$. The $s_{\varepsilon_i}$ generate a group $G_2$ isomorphic to $(\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})^l$. The Weyl group $W(R)$ is generated by $G_1$ and $G_2$, and $G_2$ is normal in $W(R)$, so $W(R)$ is isomorphic to the semi-direct product of $G_l$ by $(\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})^l$. Its order is therefore $2^l.l!$.

The symmetric algebra $S(\mathbf{R}^l)$ can be identified canonically with the algebra of polynomial functions $P(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_l)$ on $\mathbf{R}^l$. For such a polynomial to be invariant under $W(R)$, it is first of all necessary that
$$
P(\xi_1, \xi_2, \ldots, \xi_l) = P(\pm \xi_1, \pm \xi_2, \ldots, \pm \xi_l)
$$
for all choices of the signs on the right-hand side, so that
$$
P(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_l) = Q(\xi_1^2, \ldots, \xi_l^2)
$$
