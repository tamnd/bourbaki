for $W(R)$ (Th. 2), there exists, for any weight $\overline{\omega}$, a unique weight $\overline{\omega}'$ such that $\overline{\omega}'$ is a transform of $\overline{\omega}$ by $W(R)$.

We have
$$
\langle \overline{\omega}_\alpha, \beta^\vee \rangle = (\overline{\omega}_\alpha | \frac{2\beta}{(\beta|\beta)}) = \delta_{\alpha\beta}
$$
for $\alpha, \beta \in B$ ($\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ denoting the Kronecker symbol), hence
$$
s_\beta(\overline{\omega}_\alpha) = \overline{\omega}_\alpha - \delta_{\alpha\beta}\beta \quad \text{and} \quad (\overline{\omega}_\alpha |\beta) = \frac{1}{2}(\beta|\beta)\delta_{\alpha\beta}.
$$
In other words, $\overline{\omega}_\alpha$ *is orthogonal to* $\beta$ *for* $\beta \neq \alpha$, *and its orthogonal projection onto* $R\alpha$ *is* $\frac{1}{2}\alpha$. Since $\overline{\omega}_\alpha \in \overline{C}$, $(\overline{\omega}_\alpha |\overline{\omega}_\beta) \geqslant 0$ for $\alpha, \beta \in B$, i.e. the angle $(\overline{\omega}_\alpha, \overline{\omega}_\beta)$ is acute or a right angle. The dominant weights are the $\overline{\omega} \in V$ such that $2(\overline{\omega}|\alpha)/(|\alpha|\alpha)$ is an integer $\geqslant 0$ for all $\alpha \in B$.

PROPOSITION 28. *Let* $B$ *be a basis of* $R$, $B'$ *a subset of* $B$, $V'$ *the vector subspace of* $V$ *generated by* $B'$, $R' = R \cap V'$ *(which is a root system in* $V'$*),* $R'\tilde{}$ *the inverse root system (which is identified with the canonical image of* $R'$ *in* $R'\tilde{}$), $V_1$ *the orthogonal complement of* $R'\tilde{}$ *in* $V$, *and* $p$ *the projection of* $V$ *onto* $V'$ *parallel to* $V_1$. *Then,* $Q(R') = Q(R) \cap V'$, $P(R') = p(P(R))$. *The set of dominant weights of* $R'$ *is the image under* $p$ *of the set of dominant weights of* $R$.

Indeed, $Q(R)$ is the subgroup of $V$ with basis $B$, $Q(R')$ is the subgroup of $V'$ with basis $B'$ (no. 7, Cor. 4 of Prop. 20) from which $Q(R') = Q(R) \cap V'$ is immediate. If $\overline{\omega} \in P(R)$ and $\alpha \in R'$, $\langle p(\overline{\omega}), \alpha^\vee \rangle = \langle \overline{\omega}, \alpha^\vee \rangle \in \mathbf{Z}$, so $p(\overline{\omega}) \in P(R')$, and hence $p(P(R)) \subseteq P(R')$. If $\overline{\omega}' \in P(R')$, $\overline{\omega}'$ extends to a linear form $\overline{\omega}$ on $V^*$ vanishing on $(B - B')\tilde{}$; we have $\langle \overline{\omega}, \alpha^\vee \rangle \in \mathbf{Z}$ for all $\alpha \in B$, so $\overline{\omega} \in P(R)$, and $\overline{\omega}' = p(\overline{\omega})$; hence $P(R') \subseteq p(P(R))$. Thus $P(R') = p(P(R))$, and the assertion about dominant weights is proved in the same way.

PROPOSITION 29. *Let* $\rho$ *be half the sum of the roots* $> 0$.

(i) $\rho = \sum_{\alpha \in B} \overline{\omega}_\alpha$; *this is an element of* $C$.
(ii) $s_\alpha(\rho) = \rho - \alpha$ *for all* $\alpha \in B$.
(iii) $(2\rho|\alpha) = (\alpha|\alpha)$ *for all* $\alpha \in B$.

Since $R$ is reduced, $s_\alpha(R_+(C) - \{\alpha\}) = R_+(C) - \{\alpha\}$ and $s_\alpha(\alpha) = -\alpha$ for $\alpha \in B$ (no. 6, Cor. 1 of Prop. 17), so $s_\alpha(2\rho) = 2\rho - 2\alpha$. Since $s_\alpha(\rho) = \rho - \langle \rho, \alpha^\vee \rangle$, we see that
$$
\langle \rho, \alpha^\vee \rangle = 1 = \langle \sum_{\beta \in B} \overline{\omega}_\beta, \alpha^\vee \rangle.
$$
Hence, $\rho = \sum_{\beta} \overline{\omega}_\beta$, and consequently $\rho \in C$. Finally, (iii) is equivalent to $\langle \rho, \alpha^\vee \rangle = 1$.
