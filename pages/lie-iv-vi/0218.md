Lemma 10. If X has a ramification point i, the full subgraph X - {i} is the union of three chains, and if p - 1, q - 1, r - 1 are the lengths of these chains, the triple {p, q, r} is equal, up to a permutation, to one of the triples {1, 2, 2}, {1, 2, 3}, {1, 2, 4}, {1, 1, m} (for some m $ \geq 1 $).

The vertex i belongs to 3 edges (Lemma 2), and there is no other ramification point (Lemma 7), so the full subgraph X - {i} is the sum of 3 chains X_1, X_2, X_3, each of which has a terminal vertex linked to i in X. Let {i_1, i_2}, {i_2, i_3}, ..., {i_{p-1}, i_p} be the edges of X_1, {j_1, j_2}, {j_2, j_3}, ..., {j_{q-1}, j_q} those of X_2, and {k_1, k_2}, {k_2, k_3}, ..., {k_{r-1}, k_r} those of X_3, with i_1, j_1, k_1 linked to i in X. We can assume that $ p \geq q \geq r \geq 1 $. Put

$$
x = e_{i_p} + 2e_{i_{p-1}} + \cdots + pe_{i_1}
$$
$$
y = e_{j_q} + 2e_{j_{q-1}} + \cdots + qe_{j_1}
$$
$$
z = e_{k_r} + 2e_{k_{r-1}} + \cdots + re_{k_1}.
$$

Since all the edges of X are of order 3 (Lemma 7), Lemma 8 gives $ \| x \| ^2 = \frac{1}{2} p(p+1) $, $ \| y \| ^2 = \frac{1}{2} q(q+1) $, $ \| z \| ^2 = \frac{1}{2} r(r+1) $. On the other hand, $ e_i $ is orthogonal to $ e_{i_2}, e_{i_3}, \ldots, e_{i_p} $, so $ (e_i | x) = p(e_i | e_{i_1}) = -\frac{1}{2} p $; similarly $ (e_i | y) = -\frac{1}{2} q $, $ (e_i | z) = -\frac{1}{2} r $. The unit vectors $ \| x \|^{-1} x, \| y \|^{-1} y, \| z \|^{-1} z $ are mutually orthogonal, and $ e_i $ does not belong to the subspace F that they generate; the square of the distance from $ e_i $ to F is

$$
1 - (e_i | \frac{x}{\| x \|})^2 - (e_i | \frac{y}{\| y \|})^2 - (e_i | \frac{z}{\| z \|})^2
$$
$$
= 1 - \frac{1}{2} \frac{p}{p+1} - \frac{1}{2} \frac{q}{q+1} - \frac{1}{2} \frac{r}{r+1}
$$
$$
= 1 - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{p+1} - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{q+1} - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \frac{1}{r+1}.
$$

Since this quantity is > 0, we have

$$
(p+1)^{-1} + (q+1)^{-1} + (r+1)^{-1} > 1.
$$ (4)

Hence $ 3(r+1)^{-1} > 1 $, so $ r < 2 $ and finally $ r = 1 $. Then (4) gives

$$
(p+1)^{-1} + (q+1)^{-1} > \frac{1}{2}
$$ (5)

hence $ 2(q+1)^{-1} > \frac{1}{2} $, so $ q \leq 2 $. Finally, if $ q = 2 $, (5) gives

$$
(p+1)^{-1} > \frac{1}{6}. \quad \text{hence } p \leq 4.
$$
