c) Let p be a dominant weight not belonging to Q(R). Show that the saturated subset S(p) generated by p (cf. § 1, Exerc. 23) contains a unique minuscule weight. (Remark that S(p) is contained in a non-trivial class mod. Q(R); conclude by applying a) and Exerc. 24 c) of § 1.)

d) Let p be a dominant weight not belonging to Q(R). Prove that the following two properties are equivalent:

(i) p is minuscule.

(v) There is no dominant weight q ≠ p such that p - q is a linear combination of elements of B with non-negative integer coefficients.

(If q satisfies the conditions of (v), let p₁ be a minuscule weight belonging to S(q). Then, p₁ ≡ p mod. Q(R), p₁ < p, and a) shows that p is not minuscule. Hence, (i) ⇒ (v). Conversely, if p is not minuscule, let q ∈ S(p) - W.p; transforming q by an element of W if necessary, we can assume that q ∈ C̄; by Exerc. 23 of § 1, q < p, q ≠ p, and q ≡ p mod. Q(R). § 3.

The notations and assumptions are those of nos. 2, 3, 4.

1) a) Show that, for all i between 1 and l, there exists a unique derivation D_i of A[P] satisfying the following conditions:

a₁) D_i is A-linear.

a₂) D_i(e^{ω_j}) = δ_{ij}e^{ω_j} (δ_{ij} being the Kronecker symbol).

b) Let (x_i)_{1 ≤ i ≤ l} be a family of elements of A[P]^W satisfying the condition of Theorem 1. Show that

$$
\det(D_i(x_j)) = d.
$$

(Show that det(D_i(x_j)) is anti-invariant and has maximal term e^ρ, hence the result if 2 is not a zero divisor in A. Treat the general case by using the principle of permanence of algebraic identities.) ^3

2) Let P' be a subgroup of P(R) containing Q(R). Show that P' is stable under W. Construct an example where the algebra A[P]^W is not isomorphic to a polynomial algebra (take for R the product of two systems of rank 1).
