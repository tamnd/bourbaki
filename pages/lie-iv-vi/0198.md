B_R(x, y) = \sum_{\alpha \in R} \Phi_R \left( \frac{2\alpha}{\Phi_R(\alpha, \alpha)}, x \right) \left( \frac{2\alpha}{\Phi_R(\alpha, \alpha)}, y \right)

$$

It follows that, if $R$ is *irreducible*, there exists a constant $\gamma(R) > 0$ such that

$$
\sum_{\alpha \in R} \Phi_R(\alpha, x) \Phi_R(\alpha, y) \Phi_R(\alpha, \alpha)^{-2} = \gamma(R) \Phi_R(x, y).
$$ (18)

By the definition of $\gamma(R)$, we have $B_R(x, y) = 4\gamma(R) \Phi_R(x, y)$, so

$$
\Phi_{R^{\sim}}(x^*, y^*) = (4\gamma(R))^{-1} B_{R^{\sim}}(x^*, y^*)
$$

for $x^*, y^* \in V^*$. This proves that $\gamma(R) = \gamma(R^{\sim})$. On the other hand, for $\beta \in R$,

$$
\Phi_{R^{\sim}}(\beta^{\sim}, \beta^{\sim}) = (4\gamma(R))^{-1} \sum_{\alpha \in R} \langle \beta^{\sim}, \alpha \rangle^2
= \gamma(R)^{-1} \sum_{\alpha \in R} \frac{\Phi_R(\alpha, \beta)^2}{\Phi_R(\beta, \beta)^2}
$$

so, by (16),

$$
\Phi_{R^{\sim}}(\beta^{\sim}, \beta^{\sim}) = \gamma(R)^{-1} \Phi_R(\beta, \beta)^{-2} \Phi_R(\beta, \beta)
$$

or finally

$$
\Phi_R(\beta, \beta) \Phi_{R^{\sim}}(\beta^{\sim}, \beta^{\sim}) = \gamma(R)^{-1}.
$$ (19)

Further, if all the roots of $R$ have the same length $\lambda$ for $\Phi_R$, (16) and (18) show that

$$
\gamma(R) = \lambda^{-4}.
$$ (20)

Moreover, if $h$ is the Coxeter number of $W$, the Cor. of Th. 1 of Chap. V, § 6, no. 2 shows that:

$$
h \Phi_R(x, x) = \sum_{\alpha \in R} \left( x, \frac{\alpha}{\lambda} \right)^2 \text{ for all } x \in V.
$$

Comparing with (16), we deduce that

$$
\lambda = h^{-1/2} \quad \text{or} \quad \gamma(R) = h^2.
$$ (21)

Finally, formula (19) shows that the roots of $R^{\sim}$ have length $\lambda$ for $\Phi_{R^{\sim}}$.

## § 2. AFFINE WEYL GROUP

In this paragraph (except in no. 5), we denote by $R$ a reduced root system in a real vector space $V$. We denote by $W$ the Weyl group of $R$; we identify it with a group of automorphisms of the dual $V^*$ of $V$ (§ 1, no. 1), and we provide $V^*$ with a scalar product invariant under $W$. Let $E$ be the affine space underlying $V^*$; for $v \in V^*$, we denote by $t(v)$ the translation of $E$ by the vector $v$. Finally, we denote by $P$ (resp. $Q$) the group of translations $t(v)$ whose vector $v$ belongs to the group of weights $P(R^{\sim})$ (resp. to the group of radical weights $Q(R^{\sim})$) of the inverse root system $R^{\sim}$ of $R$.
