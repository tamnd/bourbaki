### 8. PRODUCT DECOMPOSITION OF THE AFFINE SPACE E

We retain the notation of Prop. 5. For $0 \leq p \leq s$, let $E_p$ be the set of orbits of the group $T'_p$ in $E$, and let $\varphi_p$ be the canonical map from $E$ to $E_p$. The action of $T$ on $E$ passes to the quotient; in particular, $T_p$ acts on $E_p$ and it is immediate (for example by taking an origin in $E$) that $E_p$ is an affine space admitting $T_p$ as its space of translations. Put $E' = E_0 \times \cdots \times E_s$; this is an affine space having $T = T_0 \oplus \cdots \oplus T_s$ as its space of translations. Let $\varphi : E \to E'$ be the product map of the $\varphi_p$; since $\varphi$ commutes with the action of $T$, this is a bijection and even an isomorphism of affine spaces. In what follows, we identify $E$ and $E'$ by means of $\varphi$; the map $\varphi_p$ is then identified with the canonical projection of $E'$ onto $E_p$.

Since $W$ leaves $T'_p$ stable, the action of $W$ on $E$ passes to the quotient and defines an action of $W$ on $E_p$ (for $0 \leq p \leq s$), and hence by restriction an action of $W_p$ on $E_p$ (for $1 \leq p \leq s$). On the other hand, let $C$ be a chamber, let $i \in I$ and let $p$ be the integer such that $i \in J_p$. For any $x \in E$, we have
$$
s_i(C)(x) = x - \lambda.e_i(C) \quad \text{with } \lambda \in \mathbf{R}.
$$
Since $e_i \in T'_q$ for $q \neq p$, it follows that
$$
\varphi_q(w(x)) = \varphi_q(x) \quad \text{for } x \in E,\ w \in W_p,\ 0 \leq q \leq s \text{ and } q \neq p.
$$
Consequently, if $w = w_1 \ldots w_s \in W$ with $w_p \in W_p$ for $1 \leq p \leq s$, then
$$
w((x_0, \ldots, x_s)) = (x_0, w_1(x_1), \ldots, w_s(x_s))
$$
for all $x_p \in E_p$ for $0 \leq p \leq s$. In other words, *the action of $W$ on $E = E'$ is exactly the product of the actions of the $W_p$ on $E_p*$ (we put $W_0 = \{1\}$). It follows that $W_p$ acts faithfully on $E_p$ and that $W_p$ *can be identified with a group of displacements of the euclidean space $E_p*$ (the space $T_p$ of translations of $E_p$ being provided, of course, with the scalar product induced by that on $T$).

PROPOSITION 6. (i) *The group $W_p$ is a group of displacements of the euclidean affine space $E_p$; it is generated by reflections; provided with the discrete topology, it acts properly on $E_p$; it is irreducible.*

(ii) *Let $\mathfrak{H}_p$ be the set of hyperplanes $H$ of $E_p$ such that $s_H \in W_p$. The set $\mathfrak{H}$ consists of the hyperplanes of the form*
$$
H = E_0 \times E_1 \times \cdots \times E_{p-1} \times H_p \times E_{p+1} \times \cdots \times E_s,
$$
*with $p = 1, \ldots, s$ and $H_p \in \mathfrak{H}_p$.*

(iii) *Every chamber $C$ is of the form $E_0 \times C_1 \times \cdots \times C_s$, where for each $p$ the set $C_p$ is a chamber defined in $E_p$ by the set of hyperplanes $\mathfrak{H}_p$; moreover, the walls of $C_p$ are the hyperplanes $\varphi_p(H_i(C))$ for $i \in J_p$.*

Let $C$ be a chamber. Put $H_i = H_i(C)$, $e_i = e_i(C)$ and $s_i = s_i(C)$ for $i \in I$ (in the notation of no. 4).
