Let $q$ be the length of $w$. From $w \in P_s$ it follows that $l(sw) = q + 1$; and from $ws' \in P_s$ it follows that $l(sws') = l(ws') - 1 \leq q$. Since $l(sws') = l(sw) \pm 1$, we have finally that $l(ws') = q + 1$ and $l(sws') = q$.

Let $(s_1, \ldots, s_q)$ be a reduced decomposition of $w$ and $s_{q+1} = s'$; then $(s_1, \ldots, s_q, s_{q+1})$ is a reduced decomposition of the element $ws'$ of length $q + 1$. By the exchange condition, there exists an integer $j$ with $1 \leq j \leq q + 1$ such that
$$
ss_1 \ldots s_{j-1} = s_1 \ldots s_j.
$$
(22)
If $1 \leq j \leq q$, we would have $sw = s_1 \ldots s_{j-1} s_{j+1} \ldots s_q$ contradicting the formula $l(sw) = q + 1$. Thus $j = q + 1$ and formula (22) can be written $sw = ws'$.

Conversely, we have the following result:

PROPOSITION 6. *Let* $(P_s)_{s \in S}$ *be a family of subsets of* $W$ *satisfying* (C) *and the following conditions*:

(A') $1 \in P_s$ *for all* $s \in S$.
(B') *The sets* $P_s$ *and* $sP_s$ *are disjoint for all* $s \in S$.

*Then*, $(W, S)$ *is a Coxeter system and* $P_s$ *consists of the elements* $w$ *of* $W$ *such that* $l(sw) > l(w)$.

Let $s \in S$ and $w \in W$. There are two possibilities:

a) $w \notin P_s$. Let $(s_1, \ldots, s_q)$ be a reduced decomposition of $w$ and
$$
w_j = s_1 \ldots s_j
$$
for $1 \leq j \leq q$; also put $w_0 = 1$. Since $w_0 \in P_s$ by (A') and since $w = w_q$ is not in $P_s$, there exists an integer $j$ with $1 \leq j \leq q$ such that $w_{j-1} \in P_s$ and $w_j = w_{j-1} s_j$ does not belong to $P_s$. By (C),
$$
sw_{j-1} = w_{j-1} s_j.
$$
We have thus proved the formula
$$
ss_1 \ldots s_{j-1} = s_1 \ldots s_{j-1} s_j
$$
which implies that $sw = s_1 \ldots s_{j-1} s_{j+1} \ldots s_q$ and $l(sw) < l(w)$.

b) $w \in P_s$: put $w' = sw$, so that $w' \notin P_s$ by (B'). By a), we then have $l(sw') < l(w')$, that is $l(w) < l(sw)$.

Comparison of a) and b) proves that $P_s$ consists of those $w \in W$ such that $l(sw) > l(w)$. The exchange condition follows from this as we have seen in a), hence $(W, S)$ is a Coxeter system (Th. 1 of no. 6).
