PROPOSITION 2. *The $ \alpha \tilde{} $ form a root system in $ V^* $, and $ \alpha \tilde{} = \alpha $ for all $ \alpha \in R $.*

The $ \alpha \tilde{} $ satisfy (RS_I) by Prop. 1. Since $ s_{\alpha, \alpha \tilde{}} $ is an automorphism of the vector space $ V $ equipped with the subset $ R $, $ {}^t(s_{\alpha, \alpha \tilde{}})^{-1} $ leaves the set $ R \tilde{} $ of the $ \alpha \tilde{} $ stable; but $ {}^t(s_{\alpha, \alpha \tilde{}})^{-1} = s_{\alpha \tilde{}, \alpha} $, which proves that $ R \tilde{} $ satisfies (RS_{II}) and that $ \alpha \tilde{} = \alpha $. Finally, $ \langle \alpha \tilde{}, \beta \rangle \in \mathbf{Z} $ for all $ \alpha \tilde{} \in R \tilde{} $ and $ \beta \in R $, so $ R \tilde{} $ satisfies (RS_{III}).

The set $ R \tilde{} $ is called the *inverse root system of* $ R $. The map $ \alpha \mapsto \alpha \tilde{} $ is a bijection from $ R $ to $ R \tilde{} $, called the *canonical bijection from* $ R $ *to* $ R \tilde{} $. Note that, if $ \alpha, \beta $ are elements of $ R $ such that $ \alpha + \beta \in R $, then $ (\alpha + \beta) \tilde{} \neq \alpha \tilde{} + \beta \tilde{} $ in general.

Since $ s_{\alpha}(\alpha) = -\alpha $, axiom (RS_{II}) shows that $ -R = R $. Evidently $ (-\alpha) \tilde{} = -\alpha \tilde{} $ and $ -1 \in A(R) $ (but it is not always true that $ -1 \in W(R) $).

The equality $ {}^t(s_{\alpha, \alpha \tilde{}})^{-1} = s_{\alpha \tilde{}, \alpha} $ shows that the map $ u \mapsto {}^t u^{-1} $ is an isomorphism from the group $ W(R) $ to the group $ W(R \tilde{}) $. We identify these two groups by means of this isomorphism; in other words, we consider $ W(R) $ as acting both in $ V $ and in $ V^* $. Similarly for $ A(R) $.
