The \omega_i are called the fundamental weights (for C, or for B). The set of linear combinations of the \omega_i with coefficients > 0 (resp. \geq 0) is C (resp. \overline{C}). The linear combinations of the \omega_i with integer coefficients \geq 0 are called the dominant weights. Every element of P(R) can be transformed by some element of W(R) to a unique dominant weight. The dominant weights are the elements \omega of V such that \frac{2(\omega|\alpha_i)}{(\alpha_i|\alpha_i)} is an integer \geq 0 for all i.

29) Let T be the group of translations of V^* whose vectors belong to Q(R^*). The group of affine transformations of V^* generated by T and W(R) is the semi-direct product of W(R) by T. This group is called the affine Weyl group of R and is denoted by W_a(R). It acts properly on V^*. For \alpha \in R and \lambda \in \mathbf{Z}, let s_{\alpha,\lambda} be the map x^* \mapsto x^* - \langle x^*, \alpha \rangle \alpha^* + \lambda \alpha^*: this is an affine reflection, and the set L_{\alpha,\lambda} of its fixed points is defined by the equation \langle x^*, \alpha \rangle = \lambda; we have L_{\alpha,\lambda} = L_\alpha + \frac{1}{2} \lambda \alpha^*. The s_{\alpha,\lambda} are the affine reflections belonging to W_a(R), and they generate the group W_a(R).

30) Let E be the union of the L_{\alpha,\lambda} for \alpha \in R and \lambda \in \mathbf{Z}. The connected components of V^* - E are called the alcoves of R. If R is irreducible, each alcove is an open simplex; in general, an alcove is a product of open simplices. The group W_a(R) acts simply-transitively on the set of alcoves. If C is an alcove, \overline{C} is a fundamental domain for W_a(R). Let \sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_q be the reflections in W_a(R) corresponding to the walls of C; let \mu_{ij} be the order of \sigma_i \sigma_j. Then W_a(R) is defined by the generators \sigma_i and the relations (\sigma_i \sigma_j)^{\mu_{ij}} = 1.

31) If p \in P(R^*), there exists an alcove C such that p is an extremal point of \overline{C}. If C' is an alcove, there is a unique radical weight that is an extremal point of \overline{C'}.

Let x^* \in V^*; the following conditions are equivalent:

a) x^* \in P(R^*);

b) for all \alpha \in R, the hyperplane parallel to L_\alpha and passing through x^* is one of the L_{\alpha,\lambda}.

Let C' be a chamber of R^*. There exists a unique alcove C contained in C' and such that 0 \in \overline{C}. Assume that R is irreducible, and let \beta be the highest root of R (for C'); then C is the set of x^* \in C' such that \langle x^*, \beta \rangle < 1.
