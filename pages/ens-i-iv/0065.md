4. Let $A$ and $B$ be relations in a logical theory $\mathscr{T}$. Let $A|B$ denote the relation "(not $A$) or (not $B$)". Prove the following theorems in $\mathscr{T}$ :
$$(\text{not } A) \Leftrightarrow (A|A),$$
$$(A \text{ or } B) \Leftrightarrow ((A|A)|(B|B)),$$
$$(A \text{ and } B) \Leftrightarrow ((A|B)|(A|B)),$$
$$(A \Rightarrow B) \Leftrightarrow (A|(B|B)).$$

5. Let $\mathscr{T}$ be a logical theory with explicit axioms $A_1$, $A_2$, ..., $A_n$. Then $A_n$ is independent of the other axioms of $\mathscr{T}$ (§ 2, Exercise) if and only if the theory whose signs and schemes are the same as those of $\mathscr{T}$, and whose explicit axioms are $A_1$, $A_2$, ..., $A_{n-1}$, "not $A_n$", is not contradictory.

## § 4

*In all the Exercises for* § 4, $\mathscr{T}$ *denotes a quantified theory.*

1. Let $A$ and $B$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ be a letter which does not appear in $A$. Then $(\forall x)(A \Rightarrow B) \Leftrightarrow (A \Rightarrow (\forall x)B)$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$.

2. Let $A$ and $B$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ be a letter, distinct from the constants of $\mathscr{T}$, which does not appear in $A$. If $B \Rightarrow A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, then $((\exists x)B) \Rightarrow A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$.

3. Let $A$ be a relation in $\mathscr{T}$ and let $x$ and $y$ be letters. The relations
$$(\forall x)(\forall y)A \Rightarrow (\forall x)((x|y)A), \qquad (\exists x)((x|y)A) \Rightarrow (\exists x)(\exists y)A$$
are then theorems in $\mathscr{T}$.

4. Let $A$ and $B$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$ and let $x$ be a letter. Show that the relations
$$(\forall x)(A \text{ or } B) \Rightarrow ((\forall x)A \text{ or } (\exists x)B),$$
$$(\exists x)A \text{ and } (\forall x)B \Rightarrow (\exists x)(A \text{ and } B)$$
are theorems in $\mathscr{T}$.

5. Let $A$ and $B$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ and $y$ be letters. If $x$ does not appear in $B$, nor $y$ in $A$, then
$$(\forall x)(\forall y)(A \text{ and } B) \Leftrightarrow ((\forall x)A \text{ and } (\forall y)B)$$
is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$.

6. Let $A$ and $R$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ be a letter. Then the relations $(\exists_A x)R \Rightarrow (\exists x)R$, $(\forall x)R \Rightarrow (\forall_A x)R$ are theorems in $\mathscr{T}$.
