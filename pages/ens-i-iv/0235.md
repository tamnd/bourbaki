(f) Show that for each ordinal $\alpha$ there exists a unique pseudo-ordinal $\mathrm{E}_\alpha$ such that $\mathrm{Ord}(\mathrm{E}_\alpha) = \alpha$ (use (e) and criterion C60). In particular, the pseudo-ordinals whose order-types are $0, 1, 2 = 1 + 1$, and $3 = 2 + 1$ are respectively

$$\emptyset, \quad \{\emptyset\}, \quad \{\emptyset, \{\emptyset\}\}, \quad \{\emptyset, \{\emptyset\}, \{\emptyset, \{\emptyset\}\}\}.$$

## § 3

¶ 1. Let E and F be two sets, let $f$ be an injection of E into F, and let $g$ be a mapping of F into E. Show that there exist two subsets A, B of E such that $\mathrm{B} = \mathrm{E} - \mathrm{A}$, and two subsets $\mathrm{A}'$, $\mathrm{B}'$ of F such that $\mathrm{B}' = \mathrm{F} - \mathrm{A}'$, for which $\mathrm{A}' = f(\mathrm{A})$ and $\mathrm{B} = g(\mathrm{B}')$. (Let $\mathrm{R} = \mathrm{E} - g(\mathrm{F})$ and put $h = g \circ f$; take A to be the intersection of the subsets M of E such that $\mathrm{M} \supset \mathrm{R} \cup h(\mathrm{M})$.)

2. If E and F are distinct sets, show that $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{F}} \neq \mathrm{F}^{\mathrm{E}}$. Deduce that if E and F are the cardinals 2 and $4(= 2 + 2)$, then at least one of the sets $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{F}}$, $\mathrm{F}^{\mathrm{E}}$ is not a cardinal.

¶ 3. Let $(\mathfrak{a}_\iota)_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}}$ and $(\mathfrak{b}_\iota)_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}}$ be two families of cardinals such that $\mathfrak{b}_\iota \geqslant 2$ for each $\iota \in \mathrm{I}$.

(a) Show that. if $\mathfrak{a}_\iota \leqslant \mathfrak{b}_\iota$ for each $\iota \in \mathrm{I}$, then

$$\sum_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}} \mathfrak{a}_\iota \leqslant \mathbf{P}_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}} \mathfrak{b}_\iota.$$

(b) Show that, if $\mathfrak{a}_\iota < \mathfrak{b}_\iota$ for each $\iota \in \mathrm{I}$, then

$$\sum_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}} \mathfrak{a}_\iota < \mathbf{P}_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}} \mathfrak{b}_\iota.$$

(Note that a product $\prod_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}} \mathrm{E}_\iota$ cannot be the union of a family $(\mathrm{A}_\iota)_{\iota \in \mathrm{I}}$ such that $\mathrm{Card}\,(\mathrm{A}_\iota) < \mathrm{Card}\,(\mathrm{E}_\iota)$ for all $\iota \in \mathrm{I}$, by observing that $\mathrm{Card}\,(\mathrm{pr}_\iota(\mathrm{A}_\iota)) < \mathrm{Card}\,(\mathrm{E}_\iota).)$

4. Let E be a set and let $f$ be a mapping of $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{E}) - \{\emptyset\}$ into E such that for each non-empty subset X of E we have $f(\mathrm{X}) \in \mathrm{X}$ ("choice function").

(a) Let $\mathfrak{b}$ be a cardinal and let A be the set of all $x \in \mathrm{E}$ such that $\mathrm{Card}\,(\overset{-1}{f}(x)) \leqslant \mathfrak{b}$. Show that, if $\mathfrak{a} = \mathrm{Card}\,(\mathrm{A})$, then $2^{\mathfrak{a}} \leqslant 1 + \mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}$ (note that if $\mathrm{Y} \subset \mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{Y} \neq \emptyset$, then $f(\mathrm{Y}) \in \mathrm{A}$).

(b) Let B be the set of all $x \in \mathrm{E}$ such that, for each non-empty subset X of $\overset{-1}{f}(x)$, $\mathrm{Card}\,(\mathrm{X}) \leqslant \mathfrak{b}$. Show that $\mathrm{Card}\,(\mathrm{B}) \leqslant \mathfrak{b}$.
