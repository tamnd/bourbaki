6. Let E and F be two ordered sets, and let $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{F})$ be the subset of the product ordered set $\mathrm{F}^{\mathrm{E}}$ consisting of the *increasing* mappings of E into F.

(a) Show that if E, F, G are there ordered sets, then the ordered set $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{F} \times \mathrm{G})$ is isomorphic to the product ordered set $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{F}) \times \mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{G})$.

(b) Show that if E, F, G are three ordered sets, then the ordered set $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E} \times \mathrm{F}, \mathrm{G})$ is isomorphic to the ordered set $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E}, \mathcal{A}(\mathrm{F}, \mathrm{G}))$.

(c) If $\mathrm{E} \neq \emptyset$, then $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{F})$ is a lattice if and only if F is a lattice.

(d) Suppose that E and F are both non-empty. Then $\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{F})$ is totally ordered if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied :

($\alpha$) F consists of a single element;

($\beta$) E consists of a single element and F is totally ordered;

($\gamma$) E and F are both totally ordered and F has two elements.

7. In order that every mapping of an ordered set E into an ordered set F with at least two elements, which is both an increasing and a decreasing mapping, should be constant on E, it is necessary and sufficient that E should be connected with respect to the reflexive and symmetric relation "$x$ and $y$ are comparable" (Chapter II, § 6, Exercise 10). This condition is satisfied in particular if E is either left or right directed.

8. Let E and F be two ordered sets, let $f$ be an increasing mapping of E into F, and $g$ an increasing mapping of F into E. Let A (resp. B) be the set of all $x \in \mathrm{E}$ (resp. $y \in \mathrm{F}$) such that $g(f(x)) = x$ (resp. $f(g(y)) = y$). Show that the two ordered sets A and B are canonically isomorphic.

10. Let E and F be two lattices. Then a mapping $f$ of E into F is increasing if and only if

$$f (\inf (x, \, y)) \leqslant \inf (f(x), f(y))$$

for all $x \in \mathrm{E}$ and all $y \in \mathrm{E}$.
