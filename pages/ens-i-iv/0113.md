COROLLARY 2. Let $(X_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of sets. Then $\prod_{i \in I} X_i = \varnothing$ if and only if there exists $i \in I$ such that $X_i = \varnothing$.

If we have $X_i \neq \varnothing$ for each $i \in I$, then it follows from Corollary 1 that $\prod_{i \in I} X_i \neq \varnothing$; conversely, if $\prod_{i \in I} X_i \neq \varnothing$, the relation $\operatorname{pr}_\alpha \left( \prod_{i \in I} X_i \right) \subset X_\alpha$ shows that $X_\alpha \neq \varnothing$ for each $\alpha \in I$.

Hence, if we have a family $(X_i)_{i \in I}$ of non-empty sets, we may introduce (as an auxiliary constant) a function $f$ with domain $I$ such that $f(i) \in X_i$ for all $i \in I$. In practice one says: "take an element $x_i$ in each $X_i$." Intuitively, we have thus "chosen" an element $x_i$ in each set $X_i$; the introduction of the logical sign $\tau$ and the criteria governing its use absolve us from the necessity of formulating an "axiom of choice" to legalize this operation (cf. Summary of Results, §4, no. 10).

COROLLARY 3. Let $(X_i)_{i \in I}$ and $(Y_i)_{i \in I}$ be two families of sets having the same index set $I$. If $X_i \subset Y_i$ for each $i \in I$, then
$$
\prod_{i \in I} X_i \subset \prod_{i \in I} Y_i.
$$
Conversely, if $\prod_{i \in I} X_i \subset \prod_{i \in I} Y_i$, and if $X_i \neq \varnothing$ for each $i \in I$, then $X_i \subset Y_i$ for each $i \in I$.

The first assertion is obvious, and the second follows from Proposition 1, Corollary 5, because we have then, for each $\alpha \in I$,
$$
X_\alpha = \operatorname{pr}_\alpha \left( \prod_{i \in I} X_i \right) \subset \operatorname{pr}_\alpha \left( \prod_{i \in I} Y_i \right) = Y_\alpha.
$$
