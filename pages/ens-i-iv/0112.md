4. PARTIAL PRODUCTS

Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of sets, and let $ J $ be a subset of $ I $. The product $ \prod_{i \in J} X_i $ is called a partial product of $ \prod_{i \in I} X_i $. If $ f $ is a function whose graph $ F $ is a member of $ \prod_{i \in I} X_i $, then $ F \circ \Delta_J $ (where $ \Delta_J $ is the diagonal of $ J \times J $) is the graph of the restriction of $ f $ to $ J $. Clearly $ F \circ \Delta_J \in \prod_{i \in J} X_i $; the mapping $ F \to F \circ \Delta_J $ of $ \prod_{i \in I} X_i $ into $ \prod_{i \in J} X_i $ is called the projection of index $ J $ and is denoted by $ \operatorname{pr}_J $.

PROPOSITION 5. Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of sets and let $ J $ be a subset of $ I $. If for each $ i \in I $ we have $ X_i \neq \varnothing $, the projection $ \operatorname{pr}_J $ is a mapping of $ \prod_{i \in I} X_i $ onto $ \prod_{i \in J} X_i $.

In view of the remarks made above, it is enough to prove the following proposition:

PROPOSITION 6. Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of sets such that $ X_i \neq \varnothing $ for all $ i \in I $. If $ g $ is a mapping of $ J \subset I $ into $ A = \bigcup_{i \in I} X_i $ such that $ g(i) \in X_i $ for all $ i \in J $, then there exists an extension $ f $ of $ g $ to $ I $ such that $ f(i) \in X_i $ for all $ i \in I $.

For each $ i \in I - J $ let $ T_i $ denote the term $ \tau_y (y \in X_i) $. Since $ X_i \neq \varnothing $ by hypothesis, we have $ T_i \in X_i $ for all $ i \in I - J $ (Chapter I, §4, no. 1). If $ G $ is the graph of $ g $, the graph $ G \cup \left( \bigcup_{i \in I - J} \{ (i, T_i) \} \right) $ is the graph of a function which has the required properties, as is immediately verified.

COROLLARY 1. Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of sets such that for each $ i \in I $ we have $ X_i \neq \varnothing $. Then for each $ \alpha \in I $ the projection $ \operatorname{pr}_\alpha $ is a mapping of $ \prod_{i \in I} X_i $ onto $ X_\alpha $.

Apply Proposition 5 to the subset $ J = \{ \alpha \} $ of $ I $ and note that $ \operatorname{pr}_\alpha $ is the composition of the canonical mapping of $ X_\alpha^{|\alpha|} $ onto $ X_\alpha $ and the mapping $ \operatorname{pr}_{|\alpha|} $.
