LEMMA 2. Every balanced word $ A $ can be put in the form

$$
A = f A_1 A_2 \ldots A_p,
$$

where the $ A_i $ are balanced and $ n(f) = p $.

Let $ f $ be the initial sign of $ A $. By Lemma 1, $ A $ can be written as

$$
f A_1 A_2 \ldots A_p,
$$

where the $ A_i $ are balanced : we need only define $ A_i $ inductively as the balanced segment of $ A $ which begins at the $ k(i) $th place, where

$$
k(i) = 2 + \sum_{j < i} l(A_j).
$$

Moreover, we have

$$
\begin{align*}
1 + l(A_1) + \cdots + l(A_p) &= l(A) = n(A) + 1 \\
&= n(f) + n(A_1) + \cdots + n(A_p) + 1 \\
&= n(f) + (l(A_1) - 1) + \cdots + (l(A_p) - 1) + 1,
\end{align*}
$$

from which it follows that $ n(f) = p $.

Now that the two lemmas have been proved, it is obvious by induction on the length of $ A $ that every balanced word $ A $ is significant, by reason of Lemma 2 and Proposition 1.

COROLLARY 1. Let $ A $ be a significant word. For each integer $ k $ such that $ 0 \leq k < l(A) $ there is exactly one significant segment of $ A $ which begins at the $ (k+1) $th place.
