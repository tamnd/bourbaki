14. Let A and B be two sets, and let R be any subset of $\mathrm{A} \times \mathrm{B}$. For each subset X of A (resp. each subset Y of B) let $\rho(\mathrm{X})$ (resp. $\sigma(\mathrm{Y})$) denote the set of all $y \in \mathrm{B}$ (resp. $x \in \mathrm{A}$) such that $(x, y) \in \mathrm{R}$ for all $x \in \mathrm{A}$ (resp. $(x, y) \in \mathrm{R}$ for all $y \in \mathrm{B}$). Show that $\rho$ and $\sigma$ are decreasing mappings, and that the mappings $\mathrm{X} \to \sigma(\rho(\mathrm{X}))$ and $\mathrm{Y} \to \rho(\sigma(\mathrm{Y}))$ are closures (Exercise 13) in $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{A})$ and $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{B})$ respectively (ordered by inclusion).

15. (a) Let E be an ordered set, and for each subset X of E let $\rho(\mathrm{X})$ (resp. $\sigma(\mathrm{X})$) denote the set of upper (resp. lower) bounds of X in E. Show that, in $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{E})$, the set $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$ of subsets X such that $\mathrm{X} = \sigma(\rho(\mathrm{X}))$ is a complete lattice, and that the mapping $i : x \to \sigma(\{x\})$ is an isomorphism (called *canonical*) of E onto an ordered subset E' of $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$ such that, if a family $(x_\iota)$ of elements of E has a least upper bound (resp. greatest lower bound) in E, the image of this least upper bound (resp. greatest lower bound) is the least upper bound (resp. greatest lower bound) in $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$ of the family of images of the $x_\iota$. $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$ is called the *completion* of the ordered set E.

(b) Show that, for every subset X of E, $\sigma(\rho(\mathrm{X}))$ is the least upper bound in $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$ of the subset $i(\mathrm{X})$ of $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$. If $f$ is any increasing mapping of E into a complete lattice F, there exists a unique increasing mapping $\bar{f}$ of $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$ into F such that $f = \bar{f} \circ i$ and $\bar{f}(\sup \mathrm{Z}) = \sup (\bar{f}(\mathrm{Z}))$ for every subset Z of $\tilde{\mathrm{E}}$.

¶ 16. A lattice E is said to be *distributive* if it satisfies the following two conditions :

(D') $\qquad \sup (x, \inf (y, z)) = \inf (\sup (x, y), \sup (x, z)),$
(D'') $\qquad \inf (x, \sup (y, z)) = \sup (\inf (x, y) \inf (x, z))$

for all $x, y, z$ in E. A totally ordered set is a distributive lattice.

(a) Show that each of the conditions (D), (D') separately implies the condition

(D) $\quad \sup (\inf (x, y), \ \inf (y, z), \ \inf (z, x))$
$$= \inf (\sup (x, y), \ \sup (y, z), \ \sup (z, x))$$
for all $x, y, z$ in E.

(b) Show that the condition (D) implies the condition

(M) $\qquad$ If $x \geqslant z$, then $\sup (z, \inf (x, y)) = \inf (x, \sup (y, z)).$
