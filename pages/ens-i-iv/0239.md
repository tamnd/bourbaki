¶ 9. A subset A of a lattice E is said to be a *sublattice* if for each pair $(x, y)$ of elements of A, $\sup_{\mathrm{E}}(x, y)$ and $\inf_{\mathrm{E}}(x, y)$ belong to A.

(a) Let $(\mathrm{C}_i)_{1 \leqslant i \leqslant n}$ be a finite family of totally ordered sets and let

$$\mathrm{E} = \prod_{i=1}^{n} \mathrm{C}_i$$

be their product. Let A be a sublattice of E. Show that A cannot have more than $n$ irreducible elements (Exercise 7) no two of which are comparable. (The proof is by *reductio ad absurdum*. Suppose that there exist $r > n$ irreducible elements $a_1$, ..., $a_r$ in A, no two of which are comparable. Consider the elements $u = \sup{(a_1, \ldots, a_r)}$ and

$$v_j = \sup_{1 \leqslant j \leqslant r,\, j \neq i} (a_j)$$

of A. By projecting onto the factors, show that $u = v_i$ for some index $i$, and hence that two of the $a_i$ are comparable.)

(b) Conversely, let F be a finite distributive lattice, let P be the set of irreducible elements of F other than the least element of F, and suppose that $n$ is the greatest number of elements in a free subset of P (§ 1, Exercise 5). Show that F is isomorphic to a sublattice of a product of $n$ totally ordered sets. (Apply Exercise 5, which shows that P is the union of $n$ totally ordered sets $\mathrm{P}_i$ with no element in common. Let $\mathrm{C}_i$ be the totally ordered set obtained by adjoining a least element to $\mathrm{P}_i$ $(1 \leqslant i \leqslant n)$. With each $x \in \mathrm{F}$ associate the family $(x_i)_{1 \leqslant i \leqslant n}$, where $x_i$ is the least upper bound in $\mathrm{C}_i$ of the set of elements of $\mathrm{P}_i$ which are $\leqslant x$.)
