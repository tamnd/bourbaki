Now let $S\{x\}$ be the relation $(\exists z)P\{x,x,z\}$, and let $\gamma = g(S\{x\})$, which is a term in $\mathscr{T}$. If $\mathscr{T}$ is not contradictory, there is no proof of the proposition $S\{\gamma\}$ in $\mathscr{T}$. For if D were such a proof, then

$$P\{\gamma, g(S\{x\}), h(D)\}$$

would be a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. This relation is just $P\{\gamma, \gamma, h(D)\}$, and consequently $(\exists z)P\{\gamma,\gamma,z\}$ would also be a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$; but since the last relation is equivalent to $(\text{not }S\{\gamma\})$, $\mathscr{T}$ would be contradictory. Furthermore, what has just been said shows that, for each definite integer $\mu$, $(\text{not }P\{\gamma,\gamma,\mu\})$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. It follows that there is no proof in $\mathscr{T}$ of the proposition $(\text{not }S\{\gamma\})$, for this relation is equivalent to $(\exists z)P\{\gamma,\gamma,z\}$, and the existence of an integer $\mu$ such that $P\{\gamma,\gamma,\mu\}$ would imply that $\mathscr{T}$ was contradictory, by virtue of the preceding argument (†). This metamathematical theorem of Gödel has been subsequently generalized in various directions ([48], Chapter XI) (**).

(*) The detailed description of $g(A)$, $h(D)$, and $P\{x, y, z\}$ is extremely long and tedious, and to write out $P\{x,y,z\}$ would require so large a number of signs as to be impossible in practice. It is this type of difficulty that we discussed in the Introduction, but no mathematician would consider that this diminishes in any way the validity of these constructions.

(†) In fact, the last part of this argument presupposes a little more than the consistency of $\mathscr{T}$, namely what is called the “$\omega$-consistency” of $\mathscr{T}$. This means that there is no relation $R\{x\}$ in $\mathscr{T}$ such that $R\{x\}$ implies $x\in\mathbf{N}$ and such that, for each definite integer $\mu$, $R\{\mu\}$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, although $(\exists x)(x\in\mathbf{N}$ and $(\text{not }R\{x\}))$ is also a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. However, Rosser has shown that Gödel’s argument can be modified so as to require only the consistency of $\mathscr{T}$ ([48], p. 208).
