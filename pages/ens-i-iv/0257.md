§ 7

1. Let I be a directed set, let $(\mathrm{J}_\lambda)_{\lambda \in \mathrm{L}}$ be a family of subsets of I, indexed by a directed set L, such that (i) for each $\lambda \in \mathrm{L}$, $\mathrm{J}_\lambda$ is directed with respect to the induced ordering; (ii) the relation $\lambda \leqslant \mu$ implies $\mathrm{J}_\lambda \subset \mathrm{J}_\mu$; (iii) I is the union of the family $(\mathrm{J}_\lambda)$. Let $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha, f_{\alpha\beta})$ be an inverse system of sets relative to I, let E be its inverse limit, and for each $\lambda \in \mathrm{L}$ let $\mathrm{F}_\lambda$ be the inverse limit of the system obtained from $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha, f_{\alpha\beta})$ by restricting the index set to $\mathrm{J}_\lambda$. For $\lambda \leqslant \mu$ let $g_{\lambda\mu}$ be the canonical mapping of $\mathrm{F}_\mu$ into $\mathrm{F}_\lambda$ (no. 1). Show that $(\mathrm{F}_\lambda, g_{\lambda\mu})$ is an inverse system of sets relative to L, and define a canonical bijection of $\mathrm{F} = \varprojlim \mathrm{F}_\lambda$ onto E.

2. Let $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha, f_{\alpha\beta})$ be an inverse system of sets relative to a directed index set, let $\mathrm{E} = \varprojlim \mathrm{E}_\alpha$, and let $f_\alpha : \mathrm{E} \to \mathrm{E}_\alpha$ be the canonical mapping for each $\alpha$. Show that, if all the $f_{\alpha\beta}$ are injective, then $f_\alpha$ is injective.

3. Let $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha, f_{\alpha\beta})$ and $(\mathrm{F}_\alpha, g_{\alpha\beta})$ be two inverse systems of sets relative to the same index set I. For each $\alpha \in \mathrm{I}$, let $u_\alpha$ be a mapping of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha$ into $\mathrm{F}_\alpha$, such that the $u_\alpha$ form an inverse system of mappings. Let $\mathrm{G}_\alpha \subset \mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ be the graph of $u_\alpha$. Show that $(\mathrm{G}_\alpha)$ is an inverse system of subsets of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ and that its inverse limit may be canonically identified with the graph of $u = \varprojlim u_\alpha$.

4. Let I be a non-empty directed set with no greatest element, and let F be the set of all sequences $x = (\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \ldots, \alpha_{2n-1}, \alpha_{2n})$ of an even number $\geqslant 2$ of elements of I with the following properties : (i) $\alpha_{2i-1} < \alpha_{2i}$ for $1 \leqslant i \leqslant n$; (ii) $\alpha_{2i-1} \not\leqslant \alpha_{2j-1}$ for $1 \leqslant j < i \leqslant n$. The set F is not empty. Put $r(x) = \alpha_{2n-1}$, $s(x) = \alpha_{2n}$. The integer $n$ is called the *length* of $x$.
