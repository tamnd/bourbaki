Proposition 8. Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of mutually disjoint sets, and let $ (f_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of functions with the same target F such that the domain of $ f_i $ is $ X_i $ for each $ i \in I $. Then there exists exactly one function $ f $ with domain $ \bigcup_{i \in I} X_i $ and target F which extends each of the functions $ f_i $ ($ i \in I $).

This is a corollary of Proposition 7, since $ f_i $ and $ f_x $ clearly agree on $ X_i \cap X_x = \varnothing $ whenever $ i \neq x $.

Definition 7. A partition of a set E is a family of non-empty mutually disjoint subsets of E which covers E.

Example. For every non-empty set A, the family $ (\{x\})_{x \in A} $ is a partition of A.

If $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ is a partition of a set E, the mapping $ i \to X_i $ of I onto the set $ \mathfrak{F} $ of elements $ X_i $ of the partition is bijective. Hence, if $ \mathfrak{F} $ is given, the partition is determined up to a one-to-one correspondence between index sets. Usually when we speak of a partition, it is the set of elements of the partition with which we are concerned.

8. SUM OF A FAMILY OF SETS

Proposition 9. Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of sets. Then there exists a set X with the following property: X is the union of a family $ (X'_i)_{i \in I} $ of mutually disjoint sets such that for each $ i \in I $ there exists a one-to-one mapping of $ X_i $ onto $ X'_i $.

Let $ A = \bigcup_{i \in I} X_i $. If $ i \in I $, the mapping $ x \to (x, i) $ ($ x \in X_i $) is a one-to-one mapping of $ X_i $ onto a subset $ X'_i $ of $ A \times I $. Moreover, the image of $ X'_i $ under the second coordinate function on $ A \times I $ is contained in the set $ \{i\} $; it follows that $ X'_i \cap X'_x = \varnothing $ whenever $ i \neq x $. We may then take $ X = \bigcup_{i \in I} X'_i $.

Definition 8. Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of sets. The sum of this family is the union of the family of sets $ (X_i \times \{i\})_{i \in I} $.
