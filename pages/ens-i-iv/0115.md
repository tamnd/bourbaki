Then we have

$$
\bigcup_{\lambda \in L} \left( \bigcap_{i \in J_\lambda} X_{\lambda, i} \right) = \bigcap_{f \in I} \left( \bigcup_{\lambda \in L} X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)} \right),
$$
$$
\bigcap_{\lambda \in L} \left( \bigcap_{i \in J_\lambda} X_{\lambda, i} \right) = \bigcup_{f \in I} \left( \bigcap_{\lambda \in L} X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)} \right)
$$

("distributivity" of union over intersection, and of intersection over union).

Let $ x $ be an element of $ \bigcup_{\lambda \in L} \left( \bigcap_{i \in J_\lambda} X_{\lambda, i} \right) $ and let $ f $ be any element of $ I $. There exists an index $ \lambda $ such that $ x \in \bigcap_{i \in J_\lambda} X_{\lambda, i} $; consequently $ x \in X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)} $ and hence

$$
x \in \bigcup_{\lambda \in L} X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)}.
$$

Since this is true for each $ f \in I $, we have

$$
x \in \bigcap_{f \in I} \left( \bigcup_{\lambda \in L} X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)} \right).
$$

Conversely, let $ x $ be an object which does not belong to the set

$$
\bigcup_{\lambda \in L} \left( \bigcap_{i \in J_\lambda} X_{\lambda, i} \right).
$$

Then for each $ \lambda \in L $ we have $ x \notin \bigcap_{i \in J_\lambda} X_{\lambda, i} $, which means that the set $ J'_\lambda $ of indices $ i \in J_\lambda $ such that $ x \notin X_{\lambda, i} $ is not empty for each $ \lambda \in L $. By Proposition 5, Corollary 2, there exists a functional graph $ f $ with domain $ L $ such that for each $ \lambda \in L $ we have $ f(\lambda) \in J'_\lambda $. Consequently $ f \in I $ and $ x \notin X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)} $ for each $ \lambda \in L $; hence

$$
x \notin \bigcup_{\lambda \in L} X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)}
$$

and thus

$$
x \notin \bigcap_{f \in I} \left( \bigcup_{\lambda \in L} X_{\lambda, f(\lambda)} \right).
$$

This completes the proof of the first formula. The second formula follows by applying the first to the family $ ((\mathbf{C}_A X_{\lambda, i})_{i \in J_\lambda})_{\lambda \in L} $, where $ A $ denotes the union $ \bigcap_{\lambda \in L} (\bigcap_{i \in J_\lambda} X_{\lambda}) $.
