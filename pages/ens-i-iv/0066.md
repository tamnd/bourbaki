7. Let $A$ and $R$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ be a letter distinct from the constants of $\mathscr{T}$. If $R \Rightarrow A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, then

$$(\exists x)R \Leftrightarrow (\exists_A x)R$$

is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. If (not $R$) $\Rightarrow A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, then

$$(\forall x)R \Leftrightarrow (\forall_A x)R$$

is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$. In particular, if $A$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$,

$$(\exists x)R \Leftrightarrow (\exists_A x)R \qquad \text{and} \qquad (\forall x)R \Leftrightarrow (\forall_A x)R$$

are theorems in $\mathscr{T}$.

§ 5

*In all the Exercises for* § 5, $\mathscr{T}$ *denotes an equalitarian theory.*

1. The relation $x = y$ is functional in $x$ in $\mathscr{T}$.

2. Let $R$ be a relation in $\mathscr{T}$ and let $x$, $y$ be distinct letters. Then the relations $(\exists x)(x = y$ and R), $(y|x)R$ are equivalent in $\mathscr{T}$.

3. Let $R$ and $S$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, let $T$ be a term in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ and $y$ be distinct letters. Suppose that $y$ is not a constant of $T$ and that $x$ does not appear in $T$. Let $\mathscr{T}'$ be the theory obtained by adjoining $S$ to the axioms of $\mathscr{T}$. If $R$ is functional in $x$ in $\mathscr{T}'$, and if $(T|y)S$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, then the relation $(T|y)R$ is functional in $X$ in $\mathscr{T}$.

4. Let $R$ and $S$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$, and let $x$ be a letter which is not a constant of $\mathscr{T}$. If $R$ is functional in $x$ in $\mathscr{T}$, and if $R \Leftrightarrow S$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$, then $S$ is functional in $x$ in $\mathscr{T}$.

5. Let $R$, $S$, $T$ be relations in $\mathscr{T}$ and let $x$ be a letter. If $R$ is functional in $x$ in $\mathscr{T}$, show that the following relations are theorems in $\mathscr{T}$ :

$$(\text{not } (\exists x)(R \text{ and } S)) \Leftrightarrow (\exists x)(R \text{ and } (\text{not } S)),$$

$$(\exists x)(R \text{ and } (S \text{ and } T)) \Leftrightarrow (\exists x)(R \text{ and } S) \text{ and } (\exists x)(R \text{ and } T)),$$
$$(\exists x)(R \text{ and } (S \text{ or } T)) \Leftrightarrow ((\exists x)(R \text{ and } S) \text{ or } (\exists x)(R \text{ and } T)).$$

6. Show that if the scheme $(\exists x)R \Rightarrow R$ provides implicit axioms in $\mathscr{T}$, then $x = y$ is a theorem in $\mathscr{T}$ (cf. Exercise 1).
