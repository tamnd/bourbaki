### 2. INVERSE OF A CORRESPONDENCE

Let $ G $ be a graph and $ A = \mathrm{pr}_1 G, \ B = \mathrm{pr}_2 G $ its projections. The relation $ (\gamma, x) \in G $ implies $ (x, \gamma) \in B \times A $; this relation therefore has a graph which consists of all ordered pairs $ (x, \gamma) $ such that $ (\gamma, x) \in G $.

Definition 5. Let $ G $ be a graph. The graph whose elements are the ordered pairs $ (x, \gamma) $ such that $ (\gamma, x) \in G $ is called the inverse of $ G $ and is denoted by $ \overline{G}^{-1} $.

For every set $ X $, $ \overline{G}^{-1}\langle X \rangle $ is called the inverse image of $ X $ under $ G $.

It is clear that the inverse of $ \overline{G}^{-1} $ is $ G $, and that
$$
\mathrm{pr}_1 \overline{G}^{-1} = \mathrm{pr}_2 G, \quad \mathrm{pr}_2 \overline{G}^{-1} = \mathrm{pr}_1 G.
$$
In particular, if $ X, Y $ are two sets, we have
$$
\overline{X \times Y}^{-1} = Y \times X.
$$
A graph $ G $ is said to be symmetric if $ \overline{G}^{-1} = G $.

Let $ \Gamma = (G, A, B) $ be a correspondence between $ A $ and $ B $. Since $ \mathrm{pr}_1 \overline{G}^{-1} \subset B $ and $ \mathrm{pr}_2 \overline{G}^{-1} \subset A $, the triple $ (\overline{G}, B, A) $ is a correspondence between $ B $ and $ A $, called the inverse of the correspondence $ \Gamma $, and denoted by $ \overline{\Gamma}^{-1} $. For every subset $ Y $ of $ B $, the image $ \overline{\Gamma}\langle Y \rangle $ of $ Y $ under $ \overline{\Gamma}^{-1} $ is also called the inverse image of $ Y $ under $ \Gamma $. Clearly the inverse of $ \overline{\Gamma}^{-1} $ is $ \Gamma $.

### 3. COMPOSITION OF TWO CORRESPONDENCES

Let $ G, G' $ be two graphs. Let $ A $ denote the set $ \mathrm{pr}_1 G $ and let $ C $ denote the set $ \mathrm{pr}_2 G' $. The relation $ (\exists \gamma)((x, \gamma) \in G \text{ and } (\gamma, z) \in G') $ implies that $ (x, z) \in A \times C $, and therefore has a graph with respect to $ x $ and $ z $.

Definition 6. Let $ G, G' $ be two graphs. The graph (with respect to $ x $ and $ z $) of the relation $ (\exists \gamma)((x, \gamma) \in G \text{ and } (\gamma, z) \in G') $ is called the composition of $ G' $ and $ G $, and is denoted by $ G' \circ G $ (or sometimes by $ G'G $).
