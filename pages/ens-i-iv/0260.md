Show that, whenever $\alpha \leqslant \beta$, the mapping $f_{\beta\alpha}$ is compatible with the equivalence relations $\mathrm{R}_\alpha$ and $\mathrm{R}_\beta$. Let $\mathrm{E}'_\alpha = \mathrm{E}_\alpha/\mathrm{R}_\alpha$, and let $f'_{\beta\alpha}$ be the mapping of $\mathrm{E}'_\alpha$ into $\mathrm{E}'_\beta$ induced by $f_{\beta\alpha}$ on passing to the quotients. Show that $f'_{\beta\alpha}$ is injective and that $(\mathrm{E}'_\alpha, f'_{\beta\alpha})$ is a direct system of sets, and define a canonical bijection of E onto $\varinjlim \mathrm{E}'_\alpha$.

8. Let $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha, f_{\beta\alpha})$ and $(\mathrm{F}_\alpha, g_{\beta\alpha})$ be two direct systems of sets, both indexed by the same directed set I. For each $\alpha \in \mathrm{I}$, let $u_\alpha$ be a mapping of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha$ into $\mathrm{F}_\alpha$, such that the $u_\alpha$ form a direct system of mappings. Let $\mathrm{G}_\alpha \subset \mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ be the graph of $u_\alpha$. Show that $(\mathrm{G}_\alpha)$ is a direct system of subsets of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha \times \mathrm{F}_\alpha$ and that its direct limit may be canonically identified with the graph of $u = \varinjlim u_\alpha$.

9. Let I be an *arbitrary* preordered set, and let $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha)_{\alpha \in \mathrm{I}}$ be a family of sets indexed by I. For each pair of indices $(\alpha, \beta)$ such that $\alpha \leqslant \beta$, let $f_{\beta\alpha}$ be a mapping of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha$ into $\mathrm{E}_\beta$, and suppose that these mappings satisfy conditions $(\mathrm{LI_I})$ and $(\mathrm{LI_{II}})$. Let G be the set which is the sum of the family $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha)$ and (with the notation of no. 5) let $\mathrm{R}\{x, y\}$ be the relation "$\lambda(x) = \alpha \leqslant \lambda(y) = \beta$ and $y = f_{\beta\alpha}(x)$" between two elements $x$, $y$ of G. Let $\mathrm{R}'$ be the equivalence relation on G whose graph is the smallest of the graphs of equivalence relations which contain the graph of R (Chapter II, § 6, Exercise 10). The set $\mathrm{E} = \mathrm{G}/\mathrm{R}'$ is called the *direct limit* of the family $(\mathrm{E}_\alpha)$ with respect to the family of mappings $(f_{\beta\alpha})$, and we write $\mathrm{E} = \varinjlim \mathrm{E}_\alpha$. When the index set I is *directed*, show that this definition agrees with that given in no. 5. In the general case, the restriction to $\mathrm{E}_\alpha$ of the canonical mapping of G into E is called the canonical mapping of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha$ into E and is denoted by $f_\alpha$. Suppose we are given, for each $\alpha \in \mathrm{I}$, a mapping $u_\alpha$ of $\mathrm{E}_\alpha$ into F such that $u_\beta \circ f_{\beta\alpha} = u_\alpha$ whenever $\alpha \leqslant \beta$; show that there exists a unique mapping $u$ of E into F such that $u = u_\alpha \circ f_\alpha$ for each $\alpha \in \mathrm{I}$.
