1. (a) Let $E$ be a set and let $\mathscr{F}(E)$ be the set of finite subsets of $E$. Show that $\mathscr{F}(E)$ is the smallest subset $\mathscr{G}$ of $\mathscr{P}(E)$ satisfying the following conditions: (i) $\emptyset \in \mathscr{G}$; (ii) the relations $X \in \mathscr{G}$ and $x \in E$ imply

$$
X \cup \{x\} \in \mathscr{G}.
$$

(b) Deduce from (a) that the union of two finite subsets $A, B$ of $E$ is finite (consider the set of subsets $X$ of $E$ such that $X \cup A$ is finite; cf. § 5, no. 1, Proposition 1, Corollary 1).

(c) Deduce from (a) and (b) that for every finite set $E$ the set $\mathscr{P}(E)$ is finite (consider the set of subsets $X$ of $E$ such that $\mathscr{P}(X)$ is finite; cf. § 5, no. 1, Proposition 1, Corollary 4).

2. Show that a set $E$ is finite if and only if every non-empty subset of $\mathscr{P}(E)$ has a maximal element (with respect to inclusion). (To show that the condition is sufficient, apply it to the set $\mathscr{F}(E)$ of finite subsets of $E$.)

3. Show that if a well-ordered set $E$ is such that the ordered set obtained by endowing $E$ with the opposite ordering is also well-ordered, then $E$ is finite (consider the greatest element $x$ of $E$ such that the segment $S_x$ is finite).
