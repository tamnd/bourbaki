1. (a) Let E be a set and let $\mathfrak{F}(\mathrm{E})$ be the set of finite subsets of E. Show that $\mathfrak{F}(\mathrm{E})$ is the smallest subset $\mathfrak{G}$ of $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{E})$ satisfying the following conditions : (i) $\emptyset \in \mathfrak{G}$; (ii) the relations $\mathrm{X} \in \mathfrak{G}$ and $x \in \mathrm{E}$ imply

$$\mathrm{X} \cup \{x\} \in \mathfrak{G}.$$

(b) Deduce from (a) that the union of two finite subsets A, B of E is finite (consider the set of subsets X of E such that $\mathrm{X} \cup \mathrm{A}$ is finite; cf. § 5, no. 1, Proposition 1, Corollary 1).

(c) Deduce from (a) and (b) that for every finite set E the set $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{E})$ is finite (consider the set of subsets X of E such that $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{X})$ is finite; cf. § 5, no. 1, Proposition 1, Corollary 4).

2. Show that a set E is finite if and only if every non-empty subset of $\mathfrak{P}(\mathrm{E})$ has a maximal element (with respect to inclusion). (To show that the condition is sufficient, apply it to the set $\mathfrak{F}(\mathrm{E})$ of finite subsets of E.)

3. Show that if a well-ordered set E is such that the ordered set obtained by endowing E with the opposite ordering is also well-ordered, then E is finite (consider the greatest element $x$ of E such that the segment $\mathrm{S}_x$ is finite).
