# EXERCISES

§ 1

1. Let $\mathscr{T}$ be a theory with no specific signs. Show that no assembly in $\mathscr{T}$ is a relation, and that the only assemblies in $\mathscr{T}$ which are terms are assemblies consisting of a single letter.

2. Let $A$ be a term or a relation in a theory $\mathscr{T}$. Show that every sign $\square$ in $A$ (if there is any) is linked to a single sign $\tau$ situated to its left. Show that every sign $\tau$ in $A$ (if there is any) is either not linked at all or else is linked to certain signs $\square$ situated to its right. Show also that no other sign is linked.

3. Let $A$ be a term or a relation in a theory $\mathscr{T}$. Show that every specific sign, if there is any, is followed by $\square$, or $\tau$, or a letter, or a substantific sign.

¶ 4. Let $A$ be a term or a relation in a theory $\mathscr{T}$, and let $B$ be an assembly in $\mathscr{T}$. Show that $AB$ is neither a term nor a relation in $\mathscr{T}$. (Use induction on the number of signs in $A$.)

5. Let $A$ be an assembly in a theory $\mathscr{T}$ and let $x$ be a letter. Show that if $\tau_x(A)$ is a term in $\mathscr{T}$, then $A$ is a relation in $\mathscr{T}$.

6. Let $A$ and $B$ be assemblies in a theory $\mathscr{T}$. If $A$ and $\Rightarrow AB$ are relations in $\mathscr{T}$, prove that $B$ is a relation in $\mathscr{T}$ (use Exercise 4).
