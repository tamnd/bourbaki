If a logical theory $ \mathscr{T} $ is contradictory, *every relation in $ \mathscr{T} $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $*. For let $ A $ be a relation in $ \mathscr{T} $ such that $ A $ and “not $ A $” are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $, and let $ B $ be any relation in $ \mathscr{T} $. By S2, (not $ A $) $ \Rightarrow ((\text{not } A) \text{ or } B) $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $; therefore, by Cl ($ \S 2 $, no. 2), “(not $ A $) or $ B $”, that is to say $ A \Rightarrow B $, is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $. A second application of Cl shows that $ B $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $.

*From now on $ \mathscr{T} $ will denote a logical theory.*

### 2. FIRST CONSEQUENCES

C6. *Let $ A, B, C $ be relations in $ \mathscr{T} $. If $ A \Rightarrow B $ and $ B \Rightarrow C $ are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $, then $ A \Rightarrow C $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $*.

For $ (B \Rightarrow C) \Rightarrow ((A \Rightarrow B) \Rightarrow (A \Rightarrow C)) $ is an axiom of $ \mathscr{T} $, by replacing $ A $ by $ B $, $ B $ by $ C $, and $ C $ by “not $ A $” in S4. By Cl ($ \S 2 $, no. 2), $ (A \Rightarrow B) \Rightarrow (A \Rightarrow C) $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $. A further application of Cl completes the proof.

C7. *If $ A $ and $ B $ are relations in $ \mathscr{T} $, then $ B \Rightarrow (A \text{ or } B) $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $*.

For $ B \Rightarrow (B \text{ or } A) $ and $ (B \text{ or } A) \Rightarrow (A \text{ or } B) $ are axioms of $ \mathscr{T} $ by virtue of S2 and S3. Now use C6.

C8. *If $ A $ is a relation in $ \mathscr{T} $, $ A \Rightarrow A $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $*.

For $ A \Rightarrow (A \text{ or } A) $ and $ (A \text{ or } A) \Rightarrow A $ are axioms, by S2 and S1. Now use C6.

C9. *If $ A $ is a relation and $ B $ a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $, then $ A \Rightarrow B $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $*.

For $ B \Rightarrow ((\text{not } A) \text{ or } B) $ is a theorem by C7, and therefore “(not $ A $) or $ B $”, that is to say $ A \Rightarrow B $, is a theorem by Cl.

C10. *If $ A $ is a relation in $ \mathscr{T} $, then “$ A $ or (not $ A $)” is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $*.

For “(not $ A $) or $ A $” is a theorem by C8; now use S3 and Cl.

C11. *If $ A $ is a relation in $ \mathscr{T} $, “$ A \Rightarrow (\text{not not } A) $” is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $*.

For this relation is “(not $ A $) or (not not $ A $)”, and the result follows from C10.

C12. *Let $ A $ and $ B $ be two relations in $ \mathscr{T} $. Then the relation*

$$
(A \Rightarrow B) \Rightarrow ((\text{not } B) \Rightarrow (\text{not } A))
$$

For

$$
((\text{not } A) \text{ or } B) \Rightarrow ((\text{not } A) \text{ or } (\text{not not } B))
