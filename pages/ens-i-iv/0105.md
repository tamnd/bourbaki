§ We record also the following Proposition on complements:

PROPOSITION 6. *Let $ f $ be a mapping of $ A $ into $ B $. For every subset $ Y $ of $ B $, we have*

$$
\overline{f}^1 \langle B - Y \rangle = \overline{f}^1 \langle B \rangle - \overline{f}^1 \langle Y \rangle.
$$

For $ x $ belongs to $ \overline{f}^1 \langle B - Y \rangle $ if and only if $ f(x) $ belongs to $ B $ but not to $ Y $, i.e., if and only if $ x $ belongs to $ \overline{f}^1 \langle B \rangle $ but not to $ \overline{f}^1 \langle Y \rangle $.

COROLLARY. *Let $ f $ be an injection of $ A $ into $ B $. For every subset $ X $ of $ A $, we have* $ f \langle A - X \rangle = f \langle A \rangle - f \langle X \rangle $.

Writing $ f = i \circ g $, where $ i $ is the canonical injection of $ f \langle A \rangle $ into $ B $, we reduce the Corollary to Proposition 6 applied to $ \overline{g}^1 $.

§ The intersection $ X \cap A $ is sometimes called the *trace* of $ X $ on $ A $. If $ \mathfrak{F} $ is a family of sets, the set of traces on $ A $ of the sets belonging to $ \mathfrak{F} $ is called the *trace* of $ \mathfrak{F} $ on $ A $.

6. COVERINGS

DEFINITION 5. *A family of sets* $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ *is said to be a covering of a set* $ E $ *(or to cover* $ E $) *if* $ E \subset \bigcup_{i \in I} X_i $. *If* $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ *and* $ (Y_\chi)_{\chi \in K} $ *are coverings of* $ E $, *the second of these coverings is said to be finer than the first* (or to be a refinement of the first, or to refine the first) *if, for each* $ \chi \in K $, *there exists* $ i \in I $ *such that*

$$
Y_\chi \subset X_i.
$$

A set of sets $ \mathscr{R} $ is a covering of $ E $ if the family of sets defined by the identity mapping of $ \mathscr{R} $ is a covering of $ E $, in other words, if $ E \subset \bigcup_{x \in \mathscr{R}} X $.

If $ \mathscr{R}, \mathscr{R}', \mathscr{R}'' $ are three coverings of $ E $ such that $ \mathscr{R}' $ refines $ \mathscr{R} $ and $ \mathscr{R}'' $ refines $ \mathscr{R}' $, it is clear that $ \mathscr{R}'' $ refines $ \mathscr{R} $.

Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a covering of $ E $. If $ J $ is a subset of $ I $ such that $ (X_i)_{i \in J} $ is still a covering of $ E $, then this covering clearly refines $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $.
