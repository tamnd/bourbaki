§ 3

1. Let $ A, B, C $ be relations in a logical theory $ \mathscr{T} $. Show that the following relations are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $:

$$
\begin{align*}
A &\Rightarrow (B \Rightarrow A), \\
(A \Rightarrow B) &\Rightarrow ((B \Rightarrow C) \Rightarrow (A \Rightarrow C)), \\
A &\Rightarrow ((\text{not } A) \Rightarrow B), \\
(A \text{ or } B) &\iff ((A \Rightarrow B) \Rightarrow B), \\
(A \iff B) &\iff ((A \text{ and } B) \text{ or } ((\text{not } A) \text{ and } (\text{not } B))), \\
(A \iff B) &\iff \text{not } ((\text{not } A) \iff B), \\
(A \Rightarrow (B \text{ or } (\text{not } C))) &\iff ((C \text{ and } A) \Rightarrow B), \\
(A \Rightarrow (B \text{ or } C)) &\iff (B \text{ or } (A \Rightarrow C)), \\
(A \Rightarrow B) &\Rightarrow ((A \Rightarrow C) \Rightarrow (A \Rightarrow (B \text{ and } C))), \\
(A \Rightarrow C) &\Rightarrow ((B \Rightarrow C) \Rightarrow ((A \text{ or } B) \Rightarrow C)), \\
(A \Rightarrow B) &\Rightarrow ((A \text{ and } C) \Rightarrow (B \text{ and } C)), \\
(A \Rightarrow B) &\Rightarrow ((A \text{ or } C) \Rightarrow (B \text{ or } C)).
\end{align*}
$$

2. Let $ A $ be a relation in a logical theory $ \mathscr{T} $. If $ A \iff (\text{not } A) $ is a theorem of $ \mathscr{T} $, then $ \mathscr{T} $ is contradictory.

3. Let $ A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_n $ be relations in a logical theory $ \mathscr{T} $.

(a) To prove the relation "$ A_1 \text{ or } A_2 \text{ or } \ldots \text{ or } A_n $" in $ \mathscr{T} $, it is enough to prove $ A_n $ in the theory obtained by adjoining to $ \mathscr{T} $ the axioms not $ A_1, $ not $ A_2, \ldots, $ not $ A_{n-1} $.

(b) If "$ A_1 \text{ or } A_2 \text{ or } \ldots \text{ or } A_n $" is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $, then to prove a theorem $ A $ in $ \mathscr{T} $ it is sufficient to prove the theorems

$$
A_1 \Rightarrow A, \quad A_2 \Rightarrow A, \quad \ldots, \quad A_n \Rightarrow A.
$$
