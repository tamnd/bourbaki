This criterion follows from the corresponding properties of the extension $ \hat{g} $ (Chapter II, § 5, no. 1, Proposition 1) and the extension $ g \times h $ (Chapter II, § 3, no. 9).

CST3. *If $ f_i $ is a bijection of $ E_i $ onto $ E'_i $, and if $ f_i^{-1} $ is the inverse bijection (*), then $ \langle f_1, \ldots, f_n \rangle^s $ is a bijection and $ \langle f_1^{-1}, \ldots, f_n^{-1} \rangle^s $ is its inverse; in other words,

### 3. TRANSPORTABLE RELATIONS

Let $ \mathscr{T} $ be a theory which is stronger than the theory of sets, let $ x_1, \ldots, x_n, s_1, \ldots, s_p $ be distinct letters which are distinct from the constants of $ \mathscr{T} $, and let $ A_1, \ldots, A_m $ be terms in $ \mathscr{T} $ in which none of the letters $ x_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq n $) and $ s_j $ ($ 1 \leq j \leq p $) appears. Let $ S_1, \ldots, S_p $ be echelon construction schemes on $ n + m $ terms. Then the relation $ T \{ x_1, \ldots, x_n, s_1, \ldots, s_p \} $:

"“$ s_1 \in S_1(x_1, \ldots, x_n, A_1, \ldots, A_m) $ and $ s_2 \in S_2(x_1, \ldots, x_n, A_1, \ldots, A_m) $
and ... and $ s_p \in S_p(x_1, \ldots, x_n, A_1, \ldots, A_m) $”

is called a *typification* of the letters $ s_1, \ldots, s_p $.

¶ Let $ R \{ x_1, \ldots, x_n, s_1, \ldots, s_p \} $ be a relation in $ \mathscr{T} $ which contains certain of the letters $ x_i, s_j $ (and possibly other letters as well). Then $ R $ is said to be *transportable (in $ \mathscr{T} $) with respect to the typification T, the $ x_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq n $) being considered as principal base sets and the $ A_h $ ($ 1 \leq h \leq m $) as auxiliary base sets*, if the following condition is satisfied: let $ y_1, \ldots, y_n, f_1, \ldots, f_n $ be distinct letters which are distinct from the $ x_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq n $), the $ s_j $ ($ 1 \leq j \leq p $), the constants of $ \mathscr{T} $, and all the letters which appear in $ R $ or in the terms $ A_h $ ($ 1 \leq h \leq m $), and let $ \mathrm{Id}_h $ ($ 1 \leq h \leq m $) denote the identity mapping of $ A_h $ on to itself. Then the relation

(1) “$ T \{ x_1, \ldots, x_n, s_1, \ldots, s_p \} $ and ($ f_1 $ is a bijection of $ x_1 $ onto $ y_1 $)
and ... and ($ f_n $ is a bijection of $ x_n $ onto $ y_n $)”

implies, in $ \mathscr{T} $, the relation

(2) $ R \{ x_1, \ldots, x_n, s_1, \ldots, s_p \} \Leftrightarrow R \{ y_1, \ldots, y_n, s'_1, \ldots, s'_p \} $,

(*) For typographical reasons we write here $ f^{-1} $ instead of $ \overline{f}^{-1} $.
