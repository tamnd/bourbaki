From this moment onward, the enlargement of the axiomatic method was an accepted fact. Although for some time yet it was felt to be useful to check “abstract” results, whenever possible, against geometrical intuition, at least it was agreed that the “classical” objects were not the only legitimate objects of study for a mathematician. Precisely because of the multiplicity of possible “interpretations” or “models”, it came to be recognized that the “nature” of mathematical objects is ultimately of secondary importance, and that it matters little, for example, whether a result is presented as a theorem of “pure” geometry or as a theorem of algebra *via* analytical geometry.
