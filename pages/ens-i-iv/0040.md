4. CONJUNCTION

Let $ A, B $ be assemblies. The assembly

$$
\not((\not A) \text{ or } (\not B))
$$

will be denoted by “$ A $ and $ B $”.
CS6. *Let $ A, B, T $ be assemblies and $ x $ a letter. Then the assembly*

$$
(T|x)(A \text{ and } B)
$$

*is identical with “$(T|x)A$ and $(T|x)B$”.*

This is an immediate consequence of CS5 (§ 1, no. 2).

CF9. *If $ A, B $ are relations in $ \mathscr{T} $, then “$ A $ and $ B $” is a relation in $ \mathscr{T} $ (called the conjunction of $ A $ and $ B $).*

This follows immediately from CF1 and CF2 (§ 1, no. 4).

C20. *If $ A, B $ are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $, then “$ A $ and $ B $” is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $.*

Suppose that “$ A $ and $ B $” is false, that is to say,

$$
\not \not ((\not A) \text{ or } (\not B))
$$

is true. By C16, “$(\not A) \text{ or } (\not B)$”, that is to say, $ A \Rightarrow (\not B) $ is true, hence “$\not B$” is true; but this is absurd. Hence “$ A $ and $ B $” is true.

C21. *If $ A, B $ are relations in $ \mathscr{T} $, then*

$$
(A \text{ and } B) \Rightarrow A, \quad (A \text{ and } B) \Rightarrow B
$$

*are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $.*

The relations $ (\not A) \Rightarrow ((\not A) \text{ or } (\not B)) $, $ (\not B) \Rightarrow ((\not A) \text{ or } (\not B)) $ are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $, by S2 (no. 1) and C7 (no. 2). Now $ ((\not A) \text{ or } (\not B)) \Rightarrow (\not (A \text{ and } B)) $ is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $ by C11. Hence $ (\not A) \Rightarrow (\not (A \text{ and } B)) $, $ (\not B)) \Rightarrow (\not (A \text{ and } B)) $ are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $. The result follows by applying C17.
