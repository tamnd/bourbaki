In practice we say: "Suppose that there exists an object x satisfying A for which R is false", and seek to establish a contradiction.

The properties of typical quantifiers are analogous to those of quantifiers:

C38. Let A and R be relations in $ \mathscr{T} $, and let x be a letter. Then the relations
$$
\begin{align*}
\not (\forall_A x)R &\iff (\exists_A x)(\text{not } R), \\
\not (\exists_A x)R &\iff (\forall_A x)(\text{not } R)
\end{align*}
$$
are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $.

C39. Let A, R, and S be relations in $ \mathscr{T} $, and let x be a letter which is not a constant of $ \mathscr{T} $. If the relation $ A \Rightarrow (R \Rightarrow S) $ [resp. $ A \Rightarrow (R \iff S) $] is a theorem in $ \mathscr{T} $, then the relations
$$
\begin{align*}
(\exists_A x)R &\Rightarrow (\exists_A x)S, & (\forall_A x)R &\Rightarrow (\forall_A x)S \\
[\text{resp. } (\exists_A x)R &\iff (\exists_A x)S, & (\forall_A x)R &\iff (\forall_A x)S]
\end{align*}
$$
are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $.

C40. Let A, R, and S be relations in $ \mathscr{T} $ and let x be a letter. Then the relations
$$
\begin{align*}
(\forall_A x)(R \text{ and } S) &\iff ((\forall_A x)R \text{ and } (\forall_A x)S), \\
(\exists_A x)(R \text{ or } S) &\iff ((\exists_A x)R \text{ or } (\exists_A x)S)
\end{align*}
$$
are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $.

C41. Let A, R, and S be relations in $ \mathscr{T} $, and let x be a letter which does not appear in R. Then the relations
$$
\begin{align*}
(\forall_A x)(R \text{ or } S) &\iff (R \text{ or } (\forall_A x)S), \\
(\exists_A x)(R \text{ and } S) &\iff (R \text{ and } (\exists_A x)S)
\end{align*}
$$
are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $.

C42. Let A, B, R be relations in $ \mathscr{T} $ and let x and y be letters. If x does not appear in B, and if y does not appear in A, then the relations
$$
\begin{align*}
(\forall_A x)(\forall_B y)R &\iff (\forall_B y)(\forall_A x)R, \\
(\exists_A x(\exists_B y)R &\iff (\exists_B y)(\exists_A x)R, \\
(\exists_A x)(\forall_B y)R &\Rightarrow (\forall_B y)(\exists_A x)R
\end{align*}
$$
are theorems in $ \mathscr{T} $.
