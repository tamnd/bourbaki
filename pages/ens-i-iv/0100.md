Definition 4. Let $ \mathscr{F} $ be a set of sets and let $ \Phi $ be the family of sets defined by the identity mapping of $ \mathscr{F} $. The union of the sets of $ \Phi $ and (if $ \mathscr{F} $ is not empty) the intersection of the sets of $ \Phi $ are called, respectively, the union and the intersection of the sets of $ \mathscr{F} $, and are denoted by $ \bigcup_{X \in \mathscr{F}} X $ and $ \bigcap_{X \in \mathscr{F}} X $.

If follows immediately from Proposition 1 that if $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ is a family of sets, then the union and (if $ I \neq \emptyset $) the intersection of the family are respectively equal to the union and the intersection of the sets of the set of elements of this family.

2. PROPERTIES OF UNION AND INTERSECTION

If $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ and $ (Y_i)_{i \in I} $ are families of sets having the same index set I, and if $ Y_i \subset X_i $ for each $ i \in I $, then it is clear that
$$
\bigcup_{i \in I} Y_i \subset \bigcup_{i \in I} X_i, \quad \text{and (if } I \neq \emptyset \text{)} \quad \bigcap_{i \in I} Y_i \subset \bigcap_{i \in I} X_i.
$$

¶ Let $ (X_i)_{i \in I} $ be a family of sets. If $ J \subset I $, we have
$$
\bigcup_{i \in J} X_i \subset \bigcup_{i \in I} X_i, \quad \text{and (if } J \neq \emptyset \text{)} \quad \bigcap_{i \in J} X_i \supset \bigcap_{i \in I} X_i.
$$
