16. Let $E$ be a finite set with $n$ elements, let $\mathscr{D}$ be a non-empty subset of $\mathscr{P}(E)$, and let $\lambda, k, l$ be three integers $\geq 1$ with the following properties : (i) if $A, B$ are distinct elements of $\mathscr{D}$, then Card $(A \cap B) = \lambda$; (ii) for each $A \in \mathscr{D}$, Card $(A) \leq k$; (iii) for each $x \in E$ the number of elements of $\mathscr{D}$ to which $x$ belongs is equal to $l$. Show that

$$
n(\lambda - 1) \leq k(k - 1),
$$

and that if $n(\lambda - 1) = k(k - 1)$ then $\lambda = k$ and Card $(\mathscr{D}) = n$. (Given $a \in E$, let $\mathfrak{L}$ be the set of all $A - \{a\}$ where $A \in \mathscr{D}$ and $a \in A$, and let $\mathfrak{G}$ be the set of all $A \in \mathscr{D}$ such that $a \notin A$. Apply the results of Exercise 15 to $\mathfrak{L}$ and $\mathfrak{G}$.)

17. Let $i, h, k$ be three integers such that $i \geq 1,\ h \geq i,\ k \geq i$. Show that there exists an integer $m_i(h, k)$ with the following properties : for each finite set $E$ with at least $m_i(h, k)$ elements, and each partition $(\mathscr{X}, \mathscr{Y})$ of the set $\mathscr{F}_i(E)$ of subsets of $i$ elements of $E$, it is impossible that every subset of $h$ elements of $E$ contains a subset $X \in \mathscr{X}$ and that every subset of $k$ elements of $E$ contains a subset $Y \in \mathscr{Y}$; in other words, if every subset of $h$ elements of $E$ contains some $X \in \mathscr{X}$, there exists a subset $A$ of $k$ elements of $E$ such that every subset of $i$ elements of $A$ belongs to $\mathscr{X}$. (Proof by induction. Show that we may take

$$
m_1(h, k) = h + k - 1,\quad m_i(i, k) = k,\quad \text{and}\quad m_i(h, i) = h,
$$

and finally $m_i(h, k) = m_{i-1}(m_i(h-1, k), m_i(h, k-1)) + 1$. If $E$ is a set with $m_i(h, k)$ elements, if $a \in E$ and if $E' = E - \{a\}$, show that if the proposition were false, then every subset of $m_i(h-1, k)$ elements of $E'$ would contain a subset $X'$ of $i-1$ elements such that $X' \cup \{a\} \in \mathscr{X}$, and that every subset of $m_i(h, k-1)$ elements of $E'$ would contain a subset $Y'$ of $i-1$ elements such that $Y' \cup \{a\} \in \mathscr{Y}$.)

(b) Let $h, k$ be two integers $\geq 1$ and let $r(h, k) = (h-1)(k-1) + 1$. Let $I$ be a finite totally ordered set with at least $r(h, k)$ elements. Show that, for each finite sequence $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ of elements of a totally ordered set $E$, there exists either a subset $H$ of $h$ elements of $I$ such that the sequence $(x_i)_{i \in H}$ is increasing, or else a subset $K$ of $k$ elements of $I$ such that the sequence $(x_i)_{i \in K}$ is decreasing. (Use (a) applied to $I \times E$.)
