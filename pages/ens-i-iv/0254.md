§ 24. Let $ \mathfrak{F} $ be a set of subsets of a set $ E $ such that for each $ A \in \mathfrak{F} $ we have $ \mathrm{Card}(A) = \mathrm{Card}(\mathfrak{F}) = a \geq \aleph_0 $. Show that $ E $ has a subset $ P $ such that $ \mathrm{Card}(P) = a $ and such that no set of $ \mathfrak{F} $ is contained in $ P $. (If $ a = \aleph_\alpha $, define by transfinite induction two injective mappings $ \xi \to f(\xi), \xi \to g(\xi) $ of $ \omega_\alpha $ into $ E $ such that the sets $ P = f(\omega_\alpha) $ and $ Q = g(\omega_\alpha) $ do not intersect and such that each of them meets every subset $ A \in \mathfrak{F} $.)

(b) Suppose, moreover, that for each subset $ \mathfrak{G} $ of $ \mathfrak{F} $ such that $ \mathrm{Card}(\mathfrak{G}) < a $, the complement in $ E $ of the union of the sets $ A \in \mathfrak{G} $ has cardinal $ \geq a $. Show that $ E $ then has a subset $ P $ such that $ \mathrm{Card}(P) = a $ and such that, for each $ A \in \mathfrak{G} $, $ \mathrm{Card}(P \cap A) < a $ (similar method).

§ 25. (a) Let $ \mathfrak{F} $ be a covering of an infinite set $ E $. The degree of disjointness of $ \mathfrak{F} $ is the least cardinal $ c $ such that $ c $ is strictly greater than the cardinals $ \mathrm{Card}(X \cap Y) $ for each pair of distinct sets $ X, Y \in \mathfrak{F} $. If $ \mathrm{Card}(E) = a $ and $ \mathrm{Card}(\mathfrak{F}) = b $, show that $ b \leq a^c $ (note that a subset of $ E $ of cardinal $ c $ is contained in at most one set of $ \mathfrak{F} $).

(b) Let $ \omega_\alpha $ be an initial ordinal and let $ F $ be a set such that $ 2 \leq p = \mathrm{Card}(F) < \aleph_\alpha $. Let $ E $ be the set of mappings of segments of $ \omega_\alpha $, other than $ \omega_\alpha $ itself, into $ F $. Then we have $ \mathrm{Card}(E) \leq p^{\aleph_\alpha} $. For each mapping $ f $ of $ \omega_\alpha $ into $ F $, let $ K_f $ be the subset of $ E $ consisting of the restrictions of $ f $ to the segments of $ \omega_\alpha $ (other than $ \omega_\alpha $ itself). Show that the set $ \mathfrak{F} $ of subsets $ K_f $ is a covering of $ E $ such that $ \mathrm{Card}(\mathfrak{F}) = p^{\aleph_\alpha} $, and that its degree of disjointness is equal to $ \aleph_\alpha $.

(c) Let $ E $ be an infinite set of cardinal $ a $, and let $ c, p $ be two cardinals $ > 1 $ such that $ p < c, p^m < a $ for all $ m < c $, and $ a = \sum_{m < c} p^m $. Deduce from (b) that there exists a covering $ \mathfrak{F} $ of $ E $ consisting of sets of cardinal $ c $, with degree of disjunction equal to $ c $, and such that $ \mathrm{Card}(\mathfrak{F}) = p^c $. In particular, if $ E $ is countably infinite, there exists a covering $ \mathfrak{F} $ of $ E $ by infinite sets such that $ \mathrm{Card}(\mathfrak{F}) = 2^{\aleph_0} $ and such that the intersection of any two sets of $ \mathfrak{F} $ is finite.

§ 26. Let $ E $ be an infinite set and let $ \mathfrak{F} $ be a set of subsets of $ E $ such that for each $ A \in \mathfrak{F} $ we have

$$
\mathrm{Card}(A) = \mathrm{Card}(\mathfrak{F}) = \mathrm{Card}(E) = a \geq \aleph_0.
$$

Show that there exists a partition $ (B_i)_{i \in I} $ of $ E $ such that

$$
\mathrm{Card}(I) = \mathrm{Card}(B_i) = a
$$

for all $ i \in I $, and such that $ A \cap B_i \neq \varnothing $ for all $ A \in \mathfrak{F} $ and all $ i \in I $.
