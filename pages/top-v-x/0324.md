§ 1

1) Let $X$ be a set, let $Y$ be a uniform space containing more than one point, and let $\mathscr{S}$ be a non-empty set of non-empty subsets of $X$. Let $Y' \subset \mathscr{F}(X; Y)$ be the set of all constant mappings of $X$ into $Y$. For each $y \in Y$, let $c_y$ be the constant mapping of $X$ into $Y$ whose value is $y$.

a) Show that $y \to c_y$ is an isomorphism of $Y$ onto the uniform subspace $Y'$ of $\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{S}}(X; Y)$.

b) $\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{S}}(X; Y)$ is Hausdorff if and only if $Y$ is Hausdorff and $\mathscr{S}$ is a covering of $X$.

c) $Y'$ is closed in $\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{S}}(X; Y)$ if and only if $\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{S}}(X; Y)$ is Hausdorff.

2) Let $X$ be a set, let $Y$ be a Hausdorff uniform space containing more than one point, and let $\mathscr{S}_1, \mathscr{S}_2$ be two sets of subsets of $X$ which satisfy conditions $(F'_I), (F'_{II})$ of no. 2. Show that if $\mathscr{S}_1 \subset \mathscr{S}_2$ and $\mathscr{S}_1 \neq \mathscr{S}_2$, then the uniformity of $\mathscr{S}_1$-convergence is strictly coarser than the uniformity of $\mathscr{S}_2$-convergence. In particular:

(i) If $X$ is a non-compact Hausdorff topological space, the uniformity of compact convergence is strictly coarser than the uniformity of uniform convergence.

(ii) If $X$ is a Hausdorff topological space which has infinite compact subsets (cf. Chapter I, § 9, Exercise 4), then the uniformity of pointwise convergence is strictly coarser than the uniformity of compact convergence.

3) Let $X$ be a set, let $\mathscr{S}$ be a covering of $X$, let $Y$ be a non-Hausdorff uniform space and let $Y_0$ be the Hausdorff uniform space associated with $Y$ (Chapter II, § 3, no. 8). Show that the Hausdorff uniform space associated with $\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{S}}(X; Y)$ is isomorphic to $\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{S}}(X; Y_0)$.
