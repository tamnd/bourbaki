7) A topological space $X$ is said to be *perfectly normal* if it is normal and if each closed subset of $X$ is a countable intersection of open sets (or, equivalently, if each open subset of $X$ is a countable union of closed sets).

$a)$ A Hausdorff space $X$ is perfectly normal if and only if, given any closed subset $A$ of $X$, there exists a real-valued continuous function $f$ on $X$ such that $f^{-1}(0) = A$ [use the method of Exercise 16 $a)$ of § 1].
