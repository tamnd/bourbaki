§ 1

1) A positive real-valued function $f$ on $X \setminus X$ is a pseudometric on $X$ if and only if $f(x, x') = 0$ for all $x \in X$ and
$$
f(x, y) \leq f(x, z) + f(y, z)
$$
for all $x, y, z$ in $X$.

2) Let $f$ be a mapping of $X \setminus X$ into $[0, +\infty]$. Then the family of sets $\{f(x, x') > s\}$, where $s$ runs through the set of all real numbers $> 0$, forms a fundamental system of entourages of a uniformity on $X$ if and only if $f$ satisfies the following conditions:
i) $f(x, x) = 0$ for all $x \in X$;
ii) for each $s > 0$ there exists $t > 0$ such that the relation $f(x, x') \leq t$ implies $f(x, x') \leq s;$
iii) for each $s > 0$ there exists $c > 0$ such that the relations $f(x, z) \leq c$ and $f(z, y) \leq c$ imply $f(x, y) \leq a$.

The conditions ii) and iii) are satisfied in particular if there exists a mapping $\varphi$ of $I = [0, +\infty]$ onto itself which is continuous and zero at the point 0, and a mapping $\psi$ of $I \setminus I$ into $I$ which is continuous and zero at the point $(0, 0)$, such that we have identically
$$
f(y, x) \leq \varphi(f(x, y)) \quad \text{and} \quad f(x, y) \leq \psi(f(x, z), f(z, y)).
$$

3) Let $X$ be a topological space, let $\overline{\mathcal{T}}$ be the topology of $X$, let $(f_i)$ be a family (no. 2') of pseudometrics on $X$, and let $\mathcal{U}$ be the uniformity defined by the family $(f_i)$.

4) The topology induced by $\mathcal{U}$ is coarser than $\overline{\mathcal{T}}$ if and only if the $f_i$ are continuous on $X \setminus X$ with respect to the product of the topology $\overline{\mathcal{T}}$ by itself.

5) The topology induced by $\mathcal{U}$ is finer than $\overline{\mathcal{T}}$ if and only if, for each $x_0 \in X$ and each neighbourhood $V$ of $x_0$ in the topology $\overline{\mathcal{T}}$ there is an index $i$ and a real number $s > 0$ such that $f_{i, x_0, x'} > s$ for all $x \in \mathbf{C}V$.
