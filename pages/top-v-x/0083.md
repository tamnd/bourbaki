PROPOSITION 10. *The topological group* $ T^n $ *is locally isomorphic to* $ R^n $.

For $ T^n = (R/Z)^n $ is isomorphic to $ R^n/Z^n $ (Chapter III, § 2, no. 9, Corollary to Proposition 26) and $ Z^n $ is a discrete subgroup of $ R^n $ (Chapter III, § 2, no. 6, Proposition 19).

It follows that the groups $ R^p \times T^{n-p} $ are all locally isomorphic to $ R^n $ ($ 0 \geq p \geq n $); in § 2, no. 2, we shall see that they are the only *connected* groups which have this property.

### 5. SUBGROUPS AND QUOTIENT GROUPS OF $ T^n $

Let us identify $ T^n $ with $ R^n/Z^n $, and let $ \varphi $ be the canonical homomorphism of $ R^n $ onto $ R^n/Z^n $. Every subgroup of $ T^n $ is of the form $ G = \varphi(H) $ where $ H $ is a subgroup of $ R^n $ which contains $ Z^n $ and is isomorphic to $ H/Z^n $ (Chapter III, § 2, no. 7, Proposition 20); for $ G $ to be *closed* in $ T^n $ it is necessary and sufficient for $ H $ to be closed in $ R^n $ (Chapter I, § 3, no. 4). Thus in order to find the closed subgroups of $ T^n $ we have to determine all the closed subgroups $ H $ of $ R^n $ which contain $ Z^n $; to do this we shall use Proposition 6 of no. 3, and we start by determining the subgroup $ H^* $ associated with $ H $. Since $ Z^n $ is associated with itself, we have $ H^* \subset Z^n $; consequently (no. 1) there exists a basis $ (a_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n} $ of $ R^n $ which generates $ Z^n $, and a basis of $ H^* $ (with respect to the ring $ Z $) consisting of $ p $ points $ b_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq p $) such that $ b_i = e_i a_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq p $), where the $ e_i $ are integers satisfying the congruences $ e_{i+1} \equiv 0 \pmod{e_i} $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq p-1 $. Let $ (a'_i) $ be the basis dual to $ (a_i) $; then $ u = \sum_{i=1}^n u_i a_i $ belongs to $ (H^*)^* = H $ if and only if $ u_i e_i \in Z $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq p $; in other words, $ H $ is the direct sum of the vector subspace $ V $ generated by $ a'_{p+1}, \ldots, a_n $, and the discrete subgroup $ K $ generated by the $ p $ points
$$
e_i^{-1} a'_i \quad (1 \leq i \leq p).
$$
On the other hand, $ Z^n $ is the direct sum of $ V \cap Z^n $ and $ K \cap Z^n $, because the $ a'_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq n $) generate $ Z^n $. The quotient group $ H/Z^n $ is therefore isomorphic to $ (V/(V \cap Z^n)) \times (K/(K \cap Z^n)) $ (Chapter III, § 2, no. 9, Corollary to Proposition 26); $ V/(V \cap Z^n) $ is isomorphic to $ T^{n-p} $, and $ K/(K \cap Z^n) $ is a *finite* group, the direct sum of $ p $ cyclic groups of orders $ e_i $, respectively ($ 1 \leq i \leq p $) (cf. no. 1).

Using the same notation, every Hausdorff quotient group of $ T^n $ is of the form $ T^n/\varphi(H) $ and is isomorphic to $ R^n/H $ (Chapter III, § 2, no. 7, Corollary to Proposition 22); if $ W $ is the vector subspace generated by $ K $, $ R^n/H $ is isomorphic to $ W/K $ (Chapter III, § 2, no. 9, Corollary to Proposition 26), i.e., to $ T^p $.
