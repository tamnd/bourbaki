§ 2

1) Let $E$ be a linearly ordered set with a smallest element $\omega$, and let $I$ be an interval of $E$, containing $\omega$, with $\omega$ as its left-hand end-point. Suppose that $E$ and $I$ satisfy axioms (GR_I), (GR_{II}), (GR_{IV}) and the following axiom:

(GR_{IIIb}) *The set of elements $x > \omega$ in $I$ is not empty, and if $x$ is any element $> \omega$ in $I$, there exists $y > \omega$ in $I$ such that $y^2 \leq x$.*

Show that there exists an increasing mapping $f$ of $I$ into $\mathbf{R}$, such that $f(\omega) = 0$ and $f(xy) = f(x) + f(y)$ whenever $x, y$ and $xy$ are in $I$; also that $f(I) \cap [0, f(b)]$ is dense in $[0, f(b)]$ for all $b \in I$.

2) Let $G$ be a non-commutative linearly ordered group (for example the multiplicative group of a non-commutative ordered division ring). In the set $\mathbf{R}_+ \times G$ consider the set $E$ consisting of $(0, e)$ ($e$ being the identity element of $G$) and all pairs $(x, y)$ where $x$ is any real number $> 0$ and $y$ is any element of $G$. Define a law of composition on $E$ by the rule $(x, y)(x', y') = (x + x', yy')$, and order $E$ lexicographically [i.e. $(x, y) < (x', y')$ if $x < x'$ or if $x = x'$ and $y < y'$]. If we take $I = E$, show that axioms (GR_I), (GR_{II}), (GR_{IIIb}) (Exercise 1) and (GR_{IV}) are satisfied; but that if $f$ is an increasing mapping of $E$ into $\mathbf{R}_+$ such that $f(zz') = f(z) + f(z')$ for all $a$ and $z'$ in $E$, then $f$ is not strictly increasing.
