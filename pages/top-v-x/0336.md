12) Let X be a topological space, Y and Z two uniform spaces, $ \mathcal{S} $ a set of subsets of X and $ \mathcal{T} $ a set of subsets of Y.

a) Let $ u_0 \in \mathcal{C}(X; Y) $. Show that if, for each $ A \in \mathcal{S} $, $ u_0(A) $ is contained in some set of $ \mathcal{T} $, then the mapping $ v \to v \circ u_0 $ of $ \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y; Z) $ into $ \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(X; Z) $ is continuous.

b) Let H be a subspace of $ \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(X; Y) $ and let $ v_0 \in \mathcal{C}(Y; Z) $. Show that if $ v_0 $ is uniformly continuous on $ H(A) $ for all $ A \in \mathcal{S} $, then the mapping $ u \to v_0 \circ u $ of H into $ \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(X; Z) $ is continuous.

(i) $ V(u_0(A)) \subset B $,
(ii) $ v_0 $ is uniformly continuous on B.
Then the mapping $ (u, v) \to v \circ u $ of $ \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(X; Y) \times \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y; Z) $ into $ \mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(X; Z) $ is continuous at $ (u_0, v_0) $. In particular, the mapping $ (u, v) \to v \circ u $ of $ \mathcal{C}_u(X; Y) \times \mathcal{C}_u(Y; Z) $ into $ \mathcal{C}_u(X; Z) $ is continuous at every point $ (u_0, v_0) $ such that $ v_0 $ is uniformly continuous on Y.
