### 2. CLOSED SUBGROUPS OF $ \mathbf{R}^n $

We know already of two types of closed subgroups of $ \mathbf{R}^n $; on the one hand, the *vector subspaces* of $ \mathbf{R}^n $, which are isomorphic to the groups $ \mathbf{R}^p $ ($ p \leq n $) (Chapter IV, § 1, no. 4, Proposition 2); on the other hand, the *discrete* subgroups (Chapter III, § 2, no. 1, Proposition 5) which are isomorphic to groups $ \mathbf{Z}^q $ ($ q \leq n $), as we have just seen. We shall now determine the structure of an *arbitrary* closed subgroup of $ \mathbf{R}^n $ by showing that such a subgroup is isomorphic to a *product* of the form

$$
\mathbf{R}^p \times \mathbf{Z}^q \quad \text{where} \quad 0 \leq p + q \leq n.
$$

The proof rests on the following proposition:

**Proposition 3.** *Every non-discrete closed subgroup of* $ \mathbf{R}^n $ *contains a line passing through* $ o $.

Let $ (x_p)_{p \in \mathbf{N}} $ be an infinite sequence of points of $ G $ such that $ x_p \neq o $ and $ \lim_{p \to \infty} x_p = o $; by hypothesis such a sequence exists. Let $ P $ be an open cube with centre $ o $, containing the $ x_p $. Let $ k_p $ denote the largest integer $ h > 0 $ such that $ hx_p \in P $ (since $ P $ is a bounded box and $ x_p \neq o $, the existence of $ k_p $ follows from Archimedes' axiom). The points $ k_p x_p $ lie in a compact set $ \overline{P} $, hence the sequence $ (k_p x_p)_{p \in \mathbf{N}} $ has a cluster point $ a \in \overline{P} $. If $ \| (k_p + 1)x_p - a \| \leq \varepsilon $, we have

$$
\| (k_p + 1)x_p - a \| \leq \varepsilon + \| x_p \|,
$$
