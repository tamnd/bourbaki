8) Let X, Y be two metric spaces and let $(f_n)$ be a sequence of Borel mappings of class $\alpha$ of X into Y (Chapter IX, § 6, Exercise 16).

a) Suppose that the sequence $(f_n)$ converges pointwise to a mapping $f : X \to Y$. Show that $f$ is of class $\alpha + 1$. [If U is an open subset of Y, note that $\bar{f}^1(U) = \bigcup_{n \geq 1} \left( \bigcap_{k \geq 0} \bar{f}_{n+k}^1(U) \right)$, and use Chapter IX, § 6, Exercise 4.]

b) Suppose that the sequence $(f_n)$ converges uniformly to $f$. Show that $f$ is of class $\alpha$. [Let F be a closed subset of Y, and for each integer $n > 0$, let $V_n$ be the set of points of Y whose distance from F is $\leq 1/n$. Show that there is an increasing sequence $n \to m(n)$ of integers such that $\bar{f}^1(F) = \bigcap_n \bar{f}_{m(n)}^1(V_n)$.]

c) Suppose that Y is of countable type. Show that, if $f : X \to Y$ is a Borel function of class $\alpha > 0$, there exists a sequence of Borel functions $g_n : X \to Y$, of class $< \alpha$, such that $(g_n)$ converges pointwise to $f$. [Show that we may take the $g_n$ to be functions which take only a finite number of values; use Exercises 4 h) and 16 c) of Chapter IX, § 6.]

9) Let X be a Baire space, Y a metric space and $(f_n)$ a sequence of continuous mappings of X into Y which converges pointwise to a function $f$. A point $x \in X$ is said to be a point of uniform convergence of the sequence $(f_n)$ if, for each $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a neighbourhood V of $x$ and an integer $p$ such that $d(f_m(y), f(g_n)) \leq \varepsilon$ for all $y \in V$ and all integers $m \geq p, n \geq p$, $d$ being the metric on Y. Show that the complement S of the set of points of uniform convergence of the sequence $(f_n)$ is meagre in X [cf. Chapter IX, § 5, Exercise 22 a).] Give an example where S is dense in X. [Take $X = Y = \mathbf{R}$, let $n \to r_n$ be a bijection of $\mathbf{N}$ onto $\mathbf{Q}$, and let $(g_n)$ be a sequence of continuous mappings of $\mathbf{R}$ into $[0, 1]$ which converges to the zero function for $x \neq 0$ and to 1 at $x = 0$, the convergence being uniform in every open set of $\mathbf{R}$ which does not contain 0. Then consider the sequence of functions $f_n(x) = \sum_{p=0}^\infty \alpha_p g_n(x - r_p)$, where the sequence $(\alpha_p)$ tends to 0 in a suitable way.]
