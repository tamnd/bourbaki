§ 20) a) Let $ X $ be a compact space, let $ A $ be an $ \mathbf{R} $-algebra of finite rank with an identity element, and endow $ A $ with the topology defined in Chapter VI, § 1, no. 5. Let $ B $ be a subalgebra of the $ \mathbf{R} $-algebra $ \mathcal{C}(X; A) $, and suppose that, for each $ \varepsilon > 0 $, each pair of distinct points $ x, y $ of $ X $ and each pair of elements $ u, v $ of $ A $, there exists $ f \in B $ such that $ \|f(x) - u\| \leq \varepsilon $ and $ \|f(y) - v\| \leq \varepsilon $ ($ \| \ \| $ being any norm defining the topology of $ A $); and suppose, further, that if $ u $ and $ v $ belong to $ \mathbf{R} $, there is a function $ f \in B $ satisfying these conditions and taking its values in $ \mathbf{R} $. Show that, under these hypotheses, $ B $ is dense in $ \mathcal{C}(X; A) $ with respect to the topology of uniform convergence. [Show first that every function of $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{R}) $ can be uniformly approximated by functions of $ B $ and then that the same is true of every constant $ a \in A $, by using the hypotheses and a partition of unity.]

b) Take $ A = \mathbf{H} $, the division ring of quaternions over $ \mathbf{R} $. Let $ B $ be a subalgebra of the $ \mathbf{R} $-algebra $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{H}) $ such that: (i) for each $ f \in B $, the conjugate $ \overline{f} $ of $ f $ [defined by $ \overline{f}(x) = \overline{f(x)} $ for all $ x \in X $] belongs to $ B $; (ii) for each $ \varepsilon > 0 $, each pair of distinct points $ x, y $ of $ X $ and each pair of elements $ u, v $ of $ \mathbf{H} $, there exists $ f \in B $ such that $ \|f(x) - u\| \leq \varepsilon $ and $ \|f(y) - v\| \leq \varepsilon $. Show that $ B $ is dense in $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{H}) $ [use a)].

c) Extend the results of Exercise 13 to the algebra $ \mathcal{C}(X; \mathbf{H}) $, and show in particular that every closed ideal of this algebra is two-sided [argue as in Exercise 19, using b)].

§ 21) a) Let $ X $ be a totally disconnected compact space, and let $ A $ be a topological ring. Endow the ring $ \mathcal{C}(X; A) $ with the topology of uniform convergence, which is compatible with the ring structure. Let $ a $ be a left ideal of $ \mathcal{C}(X; A) $ and, for each $ x \in X $, let $ \mathfrak{J}(x) $ be the closure in $ A $ of the set of $ f(x) $ for $ f \in a $; $ \mathfrak{J}(x) $ is a closed left ideal of $ A $. Show that $ \bar{a} $ is identical with the set of all $ f \in \mathcal{C}(X; A) $ such that $ f(x) \in \mathfrak{J}(x) $ for all $ x \in X $ (note that, for each $ x \in X $, the neighbourhoods of $ x $ which are both open and closed form a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $ x $).

b) Suppose in addition that $ A $ has a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $ o $ which are two-sided ideals. Let $ B $ be a subring of $ \mathcal{C}(X; A) $ which contains the constants and separates the points of $ X $. Show that $ B $ is dense in $ \mathcal{C}(X; A) $. [For each closed subset $ F $ of $ X $, each $ x \notin F $ and each neighbourhood $ U $ of $ o $ in $ A $, show that there exists $ f \in B $ such that $ f(x) = 1 $ and $ f(y) \in U $ for all $ y \in F $.]
