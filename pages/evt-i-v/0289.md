COROLLARY. — *Suppose that the hilbertian space E is the direct sum of the orthogonal subspaces M and N. The canonical isomorphism g from $ S(M) \otimes S(N) $ onto $ S(E) $ (A, III, § 6, No. 6) extends uniquely to a hilbertian space isomorphism h from $ \hat{S}(M) \hat{\otimes}_2 \hat{S}(N) $ onto $ \hat{S}(E) $.

Let $ (e_i)_{i \in I} $ (resp. $ (f_j)_{j \in J} $) be an orthonormal basis of the hilbertian space M (resp. N) and let $ M_0 $ (resp. $ N_0 $) be the vector subspace of E generated by the vectors $ e_i $ (resp. $ f_j $). Put $ E_0 = M_0 + N_0 $ and let $ g_0 $ be the canonical isomorphism from $ S(M_0) \otimes S(N_0) $ onto $ S(E_0) $. Put

$$
z_\alpha = \prod_{i \in I} e_i^{\alpha_i}/(\alpha_i!)^{1/2}, \quad t_\beta = \prod_{j \in J} f_j^{\beta_j}/(\beta_j!)^{1/2}
$$

for $ \alpha \in \mathbf{N}^{(I)} $ and $ \beta \in \mathbf{N}^{(J)} $. By prop. 4, we have thus defined the orthonormal bases $ (z_\alpha)_{\alpha \in \mathbf{N}^{(I)}} $ for $ \hat{S}(M) $, $ (t_\beta)_{\beta \in \mathbf{N}^{(J)}} $ for $ \hat{S}(N) $ and $ (z_\alpha t_\beta)_{\alpha \in \mathbf{N}^{(I)}, \beta \in \mathbf{N}^{(J)}} $ for $ \hat{S}(E) $. Since we have $ z_\alpha t_\beta = g_0(z_\alpha \otimes t_\beta) $, and since the elements $ z_\alpha \otimes t_\beta $ form an orthonormal basis of $ \hat{S}(M) \hat{\otimes}_2 \hat{S}(N) $ (V, p. 29, cor. 1), we see that $ g_0 $ extends to a hilbertian space isomorphism $ h : \hat{S}(M) \hat{\otimes}_2 \hat{S}(N) \to \hat{S}(E) $. By the construction, we have

$$
h(x_1 ... x_m \otimes y_1 ... y_n) = x_1 ... x_m y_1 ... y_n
$$

for all vectors $ x_1, ..., x_m $ in $ M_0 $ and $ y_1, ..., y_n $ in $ N_0 $. By continuity, the same relation also holds for the vectors $ x_1, ..., x_n $ in M and the vectors $ y_1, ..., y_n $ in N; in other words, $ h $ extends $ g $. The uniqueness of $ h $ is clear.
