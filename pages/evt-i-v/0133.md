Spaces of continuous linear mappings

In this chapter, all the vector spaces under consideration are vector spaces over a field $ \mathbf{K} $, which may be $ \mathbf{R} $ or $ \mathbf{C} $.

We recall (II, p. 2) that a semi-normed space is a vector space E endowed with a semi-norm $ p $ and with the topology defined by $ p $. Let $ r $ be a real number $ > 0 $. The set of all $ x \in E $ such that $ p(x) \leq r $ is called the ball (closed) of radius $ r $ of E (or of $ p $). When $ r = 1 $, this ball is also called the unit ball.

§ 1. BORNOLGY IN A TOPOLOGICAL VECTOR SPACE

1. Bornologies

Definition 1. — *A bornology on a set* $ E $ *is a subset* $ \mathcal{B} $ *of the set of all subsets of* $ E $ *satisfying the following conditions* (cf. GT, X, § 1.2, Remark 2).
(B1) *Every subset of a set of* $ \mathcal{B} $ *belongs to* $ \mathcal{B} $.
(B2) *Every finite union of a set of* $ \mathcal{B} $ *belongs to* $ \mathcal{B} $.

*We say that* $ \mathcal{B} $ *is covering if every element of* $ E $ *is contained in a set which belongs to* $ \mathcal{B} $, *or, which is the same, if* $ \mathcal{B} $ *is a cover of* $ E $.

*Example.* — Let E be a metric space; the set of all bounded subsets of E (GT, IX, § 2, No. 2) is a covering bornology on E. Let G be the group of isometries of E; the set of all subsets M of G such that for every $ x \in E $, the set $ M.x $ is a bounded subset of E, is a covering bornology on G.

If $ \mathcal{B} $ is a bornology on a set E, a subset $ \mathcal{B}_1 $ of $ \mathcal{B} $ is said to be a *base* of $ \mathcal{B} $ if every set of $ \mathcal{B} $ is contained in a set of $ \mathcal{B}_1 $.

The intersection of a family of bornologies on E is a bornology; consequently for every subset $ \mathfrak{S} $ of $ \mathfrak{P}(E) $, there exists a smallest bornology containing $ \mathfrak{S} $; this bornology is said to be *generated by* $ \mathfrak{S} $ and admits as a base the set of finite unions of sets of $ \mathfrak{S} $.
