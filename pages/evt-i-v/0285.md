Let $E_1, ..., E_n$ be prehilbertian spaces ($n \geq 2$). We define the tensor product $E_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_n$ (also denoted by $\bigotimes_{i=1}^n E_i$) by induction, by
$$
E_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_n = (E_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_{n-1}) \otimes_2 E_n.
$$
Hence, by the definition of scalar product, we have
$$(6)$$
$$
\langle x_1 \otimes ... \otimes x_n | y_1 \otimes ... \otimes y_n \rangle = \prod_{i=1}^n \langle x_i | y_i \rangle,
$$
and in particular $^1$
$$(7)$$
$$
\|x_1 \otimes ... \otimes x_n\|_2 = \|x_1\| \cdots \|x_n\|,
$$
for $x_i, y_i$ in $E_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq n$). If the $E_i$ are Hausdorff, then so is $E_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_n$.

Let $F_1, ..., F_n$ be prehilbertian spaces and $u_i \in \mathcal{L}(E_i; F_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$. By induction on $n$, prop. 2 implies that $u_1 \otimes ... \otimes u_n$ is a continuous linear mapping from $E_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_n$ into $F_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 F_n$ and that
$$(8)$$
$$
\|u_1 \otimes ... \otimes u_n\| = \|u_1\| \cdots \|u_n\|.
$$

Let $\sigma \in S_n$ be a permutation of the set $\{1, 2, ..., n\}$. Because of (6), the linear mapping $p_\sigma$ from $E_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_n$ onto $E_{\sigma^{-1}(1)} \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}$ characterized by
$$(9)$$
$$
p_\sigma(x_1 \otimes ... \otimes x_n) = x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)} \otimes ... \otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}
$$
is a prehilbertian space isomorphism ("commutativity of tensor product").

Similarly, consider a partition of $\{1, 2, ..., n\}$ into $m$ consecutive intervals $I_1, ..., I_n$ with $I_k = [a_k, a_{k+1} - 1]$ for $1 \leq k \leq m$. Put
$$
F_k = \bigotimes_{i=a_k}^{a_{k+1}-1} E_i \quad (1 \leq k \leq m).
$$
$^1$ Here again we put $\|z\|_2 = \langle z|z \rangle^{1/2}$ for $z$ in $E_1 \otimes_2 ... \otimes_2 E_n$.
