The inequality (35) follows from prop. 6 by induction on $n$; the inequality (36) can be deduced by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in $\Lambda^n(E)$ and the formula (26) of V, p. 33.

Suppose that the sequence $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ is orthogonal. Then

$$
\| x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n \|^2 = \det(\langle x_i | x_j \rangle) = \prod_{i=1}^n \| x_i \|^2
$$

since $\langle x_i | x_j \rangle = 0$ for $i \neq j$.

Now suppose that the vectors $x_1, ..., x_n$ are not null and do not form an orthogonal sequence. Since $\| x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n \|$ depends symmetrically on the vectors $x_1, ..., x_n$, we can assume that $x_1$ is not orthogonal to the subspace F of E generated by $x_2, ..., x_n$, and that F is not simply 0. We can decompose $x_1$ in the form $x'_1 + y$ with $y \neq 0$ in F and $x'_1$ orthogonal to F; then $\| x'_1 \| < \| x_1 \|$. But

$$
x_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge ... \wedge x_n = x'_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge ... \wedge x_n,
$$

and so

$$
\begin{align*}
\| x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n \| &\leq \| x'_1 \| \| x_2 \| ... \| x_n \| \\
&< \| x_1 \| \| x_2 \| ... \| x_n \|.
\end{align*}
$$

Suppose that the vectors $x_i$ and the vectors $y_i$ are not null. The equality in relation (36) is equivalent to the conjunction of the equalities

(37) $$ |\langle x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n | y_1 \wedge ... \wedge y_n \rangle| = \| x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n \| \cdot \| y_1 \wedge ... \wedge y_n \| $$

(38) $$ \| x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n \| = \| x_1 \| ... \| x_n \| , \quad \| y_1 \wedge ... \wedge y_n \| = \| y_1 \| ... \| y_n \| . $$

By the first part of the proof, the equalities (38) imply that each of the sequences $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ and $(y_1, ..., y_n)$ is orthogonal, which in turn implies that $x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n \neq 0$ and that $y_1 \wedge ... \wedge y_n \neq 0$. Under these conditions, relation (37) implies that there exists a scalar $\lambda \neq 0$ such that $y_1 \wedge ... \wedge y_n = \lambda x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n$ (V, p. 3, Remark 1); in other words, that $(x_1, ..., x_n)$ and $(y_1, ..., y_n)$ are bases of the same vector subspace of E (A, III, § 11, No. 13).
