**Proposition 5.** — *Let* $ (e_i)_{i \in I} $ *be an orthonormal basis of the hilbertian space* E. *Endow* I *with a totally ordered structure. Then the set of all elements* $ e_{i_1} \wedge ... \wedge e_{i_n} $ *for* $ i_1 < ... < i_n $ *is an orthonormal basis of* $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(E) $.

We know (A, III, § 7, No. 8) that the elements in question form a basis of the vector space $ \Lambda^n(E_0) $ where $ E_0 $ is the vector subspace of E generated by the vectors $ e_i $. Further, for $ i_1 < ... < i_n $, the matrix of scalar products $ \langle e_{i_k}|e_{i_l} \rangle $ is the unit matrix of order n; by (26), we have $ \|e_{i_1} \wedge ... \wedge e_{i_n}\| = 1 $. Finally, if $ (i_1, ..., i_n) $ and $ (j_1, ..., j_n) $ are two distinct, strictly increasing sequences of elements of I, then there exists an element $ j_\ell $ distinct from $ i_1, ..., i_n $ and so we have $ \langle e_{i_k}|e_{j_\ell} \rangle = 0 $ for $ 1 \leq k \leq n $, and by (26), $ \langle e_{i_1} \wedge ... \wedge e_{i_n}|e_{j_1} \wedge ... \wedge e_{j_n} \rangle = 0 $. In other words, the family of elements $ e_{i_1} \wedge ... \wedge e_{i_n} $, for $ i_1 < ... < i_n $, is orthonormal.

But $ E_0 $ is dense in E, and the mapping $ (x_1, ..., x_n) \mapsto x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n $ from $ E \times \cdots \times E $ into $ \Lambda^n(E) $ is continuous. Consequently, $ \Lambda^n(E_0) $ is dense in $ \Lambda^n(E) $, and proposition 5 follows.

**Corollary.** — *Suppose that the hilbertian space* E *is the direct sum of two orthogonal subspaces* M *and* N. *The canonical isomorphism* g *from* $ \Lambda(M) \otimes \Lambda(N) $ *onto* $ \Lambda(E) $ *(A, III, § 7, No. 7)* *extends in a unique way to a hilbertian space isomorphism from* $ \hat{\Lambda}(M) \hat{\otimes}_2 \hat{\Lambda}(N) $ *onto* $ \hat{\Lambda}(E) $.

The proof is analogous to that of the corollary of prop. 4 (V, p. 31).

Let E and F be two hilbertian spaces and $ u \in \mathcal{L}(E; F) $. We shall show, as in the case of symmetric powers $ \hat{S}^n(E) $ (V, p. 32) that the linear mapping $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(u) $ from $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(E) $ into $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(F) $ (A, III, § 7, No. 4) extends to a continuous linear mapping $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(u) $ from $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(E) $ into $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(F) $. We have the relations

$$
\hat{\Lambda}^n(1_E) = 1_{\hat{\Lambda}^n(E)} , \tag{27}
$$
$$
\hat{\Lambda}^n(v \circ u) = \hat{\Lambda}^n(v) \circ \hat{\Lambda}^n(u) \quad \text{if } v \text{ belongs to } \mathcal{L}(F; G) , \tag{28}
$$
$$
\|\hat{\Lambda}^n(u)\| \leq \|u\|^n . \tag{29}
$$

In general we do not have equality in formula (29) (TS, IV, § 6). Finally, we have an isomorphism $ \psi_n = \psi_{n,E} $ from $ \hat{\Lambda}^n(E) $ onto the subspace $ \overline{\mathbf{A}}'_n(E) $ of $ \hat{T}^n(E) $ defined by

$$
\psi_n(x_1 \wedge ... \wedge x_n) = \frac{1}{(n!)^{1/2}} \sum_{\sigma \in \mathfrak{S}_n} \varepsilon_\sigma x_{\sigma(1)} \otimes ... \otimes x_{\sigma(n)} . \tag{30}
