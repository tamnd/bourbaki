¶ 4) Let F and G be two vector spaces in separating duality, G (resp. F) being identified with the dual of F (resp. G) when the latter is assigned the topology σ(F, G) (resp. σ(G, F)). Let $ \mathcal{S} $ (resp. $ \mathcal{T} $) be a covering of F (resp. G) consisting of convex, balanced and bounded subsets for σ(F, G) (resp. σ(G, F)). Show that the following propositions are equivalent :
α) Every set M ∈ $ \mathcal{S} $ is precompact for the $ \mathcal{T} $-topology.
β) Every set N ∈ $ \mathcal{T} $ is precompact for the $ \mathcal{S} $-topology.
γ) On every set M ∈ $ \mathcal{S} $, the topology induced by the $ \mathcal{T} $-topology is identical with the topology induced by σ(F, G).
δ) On every set N ∈ $ \mathcal{T} $, the topology induced by the $ \mathcal{S} $-topology is identical with the topology induced by σ(G, F).
(Use prop. 5 of III, p. 17 to show that α) implies δ) and exerc. 1 of II, p. 74 to show that δ) implies β).)

5) Let E and F be two locally convex Hausdorff spaces, $ \mathcal{S} $ a family of subsets of E. III, p. 13, corollary) that every set of $ \mathcal{S} $ is bounded in E.

6) a) Let E be a locally convex Hausdorff space. For every ultrafilter $ \mathcal{U} $ on E, let $ \mathcal{U}' $ be the filter for which the convex envelopes of sets of $ \mathcal{U} $ form a base. Show that, in order that a point of E be a limit of $ \mathcal{U} $ for the weakened topology, it is necessary and sufficient that it is a limit point of $ \mathcal{U}' $ for the initial topology (use exerc. 11 of II, p. 84).
b) Let A be a convex subset of a vector space E and let $ \mathcal{T}_1', \mathcal{T}_2' $ be two locally convex Hausdorff topologies on E, and $ \mathcal{T}_1', \mathcal{T}_2' $ the corresponding weakened topologies. Show that if the topology induced on A by $ \mathcal{T}_1' $ is finer than the topology induced by $ \mathcal{T}_2' $, then the topology induced on A by $ \mathcal{T}_1' $ is finer than the topology induced on A by $ \mathcal{T}_2' $.

¶ 7) Let F be the direct sum space $ \mathbf{R}^{(\mathbf{N})} $, G the space $ \ell^1(\mathbf{N}) $ (I, p. 4, Example); F and G are put in duality by the bilinear form
$$
(x, y) \mapsto \sum_n \xi_n \eta_n
$$
for $ x = (\xi_n) \in F $ and $ y = (\eta_n) \in G $.
a) Show that, in F, every set K which is convex and compact for σ(F, G) is finite dimensional. (Assume the contrary; let $ (n_k) $ be a strictly increasing sequence of integers > 0, and $ (a_k) $ a sequence of points of K such that the components of $ a_k $ for indices > $ n_k $ are zero, but that of index $ n_k $ is ≠ 0. Show that there exists a sequence $ (t_k) $ of numbers > 0 such that $ \sum_k t_k < + \infty $ and that in the Banach space $ \mathcal{B}(\mathbf{N}) $ of all bounded sequences of real numbers, the point $ \sum_k t_k a_k $ has non-zero components for indices $ n_i $, for all i; deduce that the sequence of partial sums $ s_p = \sum_{k=1}^p t_k a_k $ cannot have a limit point in F for σ(F, G).)
