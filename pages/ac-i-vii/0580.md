Let $ \mathbf{A} $ be a Dedekind domain, $ \mathbf{K} $ its field of fractions, $ \mathbf{L} $ a finite algebraic extension of $ \mathbf{K} $ and $ \mathbf{B} $ the integral closure of $ \mathbf{A} $ in $ \mathbf{L} $, which is a Dedekind domain. Let $ \mathfrak{f} $ be an ideal of $ \mathbf{B} $. For there to exist a ring $ \mathbf{C} $ such that $ \mathbf{A} \subset \mathbf{C} \subset \mathbf{B} $ and such that $ \mathfrak{f} $ is the conductor of $ \mathbf{B} $ in $ \mathbf{A} $ (Chapter V, § 1, no. 5 ), it is necessary and sufficient that, for every prime ideal $ \mathfrak{p}' $ of $ \mathbf{B} $ containing $ \mathfrak{f} $ such that the field $ \mathbf{B}/\mathfrak{p}' $ is isomorphic to $ \mathbf{A}/(\mathfrak{p}' \cap \mathbf{A}) $ (prime ideals of *residue degree* 1), the intersections off and the transporter $ \mathfrak{f}: \mathfrak{p}' $ of $ \mathfrak{p}' $ in $ \mathfrak{f} $ with $ \mathbf{A} $ be equal. (Note that, if there exists such a ring $ \mathbf{C} $, the conductor of $ \mathbf{B} $ in the ring $ \mathbf{C}_0 = \mathbf{A} + \mathfrak{f} $ is also equal to $ \mathfrak{f} $; the existence of $ \mathbf{C} $ is therefore equivalent to the fact that $ \mathbf{A} + \mathfrak{f} $ contains no ideal of $ \mathbf{B} $ distinct from $ \mathfrak{f} $ and containing $ \mathfrak{f} $. To prove that the latter condition is equivalent to that of the statement, reduce it to the case where $ \mathbf{A} $ is a discrete valuation ring.)

¶ 21. (a) Let $ \mathbf{A} $ be an integral domain, $ f, g $ two polynomials in $ \mathbf{A}[X] $ and $ h = fg $. Let $ a, b, c $ denote the ideals of $ \mathbf{A} $ generated respectively by the coefficients of $ f, g, h $. Show that, if $ \deg(g) = n $, then $ a^{n+1}b = a^n c $. (Let $ f(X) = \sum_{i=1}^m a_i X^i, g(X) = \sum_{j=1}^n b_j X^j $; for every increasing sequence
$$
\sigma = (i_k)_{1 \leq k \leq n+1}
$$
of integers $ \leq m $ and all $ j $, let
$$
u_{\sigma,j} = a_{i_1} a_{i_2} \cdots a_{i_j+1} b_j a_{i_j+2} \cdots a_{i_{n+1}};
$$
take on the set of $ u_{\sigma,j} $ a total ordering such that, for $ j < j' $, $ u_{\sigma,j} < u_{\tau,j'} $ for all $ \sigma, \tau $, and, for all $ j $, $ u_{\sigma,j} \leq u_{\tau,j} $ if and only if $ \sigma \leq \tau $ in the lexicographical ordering on $ [0, m]^{n+1} $. Then argue by induction on this totally ordered set.)
