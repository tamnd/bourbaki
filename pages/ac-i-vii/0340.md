Let $ \mathbf{A}[X, X^{-1}] $ denote the ring of fractions $ S^{-1}\mathbf{A}[X] $ of the polynomial ring $ \mathbf{A}[X] $ in one indeterminate, $ S $ being the multiplicative subset of $ \mathbf{A}[X] $ consisting of the powers $ X^n $ of $ X $ ($ n \geq 0 $); as $ X $ is not a divisor of 0 in $ \mathbf{A}[X] $ it is immediate that the $ X^i $ ($ i \in \mathbf{Z} $) form a basis over $ \mathbf{A} $ of the $ \mathbf{A} $-module $ \mathbf{A}[X, X^{-1}] $. For every element $ a \in \mathbf{A} $ with homogeneous components $ a_i $ ($ i \in \mathbf{Z} $), we write

$$
j_{\mathbf{A}}(a) = \sum_{i \in \mathbf{Z}} a_i X^i \in \mathbf{A}[X, X^{-1}]
$$

it is immediate that $ j_{\mathbf{A}} : \mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{A}[X, X^{-1}] $ is an injective ring homomorphism.

**Proposition 20.** *Let $ \mathbf{A} = \bigoplus_{i \in \mathbf{Z}} A_i $, be a graded ring and $ \mathbf{B} $ a (commutative) graded $ \mathbf{A} $-algebra. The set $ \mathbf{A}' $ of elements of $ \mathbf{B} $ integral over $ \mathbf{A} $ is a graded subalgebra of $ \mathbf{B} $.

If $ A_i = 0 $ for $ i < 0 $ and $ \mathbf{B} $ is a reduced ring, then $ A'_i = 0 $ for $ i < 0 $.*

The diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathbf{B} & & \mathbf{A} \\
& \rho & \\
& & \\
& & \\
\mathbf{A}[X, X^{-1}] \xrightarrow{\phi'} \mathbf{B}[X, X^{-1}] \\
\end{array}
$$

(where $ \rho $ is the homomorphism defining the $ \mathbf{A} $-algebra structure on $ \mathbf{B} $ and $ \rho' $ the homomorphism canonically derived from it) is commutative, as is immediately verified from the definition (5). Let $ x $ be an element of $ \mathbf{B} $ integral over $ \mathbf{A} $; then $ j_{\mathbf{B}}(x) $ is integral over $ \mathbf{A}[X, X^{-1}] $ (no. 1, Proposition 2) and it therefore follows from no. 5, Proposition 16 that there exists an integer $ m > 0 $ such that $ X^m j_{\mathbf{B}}(x) $ is an element of $ \mathbf{B}[X] $ integral over $ \mathbf{A}[X] $. We then deduce from no. 3, Proposition 12 that the coefficients of the polynomial $ X^m j_{\mathbf{B}}(x) $ are integral over $ \mathbf{A} $; as these coefficients are by definition the homogeneous components of $ x $, it is seen that these are integral over $ \mathbf{A} $, which proves that $ \mathbf{A}' $ is a graded subalgebra of $ \mathbf{B} $.

Suppose now that $ x \in A'_i $ where $ i < 0 $; the remark at the beginning of this no. shows that $ x $ satisfies an equation of the form (4) where $ a_k' \in A_{kt} $ for $ 1 \leq k \leq n $. If $ A_j = 0 $ for $ j < 0 $, then $ x^n = 0 $ and if $ \mathbf{B} $ is a reduced ring we conclude that $ x = 0 $ and hence $ A'_i = 0 $ for all $ i < 0 $ in this case.

Recall (Chapter II, § 2, no. 9) that, if $ \mathbf{A} = \bigoplus_{i \in \mathbf{Z}} \mathbf{A}_i $ is a graded ring and $ S $ is a multiplicative subset of $ \mathbf{A} $ consisting of *homogeneous* elements, a graded ring structure is defined on $ S^{-1}\mathbf{A} $ by taking the set $ (S^{-1}\mathbf{A})_i $ of homogeneous elements of degree $ i $ to be the set of elements of the form $ a/s $, where $ a \in \mathbf{A} $ and $ s \in S $ are homogeneous and such that $ \deg(a) - \deg(s) = i $
