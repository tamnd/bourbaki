### 4. GRADED PRIME IDEALS

Let $ A = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} A_i $ be a graded commutative ring with *positive* degrees and $ m $ the graded ideal $ \bigoplus_{i \geq 1} A_i $; we shall call the two graded ideals $ a = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} a_i, b = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} b_i $ of $ A $ *equivalent* if there exists an integer $ n_0 $ such that $ a_n = b_n $ for $ n \geq n_0 $ (clearly it is an equivalence relation). A graded ideal is called *essential* if it is not equivalent to $ m $.

**Proposition 4.** *Let $ p = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} p_i $ be a graded ideal of $ A $; for $ p $ to be prime, it is necessary and sufficient that, if $ x \in A, \ y \in A, $ satisfy $ x \notin p $ and $ y \notin p, $ then $ xy \notin p $.*

The condition is obviously necessary. Conversely, if it is fulfilled, then in the graded ring $ A/p = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} A_i/p_i $ the product of two homogeneous elements $ \neq 0 $ is $ \neq 0 $ and hence $ A/p $ is an integral domain (*Algebra*, Chapter II, § 11, no. 4, Proposition 7).

**Proposition 5.** *Let $ a = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} a_i $ be a graded ideal of $ A $ and $ n_0 $ an integer $ > 0 $. For there to exist a graded prime ideal $ p = \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} p_i $ such that $ p_{n_0} = a, $ for $ n \geq n_0, $ it is necessary and sufficient that, for pair of homogeneous elements $ x, y $ of degrees $ \geq n_0, $ the relation $ xy \in a $ implies '"$ x \in a $ or $ y \in a $". If there exists $ n \geq n_0 $ such that $ a_n \neq A_n, $ the prime ideal satisfying the above conditions is unique.*

The condition of the statement is obviously necessary. If $ a_n = A_n $ for all $ n \geq n_0, $ clearly every prime ideal containing $ m $ is a solution to the problem; there may therefore be several prime ideals which solve the problem; however, any two of these ideals are obviously equivalent. Suppose then that there exists a homogeneous element $ a \in A_d $ (with $ d \geq n_0 $) not belonging to $ a_d $. Let $ p $ be the set of $ x \in A $ such that $ ax \in a $. Clearly $ p $ is an ideal of $ A $; as the homogeneous components of $ ax $ are the products by $ a $ of those of $ x $ and $ a $ is a graded ideal, $ p $ is a graded ideal; moreover, $ 1 \notin p $ and hence $ p \neq A $. To prove that $ p $ is prime, it is sufficient to show that, if $ x \in A, \ y \in A, $ satisfy $ x \notin p $ and $ y \notin p, $ then $ xy \notin p $ (Proposition 4). Then $ ax \notin a_{m+d}, ay \notin a_{n+d}, $ whence by hypothesis

$$
a^2xy \notin a_{m+n+2d};
$$

we conclude that $ axy \notin a_{m+n+d} $ since $ xy \notin p $. Finally, if $ n \geq n_0 $ and $ x \in A_n, $ the conditions $ x \in a, $ and $ ax \in a_{n+d} $ are equivalent by hypothesis and hence $ p \cap A_n = a_n $ which completes the proof of the existence of the graded prime ideal $ p $ which solves the problem.
