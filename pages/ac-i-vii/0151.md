20. Let $ \mathcal{F} $ be a topologizing and idempotent set of left ideals of a ring $ A $.
(a) Suppose that every ideal of $ \mathcal{F} $ contains a *finitely* generated ideal belonging to $ \mathcal{F} $. Let $ M $ be an $ A $-module and $ (N_i)_{i \in I} $ a right directed family of submodules of $ M $, whose union is $ M $. Show that $ M_{\mathcal{F}} $ is the union of its sub-$ A_{\mathcal{F}} $-modules $ (N_i)_{\mathcal{F}} $. In particular, for every family $ (M_\lambda)_{\lambda \in L} $ of $ A $-modules, $ \left( \bigoplus_{\lambda \in L} M_\lambda \right)_{\mathcal{F}} $ is canonically isomorphic to $ \bigoplus_{\lambda \in L} (M_\lambda)_{\mathcal{F}} $.
(b) Suppose that condition (a) holds and also that, for every surjective $ A $-module homomorphism $ u : M \to N $, $ u_{\mathcal{F}} : M_{\mathcal{F}} \to N_{\mathcal{F}} $ is surjective. Then, the canonical mapping $ A_{\mathcal{F}} \otimes_A M \to M_{\mathcal{F}} $ defined by the external law on $ M_{\mathcal{F}} $ as an $ A_{\mathcal{F}} $-module is an $ A_{\mathcal{F}} $-module isomorphism (reduce it to the case where $ M $ is free); the ring $ A_{\mathcal{F}} $ is a flat left $ A $-module and the left $ A_{\mathcal{F}} $-modules coincide with the left $ A $-modules $ M $ such that the mapping $ j_M : M \to M_{\mathcal{F}} $ is bijective.
(c) Suppose that $ A $ is an infinite product $ \prod_{t \in I} A_t $, of rings; let e, denote the unit element of $ A $, and take $ \mathcal{F} $ to be the set of left ideals of $ A $ containing the two-sided ideal $ a = \bigoplus_{t \in I} A_t $, (the $ A_t $, being canonically identified with ideals of $ A $). Show that, for every left $ A $-module $ M $, $ M_{\mathcal{F}} $ is identified with the product $ \prod_{t \in I} (e_t M) $ and in particular $ A_{\mathcal{F}} = A $. Deduce that, for every surjective $ A $-homomorphism $ u : M \to N $, $ u_{\mathcal{F}} : M_{\mathcal{F}} \to N_{\mathcal{F}} $ is surjective, but give examples of $ A $-modules $ M $ such that $ j_M $ is not bijective.
(d) Suppose that condition (a) holds and also that $ A $ is commutative. Show that, if $ M $ is 9-negligible, $ M_{(\mathcal{F})} = 0 $.
