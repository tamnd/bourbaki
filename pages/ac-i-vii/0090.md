COROLLARY 2. *If M is a Noetherian* (resp. Artinian) *A*-module, *S^{-1}M* is a Noetherian (resp. Artinian) *S^{-1}A*-module. *In particular, if the ring A is Noetherian* (resp. Artinian), *so is the ring S^{-1}A*.

### 5. IDEALS IN A RING OF FRACTIONS

PROPOSITION 11. *Let A be a ring and S a multiplicative subset of A. For every ideal b' of S^{-1}A, let b = (i_A^S)^{-1}(b') be such that b' = S^{-1}b.*

(i) *Let f be the canonical homomorphism A \to A/b. The homomorphism from S^{-1}A to (f(S))^{-1}(A/b) canonically associated with f (no. 1, Proposition 2) is surjective and its kernel is b', which defines, by taking quotients, a canonical isomorphism of (S^{-1}A)/b' onto (f(S))^{-1}(A/b). Moreover, the canonical homomorphism from A/b to (f(S))^{-1}(A/b) is injective.*

(ii) *The mapping b' \mapsto b = (i_M^S)^{-1}(b'), restricted to the set of maximal (resp. prime) ideals of S^{-1}A, is an isomorphism (with respect to inclusion) of this set onto the set of ideals of A which are maximal among those which do not meet S (resp. the set of prime ideals of A not meeting S).*

(iii) *If q' is a prime ideal of S^{-1}A and q = (i_A^S)^{-1}(q'), there exists an isomorphism of the ring of fractions A, onto the ring (S^{-1}A)_{q'}, which maps a/b to (a/1)/(b/1), where a \in A, b \in A - q.*

(i) *(f(S))^{-1}(A/b) can be identified with S^{-1}(A/b) by means of the canonical isomorphism between these two modules (no. 2, Proposition 6). The exact sequence 0 \to b \to A \to A/b \to 0 then induces an exact sequence*

$$
0 \to S^{-1}b \to S^{-1}A \to S^{-1}(A/b) \to 0
$$

(no. 4, Theorem 1) whose existence proves the first assertion of (i), taking account of the fact that b' = S^{-1}b. Since b is saturated with respect S, the conditions a \in A, s \in S, as \in b imply a \in b; the homothety of ratio s on A/b is then injective, which proves the second assertion of (i).

If b' is maximal (resp. prime), clearly b' is maximal in \mathcal{F} (resp. prime) and conversely (by (i)).
