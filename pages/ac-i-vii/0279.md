§ 5

1. Let $A$ be a commutative ring and $3$ an ideal of $A$. An $A$-module $M$ is called absolutely *Hausdorff* with respect to $3$ if, for every finitely generated $A$-module $N$, the $A$-module $N \otimes_A M$ is Hausdorff with the $\mathfrak{g}$-adic topology; an absolutely Hausdorff $A$-module is ideally Hausdorff.

(a) For $M$ to be absolutely Hausdorff with respect to $\mathfrak{g}$, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every finitely generated $A$-module $N$ and every submodule $N'$ of $N$, $\operatorname{Im}(N' \otimes_A M)$ be closed in $N \otimes_A M$ with the $3$-adic topology.

(b) Let $B$ be a commutative $A$-algebra and $\mathfrak{L}$ an ideal of $B$ containing $\mathfrak{g}B$. If $M$ is an absolutely Hausdorff $B$-module with respect to $\mathfrak{L}$, $M$ is an absolutely Hausdorff $A$-module with respect to $3$.

2. Show that every $\mathbf{Z}$-module which is Hausdorff with the $p$-adic topology ($p$ a prime number) is ideally Hausdorff, but give an example of a finitely generated $\mathbf{Z}$-module which is Hausdorff but not absolutely Hausdorff with respect to $p$ (use Exercise 1 (a)).

3. With the notation of Exercise 11 of § 3, let $N$ be the submodule of $\hat{E}$ which is the closure in $\hat{E}$ of the submodule of $\hat{E}$ generated by the vectors $pe_{2n-1} - p^n e_{2n}$ ($n \geq 1$) and let $M = \hat{E}/N$. Show that under the $p$-adic topology the submodule $pM$ of $M$ is not closed in $M$ and deduce that $M$ is a $\mathbf{Z}_p$-module which is ideally Hausdorff but not absolutely Hausdorff with respect to $p$.

4. Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $3$ an ideal of $A$ and $S = 1 + 3$. Show that, if $M$ is an absolutely Hausdorff $A$-module with respect to $\mathfrak{g}$, then $S^{-1}M = M$, in other words, for all $a \in S$, $x \mapsto ax$ is a bijection of $M$ onto itself. (Show first that the hypothesis that $M$ is Hausdorff with the $3$-adic topology implies that $x \mapsto ax$ is injective; then prove that the submodule $aM$ of $M$ is dense in $M$ with the $8$-adic topology and use Exercise 1 (a).)

5. Take $A = \mathbf{Z}$ and $3 = p\mathbf{Z}$, where $p$ is prime.
(a) Show that, if $q$ is a prime number distinct from $p$, the $\mathbf{Z}$-module $\mathbf{Z}/q\mathbf{Z}$ satisfies property (ii) of Theorem 1, but not property (i).
(b) Show that the $\mathbf{Z}$-module $\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}$ satisfies property (iv) of Theorem 1 but not property (ii).

6. Let $A$ be a commutative Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{g}$ an ideal of $A$ and $M$ an $A$-module. Show that condition (v) of Theorem 1 is equivalent to the following:
(v') For every finitely generated $A$-module $N$ and every submodule $N'$ of $N$, the canonical mapping $(M \otimes_A N')^\wedge \to (M \otimes_A N)^\wedge$ (where the two sides are the Hausdorff completions with respect to the $\mathfrak{g}$-adic topologies on $M \otimes_A N'$ and $M \otimes_A N$ respectively) is injective. (To prove that (v) implies (v'), argue as in part (E) of the proof of Theorem 1. To see that (v') implies (v), consider $A$-modules $N$ annihilated by a power of 8.)
