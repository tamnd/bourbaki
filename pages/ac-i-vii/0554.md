Formula (9) shows that:

(14) $$ N \circ i = n . 1_{D(A)} $$

For all $a \in A$ (\S 1, no. 10, Proposition 14):

(15) $$ i(\operatorname{div}_A(a)) = \operatorname{div}_B(a). $$

We deduce (by means of (13)) that, for every fractional ideal $a$ of $A$, also:

(16) $$ i(\operatorname{div}_A(a)) = \operatorname{div}_B(aB). $$

For every element $b \in B$, we know (Chapter V, \S 1, no. 3, Corollary to Proposition 11) that $N_{L/K}(b) \in A$; moreover (Chapter VI, \S 8, no. 5, formula (9)):

(17) $$ v_p(N_{L/K}(b)) = \sum_{\mathfrak{p}/p} f_{\mathfrak{p}/p} v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b) $$

whence:

(18) $$ N(\operatorname{div}_B(b)) = \operatorname{div}_A(N_{L/K}(b)). $$

Formulae (15) and (18) show that, by taking quotients, the homomorphisms $N$ and $i$ define homomorphisms which will also be denoted, by an abuse of language, by:

$$ N : C(B) \to C(A), \quad i : C(A) \to C(B). $$

Note that the homomorphism $i : C(A) \to C(B)$ is not in general injective (\S 3, Exercise 7).

Recall that for every $B$-module $R$, $R_{[A]}$ denotes the $A$-module obtained from $R$ by restricting the scalars to $A$ (Algebra, Chapter 11, \S 1, no. 13).

PROPOSITION 18. (i) *For $R$ to be a pseudo-zero $B$-module, it is necessary and sufficient that the $A$-module $R_{[A]}$ be pseudo-zero.*

(ii) *For $R$ to be finitely generated torsion $B$-module, it is necessary and sufficient that $R_{[A]}$ be a finitely generated torsion $A$-module and then:*

(19) $$ \chi_A(R_{[A]}) = N(\chi_B(R)). $$

(iii) *For $R$ to be finitely generated $B$-module, it is necessary and sufficient that $R_{[A]}$ be a finitely generated $A$-module and then:*

(20) $$ c_A(R_{[A]}) = N(c_B(R)) + r_B(R)c_A(B) $$
(21) $$ r_A(R_{[A]}) = n . r_B(R) \quad \text{(recall that } n = r_A(B)). $$
