A\{X_1, \ldots, X_n\} and let $a \in A^n$; let us write $J_t(a) = e$. There exists a system $g = (g_1, \ldots, g_n)$ of restricted formal power series without constant term in $A\{X_1, \ldots, X_n\}$ such that
(i) $M_g(0) = I$, (unit matrix).
(ii) For all $x \in A^n$,
$$(19)$$
$$f(a + ex) = f(a) + M_t(a) \cdot eg(x).$$
(iii) Let $h = (h_1, \ldots, h_n)$ be the system of formal power series without constant term (not necessarily restricted) such that $g \circ h = 1_n$ (Proposition 5). For all $y \in m^{n \times n}$,
$$(20)$$
$$f(a + eh(y)) = f(a) + M_t(a) \cdot ey.$$
For every formal power series $f \in A[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]]$,
$$(21)$$
$$f(X + Y) = f(X) + M_f(X) \cdot Y + \sum_{1 \leq i < j \leq n} G_{ij}(X, Y)Y_i Y_j,$$
where the $G_{ij}$ are well determined formal power series in
$$A[[X_1, \ldots, X_n, Y_1, \ldots, Y_n]].$$
Iff is restricted, so are the elements of $M_f$ and the $G_{ij}$, for these formal power series are polynomials iff is a polynomial and it follows from their uniqueness that for every open ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ of $A$, denoting by $p_\mathfrak{J}: A \to A/\mathfrak{J}$ the canonical mapping, the image of $G_{ij}$ under $p_\mathfrak{J}$ is the coefficient of $Y_i Y_j$ in $p_\mathfrak{J}(F)$ where $F$ is the formal power series $f(X + Y)$ in $A[[X_1, \ldots, X_n, Y_1, \ldots, Y_n]]$; whence our assertion.

This being so, writing formula (21) for each seriesft $(1 \leq i \leq n)$, we obtain for all $x \in A^n$ (no. 2, Proposition 4),
$$(22)$$
$$f(a + ex) = f(a) + M_t(a) \cdot ex + e^2 r(x)$$
where $r = (r_1, \ldots, r_r)$ is a system of restricted formal power series each of which is of total order $\geq 2$. It follows from formulae (18) of Algebra, Chapter III, § 6, no. 5 that there exists a square matrix $M' \in \mathbf{M}_n(A)$ such that
$$(23)$$
$$M_t(a) \cdot M' = eI_n,$$
whence using this in (22)
$$(24)$$
$$f(a + ex) = f(a) + M_t(a) \cdot ex + M_t(a) M' \cdot er(x).$$

Writing $g = 1, + M' \cdot r$, we see that $g$ satisfies conditions (i) and (ii); then it is sufficient to replace $x$ by $h(y)$ to obtain (iii).
