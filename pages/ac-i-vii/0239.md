(Algebra, Chapter IV, § 5, no. 5). If
$$
\mathbf{h} = (h_1, \ldots, h_r) \in (\mathbf{A}[[X_1, \ldots, X_s]])^r
$$
is a third system without constant term, then
$$(10)$$
$$(f \circ g) \circ h = f \circ (g \circ h).$$

For every integer $m$,
$$(f^{(m)} \circ g^{(m)}) \circ h^{(m)} = f^{(m)} \circ (g^{(m)} \circ h^{(m)})$$
where $f^{(m)}, g^{(m)}, h^{(m)}$ denote the systems of polynomials consisting of terms of total degree $\leq m$ in the systems of formal power series $f, g, h$. But clearly the terms of total degree $\leq m$ in the series of $(f \circ g) \circ h$ (resp. $f \circ (g \circ h)$) are the same as in $(f^{(m)} \circ g^{(m)}) \circ h^{(m)}$ (resp. $f^{(m)} \circ (g^{(m)} \circ h^{(m)})$), whence our assertion.

For every system (8), we shall denote by $M_f$ or $M_f(\mathbf{X})$, the Jacobian matrix $(\partial f_i / \partial X_j)$ ($1 \leq i \leq p, 1 \leq j \leq q$) where $i$ is the index of the rows and $j$ that of the columns; for two systems (8) and (9), where $g$ is without constant term,
$$(11)$$
$$M_{f \circ g} = (M_f(g)). M_g$$
where $M_f(g)$ is the matrix whose elements are obtained by substituting $g$, for $\mathbf{X}$, ($1 \leq j \leq q$) in each series element of $M_f$; this formula is just a reformulation of formula (9) of Algebra, Chapter IV, § 5, no. 8. We shall denote by $M_f(0)$ the matrix of constant terms of the elements of $M_f$; then we deduce from (11) that
$$(12)$$
$$M_{f \circ g}(0) = M_f(0). M_g(0).$$

Given an integer $n > 0$, we shall write
$$(13)$$
$$\mathbf{1}_n = \mathbf{X} = (X_1, \ldots, X_n) \in (\mathbf{A}[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]])^n,$$
which will be considered as a matrix with a single column.

For every system $\mathbf{f} = (f_1, \ldots, f_n) \in (\mathbf{A}[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]])^n$, $M_f$ is a square matrix of order $n$; we shall denote by $J_f$ or $J_f(\mathbf{X})$ its determinant and by $J_f(0)$ the constant term of $J_f$, equal to $\det(M_f(0))$; if $\mathbf{g} = (g_1, \ldots, g_n)$ is a system without constant term in $(\mathbf{A}[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]])^n$, then, by (11) and (12),
$$(14)$$
$$J_{f \circ g} = J_f(g) \cdot J_g$$
$$(15)$$
$$J_{f \circ g}(0) = J_f(g) J_g(0).$$

PROPOSITION 5. *Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a commutative ring and $\mathbf{f} = (f_1, \ldots, f_n)$ a system without constant term of $n$ series in $\mathbf{A}[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]]$. Suppose that $J_f(0)$ is invertible in $\mathbf{A}$. Then there exists a system without constant term $\mathbf{g} = (g_1, \ldots, g_n)$ of $n$ series in $\mathbf{A}[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]]$ such that*
$$(16)$$
$$f \circ g = \mathbf{1}_n.$$
