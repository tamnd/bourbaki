(ii) We may replace $ M $ by $ x^{-1}M $ where $ x \neq 0 $ in $ A $ and assume that $ N(p) \subset M $, for all $ p \in P $. Let $ p_1, \ldots, p_h $ be the elements of $ P $ such that $ N(p) = M $, for $ p $ distinct from the $ p_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq h $); we write:

$$
Q = M \cap N(p_1) \cap \cdots \cap N(p_h).
$$

As each of the $ N(p_i) $ contain a free lattice with respect to $ A_{p_i} $, it contains *a fortiori* a lattice of $ V $ with respect to $ A $, hence $ Q $ contains a lattice of $ V $ with respect to $ A $ (no. 1, Proposition 3) and, as $ Q $ is contained in $ M $, $ Q $ is a lattice with respect to $ A $. To prove that $ Q_p = N(p) $ for all $ p \in P $, we shall use the following lemma:

**Lemma 1.** *Let $ p $ and $ p' $ be two prime ideals of $ A $ such that (0) is the only prime ideal of $ A $ contained in $ p \cap p' $. Then, for every sub-A-module $ E $ of $ V $, $ (E_p)_{p'} = K.E. $*

Let $ S $ be the multiplicative subset $ (A - p)(A - p') $ of $ A $; by Chapter II, $ \S 2 $, no. 3, Proposition 7, $ (E_p)_{p'} = S^{-1}E $. Further, $ A \subset S^{-1}A \subset K $; the prime ideals of $ S^{-1}A $ correspond to the prime ideals $ q $ of $ A $ such that $ q \cap S = \varnothing $ (Chapter II, $ \S 2 $, no. 5, Proposition 11) and by hypothesis (0) is the only prime ideal of $ A $ not meeting $ S $; hence $ S^{-1}A = K $ and $ S^{-1}E = K.E. $

We now return to the proof of (ii). If $ p \in P $ is distinct from the $ p_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq h $), Lemma 1 applied to $ N(p_i) $ gives $ ((N(p_i))_{p_i})_p = (K.N(p_i))_p = V $, since the $ p_i $ and $ p $ are of height 1. Then

$$
Q_p = M_p \cap (N(p_1))_p \cap \cdots \cap (N(p_h))_p = M_p = N(p)
$$

(Chapter II, $ \S 2 $, no. 4). On the other hand, if $ p $ is equal to $ p_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq h $), then $ (N(p_i))_{p_j} = V $ for $ i \neq j $ by the argument as above and $ (N(p_i))_{p_i} = N(p_i) $, whence

$$
Q_{p_i} = M_{p_i} \cap N(p_i) = N(p_i).
$$

We have therefore proved that $ Q_p = N(p) $ for all $ p \in P $. Then

$$
N = Q^{**} = \bigcap_{p \in P} Q_p
$$

is reflexive and satisfies the relations $ N_ = Q_ = N(p) $ for all $ p \in P $; the uniqueness property follows immediately from Theorem 2 of no. 2.

*Remark.* Let $ L $ be a free lattice of $ V $ with respect to $ A $. Since $ A $, is a principal ideal domain for $ p \in P $, $ N(p) $ is a free $ A $-module of the same rank as $ L $ and there exists $ u(p) \in \mathbf{GL}(V) $ such that $ u(p)(L,) = N; $; this condition moreover determines $ u(p) $ to within right multiplication by an element of $ \mathbf{GL}(L_p) $. The condition $ N(p) = L $, for almost all $ p \in P $ means that necessarily $ u(p) \in \mathbf{GL}(L_p) $
