b' is another element of $ \mathbf{A} $ such that $ f(b') = 0 $ and $ b' \equiv a \pmod{\mathfrak{m}} $, then $ e(b - b') = 0 $. In particular, $ b $ is unique if $ e $ is not a divisor of zero in $ \mathbf{A} $.

Let $ f(a) = e^2 c $ where $ c \in \mathfrak{m} $; formula (20) for $ n = 1 $ gives
$$
f(a + eh(y)) = e^2(c + y)
$$
and it is therefore sufficient to take $ y = -c $, $ b = a + eh(-c) $. Moreover if $ b = a + ex, b' = a + ex', x \in \mathfrak{m}, x' \in m, f(b) = f(b') = 0 $, we deduce from (19) that $ e^2(g(x) - g(x')) = 0 $. As $ g(\mathbf{X}) - g(\mathbf{Y}) = (\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{Y})u(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) $, where $ u $ is restricted and $ u(0, 0) = 1 $, $ g(x) - g(x') = (x - x')v $, where $ v \in \mathbf{A} $ is invertible, for, since $ m $ is closed, $ v - 1 = u(x, x') - 1 \in \mathfrak{m} $ and $ m $ is contained in the Jacobson radical of $ \mathbf{A} $; whence the relation $ e(b - b') = 0 $.

Remark. The corollary applies notably when $ e $ is invertible in $ \mathbf{A} $; we can then also deduce the existence of $ b $ from Hensel's Theorem, for the canonical image of $ f(\mathbf{X}) $ in $ (\mathbf{A}/\mathfrak{m})\{\mathbf{X}\} $ is of the form $ (\mathbf{X} - a)f_1(\mathbf{X}), \mathbf{X} - a $ and $ f_1(\mathbf{X}) $ being strongly relatively prime, for $ f_1(\alpha) = f'(\alpha) $ is the image of $ e $ (no. 1, Example).

Examples
(1) Let $ p $ be a prime number $ \neq 2 $ and $ n $ an integer whose class mod. $ p $ is a square $ \neq 0 $ in the prime field $ \mathbf{F}_p $. If $ \mathbf{Z}_p $ is the ring of $ p $-adic integers (\S 2, no. 12, Example 3), the application of Corollary 1 to the polynomial $ \mathbf{X}^2 - n $ shows that $ n $ is a square in $ \mathbf{Z}_p $; for example 7 is a square in $ \mathbf{Z}_3 $.
(2) Let $ \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{K}[[\mathbf{Y}]] $ be the ring of formal power series in one indeterminate with coefficients in a commutative field $ \mathbf{K} $; with the $ (\mathbf{Y}) $-adic topology, the ring $ \mathbf{A} $ is Hausdorff and complete (\S 2, no. 6, Corollary to Proposition 6) and the mapping $ f(\mathbf{Y}) \mapsto f(0) $ defines by passing to the quotient ring an isomorphism of $ \mathbf{A}/(\mathbf{Y}) $ onto the field $ \mathbf{K} $. By Corollary 1, if $ F(\mathbf{Y}, \mathbf{X}) $ is a polynomial in $ \mathbf{X} $ with coefficients in $ \mathbf{A} $ and $ a $ is a simple root of $ F(0, \mathbf{X}) $ in $ \mathbf{K} $, there exists a unique formal power series $ f(\mathbf{Y}) $ such that $ f(0) = a $ and $ F(\mathbf{Y}, f(\mathbf{Y})) = 0 $.

Corollary 2. Let $ \mathbf{A} $ be a ring and $ \mathfrak{m} $ an ideal of $ \mathbf{A} $ such that the ordered pair $ (\mathbf{A}, m) $ satisfies Hensel's conditions. Let $ r, n $ be integers such that $ 0 \leq r < n $ and $ \mathbf{f} = (f_{r+1}, \ldots, f_n) $ is a system of $ n - r $ elements of $ \mathbf{A}\{\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{X}_n\} $; let $ J^{(n-r)}_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}) $ denote the minor of $ M_{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{X}) $ consisting of the columns of index $ j $ such that $ r + 1 \leq j \leq n $. Let $ a \in \mathbf{A}^n $ be such that $ J^{(n-r)}_{\mathbf{f}}(a) $ is invertible in $ \mathbf{A} $ and $ \mathbf{f}(a) \equiv 0 \pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{(n-r)}} $. Then there exists a unique $ \mathbf{x} = (x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in \mathbf{A}^n $ such that $ x_k = a_k $ for $ 1 \leq k \leq r $, $ \mathbf{x} \equiv a \pmod{\mathfrak{m}^n} $ and $ \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}) = 0 $.

Substituting $ a $, for $ X_k $ for $ 1 \leq k \leq r $ in the $ f_i $ (no. 2, Remark 3 ), we see immediately that we may restrict our attention to the case where $ r = 0 $ to prove the corollary. Theorem 1 and Proposition 7 show then that $ \mathbf{f} $ defines a bijection of $ a + \mathfrak{m}^n $ onto $ \mathbf{f}(a) + \mathfrak{m}^n = \mathfrak{m}^n $; the corollary follows from the fact that $ 0 \in \mathfrak{m}^n $.
