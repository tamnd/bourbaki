(a) Show that A is completely integrally closed.
(b) For all $ x \in A $, let $ Z(x) $ be the set of zeros of $ x $ (which is a closed subset of $ \mathbf{C} $, which is discrete (and therefore countable) if $ x \neq 0 $). If $ a $ is an ideal of A distinct from A and (0), show that the $ Z(x) $ for $ x \in a $ form a filter base $ \mathcal{F}_a $. (If $ x $ and $ y $ are two integral functions with no zero in common, show, using the Mittag-Leffler Theorem, that it is possible to write $ 1/xy = u/x + v/y $, where $ u $ and $ v $ are in A, and deduce that $ Ax + Ay = A $). Conversely, for every filter $ \mathcal{F} $ on $ \mathbf{C} $ to which a closed discrete subset of $ \mathbf{C} $ belongs, the set $ a(\mathcal{F}) $ of $ x \in A $ for which $ Z(x) \in \mathcal{F} $ is an ideal of A. Show that $ \mathcal{F}_{a(s)} = \mathcal{F} $ for every filter $ \mathcal{F} $ to which a closed discrete subset of $ \mathbf{C} $ belongs and $ a(\mathcal{F}) \supseteq a $ for every ideal $ a $ of A distinct from A and (0).
(c) If $ p \neq (0) $ is a prime ideal of A, show that $ \mathcal{F}_p $ is an ultrafilter. (Restricting it to the case where all the sets of $ \mathcal{F}_p $ are infinite, show that, if $ M \in \mathcal{F}_p $ is the union of two infinite sets $ M', M'' $ with no point in common, one of these sets belongs to $ \mathcal{F}_p $.) Conversely, for every ultrafilter $ \mathcal{U} $ on $ \mathbf{C} $ to which a closed discrete subset of $ \mathbf{C} $ belongs, $ a(\mathcal{U}) $ is a maximal ideal of A; obtain the converse.
(d) Let $ \mathcal{U} $ be an ultrafilter on $ \mathbf{C} $, the image under the canonical injection $ \mathbf{Z} \to \mathbf{C} $ of a non-trivial ultrafilter on $ \mathbf{Z} $. Show that there exist non-maximal prime ideals $ p $ of A such that $ \mathcal{F}_p = \mathcal{U} $. (For all $ x \in a(\mathcal{U}) $ and all $ n \in \mathbf{Z} $, let $ \omega_x(n) $ be the order of $ x $ at the point $ n $ ($ \omega_x(n) = 0 $ if $ x(n) \neq 0 $); consider the set $ p $ of $ x \in a(\mathcal{U}) $ such that $ \lim_n \omega_x = +\infty $.)
(e) With the notation of (d), let m be the maximal ideal $ a(\mathcal{U}) $. Show that the domain $ A_{m'} $ is not completely integrally closed (consider the meromorphic function $ 1/(\sin \pi z) $). \*

¶ 13. Let A be a local integral domain and K its field of fractions. Consider the two following properties:
(i) A is Henselian (Chapter 111, § 4, Exercise 3).
(ii) For every finite extension L of K, the integral closure A' of A in L is a local ring.
Show that (i) implies (ii) and that the converse is true if A is integrally closed. (To see that (i) implies (ii), note that A' is the union of a right directed family of finite A-algebras and use the definition of a Henselian ring. To see that (ii) implies (i) if A is integrally closed, show that, if $ P \in A[X] $ is an irreducible monic polynomial and $ f : A \to k $ is the canonical homomorphism of A onto its residue field $ k $, $ f(P) $ is irreducible in $ K[X] $ and consider the field $ L = K[X]/(f) $.)
If condition (ii) is fulfilled, the integral closure A of A in any extension of K is a local ring. Consider the case of complete Hausdorff local rings; if A is a complete Hausdorff Noetherian local ring, every A-algebra contained in A' and which is a finitely generated A-module is a complete Hausdorff local ring.
