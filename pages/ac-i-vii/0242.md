**Proposition 7.** *Let $ A $ be a ring and $ m $ an ideal of $ A $ such that the ordered pair $ (A, m) $ satisfies Hensel's conditions. Let $ f = (f_1, \ldots, f_n) $ be a system without constant term of series in $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]] $ such that $ J_f(0) $ is invertible in $ A $. Then, for all $ x \in m^{x^n} $, $ f(x) \in m^{x^n} $ and $ x \mapsto f(x) $ is a bijection of $ m^{x^n} $ onto itself, the inverse bijection being $ x \mapsto g(x) $, where $ g $ is given by relation (16) in no. 4.*

The fact that $ f(x) \in m^{x^n} $ is obvious when theft are polynomials and follows in the general case from Proposition 6 and the fact that $ m $ is closed in $ A $. The other assertions of the proposition are then immediate consequences of (16), (17) and (18).

**Corollary.** *Let $ q $ be a closed ideal of $ A $ contained in $ m $. Then the relation $ x \equiv x' \pmod{q^{x^n}} $ is equivalent to $ f(x) \equiv f(x') \pmod{q^{x^n}} $.*

For every formal power series $ f \in A[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]] $,

f(X_1, \ldots, X_n) - f(Y_1, \ldots, Y_n) = \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - Y_i) h_i(X_1, \ldots, X_n, Y_1, \ldots, Y_n)

where the $ h_i $ belong to $ A[[X_1, \ldots, X_n, Y_1, \ldots, Y_n]] $ (Algebra, Chapter IV, § 5, no. 8, Proposition 9); it follows immediately that the relation $ x \equiv x' \pmod{q^{x^n}} $ implies $ f(x) \equiv f(x') \pmod{q^{x^n}} $.
