(3) If $ M $ is a finitely generated $ A $-module and $ T $ its torsion submodule, the dual $ M^* $ of $ M $ is the same as the dual of $ M/T $, since, for every linear form $ f $ on $ M $, the image $ f(T) $ is a torsion submodule of $ A $ and hence zero. As $ M/T $ is isomorphic to a lattice of a vector space over $ K $, it is seen that the dual of every finitely generated $ A $-module is reflexive.

Proposition 7. Let $ 0 \to M \to N \to Q \to 0 $ be an exact sequence of $ A $-modules. Suppose that $ N $ is finitely generated and torsion-free.
(i) If $ M $ is reflexive, then $ \operatorname{Ass}(Q) \subset P \cup \{\{0\}\} $ (in other words, every ideal associated with $ Q $, is, either $ (0) $, or of height 1 ).
(ii) Conversely, if $ N $ is reflexive and $ \operatorname{Ass}(Q) \subset P \cup \{\{0\}\} $, then $ M $ is reflexive.

As $ A $ is Noetherian, $ M $ is also finitely generated; if we write $ V = M_{(K)} $, $ W = N_{(K)} $, $ M $ (resp. $ N $) is canonically identified with a lattice of $ V $ (resp. $ W $) (no. 1, Proposition 1). Consider the two exact sequences:
$$
0 \to V/M \to W/M \to W/V \to 0 \\
0 \to Q \to W/M \to W/N \to 0.
$$
(i) We deduce (Chapter IV, § 1, no. 1, Proposition 3) that:
$$
\operatorname{Ass}(Q) \subset \operatorname{ASS}(W/M) \subset \operatorname{ASS}(V/M) \cup \operatorname{ASS}(W/V).
$$
If $ M $ is reflexive, then $ \operatorname{Ass}(V/M) \subset P $ (Theorem 2); on the other hand, clearly $ \operatorname{Ass}(W/V) $ is, either empty, or reduced to $ \{0\} $; whence (i).
(ii) Similarly:
$$
\operatorname{Ass}(V/M) \subset \operatorname{Ass}(W/M) \subset \operatorname{Ass}(Q) \cup \operatorname{Ass}(W/N).
$$
The hypotheses therefore imply that $ \operatorname{Ass}(V/M) \subset P \cup \{\{0\}\} $. But $ V/M $ is a torsion $ A $-module and hence $ \{0\} \notin \operatorname{Ass}(V/M) $; Theorem 2 then shows that $ M $ is reflexive.
