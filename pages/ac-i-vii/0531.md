(2) If $\mathrm{rg}_K(V) = n$, every *free* sub-A-module L of V has a basis containing at most n elements, every subset of V which is free over A being free over K; for L to be a lattice of V, it is necessary and sufficient that L have a basis of n elements (in other words, that $\mathrm{rg}_A(L) = n$).

(3) If A is *a principal ideal domain*, every lattice M of V is a finitely generated A-module (since A is Noetherian) which is torsion-free and hence a *free* A-module (*Algebra*, Chapter VII, § 4, no. 3, Corollary 2 to Theorem 2).

**Proposition 1.** *For a sub-A-module M of V to be a lattice of V, it is necessary and sufficient that KM = V and that M be contained in a finitely generated sub-A-module of V.*

The conditions are obviously necessary, for a free sub-A-module of V with the same rank as V generates V. Conversely, if KM = V, M contains a basis $(a_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ of V over K and hence it contains the free sub-A-module L, generated by the $a_i$; on the other hand, if $M \subset M_1$, where M, is a sub-A-module of V generated by a finite number of elements b, and $(e_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ is a basis of V over K, there exists an element s $\neq 0$ of A such that each of the b, is a linear combination of the $s^{-1}e_i$ with coefficients *in* A; if L, is the free sub-A-modules of V generated by the $s^{-1}e_i$, then $M \subset L_2$.

**Corollary.** *Suppose that A is Noetherian; for a sub-A-module M of V to be a lattice of V, it is necessary and sufficient that KM = V and M be finitely generated.*

*Remark* (1). Recall that for every sub-A-module M of V the canonical mapping $M \otimes_A K \to V$ is injective and has image KM (*Algebra*, Chapter II, § 7, no. 10, Proposition 26); to say that KM = V means therefore that this mapping is *bijective*.

**Proposition 2.** *Let M be a lattice of V and M, a sub-A-module of V. If there exist two elements x, y of $K^*$ such that $xM \subset M_1 \subset yM$, $M_1$ is a lattice of V; conversely, if $M_1$ is a lattice of V, there exist two non-zero elements a, b of A such that $aM \subset M, \subset b^{-1}M$.*

If $L_1, L_2$ are two free lattices of V such that $L, \subset M \subset L,$, the relations $xM \subset M_1 \subset yM$ imply $xL_1 \subset M, \subset yL_2$ and $xL_1$ and $yL_2$ are free lattices; conversely, if M, is a lattice and $(e_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ a basis of L, over A, the relation KM, = V implies the existence of $x = a/s \in K^*$ (where a and s are non-zero elements of A) such that $xe_i \in M,$ for all $i$, whence $xM \subset xL_2 \subset M_1$ and *a fortiori* $aM \subset M,$; exchanging the roles of M and M, it can be similarly shown that there exists $b \neq 0$ in A such that $bM_1 \subset M$.

**Proposition 3.** (i) *If $M_1$ and $M_2$ are lattices of V, so are $M_1 \cap M,$ and $M_1 + M_2$.

(ii) *If W is a vector subspace of V and M is a lattice of V, $M \cap W$ is a lattice of W.*
