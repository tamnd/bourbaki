6. RELATIVE INVARIANT OF TWO LATTICES

We keep the notation and hypotheses of nos. 2 to 5. Let V be a vector space of finite rank $ n $ over $ K $ and M a lattice of V with respect to A. Let W be the exterior power $ \bigwedge^n V $, which is a vector space of *rank 1* over $ K $ and let $ M_w $ denote the lattice of W generated by the image of $ M^n $ under the canonical mapping $ V^n \to \bigwedge^n V $ (no. 1, Proposition 3 (iii); note that $ M_w $ is not necessarily isomorphic to $ \bigwedge^n M $ (*Algebra*, Chapter III, § 5, Exercise 9)). If $ e $ is a basis of W over K, we may write $ M = a.e $, where a is a fractional ideal $ \neq 0 $ of A.

Let $ M' $ be another lattice of V and let us write $ M'_w = a'.e $, where $ a' $ is a fractional ideal $ \neq 0 $ of A; the divisor $ \operatorname{div}(a) - \operatorname{div}(a') $ *does not depend on the choice of basis* $ e $ of W, a and $ a' $ being multiplied by the same element of $ K^* $ when the basis is changed; we shall write $ \chi(M, M') = \operatorname{div}(a) - \operatorname{div}(a') $ and say that this divisor is the *relative invariant* of $ M' $ *with respect to* M. Clearly, if M, $ M', M'' $ are three lattices of V, then:

(3)
$$
\chi(M, M') + \chi(M', M'') + \chi(M'', M) = 0
$$
(4)
$$
\chi(M, M') + \chi(M', M) = 0.
$$

For all $ p \in P $, it follows immediately from the definitions that $ (M_w)_p = (M_p)_w $; moreover, since M, is then *afree* A,-module since A, is a principal ideal domain, a basis of M, over A, is a basis of V over K, hence $ (M_p)_w = \bigwedge^n (M_p) $ (Chapter II, § 2, no. 8) and the fractional ideal $ a_p = aA_p $ is a principal ideal domain. If we set $ a_p = p^{n_p}A_p $, $ a' = p^{n'_p}A_p $, then:
$$
\chi(M, M') = \sum_{p \in P} (n_p - n'_p).p,
$$
which may also be written as:
(5)
$$
\chi(M, M') = \sum_{p \in P} \chi(M_p, M'_p)
$$
identifying $ D(A_p) $ with the sub-Z-module of $ D(A) $ generated by $ p $.

PROPOSITION 13. *Let M be a lattice of V and u a K-automorphism of V. Then* :
(6)
$$
-\chi(M, u(M)) = \operatorname{div}(\det(u)).
$$
