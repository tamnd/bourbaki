If there exists an ideal $ p' $ of $ B $ such that $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(p') = p $, then $ \rho(p) \subset p' $, whence $ B_{\rho}(p) \subset p' $ and $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(B_{\rho}(p)) \subset \bar{\rho}^{-1}(p') \subset p $; as the converse inclusion is obvious, $ \bar{\sigma}^{-1}(B_{\rho}(p)) = p $. Conversely, suppose that $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(B_{\rho}(p)) = p $ and consider the multiplicative subset $ S = \rho(A - p) $ of $ B $; the hypothesis shows that
$$
S \cap B_{\rho}(p) = \varnothing;
$$
by Corollary 2 there exists a prime ideal $ p' $ of $ B $ containing $ B_{\rho}(p) $ and not meeting $ S $; then $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(p') $ contains $ p $ and cannot contain any element of $ A - p $ and hence is equal to $ p $.

**Corollary 4.** *Let $ A, B $ be two rings and $ \rho $ a homomorphism from $ A $ to $ B $.
(i) Suppose that there exists a $ B $-module $ E $ such that $ \rho_*(E) $ is a faithfully flat $ A $-module. Then, for every prime ideal $ p $ of $ A $, there exists a prime ideal $ p' $ of $ B $ such that $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(p') = p $.
(ii) Conversely, suppose that $ B $ is a flat $ A $-module. Then, if, for every prime ideal $ p $ of $ A $, there exists an ideal $ p' $ of $ B $ such that $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(p') = p $, $ B $ is a faithfully flat $ A $-module.*

(i) The hypothesis implies that, for every ideal $ a $ of $ A $, $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(B_{\rho}(a)) = a $ (Chapter I, § 3, no. 5, Proposition 8 (ii)) and it is sufficient to apply Corollary 3.
(ii) It is sufficient to show that, for every maximal ideal $ m $ of $ A $, there exists a maximal ideal $ m' $ of $ B $ such that $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(m') = m $ (Chapter I, § 2, no. 5, Proposition 9 (e)). Now there exists by hypothesis an ideal $ q $ of $ B $ such that $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(q) = m $; as $ q \neq B $, there exists a maximal ideal $ m' $ of $ B $ containing $ q $ and consequently $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(m') \supseteq m $; but, as $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(m') $ cannot contain 1, $ \bar{\rho}^{-1}(m') = m $.

**Corollary 5.** *Let $ A $ be a ring, $ S $ a multiplicative subset of $ A $ and $ B $ a ring such that $ i_A^S(A) \subset B \subset S^{-1}A $. Let $ q $ be a prime ideal of $ B $ such that the prime ideal $ p = (i_A^S)^{-1}(q) $ of $ A $ does not meet $ S $ and let $ p' $ be the prime ideal $ S^{-1}p $ of $ S^{-1}A $. Then $ p' \cap B = q $.*

Let $ S' = i_A^S(S) $; a canonical isomorphism has been defined from $ S'^{-1}B $ to $ S^{-1}A $ (no. 1, Corollary 4 to Proposition 2); we identify these two rings by means of this isomorphism. As $ q \cap S' = \varnothing $, $ q' = S'^{-1}q $ is the unique prime ideal of $ S^{-1}A = S'^{-1}B $ such that $ q' \cap B = (i_A^S)^{-1}(q') = q $ (Proposition 11 (ii)), whence $ (i_A^S)^{-1}(q') = p $; consequently $ q' = p' $ (Proposition 11 (ii)).

In the notation of Corollary 5, there are canonical isomorphisms of $ A $, and $ B_q $ onto $ (S^{-1}A)_q $. (Proposition 11 (iii)) and hence a *canonical isomorphism* $ A, \to B_q $.
