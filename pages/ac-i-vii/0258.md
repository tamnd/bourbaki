17. A linear topology $ \mathcal{T} $ on an A-module E is called minimal if it is Hausdorff and there exists no Hausdorff linear topology strictly coarser than $ \mathcal{T} $.

(a) For a Hausdorff linear topology $ \mathcal{T} $ on E to be minimal, it is necessary and sufficient that every filter base $ \mathcal{B} $ on E, consisting of affine linear varieties and having a single cluster point, be convergent to this point. (To see that the condition is necessary, observe that, when M runs through $ \mathcal{B} $ and V a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of 0 consisting of submodules, the $ M + V $ form a filter base with the same cluster points as $ \mathcal{B} $. To see that the condition is sufficient, note that a filter base consisting of open submodules, whose intersection is reduced to 0, is a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of 0 in a Hausdorff linear topology coarser than $ \mathcal{T} $.)

(b) For a discrete topology on an A-module E to be minimal, it is necessary and sufficient that E be Artinian.

(c) If $ \mathcal{T} $ is minimal, the topology induced by $ \mathcal{T} $ on any closed submodule of E is minimal.
