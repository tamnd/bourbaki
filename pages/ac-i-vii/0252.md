§ 1

1. Let $ A $ be a graded commutative ring of type $ \mathbf{Z} $ and $ (A_n) $ its graduation; we set $ A^{\geq} = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_n, \quad A^{\leq} = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_n $; these are graded subrings of $ A $.

(a) For every homogeneous element $ f $ of $ A $ of degree $ d $, the ring of fractions $ A_f $ (corresponding to the multiplicative subset consisting of the $ f^n $, where $ n \geq 0 $) has a canonical graded ring structure (Algebra, Chapter 11, § 11); we denote by $ A_{(f)} $ the subring of $ A $, consisting of elements of degree 0. Show that, if $ d > 0 $, then $ (A''_f) = A_{(f)} $, $ A_{(f)} $ is isomorphic to $ A^{(d)}/(f - 1)A^{(d)} $ and $ ((A_f)^{\geq})_{/f/1} $ is a graded ring isomorphic to $ A_{(f)} $.

(b) Let the polynomial ring $ B = A[X] = A \otimes_{\mathbf{Z}} \mathbf{Z}[X] $ be given the graduation the tensor product of those on $ A $ and $ \mathbf{Z}[X] $, a graduation compatible with the ring structure on $ B $. Show that, if $ d > 0 $, $ B_{(f)} $ is isomorphic to $ (A_n)^{\leq} $ and $ (A^{(d)})_f $ is a graded ring isomorphic to $ A_{(f)} \otimes_{\mathbf{Z}} \mathbf{Z}[X, X^{-1}] $.

(c) Let $ g $ be another homogeneous element of $ A $ of degree $ e $. Show that, if $ d > 0 $ and $ e > 0 $, $ A_{(f,g)} $ is isomorphic to $ (A_{(f)})_{g^d/f^e} $.

(d) Suppose $ d > 0 $ and $ A_n = \{0\} $ for $ n < 0 $. Show that, if $ A $ is Noetherian, so is $ A_{(f)} $ (use § 2, no. 10, Corollary 4 to Theorem 2). \*

2. Let $ A $ be a graded commutative ring of type $ \mathbf{Z} $ such that $ A_n = 0 $ for $ n < 0 $. For all $ n \geq 0 $, let $ A_{[n]} $ denote the graded ideal $ \bigoplus_{m \geq n} A_m $ of $ A $; the $ A $-algebra $ A^h = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_{[n]} $ is a ring graded by the $ A_{[n]} = A_n^h $; for all $ f \in A, (d > 0) $, let $ f^h $ denote the element of $ A^h $ whose component are zero except for that of degree $ d $, which is equal to $ f $.

(a) Show that, iff $ \in A_d $, where $ d > 0 $, the ring $ A_{(f^h)}^h $ is isomorphic to $ (A_f)^{\geq} $ (notation of Exercise 1).

(b) For $ A^h $ to be a finitely generated $ A $-algebra, it is necessary and sufficient that $ A $ be a finitely generated $ A_{(f)} $-algebra.
