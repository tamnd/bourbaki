19. (a) Let $\phi : A \to B$ be a ring homomorphism. Show that, for every ideal $b$ of $B$, $\overline{\phi(V(b))} = V(\phi^{-1}(b))$.

(b) Deduce from (a) that, for $^a\phi(\operatorname{Spec}(B))$ to be dense in $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, it is necessary and sufficient that $\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)$ be a nil ideal of $A$.

(c) Show that, if every $b \in B$ may be written as $b = h\phi(a)$, where $h$ is invertible in $B$ and $a \in A$, $^a\phi$ is a homeomorphism of $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ onto a subspace of $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$.

20. Give an example of a ring homomorphism $\phi : A \to B$ such that every maximal ideal of $A$ is of the form $\phi(m)$, where $m$ is a maximal ideal of $B$, but there exist prime ideals of $A$ which are not inverse images under $\phi$ of ideals of $B$ (take $B$ to be a field).

21. Let $(A_\alpha, \phi_{\beta\alpha})$ be a direct system of rings whose indexing set is directed, $A = \varprojlim A_\alpha$, and $\phi_\alpha : A_\alpha \to A$ the canonical homomorphism. If we write $X_\alpha = \operatorname{Spec}(A_\alpha)$, $(X_\alpha, {}^a\phi_{\beta\alpha})$ is an inverse system of topological spaces and, if $X = \operatorname{Spec}(A)$, the ${}^a\phi_\alpha : X \to X_\alpha$, form an inverse system of continuous mappings. Show that $u = \varprojlim {}^a\phi_\alpha$ is a homeomorphism of $X$ onto $\varprojlim X_\alpha$. (Prove first that, if, for all $a$, $p_\alpha$ is a prime ideal of $A_\alpha$, such that $p_\alpha = \phi_{\beta\alpha}^{-1}(p_\beta)$ for all $a \leq \beta$, the union $p$ of the $\phi_\alpha(p_\alpha)$ is a prime ideal of $A$; conversely, every prime ideal $p$ of $A$ is the union of the $\phi_\alpha(p_\alpha)$, where $p_\alpha = \phi_\alpha(p)$. Finally, if $f_\alpha \in A_\alpha$ and $f = \phi_\alpha(f_\alpha)$, note that the relation $p \in X$, is equivalent to
$$
u(p) \in \overline{\operatorname{pr}_\alpha((X_\alpha)_{f_\alpha})}.
$$

22. (a) Let $M$ be an $A$-module and $(N_i)$ a finite family of submodules of $M$. Show that $\operatorname{Supp}\left(M / \bigcap_i N_i\right) = \bigcup_i \operatorname{Supp}(M/N_i)$ (note that $M / \bigcap_i N_i$ is isomorphic to a submodule of the direct sum of the $M/N_i$).

(b) Let $p$ be a prime number; the support of the $\mathbf{Z}$-module $Z$ is distinct from the closure of the union of the supports of the $\mathbf{Z}$-modules $\mathbf{Z}/p^k\mathbf{Z}$ ($k \in \mathbf{N}$).

(c) Let $M$ be the direct sum of the $\mathbf{Z}$-modules $\mathbf{Z}/p^k\mathbf{Z}$ ($k \in \mathbf{N}$); show that $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$ is closed but distinct from $V(a)$, where $a$ is the annihilator of $M$.

(d) Let $N$ be the direct sum of the $\mathbf{Z}$-modules $\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}$ ($n \geq 1$); show that $\operatorname{Supp}(N)$ is not closed in $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})$.

(e) Deduce from (c) and (d) an example of an $A$-module $M$ such that, if $a$ is its annihilator, $\operatorname{Supp}(M)$ is not closed and its closure is distinct from $V(a)$.

(f) Show that the support of the $\mathbf{Z}$-module $\prod_{k=1}^m (\mathbf{Z}/p^k\mathbf{Z})$ is distinct from the closure of the union of the supports of the factor modules $\mathbf{Z}/p^k\mathbf{Z}$.
