COROLLARY 1. *Let* $ 0 \to M, \xrightarrow{u} M_{n-1} \to \cdots \to M_0 \to 0 $ *be an exact sequence of finitely generated A-modules.* Then

$$
\sum_{i=0}^n (-1)^i c(M_i) = 0.
$$

We argue by induction on $ n $, the case $ n = 2 $ being Proposition 16 (i). If $ M'_{n-1} = \operatorname{Coker}(u) $, there are the two exact sequences:

$$
\begin{align*}
0 &\to M, \to M_{n-1} \to M'_{n-1} \to 0 \\
0 &\to M'_{n-1} \to M_{n-2} \to \cdots \to M_0 \to 0.
\end{align*}
$$

The first shows that $ M'_{n-1} $ is finitely generated and the induction hypothesis gives

$$
(-1)^{n-1} c(M'_{n-1}) + \sum_{t=0}^{n-a} (-1)^t c(M_t) = 0
$$

and

$$
c(M'_{n-1}) = c(M_{n-1}) - c(M_n),
$$

whence the corollary.

An exact sequence

$$
0 \to L_n \to L_{n-1} \to \cdots \to L_0 \to E \to 0
$$

where the $ L_i $ ($ 0 \leq i \leq n $) are *finitely generated free* A-modules, is called a *finite free resolution* of the A-module E.

COROLLARY 2. *If a divisorial fractional ideal* $ a \neq 0 $ *of* $ \mathbf{A} $ *admits a finite free resolution, it is principal.*

In fact we apply Corollary 1 to a finite free resolution of $ a $:

$$
0 \to L_n \to L_{n-1} \to \cdots \to L_0 \to a \to 0.
$$

By virtue of Proposition 16 (v), $ c(a) = 0 $ and hence, by virtue of Proposition 16 (iv), $ \operatorname{div}(a) $ is principal; as $ a $ is assumed to be divisorial, it is principal (\S 1, no. 1).

COROLLARY 3. *If every divisorial ideal* $ \neq 0 $ *of* $ \mathbf{A} $ *admits a finite free resolution,* $ \mathbf{A} $ *is factorial.*

This is an immediate consequence of Corollary 2 and § 3, no. 1, Definition 1.

\* We shall see later that a regular local ring satisfies the hypothesis of Corollary 3 and therefore is *factorial.* \*
