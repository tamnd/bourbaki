Let $ a $ be an invertible ideal in $ \mathbf{A} $ and let $ b $ be any ideal $ \neq 0 $ in $ \mathbf{A} $; show that there exists an ideal $ c $ such that $ b + c = \mathbf{A} $ and $ ac $ is principal (Observe that, if $ (p_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n} $ is a finite family of distinct maximal ideals of $ \mathbf{A} $ and we write $ r_i = \prod_{j \neq i} p_j $, then $ a = \sum_i a r_i $ and $ \bigcap_i a r_i = a p_1 p_2 \ldots p_n $; using Chapter II, § 1, no. 2, Proposition 6, deduce the existence of an element $ x \in a $ such that $ x^{-1}a + b = \mathbf{A} $.) (b) Let $ K $ be a field and $ \mathbf{A} $ the subring of the ring of formal power series $ K[[T]] $ consisting of the series $ a, + T^n P(T) $, where $ a, \in K, P(T) \in K[[T]] $, for a given integer $ n $. Show that $ \mathbf{A} $ is a Noetherian local integral domain with a single prime ideal $ m \neq 0 $, but that the smallest cardinal of a system of generators of $ m $ is $ n $.

§ 3

1. For an integral domain to be factorial, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a mapping $ x \mapsto s(x) $ of $ \mathbf{A} - \{0\} $ to $ \mathbf{N} $ such that $ s(xy) = s(x) + s(y) $, the relation $ s(x) = 0 $ implies that $ x $ is invertible in $ \mathbf{A} $ and finally, for any two elements $ x, y $ of $ \mathbf{A} - \{0\} $, neither of which divides the other, there exist elements $ a, b, z, t $ of $ \mathbf{A} - \{0\} $ such that $ ax + by = zt, s(z) < s(x) $, $ t $ being prime to $ x $ and toy. (If this condition is fulfilled, show first that every non-zero element of $ \mathbf{A} $ is a product of extremal elements and then that, for every extremal element $ p, Ap $ is a prime ideal; conclude with the aid of Theorem 1 (d)).

2. (a) Let $ \mathbf{A} $ be an integral domain, the union of a right directed family $ (\mathbf{A}_\lambda) $ of subrings. Suppose that each of the $ \mathbf{A}_\lambda $ is factorial and that, if $ \lambda \leq \mu $, every extremal element of $ \mathbf{A}_\lambda $ is extremal in $ \mathbf{A}_\mu $. Show that $ \mathbf{A} $ is a factorial domain whose set of extremal elements is the union of the sets of extremal elements of each of the $ \mathbf{A}_\lambda $ (cf. § 2, Exercise 19 (e)).

(b) Deduce from (a) that for every factorial domain $ \mathbf{A} $ the polynomial domain $ \mathbf{A}[X_\lambda]_{\lambda \in L} $ in any family of indeterminates is factorial.

(c) Let $ \mathbf{A} $ be a factorial domain such that every domain of formal power series $ \mathbf{A}[[X_1, \ldots, X_n]] $ in a finite number of indeterminates is factorial. Show that every domain of formal power series $ \mathbf{A}[[X_\lambda]]_{\lambda \in L} $ in any family of indeterminates (Algebra, Chapter IV, § 5, Exercise 1) is factorial (same method).

3. (a) Let $ \mathbf{A} = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \mathbf{A}_n $ be a graded algebra with positive degrees over a field $ k $; suppose that $ \mathbf{A}_0 = k $ and that $ \mathbf{A} $ is a Krull domain. Show that for $ \mathbf{A} $ to be factorial, it is necessary and sufficient that every graded prime ideal $ p $ of $ \mathbf{A} $ of height 1 be of the form $ p = \mathbf{A}a $, where $ a $ is a homogeneous element (use Exercise 16 of § 1). Show that every homogeneous element $ \neq 0 $ of $ \mathbf{A} $ is a product of homogeneous extremal elements.
