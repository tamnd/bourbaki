Suppose then that for every index $j$, $\mathfrak{a} \cap m \cap p^{(j)}$ is not contained in any of the $p^{(k)}$ of index $k \neq j$; the induction hypothesis therefore implies the existence of a homogeneous element $y_j \in \mathfrak{a} \cap m \cap S_j$ belonging to none of the $S_j$ for $k \# j$; as the $y_j$ are all of degrees $\geq 1$, we may assume by replacing them by suitable powers (since the $p^{(k)}$ are prime) that $y_1$ and $\prod_{j=2}^n y_j$ are of the same degree. Then $z = y_1 + \prod_{j=2}^n y_j$ is homogeneous of degree $\geq 1$ and the same argument as in Chapter II, § 1, no. 1, Proposition 2 shows that $z$ solves the problem.

## § 2. GENERAL RESULTS ON FILTERED RINGS AND MODULES

### 1. FILTERED RINGS AND MODULES

DEFINITION 1. An increasing (resp. decreasing) sequence $(G_n)_{n \in \mathbf{Z}}$ of subgroups of a group $G$ is called an increasing (resp. decreasing) filtration on $G$.
A group with a filtration is called a filtered group.

If $(G_n)_{n \in \mathbf{Z}}$ is an increasing (resp. decreasing) filtration on a group $G$ and we write $G'_n = G_{-n}$, clearly $(G'_n)_{n \in \mathbf{Z}}$ is a decreasing (resp. increasing) filtration on $G$. We may therefore restrict our study to decreasing filtrations and hence forth when we speak of a filtration, we shall mean a decreasing filtration, unless otherwise stated.

Given a decreasing filtration $(G_n)_{n \in \mathbf{Z}}$ on a group $G$, clearly $\bigcap_{n \in \mathbf{Z}} G_n$ and $\bigcup_{n \in \mathbf{Z}} G_n$ are subgroups of $G$; the filtration is called separated if $\bigcup_{n \in \mathbf{Z}} G_n$ is reduced to the identity element and exhaustive if $\bigcup_{n \in \mathbf{Z}} G_n = G$.

DEFINITION 2. Given a ring $A$, a filtration $(A_n)_{n \in \mathbf{Z}}$ over the additive group $A$ is called compatible with the ring structure on $A$ if

(1)
$$
