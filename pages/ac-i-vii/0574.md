§ 2

1. (a) Show that, if $a$ is an ideal $\neq 0$ in a Dedekind domain $A$, the ring $A/a$ is a principal ideal ring (Algebra, Chapter VII, § 1, Exercise 5) (note that $A/a$ is semi-local and argue as in Proposition 1 of no. 2). Deduce again that every fractional ideal of $A$ is generated by at most two elements (cf. Exercise 11 (a)).

(b) In the polynomial ring $A = k[X, Y]$ over a field $k$, let $m$ be the ideal $AX + AY$. Show that for every integer $n$ the minimum number of generators of the ideal $m^n$ is $n + 1$.

2. (a) Let $a, b$ be two integral ideals of a Dedekind domain $A$; show that there exists an integral ideal $c$ which is prime to $b$ and such that $ac$ is principal (use Proposition 9 of § 1, no. 5).

(b) Let $a, b$ be two integral ideals of $A$; show that there exists $x \neq 0$ in the field of fractions of $A$ such that $xa$ is an integral ideal of $A$ which is prime to $b$ (same method). Deduce that the module $a/ab$ is isomorphic to $A/b$.

3. Let $A$ be a Dedekind domain, $K$ its field of fractions, $P$ the set of prime ideals $\neq 0$ of $A$ and for all $\varrho \in P$ let $v_\varrho$ be the corresponding essential valuation on $K$. For every sub-A-module $M$ of $K$ and all $\varrho \in P$ we write $v_\varrho(M) = \inf v_\varrho(x)$ (taken in $\overline{\mathbf{R}}$); if $M \neq 0$, $v_\varrho(M) < +\infty$ for all $\varrho \in P$ and $v_\varrho(M) \leq 0$ for almost all $\varrho \in P$. If $M, N$ are two sub-A-modules of $K$, show that the relation $M \subset N$ is equivalent to $v_\varrho(N) \leq v_\varrho(M)$ for all $\varrho \in P$ (use Proposition 9 of § 1, no. 5). Conversely, for every family $(v_\varrho)_{\varrho \in P}$ of elements equal to an integer or $-\infty$ and such that $v_\varrho \leq 0$ for almost all $\varrho \in P$, there exists a unique sub-A-module $M$ of $K$ such that $v_\varrho(M) = v_\varrho$ for all $\varrho \in P$.

4. Let $A$ be a Dedekind domain and $K$ its field of fractions. If $L$ is a sub-field of $K$ such that $A$ is integral over $A \cap L$, then $A \cap L$ is a Dedekind domain (show first that $A \cap L$ is a Krull domain and then that every prime ideal of $A \cap L$ is maximal, using Chapter V, § 2, no. 1, Theorem 1).

5. (a) Let $k$ be a field, $K$ the field $k(X, Y)$ of rational functions in two indeterminates over $k$ and $A$ the polynomial ring $K[Z]$ in one indeterminate, which is a principal ideal domain. Let $L$ be the subfield $k(Z, X + YZ)$ of $k(X, Y, Z)$; show that $A \cap L$ is not a Dedekind domain (prove that there are prime ideals which are not maximal and $\neq 0$ in this ring).
