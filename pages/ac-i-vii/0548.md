**Proposition 14.** *If M, M' are two lattices of V such that M' \subset M, M/M' is a finitely generated torsion A-module and:*

$$
\chi(M, M') = -\chi(M/M').
$$

Clearly M/M' \subset V/M' is a finitely generated torsion module; on the other hand, for all $ p \in P $, we know (*Algebra*, Chapter VII, § 4, no. 2, Theorem 1) that there exist bases $(e_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ of M, and $(e'_i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ of $M'_p$ such that $e'_i = \pi^{v_i} e_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$ and integers $v_i \geq 0$, $ \pi $ being a uniformizer of $A_p$. Therefore (in the notation introduced above) $n'_p - n_p = \sum_{i=1}^n v_i$; and on the other hand,

$$(M/M')_p = M_p/M'_p$$ is isomorphic to the torsion A,-module $ \bigoplus_{i=1}^n A_p/p^{v_i} A_p $ and hence its length is $ \sum_{i=1}^n v_i $, which proves the proposition, in view of (5) and Definition 4 of no. 5.

**Corollary.** *Let L,, L_2 be twofree A-modules & the same rank n and let f: L_1 \to L_2 be a homomorphism. Let U be the matrix off with respect to bases of L, and L_2. For Coker(f) to be a torsion A-module, it is necessary and sufficient that \det(U) \neq 0 and then:*

$$
\chi(\operatorname{Coker}(f)) = \operatorname{div}(\det(U)).
$$

L_1 and L_2 can be considered as lattices in $V_1 = L_1 \otimes_A K$ and $V_2 = L_2 \otimes_A K$ respectively, f extending to a K-homomorphism $f_{(K)}: V_1 \to V_2$. Then

$$(\operatorname{Coker}(f))_{(K)} = \operatorname{Coker}(f_{(K)})$$

and to say that $\operatorname{Coker}(f)$ is a torsion A-module means that $\operatorname{Coker}(f_{(K)}) = 0$; now, it amounts to the same to say that $f_{(K)}$ is surjective or that $\det(U) \neq 0$, whence the first assertion. On the other hand, we may write $f(L,) = u(L_2)$, where $u$ is an endomorphism of L, of determinant $\det(U)$; as

$$\operatorname{Coker}(f) = L_2/u(L_2),$$

formula (8) follows from (7) and (6).

*Example.* If $A = \mathbf{Z}$, the divisor group of $A$ is identified with the multiplicative group $\mathbf{Q}_+^*$ of rational numbers > 0. For every finite commutative group T, $\chi(T)$ is the *order* of T; the above corollary shows that the order of the group $\operatorname{Coker}(f)$ is equal to the *absolute value* of $\det(U)$ (cf. *Algebra*, Chapter VII, § 4, no. 7, Corollary 3 to Theorem 3).
