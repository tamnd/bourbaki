COROLLARY 4. Let $ A $ be a Zariski ring such that $ \hat{A} $ is an integral domain, $ L $ the field of fractions of $ \hat{A} $ and $ K \subset L $ the field of fractions of $ A $; then $ \hat{A} \cap K = A $.

Clearly $ A \subset \hat{A} \cap K $; on the other hand, if $ x \in \hat{A} \cap K $, then $ \hat{A}x \subset \hat{A} $ and hence, as $ \hat{A}x = \hat{A} \otimes_A (Ax) $ (no. 4, Theorem 3), $ \hat{A} \otimes_A ((Ax + A)/A) = 0 $. As $ \hat{A} $ is a faithfully flat $ A $-module (Proposition 9), we deduce that $ Ax \subset A $, whence $ x \in A $.

COROLLARY 5. Let $ A $ be a commutative Noetherian ring, $ E, F $ twofinitely generated $ A $-modules and $ u : E \to F $ an $ A $-homomorphism. For every maximal ideal $ m $ of $ A $, let $ A(m) $ (resp. $ E(m), F(m) $) denote the Hausdorff completion of $ A $ (resp. $ E, F $) with respect to the $ m $-adic topology and $ u(m) : E(m) \to F(m) $ the corresponding homomorphism to $ u $. For $ u $ to be injective (resp. surjective, bijective, zero), it is necessary and sufficient that $ u(m) $ be so for every maximal ideal $ m \notin A $.

We know that for $ u $ to be injective (resp. surjective, bijective, zero), it is necessary and sufficient that $ u_m : E_m \to F $, be so for every maximal ideal $ m $ of $ A $ (Chapter II, § 3, no. 3, Theorem 1). We now note that $ A $ is a Noetherian local ring (Chapter II, § 2, no. 4, Corollary 2 to Proposition 10) and hence a Zariski ring and there is a canonical $ A $-algebra isomorphism $ \hat{A}_m \to A(m) $ (§ 2, no. 13, Proposition 18). On the other hand (beginning of no. 4), there is a commutative diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
E_m \otimes_{A_m} A(m) & \longrightarrow & E \otimes_A A(m) \longrightarrow E(m) \\
u_m \otimes 1 \downarrow & & \downarrow u \otimes 1 \downarrow u(m) \\
F_m \otimes_{A_m} A(m) & \longrightarrow & F \otimes_A A(m) \longrightarrow F(m)
\end{array}
$$

where the horizontal arrows on the left arise from the associativity of the tensor product and the isomorphisms $ E_m \to E \otimes_A A_m, F_m \to F \otimes_A A_m $; as $ E $ and $ F $ are finitely generated $ A $-modules, it follows from no. 4, Theorem 3 that the rows of this diagram consist of isomorphisms; thus we are reduced to proving that $ u_m $ being injective (resp. surjective, bijective, zero) is equivalent to $ u_m \otimes 1 $ being so. But this follows from the fact that $ \hat{A}_m $ (and hence also $ A(m) $) is a faithfully flat $ A_m $-module by Proposition 9 (Chapter I, § 3, no. 1, Propositions 1 and 2).

PROPOSITION 10. Let $ A, B $ be two Zariski rings, $ \hat{A}, \hat{B} $ their completions, $ f : A \to B $ a continuous ring homomorphism and $ \hat{f} : \hat{A} \to \hat{B} $ the homomorphism obtained from $ f $ by passing to the completions; if $ \hat{f} $ is bijective, the $ A $-module $ B $ is faithfully flat.

As $ A $ and $ B $ are Hausdorff, the hypothesis that $ \hat{f} $ is bijective implies first that $ f $ is injective. Identifying (algebraically) $ A $ with $ f(A) $ by means off and $ \hat{A} $ with $ \hat{B} $ by means of $ \hat{f} $, we then obtain the inclusions $ A \subset B \subset \hat{A} = \mathbf{8} $; we know that $ \hat{A} $ is a faithfully flat $ A $-module and a faithfully flat $ B $-module (Proposition 9); we conclude that $ B $ is a faithfully flat $ A $-module (Chapter I, § 3, no. 4, Remark 2).
