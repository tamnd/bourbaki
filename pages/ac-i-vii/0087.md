Clearly the elements of $ u(S) $ are invertible in $ A' $, which shows that there exists a homomorphism $ h : S^{-1}A \to A' $ such that $ h \circ i^{S}_{A} = u $ (no. 1, Proposition 1). Then

$$
j \circ h \circ i^{A}_{S} = j \circ u = j \circ \rho'_\alpha \circ i^{A^\alpha}_{S^\alpha} = \rho_\alpha \circ i^{S}_{A^\alpha} = i^{S}_{A}
$$

for all $ \alpha \in I $ and consequently $ j \circ h $ is the identity automorphism of $ S^{-1}A $. On the other hand, for all $ \alpha \in I $,

$$
h \circ j \circ \rho'_\alpha \circ i^{S}_{A^\alpha} = h \circ \rho_\alpha \circ i^{S}_{A^\alpha} = h \circ i^{S}_{A} = u = \rho'_\alpha \circ i^{S}_{A^\alpha},
$$

COROLLARY. *Under the hypotheses of Proposition 9, let M be an A-module. We write $ f_{\beta \alpha} = i^{S_\beta, S_\alpha}_{M} $ for $ \alpha \leq \beta, f_\alpha = i^{S, S_\alpha}_{M} $ for all $ \alpha \in I $ and let $ f'_\alpha $ be the canonical mapping from $ S_\alpha^{-1}M $ to $ \lim \rightarrow S_\alpha^{-1}M $; then there exists an $ S^{-1}A $-isomorphism $ g $ of $ S^{-1}M $ onto $ \lim \rightarrow S_\alpha^{-1}M $ such that $ g \circ f_\alpha = f'_\alpha $ for all $ a \in I $.*

The corollary follows immediately from the definitions $ S_\alpha^{-1}M = M \otimes_A S_\alpha^{-1}A $ and $ S^{-1}M = M \otimes_A S^{-1}A $ and the fact that taking direct limits commutes with tensor products (*Algebra*, Chapter 11, § 6, no. 3, Proposition 7).

### 4. PROPERTIES OF MODULES OF FRACTIONS

*Throughout this no., A denotes a ring and S a multiplicative subset of A.*

Let $ (M_\alpha, \phi_{\beta \alpha}) $ be a direct system of A-modules; then $ (S^{-1}M_\alpha, S^{-1}\phi_{\beta \alpha}) $ is a direct system of $ S^{-1}A $-modules and the fact that taking direct limits commutes with tensor products (*Algebra*, Chapter II, § 6, no. 3, Proposition 7) allows us to define a canonical isomorphism

$$
\lim \rightarrow (S^{-1}M_\alpha) \to S^{-1} \lim \rightarrow M_\alpha.
$$
