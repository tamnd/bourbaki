## 4. MODULES OVER INTEGRALLY CLOSED NOETHERIAN DOMAINS

Throughout this paragraph, $ \mathbf{A} $ will be a *commutative integral domain* with field of fractions $ K $. Starting with no. 2, $ \mathbf{A} $ will be further assumed to be *Noetherian and integrally closed* (and hence a Krull domain ($ \S $ 1, no. 3, Corollary to Theorem 2)); then $ P(\mathbf{A}) $, $ D(\mathbf{A}) $ and $ C(\mathbf{A}) $ will respectively denote the set of prime ideals of $ \mathbf{A} $ of height 1 ($ \S $ 1, no. 6), the divisor group of $ \mathbf{A} $ ($ \S $ 1, no. 3) and the divisor class group of $ \mathbf{A} $ ($ \S $ 1, no. 10), these latter being written *additively*.

The general method of studying finitely generated modules over an integrally closed Noetherian domain $ \mathbf{A} $ consists of "localizing" the modules with respect to all the prime ideals $ p \in P(\mathbf{A}) $ of *height* 1 in $ \mathbf{A} $; as $ \mathbf{A} $, is then a *discrete valuation ring* ($ \S $ 1, no. 6, Theorem 4), the structure of finitely generated $ \mathbf{A}_p $-modules is well known (*Algebra*, Chapter VII, $ \S $ 4) and therefore gives information about the structure of finitely generated $ \mathbf{A} $-modules. In the particular case where $ \mathbf{A} $ is a Dedekind domain, we can arrive at as complete a theory as when $ \mathbf{A} $ is a principal ideal domain (no. 10).

### 1. LATTICES

Definition 1. *Let $ V $ be a finite-dimensional vector space over the field $ K $. A lattice of $ V $ with respect to $ \mathbf{A} $ (or simply a lattice of $ V $) is defined to be any sub-$ \mathbf{A} $-module $ M $ of $ V $ satisfying the following condition:*

There exist two free sub-$ \mathbf{A} $-modules $ L_1,\ L_2 $ of $ V $ such that $ L_1 \subset M \subset L_2 $ and $ \mathrm{rg}_{\mathbf{A}}(L_1) = \mathrm{rg}_K(V) $.

Examples
(1) If we take $ V = K $, the lattices of $ K $ are just *the fractional ideals* $ \neq (0) $ of $ K $ ($ \S $ 1, no. 1, Definition 1).
