Every element of $ 1 + (S^{-1}m) $ is of the form
$$
1 + (m/(1 + m')) = (1 + m + m')/(1 + m)
$$
where $ m \in m $ and $ m' \in m $; it is therefore invertible in $ S^{-1}A $, which proves that $ S^{-1}m $ is contained in the Jacobson radical of $ S^{-1}A $; as $ S^{-1}A $ is Noetherian (Chapter II, § 2, no. 4, Corollary 2 to Proposition 10), $ S^{-1}A $ is a Zariski ring with the $ (S^{-1}m) $-adic topology, which proves (i). Let us show (ii). For all $ n > 0 $,
$$
\bar{f}^{-1}((S^{-1}m)^nE) = \bar{f}'(S^{-1}(m^nE)) = m^nE:
$$
for clearly first of all $ f(m^nE) \subset S^{-1}m^nE $; conversely, let $ x $ be an element of $ \bar{f}^{-1}(S^{-1}m^nE) $; then there exist elements $ m', m'' $ of $ m $ and $ x'' \in m^nE $ such that $ (1 + m')((1 + m'')x - x'') = 0 $, whence $ (1 - m)x = x' $, where
$$
m = -(m' + m'' + m'm'') \in m
$$
and $ x' = (1 + m')x'' \in m^nE $; we conclude that
$$
x = (1 + m + \cdots + m^{n-1})x' + m^n x \in m^nE.
$$
This proves that $ f $ is a strict morphism. Moreover, the kernel of $ f $, which is the set of $ x \in E $ for which there exists some $ s \in S $ such that $ sx = 0 $, is identical with the kernel of the canonical mapping $ j : E \to \hat{E} $ (no. 2, Proposition 5). Then there is a topological isomorphism $ f_0 : j(E) \to f(E) $ such that $ f = f_0 \circ j $; as $ \hat{f} $ is a topological isomorphism, the problem reduces to verifying that $ f(E) $ is dense in $ S^{-1}E $. Now every element of $ S^{-1}E $ may be written as $ x/(1 - m) $, where $ m \in m $, and it is immediately verified that
$$
x/(1 - m) \equiv ((1 + m + \cdots + m^{n-1})x)/1 \pmod{S^{-1}m^nE}
$$
which completes the proof.
