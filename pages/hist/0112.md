$^{24}$ Lasker proceeds by induction on the maximal dimension $ h $ of the irreducible components of the variety $ V $ of zeros of the ideal $ a $ under consideration. In modern terms, he considers first the prime ideals $ p_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq r $) containing $ a $ which correspond to the irreducible components of maximal dimension $ h $ of $ V $. To each $ p_i $, he associates the saturated $ q_i $ of $ a $ relative to $ p_i $; he considers then the Noether quotient $ b_i = a : q_i $ of $ q_i $ in $ a $, takes in $ \sum_i b_i $ an element $ c $ not belonging to any of the $ p_i $ and shows on the one hand that $ a $ is the intersection of the $ q_i $ and of $ a + (c) = a' $, and on the other hand that the variety $ V' $ of zeros of $ a' $ only has irreducible components of dimension $ \leq h - 1 $, which enables him to conclude by induction.
