By his own testimony ([79], v. I, p. 218) it appears that Dedekind had begun to attack the problem of "ideal factors" of $ p $ in a field $ \mathbf{Q}(\xi) $, where $ P \in \mathbf{Z}[X] $ is the minimal polynomial of $ \xi $, in the following way (at least in the "unramified" case), that is to say when in $ \mathbf{F}_p[X] $ the polynomial $ \overline{P} $ corresponding to $ P $ does not have multiple roots): we write, in $ \mathbf{Z}[X] $,

$$
P = P_1 P_2 \ldots P_h + pG
$$

where the $ \overline{P}_i $ are irreducible and distinct in $ \mathbf{F}_p[X] $; it can be assumed that $ G $ is not divisible (in $ \mathbf{Z}[X] $) by any of the $ P_i $ and for all $ i $, we set $ W_i = \prod_{j \neq i} P_j $; then, if $ f \in \mathbf{Z}[X] $, we will say that $ f(\xi) $ contains the "ideal factor" $ p_i $ of $ p $ corresponding to $ P_i $ $ k $ times if we have

$$
fW_i^k \equiv 0 \pmod{p^k, P}
$$

and

$$
fW_i^{k+1} \not\equiv 0 \pmod{p^{k+1}, P}.
$$

The relationship with the method followed by Kummer for the "Kummer fields" is manifest here, and one can equally, in this way, rejoin easily the initial definition of Kummer for the cyclotomic fields (see for example the work of Zolotareff [343] who, at first independently of Dedekind, develops these ideas a little later).
