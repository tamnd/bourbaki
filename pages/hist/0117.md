The remark of Hensel reduces implicitly to embedding $k$ in the product of all its completions; that is what is done explicitly by Chevalley in 1936 with his theory of "idèles" [62 b], which perfects the previous analogous ideas of Prüfer and von Neumann (these latter limit themselves to embedding $k$ in the product of its $p$-adic completions).$^{32}$ Although this is a bit outside our remit, it is important to mention here that, thanks to an appropriate topology on the group of idèles, all the techniques of locally compact groups (including Haar measure) can thus be applied to Number Theory in a very effective way.

In a more general setting, the theorem of Krull [187 f], characterising an integrally closed ring as the intersection of valuation rings (which comes back to embedding the ring under consideration in the product of valuation rings) often eases the study of these rings, although the method is not really manageable except for the essential valuations of Krull rings. Examples (fairly elementary) are besides often found in Krull [187 i] of the method of "passing from the local to the global" consisting of proving a property of an integral domain $A$ by reducing it to verifying it for the "localisations" $A_p$ of $A$ at all its prime ideals;$^{33}$ more recently, Serre has noticed that this method is valid for arbitrary commutative rings $A$, that it is applicable also to $A$-modules and to their homomorphisms and that it is sufficient often to "localise" at the maximal ideals of $A$: a point of view that is tied closely to ideas on "spectra" and on sheaves defined on these spectra (see below, p. 115).
