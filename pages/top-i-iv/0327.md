¶ 21) a) Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a locally bounded Hausdorff topology on a division ring $K$, with respect to which $K$ is connected. Show that there are non-zero topologically nilpotent elements [Exercise 9 a)] in $K$. (If $U$ is a symmetric bounded neighbourhood of $o$, and $t$ is a non-zero element of $K$ such that $Ut + tU \subset U$, show that $K$ is the union of the sets $Ut^{-n}$, by using Proposition 6 of § 2, no. 2).

b) Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a locally bounded Hausdorff topology on $K$, with respect to which $K$ has non-zero topologically nilpotent elements. Show that there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $o$ such that, for each neighbourhood $V$ of $o$, there is an integer $n_0$ such that $U^n \subset V$ whenever $n \geq n_0$; this implies that all the elements of $U$ are topologically nilpotent and that every point of $K$ has a countable fundamental system of neighbourhoods with respect to $\mathcal{T}$. (Let $t \neq o$ be topologically nilpotent and let $W$ be a bounded neighbourhood of $o$; take $U$ to be a bounded neighbourhood of $o$ such that $UW \subset Wt$.) The set $T$ of topologically nilpotent elements of $K$ is therefore a neighbourhood of $o$ with respect to $\mathcal{T}$.

¶ 22) In a division ring $K$ endowed with a Hausdorff topology $\mathcal{T}$ compatible with the ring structure of $K$, a set $R$ containing $o$ is said to be retrobounded if $(\mathbf{C}R)^{-1}$ is bounded. $\mathcal{T}$ is said to be locally retrobounded if there is a fundamental system of retrobounded neighbourhoods of $o$ in $K$ with respect to $\mathcal{T}$.

a) A locally retrobounded topology $T$ is locally bounded, and is a minimal element in the set of all Hausdorff topologies compatible with the ring structure of $K$; also $\mathcal{T}$ is compatible with the division ring structure of $K$ [use Exercise 19 a)].

b) Suppose from now on, in this exercise, that $\mathcal{T}$ is a locally retrobounded topology on $K$. Show that, for every neighbourhood $V$ of $o$ in $K$, $x \to x^{-1}$ is uniformly continuous on $\mathbf{C}V$. Hence show that the completion $\hat{K}$ of $K$ is a topological division ring whose topology is locally retrobounded.

c) Every neighbourhood of $o$ in $K$ (with respect to $\mathcal{T}$) contains a neighbourhood $V$ of $o$ such that $E(V) = (\mathbf{C}V) \cap (\mathbf{C}V)^{-1}$ is not empty. Show that the three uniformities on $E(V)$, induced by the additive uniformity and the two multiplicative uniformities on $K$, coincide. Deduce that, if $K$ is complete, then $K^*$ is complete with respect to either multiplicative uniformity.
