2) Let $(\varepsilon_n)$ be a strictly decreasing sequence of finite numbers > 0 which tend to 0. For each $x \in \mathbf{R}$, let $r_n(x)$ be the multiple of $\varepsilon_n$ which is the approximation by defect to $x$ to within $\varepsilon_n$. Then the sequence $(r_n(x))$ is increasing for all $x \in \mathbf{R}$ if and only if $\varepsilon_n$ is an integer multiple of $\varepsilon_{n+1}$ for each $n$.

3) Let $a$ be an integer > 1. A real number $x$ is rational if and only if its expansion to base $a$, say $x = p_0 + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} u_n a^{-n}$, is periodic, that is to say if and only if there exist two integers $n_0$ and $r > 0$ such that $u_{n+r} = u_n$ for all $n \geq n_0$.

¶ 4) Let $(u_n)$ be a summable sequence of numbers > 0 in $\mathbf{R}$ which satisfies the following conditions : $u_{n+1} \leq u_n$ and $u_n \leq \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} u_{n+k}$ for all $n \geq 0$. Show that for every number $a$ such that $0 < a \leq s = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} u_n$, there exists a subset I of $\mathbf{N}$ such that $a = \sum_{n \in I} u_n$. These conditions are satisfied in particular if $u_{n+1} \leq u_n \leq 2u_{n+1}$ for all $n$. Consider the case of dyadic expansions.

¶ 5) For each real number $x \in ]0, 1]$, there is a unique infinite increasing sequence $(q_n)$ of integers > 0 such that $x = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 1/(q_1 q_2 \cdots q_n)$. The number $x$ is rational if and only if $q_{n+1} = q_n$ from a certain value of $n$ onwards.

¶ 6) For each real number $x > 1$, there is a unique infinite sequence $(t_n)$ of integers $\geq 1$ such that $t_{n+1} \geq t_n^2$ for all $n \geq 1$ and such that $x = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t_n} \right)$ (use Exercise 19 of § 7). The number $x$ is rational if and only if $t_{n+1} = t_n^2$ form a certain value of $n$ onwards. [To show that the condition is necessary, show that if we put $x_n = \prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t_k} \right)$ then the denominators of the fractions $\frac{x_n}{x_n - 1}$ (in their irreducible form) form a decreasing sequence].
