d) The field $ K = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}) $, considered as a vector space over $ \mathbf{Q} $, is the direct sum of the vector subspace $ G' $ generated by $ 1 $ and $ \sqrt[3]{2} $, and the vector subspace $ G'' $ generated by $ \sqrt[3]{4} $. Consider the topologies on $ G' $ and $ G'' $ induced by the topology of $ \mathbf{R} $; let $ \mathcal{T} $ be the topology on $ K $ which is the product of the topologies of $ G' $ and $ G'' $. Show that $ \mathcal{T} $ is compatible with the additive group structure of $ K $ and is finer than $ \mathcal{T}_0(K) $ ($ K $ being considered as an ordered subfield of $ \mathbf{R} $) but is not compatible with the ring structure of $ K $.

§ 3) a) If $ f $ is an isomorphism of the field $ \mathbf{R} $ onto a subfield of $ \mathbf{R} $ show that $ f $ must be the identity mapping of $ \mathbf{R} $ onto itself. [Remark that all rational numbers are invariant under $ f $ and that
$$
f(x^2) = (f(x))^2 \geqslant 0,
$$
so that $ f $ is increasing].

b) Let $ K_0 $ be the field $ \mathbf{R}(X) $ of rational fractions in one indeterminate over $ \mathbf{R} $, ordered by taking the polynomials $ > 0 $ to be those whose leading coefficient is $ > 0 $. Let $ K $ be the maximal ordered algebraic extension of $ K_0 $. Show that there is an order-preserving automorphism $ f $ of $ K $ such that $ f(\xi) = \xi $ for all $ \xi \in \mathbf{R} $ and $ f(X) = X^2(*) $.

§ 4

1) Show that every bounded half-open interval of $ \mathbf{R} $ is homeomorphic to every unbounded closed interval of $ \mathbf{R} $. If $ a < b $, then no two of the three intervals $ ]a, b[, [a, b[, [a, b] $ are homeomorphic.

2) Show that the mapping $ x \to x/(1 + |x|) $ is a homeomorphism of $ \mathbf{R} $ onto the open interval $ ]-1, 1[ $.

3) Let $ I $ denote the interval $ [0, 1] $ of $ \mathbf{R} $, and let $ f $ be a homeomorphism of $ I $ onto itself such that $ f(0) = 0 $ and $ f(1) = 1 $. Show that there is a continuous mapping $ g $ of $ I \times I $ into $ I $ such that: (i) for each $ x \in I $, $ g(x, 0) = x $ and $ g(x, 1) = f(x) $; (ii) for each $ y \in I $ the partial mapping $ x \to g(x, y) $ is a homeomorphism of $ I $ onto itself such that $ g(0, y) = 0 $ and $ g(1, y) = 1 $.

4) Show that the uniformity induced on $ \mathbf{R} $ by the unique uniformity of $ \overline{\mathbf{R}} $ is strictly coarser than the additive uniformity of $ \mathbf{R} $.

5) As the point $ (x, y) $ tends to $ (+\infty, -\infty) $ while remaining in $ \mathbf{R} \times \mathbf{R} $, shows that the set of cluster points of the function $ x + y $ is $ \overline{\mathbf{R}} $.

(*) Use the property of uniqueness, up to isomorphism, of the maximal ordered algebraic extension of an ordered field [B. L. van der Waerden, Moderne Algebra, vol. 1, 1st edition (Berlin, Springer, 1930) pp. 232-234].
