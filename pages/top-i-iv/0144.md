¶ 20) a) An open subset $U$ of a topological space $X$ is said to be *regular* if $U = \alpha(u)$ (§ 1, Exercise 3); equivalently, $U$ is regular if $U$ is the interior of a closed set. $X$ is said to be *semi-regular* (and its topology $\mathcal{T}$ is said to be *semi-regular*) if the regular open subsets of $X$ form a base of $\mathcal{T}$. Show that if $\mathcal{T}$ satisfies axiom $(O_{III})$ then $\mathcal{T}$ is semi-regular.

b) Prove that the intersection of two regular open sets is a regular open set. Deduce that the regular open sets with respect to $\mathcal{T}$ form a base of a topology $\mathcal{T}^*$ on $X$, which is coarser than $\mathcal{T}$ and semi-regular; $\mathcal{T}^*$ is said to be the semi-regular topology *associated* with $\mathcal{T}$. Show that for each subset $U$ of $X$ which is open in $\mathcal{T}$, the closure of $U$ with respect to $\mathcal{T}^*$ is the same as its closure in $\mathcal{T}$, and that if $X$ is accessible for $\mathcal{T}$ the isolated points of $X$ with respect to $\mathcal{T}^*$ are the same as the isolated points of $X$ with respect to $\mathcal{T}$. Show that $\mathcal{T}^*$ is Hausdorff if and only if $\mathcal{T}$ is [use Exercise 3 d) of § 1], and finally that if $Y$ is a regular space then the continuous mappings of $X$ into $Y$ are the same in both topologies $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}^*$.

c) Let $X$ be a semi-regular space and let $\mathcal{T}_0$ be its topology. Show that a topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $X$ is such that $\mathcal{T}^* = \mathcal{T}_0$ if and only if there is a set $\mathfrak{M}$ of *dense* subsets of $X$ (in the topology $\mathcal{T}_0$) such that every finite intersection of sets of $\mathfrak{M}$ belongs to $\mathfrak{M}$, and such that the topology $\mathcal{T}$ is generated by the union of $\mathfrak{M}$ and the set of subsets of $X$ which are open in the topology $\mathcal{T}_0$. [Consider the dense open subsets (in the topology $\mathcal{T}$) and notice that every open set in $\mathcal{T}$ is the intersection of a dense open set (in the topology $\mathcal{T}$) and an open set (in the topology $\mathcal{T}_0$).] Hence construct examples of non-regular Hausdorff topologies which are finer than a regular topology (*and even finer than a topology of a compact metrizable space*).

21) Let $X$ be a Hausdorff space with no isolated points, and let $\mathcal{T}_0$ be the topology of $X$. Let $\mathfrak{M}$ denote the set of complements of countable subsets $A$ of $X$ such that $\overline{A}$ has only a finite number of non-isolated points. Show that if $\mathcal{T}$ is the topology on $X$ generated by the union of $\mathfrak{M}$ and the set of subsets which are open in $\mathcal{T}_0$, then $\mathcal{T}^* = \mathcal{T}_0$ (Exercise 20) and that every sequence which converges with respect to $\mathcal{T}$ has only a finite number of distinct terms (which implies that, with respect to $\mathcal{T}$, no point of $X$ has a countable fundamental system of neighbourhoods, although $X$ itself may be countable and therefore every point of $X$ is then the intersection of a countable family of neighbourhoods of this point).
