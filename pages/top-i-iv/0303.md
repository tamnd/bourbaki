4) A paratopological group is a group $ G $ endowed with a topology satisfying axiom $ (\mathrm{GT}_1) (*) $.

a) A filter $ \mathcal{B} $ on a group $ G $ is the neighbourhood filter of $ e $ for a topology which makes $ G $ a paratopological group if and only if $ \mathcal{B} $ satisfies axioms $ (\mathrm{GV}_1) $ and $ (\mathrm{GV}_{\mathrm{III}}) $ and $ e \in V $ for all $ V \in \mathcal{B} $. The corresponding topology is then unique; it is Hausdorff if and only if the intersection of the sets $ V.V^{-1} $, where $ V $ runs through $ \mathcal{B} $, consists of the point $ e $ alone.

b) On the group $ \mathbf{Z} $ of integers, let $ V_n $ be the set consisting of $ 0 $ and all integers $ m \geq n $, for each $ n > 0 $. Show that the $ V_n $ form a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $ 0 $ for a non-Hausdorff topology on $ \mathbf{Z} $, and that with this topology $ \mathbf{Z} $ is a paratopological group in which $ \{0\} $ is closed.

c) A paratopological group $ G $ is a topological group if and only if, for each subset $ A $ of $ \overline{G} $, the intersection of the sets $ AV $ (or of the sets $ VA $) is the closure $ \overline{A} $ of $ A $ (as $ V $ runs through the neighbourhood filter of $ e $) (to see that the condition is sufficient, take $ A $ to be the complement of an open neighbourhood of $ e $).
