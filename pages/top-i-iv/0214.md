6) Let $X$ be a uniform space, $\mathcal{U}$ its uniformity, $\tilde{\mathcal{U}}$ the corresponding uniformity on $\mathfrak{P}(X)$ ($\S 1$, Exercise 5).
a) Show that the mapping $x \to \{x\}$ is an isomorphism of the uniform space $X$ onto a subspace of the uniform space $\mathfrak{P}(X)$.
b) Show that the uniformity induced by $\tilde{\mathcal{U}}$ on the set $\mathfrak{F}(X)$ of non-empty closed subsets of $X$ is Hausdorff, and that $\tilde{\mathcal{U}}$ is the inverse image under the mapping $M \to \overline{M}$ of the uniformity induced by $\tilde{\mathcal{U}}$ on $\mathfrak{F}(X)$.
c) Show that the mapping $(M, N) \to M \cup N$ of $\mathfrak{P}(X) \times \mathfrak{P}(X)$ into $\mathfrak{P}(X)$ is uniformly continuous.
d) Let $Y$ be another uniform space. If $f : X \to Y$ is a uniformly continuous mapping, show that the mapping $M \to f(M)$ of $\mathfrak{P}(X)$ into $\mathfrak{P}(Y)$ is uniformly continuous.
e) Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a compact subset of the set $\mathfrak{F}(X)$ of non-empty closed subsets of $X$, $\mathfrak{F}(X)$ carrying the topology induced by the topology $\mathcal{T}(\tilde{\mathcal{U}})$ induced by $\tilde{\mathcal{U}}$. Show that the set $\bigcup_{M \in \mathcal{B}} M$ is closed in $X$.

*1) Let $\mathcal{U}$ denote the additive uniformity on the real line $\mathbf{R}$, and let $\mathcal{U}'$ denote the inverse image of $\mathcal{U}$ under the mapping $x \to x^3$ of $\mathbf{R}$ onto itself. Show that $\mathcal{U}'$ is strictly finer than $\mathcal{U}$, but that the Cauchy filters are the same for both uniformities.*

2) a) Let $X$ be an infinite set and let $\mathfrak{F}$ be a non-trivial ultrafilter on $X$. Show that if $X$ carries the uniformity $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{F})$ ($\S 1$, Exercise 1) then the complement of $X$ in its completion $\hat{X}$ consists of a single point, and that the topological space $\hat{X}$ can be identified with the space associated with the ultrafilter $\mathfrak{F}$ (Chapter I, § 6, no. 5, Example).
*b) Deduce from a) an example of a uniformity on $\mathbf{R}$ which is finer than the additive uniformity and with respect to which $\mathbf{R}$ is not complete.*

*3) a) Let $\mathcal{U}$ denote the additive uniformity on the real line $\mathbf{R}$, let $\mathcal{U}_1$ denote the uniformity induced on $\mathbf{R}$ by that of the extended line $\overline{\mathbf{R}}$, and let $\mathcal{U}_2$ denote the uniformity induced on $\mathbf{R}$ by that of the one-point compactification $\tilde{\mathbf{R}} = P_1(\mathbf{R})$ of $\mathbf{R}$.
