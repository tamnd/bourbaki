3) Let F be a finite set of integers > 0, and let $ \beta_n $ be the number of finite sequences $ (x_i) $ of at most n terms, all of whose terms lie in F and such that $ \sum x_i = n $. Show that the formal power series $ \sum_{n=0}^m \beta_n X^n $ over $ \mathbf{Q} $ is the expansion of the rational fraction

$$
\frac{1}{1 - \sum_{a \in F} X^a}
$$

4) Let E be a vector space with an infinite basis over a field K of characteristic 2 ; let B be the exterior algebra $ \Lambda E $ on this space, which is a commutative ring. Give an example of a formal power series $ u \in B[[X]] $ such that $ u^2 = 0 $ but such that there exists no element $ \gamma \neq 0 $ of B for which $ \gamma u = 0 $.

5) Let $ E = A[X_1, ..., X_p] $, $ F = A[[X_1, ..., X_p]] $.
a) If D is a Z-derivation of E into F, we have $ \omega(Du) \geq \omega(u) - 1 $ for every non-zero $ u $ in E.
b) If $ D' $ is a Z-derivation of F, then $ \omega(D'v) \geq \omega(v') - 1 $ for every non-zero $ v $ in F.
c) If E is equipped with the topology induced by that of F, then D and $ D' $ are continuous.
d) Every Z-derivation of E into F extends in a unique fashion to a Z-derivation of F.

f) $ (D_1, ..., D_p) $ is a basis of the F-module $ \mathcal{D} $.

8) We denote by $ (1 + X)^T $ the element $ \exp(T \log (1 + X)) $ of $ \mathbf{Q}[[X, T]] $.
a) Show that $ (1 + X)^T = \sum_{n \geq 0} \frac{T(T-1)...(T-n+1)}{n!} X^n $. (The coefficient of $ X^n $ is a polynomial in T whose value is known for $ T = n \in N $.)

1) (Algebra of sequences of exponential type) Given two integers $ p, q \in N $, we denote by $ ((p, q)) $ the binomial coefficient $ \frac{(p+q)!}{p! q!} $. Let k be a commutative ring and B an associative commutative unital k-algebra. By a sequence of exponential type of elements of B we understand a sequence $ a = (a_p)_{p \in N} $ such that $ a_0 = 1 $, and $ a_p a_q = ((p, q)) a_{p+q} $ for any $ p, q \in N $. The set of all sequences of exponential type of B is written $ \mathcal{E}(B) $.
a) Verify that there is on $ \mathcal{E}(B) $ a unique k-algebra structure such that

$$
(a + b)_p = \sum_{r+s=p} a_r b_s,
$$
$$
(ab)_p = p! a_p b_p,
$$
$$
(\lambda a)_p = \lambda^p a_p,
$$
