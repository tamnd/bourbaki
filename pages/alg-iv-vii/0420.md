† 8) Let A be a principal ideal domain which is not a field.
a) Show that the product A-module A'' admits no countable system of generators. (If A is countable, consider Card(A^N); otherwise notice that, if for all x \in A we let v_x denote the element $(x^n)_{n \geq 0}$ of A'', then the $v_x$ form a free system).
b) Let $\pi$ be an irreducible element of A. Let S denote the submodule of A'' consisting of those sequences $z = (z_n)_{n \in \mathbf{N}}$ for which there exists a sequence $(k(n))_{n \in \mathbf{N}}$ of integers tending to $+\infty$ (depending on z), such that $z_n \in A \pi^{k(n)}$ for all n. Show that the $(A/\pi)$-vector space $S/\pi S$ has a countable basis.
c) Deduce from a) and b) that $A^N$ is not a free A-module. (Otherwise, S would be a free A-module with a countable basis; but $(x,$ $(x_n \pi^n)$ is an injective A-linear map from $A^N$ into S.)

9) Let A be a principal ideal domain and let M be the dual of the A-module $A^N$. Let $f : M \to A^N$ be the transpose map of the canonical injection $A^{(N)} \to A''$ (where $A^N$ is identified with the dual of $A^{(N)}$).
a) Let $\pi$ be an irreducible element of A and let $\varphi : A^N/A^{(N)} \to A$ be a linear form; show that if $u \in A^N/A^{(N)}$ is the class of an element $(u_n) \in A''$ such that $u_n \in (\pi^n)$ for all $n$, then $\varphi(u) = 0$. If A contains two non-associate irreducible elements, deduce that f is injective. (Using the above and Bezout's identity, show that the dual of $A^N/A^{(N)}$ is zero.)
b) Give A the topology defined in Ex. 5 of VII, p. 60. Show that, if f(M) is not contained in $A^{(N)}$, then A is complete. (Reduce the problem to showing that, if $\varphi : A^N \to A$ is a linear form such that $\varphi(e_n) \neq 0$ for every element $e_n$ of the canonical basis of $A^{(N)}$, then $\operatorname{Im}(\varphi)$ contains the sum of any series of elements of A which tends to 0 sufficiently quickly.)
c) If A contains two non-associate irreducible elements, then the canonical map from $A^{(N)}$ (resp. A') into its double dual is bijective (use a) and b)).

10) Let M be a nonzero Z-submodule of the ring of p-adic integers $\mathbf{Z}_p$ such that M is a pure Z-submodule of the Z-module $\mathbf{Z}_p$ (VII, p. 55, Ex. 7). Show that $M + p \mathbf{Z}_p = \mathbf{Z}_p$; deduce that M is indecomposable (observe that $\mathbf{Z}_p/p \mathbf{Z}_p$ is isomorphic to $\mathbf{Z}/p \mathbf{Z}$). Give an example of a decomposable Z-submodule of $\mathbf{Z}_p$ (observe that $\mathbf{Z}_p$ has the cardinality of the continuum).
