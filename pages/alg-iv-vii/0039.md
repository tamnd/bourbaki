Let $ f = (f_i)_{i \in I} \in (\mathbf{A}[[J]])^I $ and $ g = (g_j)_{j \in J} \in \mathbf{A}_{J, K} $. We denote by $ f(g) $ or $ f \circ g $ the element $ (f_i((g_j),_{\epsilon_J}))_{i \in I} $ of $ (\mathbf{A}[[K]])^I $. If $ f \in \mathbf{A}_{I, J} $, we have $ f \circ g \in \mathbf{A}_{,, K} $ because the mapping $ f \mapsto f((g_j)_{i, J}) $ of $ \mathbf{A}[[I]] $ into $ \mathbf{A}[[K]] $ is continuous.

Let $ f \in (\mathbf{A}[[J]])^I $, $ g \in \mathbf{A}_{J, K} $, $ h \in \mathbf{A}_{K, L} $. Then $ g \circ h \in \mathbf{A}_{J, L} $ and by (4), we have
$$
(f \circ g) \circ h = f \circ (g \circ h).
$$

4. Invertible formal power series

PROPOSITION 5. — *In the ring $ \mathbf{A}[[T]] $ of formal power series in one indeterminate the polynomial $ 1 - T $ is invertible, and we have* $ (1 - T)^{-1} = \sum_{n=0}^m T^n $.

For
$$
(1 - T) \left( \sum_{n=0}^\infty T^n \right) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty T^n - \sum_{n=0}^\infty T^{n+1} = 1.
$$

PROPOSITION 6. — *Let $ u \in \mathbf{A}[[I]] $; then for $ u $ to be invertible in $ \mathbf{A}[[T]] $ it is necessary and sufficient that its constant term should be invertible in $ \mathbf{A} $.*

Suppose that there exists $ v \in \mathbf{A}[[I]] $ such that $ uv = 1 $. Let $ a, \beta $ be the constant terms of $ u $ and $ v $, then $ \alpha \beta = 1 $, so $ a $ is invertible.

Conversely, suppose that the constant term $ a $ of $ u $ is invertible. Then there exists a formal power series $ t \in \mathbf{A}[[I]] $ such that $ u = \alpha (1 - t) $ and $ \omega(t) > 0 $. Now there is a ring homomorphism $ \varphi : \mathbf{A}[[T]] \to \mathbf{A}[[I]] $ such that $ \varphi(T) = t $, and $ 1 - T $ is invertible in $ \mathbf{A}[[T]] $ (Prop. 5); consequently $ 1 - t $ is invertible in $ \mathbf{A}[[I]] $, and hence so is $ u $.

*Remark.* — Let $ \mathcal{A} $ be the set of all formal power series with constant term 1. By Prop. 6, $ \mathcal{M} $ is a commutative group under multiplication; the multiplicative group of $ \mathbf{A}[[I]] $ is thus the direct product of $ \mathcal{M} $ and the multiplicative group of $ \mathbf{A} $. We shall equip $ \mathcal{A} $ with the topology induced from that of $ \mathbf{A}[[I]] $. For each $ \beta \in \mathbf{N}^{(1)} $ we have in IV, p. 26 defined the ideal $ a_\beta $ of $ \mathbf{A}[[I]] $; then $ 1 + a_\beta $ is a subgroup of $ \mathcal{M} $ and the family $ (1 + a_\beta) $ is a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of 1 in $ \mathcal{M} $. Since the multiplication in $ \mathcal{M} $ is continuous, we see that $ \mathcal{A} $ is a topological group (Gen. Top., III, p. 223); in other words, the *mapping* $ f \mapsto f^{-1} $ *is continuous in* $ \mathcal{M} $.

Let $ K $ be a commutative field and $ \mathfrak{D} $ the subring of the field of rational fractions $ K((X_i)_{i,,}) $ formed of rational fractions in which the element 0 of $ K^1 $ is substitutable. If $ f \in \mathfrak{D} $, we have $ f = \frac{u}{v} $, where $ u $ and $ v $ are polynomials such that the constant term of $ v $ is $ \neq 0 $, hence $ v $ is invertible in $ K[[I]] $. We can verify at once that the element $ uv^{-1} $ of $ K[[I]] $ depends only on $ f $; we say that the formal power series $ uv^{-1} $ is the *expansion at the origin of the rational fraction* $ \frac{u}{v} $. The mapping $ f \mapsto uv^{-1} $ is an injective homomorphism of $ \mathfrak{D} $ into $ K[[I]] $; we shall often identify $ \mathfrak{D} $ with its image under this mapping.
