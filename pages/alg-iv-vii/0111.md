relation R in T we associate an element $\lambda(R)$ of $D_k$ as follows: if $U_1, ..., U_l$ are the equivalence classes under R, numbered so that $\mathrm{Card}(U_i) \geq ... \geq \mathrm{Card}(U_l)$, put $\lambda(R) = (\alpha_1, ..., \alpha_k)$ with $\alpha_i = \mathrm{Card}(U_i)$ if $1 \leq i \leq l$ and $\alpha_i = 0$ if $1 < i \leq k$. Let $X_n$ ($n \in \mathbf{N}$) be indeterminates; for each $R \in \mathcal{H}(T)$ put $r(R) = \sum_{f} \prod_{i \in I} X_{f(i)}$, where the summation is extended to the set of mappings $f : T \to \mathbf{N}$ such that R is the equivalence relation associated with $f$. Put $s(R) = \sum_{S \neq R} r(S)$.

a) Prove that $r(R) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} r_i . M(\lambda(R))$ if $r_i$ of the equivalence classes under R arc of cardinal i for $1 \leq i \leq k$. (See formula (11) of IV, p. 65 for the definition of $M(\alpha)$.)

b) Prove the relation $S(R) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} p_i^{r_i}$ where the $r_i$ are as in a) and $p_m = \sum_{n \in \mathbf{N}} X_n^m$ for every integer $m \geq 1$.

c) Show that $r(R) = \sum_{S \neq R} \mu(R, S) s(S)$, where $\mu(R, S)$ is defined as in Exercise 14.

d) Hence deduce an expression of the $s_k$ as a polynomial in $p_1, ..., p_k$ with rational coefficients, where $s_k = \sum_{i_1 < ... < i_k} X_{i_1} ... X_{i_k}$.

e) For $a \in D_k$ put $S'(\alpha) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} s_{\alpha_i}$. Show that $S'(\alpha) = \sum_{\beta \in D_k} c'_{\alpha \beta} . M(\beta)$, where the integer $c'_{\alpha \beta}$ is defined as follows: suppose that $r_i$ (resp. $s_i$) of the components of $a$ (resp. $\beta$) are equal to i (for $1 \leq 1 \leq k$). Then $c'_{\alpha \beta}$ is the product $Nr,!... r_k!s_1!... s_k!/k!$, where N is the number of pairs of equivalence relations R, S on T such that $\lambda(R) = a, \lambda(S) = \beta$ and such that the intersection of an equivalence class under R with an equivalence class under S has at most one element. Deduce the symmetry relation $c'_{\alpha \beta} = c'_{\beta \alpha}$.

f) Show that there is an involution $a \mapsto \tilde{\alpha}$ in $D_k$, characterized by the fact that the relations $i \leq a,$ and $j \leq \tilde{\alpha}_i$ are equivalent, for i, j in the range from 1 to k. Show that $S'(\alpha) = S(\tilde{\alpha})$ and $c'_{\alpha \beta} = c_{\tilde{\alpha} \tilde{\beta}}$. Hence obtain the relation $c_{\alpha \beta} = c_{\tilde{\alpha} \tilde{\beta}}$ (with the notation of IV, p. 66).
