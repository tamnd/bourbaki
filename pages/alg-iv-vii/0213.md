COROLLARY. — Let L' be an extension of a field K, generated by two linearly disjoint subextensions K' and L. Suppose that L is p-radical of height $ \leq 1 $ over K; *then* L' is p-radical of height $ \leq 1 $ over K'. Moreover, for a family of elements of L to be p-free over K (resp. a p-basis of $ L $ over K) it is necessary and sufficient that it should be p-free over K' (resp. a p-basis of L' over K').

We have $ L'^p \subset K \subset K' $ and $ L' = K'(L) $, whence $ L'^p = K'^p(L'^p) \subset K' $. In other words, L' is a p-radical extension of height $ \leq 1 $ of K'. The other assertions of the corollary follow at once from Prop. 1 and from V, p. 14, Prop. 5.

Remark. — Let K be a field, L a p-radical extension of height $ \leq 1 $ of K and $ (x_i)_i $, a family of elements of L. Let A be the polynomial algebra $ K[(X_i)_{i \in I}] $, a the ideal of A generated by the polynomials $ X_i^p - x_i^p $ and $ \varphi : A \to L $ the K-algebra homomorphism such that $ \varphi(X_i) = x_i $ for each $ i \in I $. The family $ (x_i)_i $, is p-free if and only if the kernel of $ \varphi $ is equal to a. For $ K[(X_i)]/a $ may be identified with the algebra $ \bigotimes_{i \in I} K[X_i]/(X_i^p - x_i^p) $.

Let L be a p-radical extension of height $ \leq 1 $ of the field K. A subset S of $ L $ is said to be p-free (resp. a p-basis) if the family defined by the identity mapping of S onto itself is p-free (resp. a p-basis). For a family $ (x_i)_i $, of elements of $ L $ to be p-free (resp. a p-basis) it is necessary and sufficient that the mapping $ i \mapsto x_i $ should be a bijection of I on a p-free subset (resp. a p-basis) of L. By Prop. 1, every subset of a p-free set is p-free; conversely, if S is a subset of L all of whose finite subsets are p-free, then S is p-free. Finally, a p-basis of L over K is a p-free subset B such that $ L = K(B) $.

PROPOSITION 2. — Let K be a field, $ L $ a p-radical extension of height $ \leq 1 $ of K and S a subset of L. For S to be p-free it is necessary and sufficient that $ K(T) \neq K(S) $ for every subset $ T \neq S $ of S.

Suppose first that S is p-free and let $ T \neq S $ be a subset of S. Denote by A the set of families with finite support $ a = (\alpha_s)_{s \in S} $ of integers between 0 and $ p-1 $; let $ A' $ be the subset of A consisting of the families $ a = (\alpha_s)_{s \in S} $ such that $ a_s = 0 $ for all s in $ S - T $. We also put $ u_a = \prod_{s \in S} s^{\alpha_s} $ for $ a \in A $. Then (V, p. 98, Prop. 1) the family $ (u_a)_{a \in A'} $ is a basis of $ K(S) $ over K and the subfamily $ (u_a)_{a \in A'} $ is a basis of $ K(T) $ over K. Since $ A' \neq A $, we have $ K(T) \neq K(S) $.

Suppose now that S is not p-free. Then there exists an integer $ n \geq 1 $ and a sequence of elements $ x_1, ..., x_n $ of S such that $ (x_1, ..., x_{n-1}) $ is p-free but $ (x_1, ..., x_n) $ is not. We have $ [K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1}):K] = p^{n-1} $ and $ [K(x_1, ..., x_n):K] < p^n $. Since $ [K(x_1, ..., x_n):K] $ is a multiple of $ [K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1}):K] $, we thus have

$$
[K(x_1, ..., x_n):K] = [K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1}):K].
$$

whence $ x_n \in K(x_1, ..., x_{n-1}) $. This shows that $ K(S - \{x_n\}) = K(S) $.
