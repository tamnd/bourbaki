THEOREM 1. — Let us put $ E = A[\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{X}_n] $ and $ S = A[\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{X}_n]^{\text{sym}} $.

a) The $ A $-algebra $ S $ of symmetric polynomials is generated by $ s_1, \ldots, s_n $.

b) The elements $ s_1, \ldots, s_n $ of $ E $ are algebraically independent over $ A $ (IV, p. 4).

c) The family of monomials $ X^\nu = X_1^{\nu(1)} \ldots X_n^{\nu(n)} $ such that $ 0 \leq \nu(i) < i $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq n $ is a basis of the $ S $-module $ E $. In particular, $ E $ is a free $ S $-module of rank $ n! $.

We shall prove the theorem by induction on $ n $, the case $ n = 0 $ being trivial. Let us write $ B = A[X,] $ and denote by $ s_k' $ the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $ k $ in $ \mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{X}_{-1} $; we thus have $ B[\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, X,_,] = A[\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, X,] $. If we replace $ n $ by $ n-1 $ and $ A $ by $ B $ in the statement of Th. 1, we can formulate the induction hypothesis as follows:

(A) The $ B $-algebra $ S' $ of polynomials $ f \in A[\mathbf{X}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{X}_n] $ invariant under all permutations of $ X,, \ldots, X,_{-1} $ is generated by $ s_1', \ldots, s_{n-1}' $.

(B) The elements $ s_1', \ldots, s_{n-1}' $, of $ E $ are algebraically independent over $ B $.

(C) The family of monomials $ X_1^{\nu(1)} \ldots X_{n-1}^{\nu(n-1)} $ such that $ 0 \leq \nu(i) < i $ for $ 1 \leq i \leq n-1 $ is a basis of the $ S' $-module $ E $.

We have the obvious relation

(5)
$$
s_k = s_k' + s_{k-1}' \mathbf{X}_n \quad (1 \leq k \leq n-1),
$$
from which we obtain by induction on $ k $

(6)
$$
s_k' = (-1)^k \mathbf{X}_n^k + \sum_{i=1}^k (-1)^{k-i} s_i \mathbf{X}_n^{k-i} \quad (1 \leq k \leq n-1).
$$

We have $ S \subset S' $, hence $ s_1, \ldots, s_n $ belong to $ S' $; by (A) and formula (6) the B-algebra $ S' $ is therefore generated by $ s_1, \ldots, s_{n-1}' $.

By (B) there exists an endomorphism $ \mu $ of the B-algebra $ S' $ such that

(7)
$$
u(s_k') = (-1)^k \mathbf{X}_n^k + \sum_{i=1}^k (-1)^{k-i} s_i' \mathbf{X}_n^{k-i} \quad (1 \leq k \leq n-1).
$$

By (5), we have $ u(s_k) = u(s_k') + u(s_{k-1}') \mathbf{X}_n $, whence $ u(s_k) = s_k' $ by an easy calculation. Let $ P \in B[Y_1, \ldots, Y,_{-1}] $; then from $ P(s_1, \ldots, s_{n-1}) = 0 $ we deduce
$$
0 = u(P(s_1, \ldots, s_{n-1})) = P(s_1', \ldots, s_{n-1}'),
$$
whence $ P = 0 $ by (B). It follows that the B-algebra $ S' $ is generated by the algebraically independent elements $ s_1, \ldots, s_{n-1} $. This property may be reformulated as follows:
(D) The $ A $-algebra $ S' $ is generated by the algebraically independent elements $ s_1, \ldots, s_{n-1}, \mathbf{X}_n $.
