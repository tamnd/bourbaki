a) Let M be a submodule of E; show that, if $ M^0 $ is the submodule of $ E^* $ orthogonal to M, then the module $ E^*/M^0 $ is torsion-free, and consequently $ M^0 $ is a direct factor in $ E^* $; the submodule $ M^{00} $ of E orthogonal to $ M^0 $ is equal to $ E \cap KM $ (considering E as being canonically embedded in an n-dimensional vector space over K). We can consider $ E^*/M^0 $ as being canonically embedded in the dual $ M^* $ of M; show that the invariant factors of $ E^*/M^0 $ with respect to $ M^* $ are equal to the invariant factors of M with respect to E (use Th. 1 of VII, p. 18).

b) Let F be a second finitely generated, torsion-free A-module, and let $ u $ be a linear transformation from E into F. Show that the invariant factors of $ 'u(F^*) $ with respect to $ E^* $ are equal to those of $ u(E) $ with respect to F (proceed as in Prop. 5 of VII, p. 21).

8) Let G be a module of finite length over a principal ideal domain A. For any pair of free modules M and N such that $ N \subset M $ and G is isomorphic to $ M/N $, the invariant factors of N with respect to M which are distinct from A are identical to the invariant factors of G (VII, p. 20, Def. 2); their number is called the rank of G.

a) Show that the rank $ r $ of G is the least number of cyclic submodules of G of which G is the direct sum, and is equal to the greatest of the ranks of the $ \pi $-primary components of G.

b) Show that the rank of a submodule or a quotient module of G is at most equal to the rank of G (consider G as a quotient of two free modules of rank r).

c) For each $ A \in A $, show that the rank of the submodule AG is equal to the number of invariant factors of G which do not divide A (notice that, if $ E = A/(A\alpha) $, then the module $ \lambda E $ is isomorphic to $ (A\lambda)/((A\alpha) \cap (A\lambda)) $). Deduce that the k-th invariant factor of G (in decreasing order) is a gcd of those $ A \in A $ such that AG has rank $ \leq r - k $.

d) Let $ A\alpha_k $ ($ 1 \leq k \leq r $) be the invariant factors of G arranged in decreasing order. Let H be a submodule of G of rank $ r - q $, and let $ A\beta_k $ ($ 1 \leq k \leq r - q $) be its invariant factors, arranged in decreasing order. Deduce from b) and c) that $ \beta_k $ divides $ \alpha_{k-q} $ for $ 1 \leq k \leq r - q $. Similarly show that, if $ G/H $ has rank $ r - p $ and invariant factors $ Ay, (1 \leq k \leq r - p) $ in decreasing order, then $ \gamma_k $ divides $ a_{r-p} $ for $ 1 \leq k \leq r - p $.

e) Conversely, let L be a torsion module of rank $ r - q $, whose invariant factors $ A\lambda_k $ ($ 1 \leq k \leq r - q $) are such that $ \lambda_k $ divides $ \alpha_{k+q} $ for $ 1 \leq k \leq r - q $. Show that there exist submodules M and N of G such that L is isomorphic to M and to G/N (decompose G into a direct sum of submodules isomorphic to $ A/A\alpha_k $).

9) Let A be a principal ideal domain and K its field of fractions; let E be a vector space over K, let M be a finitely generated A-module of rank $ n $ contained in E, let N be a finitely generated A-module of rank p contained in KM, and let P be a finitely generated $ \Lambda $-module of rank q contained in KN.

a) Let $ A\alpha_i $ ($ 1 \leq i \leq p $) be the invariant factors of N with respect to M, arranged in decreasing order (Ex. 3). Show that, for $ 1 \leq k \leq p $, the element $ \alpha_k $ is a gcd of those elements $ A \in K $ such that the rank of the quotient module $ (N + \lambda(KN \cap M))/N $ is $ \leq p - k $ (cf. Ex. 8, d)).

b) Let $ A\beta_j $ ($ 1 \leq j \leq q $) be the invariant factors of P with respect to M and Ay, ($ 1 \leq j \leq q $) the invariant factors of P with respect to N, arranged in decreasing order. Show that $ \alpha_1 \gamma_j $ divides $ \beta_j $ for $ 1 \leq j \leq q $ (cf. Ex. 8).

c) Show that $ \gamma_1 \alpha_j $ divides $ \beta_j $ for $ 1 \leq j \leq q $. (First consider the case $ n = p = q $, and apply Ex. 8. In general, show that we may always assume $ p = n $, and consider a submodule Q of M such that $ P + \rho Q $ has rank $ p $ for all $ \rho \in A $; then choose the ideal $ Ap $ sufficiently small, and apply Prop. 4 of VII, p. 20.)
