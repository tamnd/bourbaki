If the integer m satisfies $0 \leq k \leq m \leq n$, then the mapping $f \mapsto f (X_1, \ldots, X_m, 0, \ldots, 0)$ is an isomorphism of $S_k^{(n)}$ onto $S_k^{(m)}$.

Now the weight of a monomial $S_1^{\alpha(1)} \cdots S_n^{\alpha(n)}$ is equal to the integer $\alpha(1) + 2\alpha(2) + \ldots + n\alpha(n)$; since the integers $\alpha(1), \ldots, \alpha(n)$ are positive, the relation
$$

$$
implies $\alpha(j) = 0$ for $k < j \leq n$, whence the assertion.

*Remark.* — Let n and k be two positive integers. We denote by $\Delta_{k,n}$ the set of elements of length k in $N^n$ and further equip $A$, , with the order relation, written $a \leq \beta$, induced by the lexicographic order on $N^n$ (*Set Theory*, III, p. 157), and define an action of the group $\mathfrak{S}_n$ on $N^n$ by $(\sigma a)(i) = a(\sigma^{-1}(i))$ for $\sigma \in \mathfrak{S}_n, a \in N^n$ and $l \leq i \leq n$. Moreover, we denote by $D_k$ the set of elements $a = (\alpha(1), \ldots, \alpha(k))$ of $\mathbf{N}^k$ such that
$$
\alpha(1) \geq \alpha(2) \geq \cdots \geq \alpha(k), \quad \alpha(1) + \cdots + \alpha(k) = k.
$$

Suppose that $k \leq n$ and let us identify $\mathbf{N}^k$ with a subset of $N^n$ by the mapping $(a(1), \ldots, a(k)) \mapsto (a(1), \ldots, a(k), 0, \ldots, 0)$.
