1) Let $ r_k $ be the polynomial with integer coefficients such that
$$
p_k = r_k(s_1, \ldots, s_k) .
$$
a) Show that $ r_k(Y_1, \ldots, Y_s) $ contains the terms $ Y_1^k $ and $ (-1)^{k-1} k Y_k $.
b) Show that
$$
r_k(Y_1, \ldots, Y_k) = \sum_{p=0}^{k-1} (-1)^p \left| \begin{array}{cccccc}
Y_{p+1} & Y_{p+2} & Y_{p+3} & \ldots & Y_{k-1} & Y_k \\
1 & Y_1 & Y_2 & \ldots & Y_{k-p-2} & Y_{k-p-1} \\
0 & 1 & Y_1 & \ldots & Y_{k-p-3} & Y_{k-p-2} \\
0 & 0 & 0 & \ldots & Y_1 & Y_2 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & \ldots & 1 & Y_1
\end{array} \right|
$$

3) Let $ f $ be a symmetric polynomial of $ K[X_1, \ldots, X_n] $ and $ \varphi $ the unique polynomial of $ K[Y_1, \ldots, Y_n] $ such that $ f(X_1, \ldots, X_n) = \varphi(s_1, \ldots, s_n) $. Show that the total degree of $ \varphi $ is equal to the degree of $ f $ in any one of the $ X_i $.

\* 4) Let $ K $ be a field of characteristic 0. Show that if $ \alpha_i \ (1 \leq i \leq n) $ are $ n $ elements of $ K $ such that $ \alpha_1^k + \alpha_2^k + \ldots + \alpha_n^k = 0 $ for $ n $ consecutive values $ h, h+1, \ldots, h+n-1 $ of $ k $, then $ a_1 = a_2 = \cdots = a_n = 0 $. Show by an example that the property no longer holds if the $ n $ values of $ k $ are not consecutive or if $ K $ is not of characteristic 0. \*
