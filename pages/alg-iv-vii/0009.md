This page is from Bourbaki, Elements of Mathematics, Algebra. The following hold for these volumes.

- The transcription is Markdown and the headings are marked with hashes, because the structure of the Book is read back out of them. A chapter opens on the word CHAPTER, the numeral and the title of the chapter, and the three go on one line: ## CHAPTER VII Cartan Subalgebras and Regular Elements. A § heading is ## § 1. PRIMARY DECOMPOSITION OF LINEAR REPRESENTATIONS. A numbered no. inside a § is ### 1. DECOMPOSITION OF A FAMILY OF ENDOMORPHISMS. An appendix is ## APPENDIX 1, and where the volume prints the word alone with the title set under it, the title goes on the next line as a heading of its own. A historical note is # HISTORICAL NOTE. A run of exercises printed at the end of a § is ### Exercises, and an EXERCISES standing over a whole chapter's worth of them is # EXERCISES, with the § 1 that marks each block left on a line of its own under it.
- A paragraph is one line. The breaks inside a printed paragraph are the width of the column and nothing more, so join those lines with a single space and break the line only where the page breaks the paragraph. A heading, a display formula, a footnote and each item of a numbered list stand on their own line in the same way.

Transcribe it verbatim on its own first line, keeping the page label such as A I.24 or A IV.7 and the locator such as § 2.5 exactly as they are printed. The page map depends on that line, so do not tidy it, do not move it, and do not drop it when it is only a page number.

Write each of them as \* with the backslash, in the place the page prints it, and write nothing else there. The backslash is not optional: a bare asterisk at the head of a line opens a bullet list and the passage comes out as an indented item. It is an asterisk and not a star, a snowflake, a bullet or any other ornament, whatever the glyph on the page looks like to you.
