PROPOSITION 6. — *Let $ f \in A[X] $ be a monic polynomial of degree n, and let P be a symmetric polynomial in $ X_1, \ldots, X_n $ with coefficients in A. Then there exists precisely one element a of A with the following property :*
*(FS)* *For any ring homomorphism $ \rho : A \to B $ and decomposition $ ^0f(X) = \prod_{i=1}^n (X - \xi_i) $ in B[X] we have $ \rho(a) = P(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n) $.*

Write $ f = X^n + \sum_{k=1}^n a_k X^{n-k} $, then by Th. 1, IV, p. 62 there exists a polynomial $ \Pi $ in n indeterminates with coefficients in A, such that $ P = \Pi(s_1, \ldots, s_n) $. Put $ a = \Pi(-a_1, a,, \ldots, (-1)^n a_n) $. Under the hypothesis (FS) we have
$$
s_k(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n) = (-1)^k \rho(a_k)
$$
whence
$$
\begin{align*}
\rho(a) &= \Pi(-\rho(a_1), \rho(a_2), \ldots, (-1)^n \rho(a_n)) \\
&= \Pi(s_1(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n), \ldots, s_n(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n)) \\
&= P(\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n).
\end{align*}
$$

This proves the existence of an element a satisfying (FS). The uniqueness of a follows from the Cor. to Prop. 5, because we have $ a \cdot 1 = P(x_{1,f}, \ldots, x_{n,f}) $ in the universal decomposition algebra $ E_f $.

With the notation of Prop. 6 one sometimes writes $ a = P^*(f) $. Here are some examples.

*Examples. — 1) If $ P = s_k $, then $ P^*(f) = (-1)^k a_k $.
\* 2) Let g be a polynomial in $ A[X] $ and put
$$
P(X_1, \ldots, X_n) = g(X_1) \ldots g(X_n).
$$
Then $ P^*(f) $ is just the resultant $ \operatorname{res}(f, g) $, by Cor. 1 of IV, p. 80.
3) Put $ A(X_1, \ldots, X_n) = \prod_{i<j} (X_i - X_j)^2 $, then $ \Delta^*(f) $ is just the discriminant of the monic polynomial $ f $ (IV, p. 82, formula (46)). \*
