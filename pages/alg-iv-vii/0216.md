COROLLARY 2.—Let L be a p-radical extension of height ≤ l of a field K and let E be a subextension of L. Let U be the subspace of Ω_K(L) generated by the differentials of elements of E. Then E consists of the elements of L whose differential belongs to U. In particular we have d_{L/K}x ≠ 0 for all x ∈ L − K.

Let x be an element of L not belonging to E. Then {1, x, ..., x^{p^{l-1}}} is a basis of E(x) over E. Let A be the E-linear mapping of E(x) into L such that Δ(x^i) = ix' for 0 ≤ i < p. We verify at once that A is an E-derivation of E(x) into L, and in particular a K-derivation. By Cor. 1 to Prop. 5 there exists a K-derivation D of L into L extending A. By the universal property of Ω_K(L), there exists a linear form u on this vector L-space such that D = u ∘ d_{L/K}. We have D(x) = x and D|E = 0; therefore we have u(d_{L/K}x) ≠ 0 and u|U = 0, whence d_{L/K}x ∉ U.

The last assertion is the particular case E = K; then we have U = 0.

THEOREM 1.—Let L be a p-radical extension of height ≤ l of a field K and (a_i)_{i ∈ I}, a family of elements of L.

a) For (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to be p-free over K it is necessary and sufficient that the family (dx_i)_{i ∈ I} should be free in the vector L-space Ω_K(L).

b) For (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to generate L over K it is necessary and sufficient that the family (dx_i)_{i ∈ I} should generate the vector L-space Ω_K(L).

c) For (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to be a p-basis of L over K it is necessary and sufficient that the family (dx_i)_{i ∈ I} should be a basis of the vector L-space Ω_K(L).

To begin with let us remark that the differential dx of an element of the field K((x_i)_{i ∈ I}) is a linear combination with coefficients in L of the differentials dx_i, i ∈ I. For the family (x_i)_{i ∈ I} to be p-free it is necessary and sufficient that x_i ∉ K(x_j)_{j ∈ I - {i}} for all i ∈ I (V, p. 99, Prop. 2). By Cor. 2 of Prop. 5 this means that dx_i is not a linear combination of the dx_j for j ≠ i in I. Assertion a) follows from this. Now b) follows at once from Cor. 2 to Prop. 5 and c) follows from a) and b).

The following corollary makes Cor. 1 of Prop. 5 more specific:

COROLLARY.—Let (x_i)_{i ∈ I} be a p-basis of L over K. Let V be a vector space over L, A a derivation of K into V which vanishes on L^p and (u_i)_{i ∈ I} a family of elements of V. Then there exists a unique derivation D of L in V which extends A and maps x_i to u_i for all i ∈ I.

By Cor. 1 of Prop. 5 there exists a derivation D_0 of L into V extending A. The derivations of L in V extending A are precisely the mappings of the form D = D_0 + u ∘ d_{L/K} where u is an L-linear mapping of Ω_K(L) into V. We have D(x_i) = u_i if and only if the linear mapping u satisfies the conditions u(dx_i) = u_i − D_0(x_i). Since the family dx_i is a basis of Ω_K(L), this determines u uniquely and the corollary follows.
