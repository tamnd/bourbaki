Exercises

§ 1

1) Let $ A $ be a not necessarily commutative ring, not zero and without divisors of zero (I, p. 98). Show that if there exists an integer $ m \geq 1 $ such that $ mA = 0 $, then the set of all integers having this property is an ideal $ p\mathbf{Z} $, where $ p $ is a prime number, and $ A $ is a ring of characteristic $ p $.

2) Let $ m, n $ be integers such that $ 0 \leq n \leq m $. Let $ p $ be a prime number and let $ m = a_r + \alpha_1 p + \ldots + \alpha_n p' $ and $ n = \beta_0 + \beta_1 p + \ldots + \beta_n p' $ be the expansions of $ m $ and $ n $ in base $ p $, where we thus have $ 0 \leq \alpha_i < p, \ 0 \leq \beta_i < p $ for $ 0 \leq i \leq r $; suppose that $ a_r \neq 0 $. Show that $ \binom{m}{n} \equiv 0 \pmod{p} $ if $ \beta_i > a_r $ for at least one index $ i $. If on the other hand, $ \beta_i \leq \alpha_i $ for $ 0 \leq i \leq r $, then

$$
\left( \begin{array}{c} m \\ n \end{array} \right) \equiv \left( \begin{array}{c} \alpha_0 \\ \beta_0 \end{array} \right) \cdots \left( \begin{array}{c} \alpha_r \\ \beta_r \end{array} \right) \pmod{p}
$$

(In $ (\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z})[X] $ we have $ (1 + X)^m = (1 + X)^{\alpha_0}(1 + X^p)^{\alpha_1} \ldots (1 + X^{p'})^{\alpha_r} $.)

3) Let $ A $ be a commutative ring. For every polynomial $ f \in A[X] $ we write in the polynomial ring $ A[X, Y] $ of polynomials in two indeterminates over $ A $,

$$
f(X + Y) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \Delta_m f(X) Y^m,
$$

where $ \Delta $ is thus an $ A $-linear mapping of $ A[X] $ into itself.
a) If $ Z $ is an indeterminate and $ \tau_Z $ the $ A $-linear mapping of $ A[X] $ into $ A[X, Z] $ such that $ \tau_Z f = f(X + Z) $, show that $ \tau_Z \circ \Delta = \Delta \circ \tau_Z $ (where on the right $ \Delta $ is the mapping defined above in the ring $ B[X] $, where $ B = A[Z] $). Deduce that in $ A[X] $ we have

$$
\Delta_m \Delta_n = \Delta_n \Delta_m = \binom{m+n}{m} \Delta_{m+n}
$$

for integers $ m, n \geq 0 $ (cf. IV, p. 8).
b) Suppose that $ A $ is a ring of characteristic $ p > 0 $ and for each integer $ k \geq 0 $ put $ D_k = \Delta_{p^k} $. Show that if $ f \in A[X] $ and $ g \in A[X^{p^k}] $ then $ D_k(fg) = g \cdot D_k f + f \cdot D_k g $.
Every polynomial $ f \in A[X] $ can be uniquely written in the form $ f(X) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} X^{mp^k} g_m(X) $ where $ \deg(g_m) < p^k $; show that

$$
D_k f(X) = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} m X^{(m-1)p^k} g_m(X)
$$

and deduce that $ D_k^p = 0 $.
