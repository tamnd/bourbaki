28) a) Let K be an algebraically closed commutative field, let f be a nonconstant monic polynomial in K[X], and let g be a polynomial in K[X, Y]; suppose that for each of the roots α_i of f there exists a root β_i of g(α_i, Y) = 0 such that $\frac{\partial g}{\partial Y}(α_i, β_i) \neq 0$. Then show that there exists a polynomial h(X) ∈ K[X] such that g(X, h(X)) ≡ 0 (mod f(X)). (Using Prop. 4, reduce to the case f(X) = X^m (for some m ≥ 1); notice that the ring K[X]/(X^m) is isomorphic to the quotient ring K[[X]]/(X^m), and use the Cor. of IV, p. 37).
b) In particular show that if m is an integer not divisible by the characteristic of K, then for every pair of coprime monic polynomials f and f_0 in K[X], there exists a polynomial h(X) in K[X] such that (h(X))^m ≡ f_0(X) (mod f(X)).

§ 2

1) Let A be a principal ideal domain, let M be a torsion A-module, let x, y be two elements of M, and let Aα and Aβ be the annihilators of x, y respectively; if δ is a gcd of α and β, show that the annihilator Ay of x + y is such that y divides the lcm αβ/δ of α and β, and is a multiple of $\alpha \beta / \delta^2$. When α and β are distinct powers $\pi^\mu, \pi^\nu$ of the same irreducible element π of A, show that $y = \pi^{\sup(\mu, \nu)}$.

2) Let A be a principal ideal domain, let π be an irreducible element of A and let M be a π-primary module; show that for all x ∈ M and all a ∈ A not a multiple of π, there exists a unique y ∈ M such that x = αy (use the Bezout identity); put y = $\alpha^{-1}x$. Deduce that, if A,, denotes the principal ideal domain consisting of elements $\beta/\alpha$ of the field of fractions of A, such that α, β ∈ A and α is not a multiple of π (VII, p. 48, Ex. 4), then there exists a unique A(,,)-module structure on M such that restriction of the ring of operators to A gives the original A-module structure on M. The A(,,)-module thus defined is said to be canonically associated to M; its submodules are identical to the A-submodules of M.

3) Let A be a principal ideal domain.
a) An A-module M is injective (II, p. 389, Ex. 11) if and only if, for all x ∈ M and all a ≠ 0 in A, there exists y ∈ M such that x = ay; the A-module M is then said to be divisible. If E is an arbitrary A-module, then the sum of all the divisible submodules of E is the greatest divisible submodule Δ(E) of E. We have Δ(F) ⊂ A(E) for every submodule F of E.
b) Let M be a divisible A-module, let π be an irreducible element of A and let x,, be an element of M whose annihilator has the form Aπ^n (n > 0 an integer). Define a sequence (x_s) of elements of M recursively by the conditions $x_s = \pi x_{s-1}$ for each integer s ≥ 0. Show that the submodule of M generated by the sequence (x_s) is isomorphic to the π-primary component U_π of the module U = K/A, where K is the field of fractions of A.
c) Show that U_π is a divisible module, and that every submodule of U_π other than U_π and {0} is cyclic and generated by the class mod A of some power of π; deduce that U_π is indecomposable (II, p. 393, Ex. 21).
d) Show that every indecomposable divisible A-module is isomorphic either to K or to one of the modules U_π (use a) and b)).
e) Show that every divisible A-module is isomorphic to the direct sum of a family of indecomposable divisible submodules (apply Zorn's Lemma to families of indecomposable divisible submodules whose sums are direct).
