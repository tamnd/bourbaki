Suppose henceforth that $N$ is of finite degree over $K$. Given $x \in N$, for $\{x\}$ to be a basis of the $\mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$-module $N$ it is necessary and sufficient that the family $(\sigma(x))_{\sigma \in \Gamma}$ should be a basis of $N$ over $K$. Such a basis is called a *normal basis of $N$ over $K$*.

**Theorem 6.** — *Let $N$ be a Galois extension of finite degree over $K$ and let $\Gamma$ be the Galois group of $N$ over $K$. Then there exists a normal basis of $N$ over $K$; in other words, the $\mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$-module $N$ is free of rank 1.*

We shall give two proofs of this statement. The first uses the following lemma which will be proved in Chapter VIII (\$ 2, No. 5).

\* Lemma 3. — Let $A$ be a $K$-algebra, $M_1$ and $M_2$ two $A$-modules of finite rank over $K$ and suppose that there exists an extension $L$ of $K$ such that the modules $L \otimes_K M_1$ and $L \otimes_K M_2$ over the ring $L \otimes_K A$ are isomorphic. Then the $A$-modules $M_1$ and $M_2$ are isomorphic.

We shall apply Lemma 3 in the case where $A = \mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$, $M_1 = N$, $M_2 = A$, and $L = N$. By the Cor. of V, p. 64 there exists a $K$-isomorphism $\varphi$ of $N \otimes_K N$ onto $N \otimes_K \mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$ which maps $x \otimes y$ to $\sum x u^1(y) \otimes \sigma$. It is clear that $\varphi$ is an isomorphism of $N \otimes_K \mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$-modules and so the theorem follows from Lemma 3.

For the second proof we shall use the following proposition:

**Proposition 10.** — *Let $x \in N$, then for $\{x\}$ to form a basis of the $\mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$-module $N$ it is necessary and sufficient that $\det(\sigma \tau(x))_{\sigma, \tau \in \Gamma} \neq 0$.

Since $\mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$ and $N$ have the same dimension over $K$, to say that $\{x\}$ is a basis of $N$ over $\mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$ means that the mapping $a \mapsto ax$ of $\mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$ into $N$ is injective. This in turn means that the mapping $b \mapsto b(1 \otimes x)$ of $N \otimes_K \mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$ into $N \otimes_K N$ is injective (II, p. 306, Prop. 14). Now there is an $N \otimes_K \mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$-module isomorphism of $N \otimes_K N$ onto $N \otimes_K \mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$ which maps $1 \otimes x$ to $\sum \sigma^{-1}(x) \otimes \sigma$. It follows that $\{x\}$ is a basis of $N$ over $\mathbf{K}^{(\Gamma)}$ if and only if, for every non-zero family of elements $(n_\tau)$, of $N$ we have $\left( \sum n_\tau \otimes \tau \right) \left( \sum \sigma^{-1}(x) \otimes \sigma \right) \neq 0$. But this last relation means that there exists $\sigma \in \Gamma$ such that $\sum n_\tau \sigma^{-1}(x) \neq 0$, whence the proposition.
