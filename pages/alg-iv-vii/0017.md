We have:

$$
\sum_{\nu} (\Delta^\nu u)(\mathbf{X})(\mathbf{Y} + \mathbf{Z})^\nu = u(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y} + \mathbf{Z})
$$
$$
= \sum_\sigma (\mathrm{Auu})(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y})Zu
$$
$$
= \sum_{\rho, \sigma} (\Delta^\rho \Delta^\sigma u)(\mathbf{X}) Y^\rho Z^\sigma,
$$

hence by I, p. 99, Cor. 2:

(13)
$$
\Delta^\rho \Delta^\sigma u = \frac{(\rho + \sigma)!}{\rho! \sigma!} \Delta^{\rho + \sigma} u.
$$

PROPOSITION 5. — *For any* $ u \in A[\mathbf{X}] $ *and* $ \nu \in \mathbf{N}^{(I)} $ *we have*

$$
D^\nu u = \nu! \; \Delta^\nu u
$$

Suppose first that $ \nu $ has length 1 ; then there exists an element $ i $ of I such that $ \nu = \varepsilon_i $, that is, $ \nu_i = 1 $ and $ \nu_j = 0 $ for all $ j \neq i $ in I. Formula (12) shows that $ A'' $ is a derivation of the A-algebra $ A[\mathbf{X}] $ which clearly has the value zero on $ X_j $ for $ j \neq i $ and the value 1 on $ X_i $. We thus have $ \Delta^{\varepsilon_i} = D_i $ for each $ i \in I $.

By (13) we have

(14)
$$
(\rho! \; \Delta^\rho) . (\sigma! \; \Delta^\sigma) = (\rho + \sigma)! \; \Delta^{\rho + \sigma}
$$

in the endomorphism algebra of the A-module $ A[\mathbf{X}] $. It now follows by induction on the length of $ \nu $ that $ \nu! \; A'' = D^\nu $.

If A is a Q-algebra, the formulae (9), (10), (11) may thus be written

(15)
$$
u(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y}) = \sum_\nu \frac{1}{\nu!} (D^\nu u)(\mathbf{X}) \mathbf{Y}^\nu
$$

(16)
$$
u(\mathbf{X}) = \sum_\nu \frac{1}{\nu!} (D^\nu u)(\mathbf{a})(\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{a})^\nu
$$

(17)
$$
u(\mathbf{X}) = \sum_\nu \frac{1}{\nu!} (D^\nu u)(0) \mathbf{X}^\nu
$$

The formulae (15), (16), (17) are all three called « Taylor's formula ».

PROPOSITION 6 (*« Euler's identity »*). — *Let* $ u \in A[\mathbf{X}] $ *be a homogeneous polynomial of degree* r ; *we have*

$$
\sum_{i \in I} X_i \cdot D_i u = ru.
$$
