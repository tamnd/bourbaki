8) Put

$$
s_k = \sum A_{m_1 \ldots m_n} p_1^{m_1} \cdots p_n^{m_n},
$$

where the $A_{m_1 \ldots m_n}$ are rational numbers and the sum is extended over all families $(m_1, \ldots, m_n)$ such that $\sum i \cdot m_i = k$. Show that $\sum A_{m_1 \ldots m_n} = 0$ for $k > 1$ and $\sum |A_{m_1 \ldots m_n}| = 1$ for $k \geq 1$. Deduce that on writing $q_j = -p_j$ we have

$$
(-1)^k s_k = \sum |A_{m_1 \ldots m_n}| q_1^{m_1} \cdots q_n^{m_n}.
$$

9) a) Let K be a field and $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ indeterminates. Show that for any exponents $v_1 < v_2 < \ldots < v_n$ the determinant $\det(X_i^{v_j})$ is non-zero in $K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$.

b) Let $s_k$ ($1 \leq k \leq n$) be the elementary symmetric polynomials in the $X_i$ and $r_1, \ldots, r_n$ n elements of the field $K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$. We put (with a new indeterminate T)

$$
R(T) = \frac{1 + r_1 T + \cdots + r_n T^n}{1 + s_1 T + \cdots + s_n T^n} = 1 + u_1 T + \cdots + u_k T^k + \cdots.
$$

Show that if $n$ of the coefficients $u_k$ are zero, then all the $u_k$ are zero. (Decompose $R(T)$ into simple fractions.)

*c) Suppose that K is of characteristic zero. Let $\{v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n\}$ be a set of n elements $\geq 1$ of $\mathbf{N}$, and let M be the complement of this set in $\mathbf{N}$; suppose also that M is stable under addition. Show that if $y_1, \ldots, y_n$ are n elements of $K(X_1, \ldots, X_n)$ such that

$$
P_{v_j}(y_1, \ldots, y_n) = P_{v_j}(X_1, \ldots, X_n)
$$

for $1 \leq j \leq n$, then $y_j = X_j$ for $1 \leq j \leq n$ except possibly for a permutation. (Consider the expansion of $\log R(T) = v_1 T + v_2 T^2 + \cdots + v_k T^k + \cdots$, observe that $v_{v_j} = 0$ for $1 \leq j \leq n$ and examine the relations between the $v_k$ and the $u_k$.)

10) Let $a \in \mathbf{A}$. For each polynomial $h \in \mathbf{A}[X]$ put $h_a(X) = h(X + a)$; show that $\operatorname{res}(f_a, g_a) = \operatorname{res}(f, g)$, $\operatorname{dis}(f_a) = \operatorname{dis}(f)$.

11) In this exercise we assume that A is a field, $f(0) \neq 0$ and $\deg g < \deg f = p$. Let $\sum \alpha_n X^n$ be the expansion in a formal power series of the rational fraction $g(X)/f(X)$; for each integer $k \geq 0$ let $H_n^{(k)}$ be the Hankel determinant defined in Exercise 1 of IV, p. 90.
a) Show that $H_n^{(k)} = 0$ for $k > p$ and calculate $H_0^{(p)}$ as function of $\operatorname{res}(f, g)$.
b) Under what conditions do we have $H_0^{(p)} = H_0^{(p-1)} = \ldots = H_0^{(p-r+1)} = 0$?

12) There exists a unique polynomial $P_{p,q}(T_0, \ldots, T_p, U_0, \ldots, U_q)$ with integer coefficients such that for every ring A and any polynomials $f = t_p X^p + \ldots + t_0, g = u_q X^q + \ldots + u_0$ of $\mathbf{A}[X]$ we have

$$
\operatorname{res}_{p,q}(f, g) = P_{p,q}(t_0, \ldots, t_p, u_0, \ldots, u_q).
$$

If we assign the weight i to $T_i$ and $U_i$, then $P_{p,q}$ is isobaric of weight pq.
