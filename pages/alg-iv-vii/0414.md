4) A family (x,) of nonzero elements of a module M over a principal ideal domain A is said to be *pseudofree* if every relation of the form $ \sum \alpha_i x_i = \beta y $ ($ y \in M,\ a_i \in A,\ \beta \in A $) implies the existence of a family of elements $ \alpha'_i \in A $ such that $ \alpha_i x_i = \beta \alpha'_i x_i $ for each index $ i $. The relation $ i \neq k $ then implies $ x_i \neq x_k $; the set of elements of a pseudofree family is called a pseudofree subset of M.
a) Let $ \pi $ be an irreducible element of A. Show that, in an A-module with annihilator $ A\tau^n \ (n \geq 1) $, any element with annihilator $ A\tau^n $ is pseudofree (use Bezout's identity).
b) Let (x,) be a pseudofree family in an A-module M, and let N be the submodule of M generated by this family. Show that for every element $ \dot{x} $ of $ M/N $ there exists an element $ x \in \dot{x} $ whose annihilator is equal to the annihilator of $ \dot{x} $ in $ M/N $. If also $ \dot{x} $ is pseudofree in $ M/N $, show that the family consisting of the (x,) and x is pseudofree in M.
c) Deduce from a) and b) that if the A-module M has nonzero annihilator, then it is a direct sum of cyclic modules (reduce to the case of a $ \tau $-primary module, and apply Zorn's Lemma to pseudofree families of elements of M) (cf. VII, p. 19, Th. 2).

5) Let M be a finitely generated torsion module over a principal ideal domain A. Show that M is a module of finite length; consequently every nonempty set of submodules of M has a maximal element and a minimal element.

6) Let M be a torsion module over a principal ideal domain A, which is not finitely generated, but all of whose proper submodules are finitely generated. Show that there exists an irreducible element $ \tau_1 $ of A such that M is isomorphic to the $ \tau $-primary component $ U_{\pi} $ of the module $ U = K/A $ (Ex. 3, c)). (First of all show that M cannot have more than one nonzero $ \pi $-primary component, and hence is a $ \tau $-primary module for some irreducible element $ \tau \in A $. Then show that $ M = \pi M $ by observing that $ M/\pi M $ could not be finitely generated if it were nonzero; but $ M/\pi M $ is a vector space over the field $ A/\pi A $, and would contain a proper subspace which was not finitely generated.)
