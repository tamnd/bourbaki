### 3. CENTRALIZERS

Recall that two elements $ x, y $ of a group are called permutable if $ (x, y) = e $, or $ (\mathrm{Int}\, x)y = y $, or $ (\mathrm{Int}\, y)x = x $; and that two elements $ a, b $ of a Lie algebra are called permutable if $ [a, b] = 0 $, or $ (\mathrm{ad}\, a).b = 0 $, or $ (\mathrm{ad}\, b).a = 0 $. Let $ G $ be a Lie group, $ x \in G $, $ a \in \mathbf{L}(G) $; $ x $ and $ a $ are called permutable if $ (\mathrm{Ad}\, x).a = a $, that is if $ xa = ax $ in $ \mathbf{T}(G) $.

Let $ G $ be a Lie group, $ g $ its Lie algebra, $ A $ a subset of $ G $ and $ a $ a subset of $ g $. Let $ Z_G(A) $ (resp. $ Z_G(a) $) denote the set of elements of $ G $ which are permutable with all the elements of $ A $ (resp. $ a $). It is a closed subgroup of $ G $. Let $ \mathfrak{z}_g(A) $ (resp. $ \mathfrak{z}_g(a) $) denote the set of elements of $ g $ which are permutable with all the elements of $ A $ (resp. $ a $). It is a closed Lie subalgebra of $ g $.

**Proposition 7.** *Let $ G $ be a finite-dimensional Lie group, $ g $ its Lie algebra and $ a $ a subset of $ g $. Then $ Z_G(a) $ is a Lie subgroup of $ G $ with Lie algebra $ \mathfrak{z}_g(a) $.*

This follows from § 3, Proposition 44 and Corollary 2 to Proposition 39.

**Proposition 8.** *Let $ G $ be a finite-dimensional real or complex Lie group, $ g $ its Lie algebra and $ A $ a subset of $ G $. Then $ Z_G(A) $ is a Lie subgroup of $ G $ with Lie algebra $ \mathfrak{z}_g(A) $.*

Suppose that $ A $ consists of a single point $ a $. Then $ Z_G(A) $ is the set of fixed points of $ \mathrm{Int}\, a $; hence $ Z_G(A) $ is a Lie subgroup of $ G $ and $ \mathbf{L}(Z_G(A)) $ is the set of fixed points of $ \mathrm{Ad}\, a $, that is $ \mathfrak{z}_g(A) $ (§ 3, no. 8, Corollary 1 to Proposition 29). The general case follows by means of § 6, no. 2, Corollary 3 to Proposition 1.

**Proposition 9.** *Let $ G $ be a finite-dimensional real or complex Lie group, $ g $ its Lie algebra, $ A $ an integral subgroup of $ G $ and $ a = \mathbf{L}(A) $. Then $ Z_G(A) = Z_G(a) $, $ \mathfrak{z}_g(A) = \mathfrak{z}_g(a) $ and $ Z_G(A) $ is a Lie subgroup of $ G $ with Lie algebra $ \mathfrak{z}_g(a) $.*

Let $ x \in G $. Then
$$
x \in Z_G(A) \iff A \subset Z_G(\{x\}) \\
\iff a \subset \mathbf{L}(Z_G(\{x\})) \quad (\text{§ 6, Corollary 2 to Proposition 3}) \\
\iff a \subset \mathfrak{z}_g(\{x\}) \quad (\text{Proposition 8}) \\
\iff x \in Z_G(a)
$$
and hence $ Z_G(A) = Z_G(a) $. Let $ u \in g $. Then
$$
u \in \mathfrak{z}_g(A) \iff A \subset Z_G(\{u\}) \\
\iff a \subset \mathbf{L}(Z_G(\{u\})) \quad (\text{§ 6, Corollary 2 to Proposition 3}) \\
\iff a \subset \mathfrak{z}_g(\{u\}) \quad (\text{Proposition 7}) \\
\iff u \in \mathfrak{z}_g(a)
$$
and hence $ \mathfrak{z}_g(A) = \mathfrak{z}_g(a) $. The last assertion then follows from Proposition 7 or Proposition 8.
