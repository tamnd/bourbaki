PROPOSITION 20. *Let G be a finite-dimensional real Lie group.*

(i) *There exist a complex Lie group $\tilde{G}$ and an $\mathbf{R}$-analytic morphism $\gamma$ of G into $\tilde{G}$ with the following properties: for every complex Lie group H and every $\mathbf{R}$-analytic morphism $\phi$ of G into H, there exists one and only one $\mathbf{C}$-analytic morphism $\psi$ of $\tilde{G}$ into H such that $\phi = \psi \circ \gamma$.*

(ii) *If $(\tilde{G}', \gamma')$ has the same properties as $(\tilde{G}, \gamma)$, there exists one and only one isomorphism $\theta$ of $\tilde{G}$ onto $\tilde{G}'$ such that $\theta \circ \gamma = \gamma'$.*

(iii) *The $\mathbf{C}$-linear mapping of $L(G) \otimes \mathbf{C}$ into $L(\tilde{G})$ which extends $L(\gamma)$ is surjective; in particular $\dim_{\mathbf{C}}(\tilde{G}) \leq \dim_{\mathbf{R}}(G)$.*

Assertion (ii) is obvious. We prove the existence of an ordered pair $(\tilde{G}, \gamma)$ with properties (i) and (iii).

(a) Suppose first that G is connected. Let $g = L(G)$, $g_C = g \otimes_{\mathbf{R}} \mathbf{C}$ be the complexification of g, S (resp. S') the simply connected real (resp. complex) Lie group with Lie algebra g (resp. $g_C$) and $\sigma$ the unique $\mathbf{R}$-analytic morphism of S into S' such that $L(\sigma)$ is the canonical injection of g into $g_C$. Let $\pi$ be the unique $\mathbf{R}$-analytic morphism of S onto G such that $L(\pi) = \mathrm{Id}_{L(G)}$ and F = Ker $\pi$.

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
S & \xrightarrow{\sigma} & S' \\
\downarrow \pi & & \downarrow \lambda \\
G & \xrightarrow{\gamma} & G \\
& \searrow \phi & \swarrow \psi \\
& & H
\end{array}
$$

For every complex Lie group H and every $\mathbf{R}$-analytic morphism $\phi$ of G into H, $L(\phi): g \to L(H)$ has a unique $\mathbf{C}$-linear extension to $g_C$ and this extension is of the form $L(\phi^*)$, where $\phi^*$ is a $\mathbf{C}$-analytic morphism of S' into H. Then
$$
L(\phi \circ \pi) = L(\phi) \circ L(\pi) = L(\phi) = L(\phi^*) \circ L(\sigma) = L(\phi^* \circ \sigma)
$$
and hence $\phi \circ \pi = \phi^* \circ \sigma$. Therefore $\phi^*(\sigma(F)) = \phi(\pi(F)) = \{e\}$, whence
$$
\sigma(F) \subset \mathrm{Ker}\, \phi^*.
$$
