(IV) Any one of the following properties characterizes nilpotent Lie algebras:
(1) $\mathcal{C}^p g = \{0\}$ for sufficiently large $p$; (2) $\mathcal{C}_p g = g$ for sufficiently large $p$;
(3) there exists a decreasing sequence $g = g_0 \supset g_1 \supset \cdots \supset g_p = \{0\}$ of ideals of $g$ such that $[g, g_i] \subset g_{i+1}$; (4) there exists a decreasing sequence $g = g'_0 \supset g'_1 \supset \cdots \supset g'_{r'} = \{0\}$ of ideals of $g$ such that $[g, g'_i] \subset g'_{i+1}$ and the $g'_i / g'_{i+1}$ are 1-dimensional; (5) there exists an integer $i$ such that
$$
(\operatorname{ad} x_1) \circ (\operatorname{ad} x_2) \circ \cdots \circ (\operatorname{ad} x_i) = 0
$$
for all $x_1, \ldots, x_i$ in $g$; (6) for all $x \in g$, $\operatorname{ad} x$ is nilpotent.

(V) $g$ commutative $\Rightarrow$ $g$ nilpotent $\Rightarrow$ the Killing form of $g$ is zero $\Rightarrow$ $g$ solvable.
$g$ commutative $\Rightarrow$ $g$ reductive.
$g$ semi-simple $\Rightarrow$ $g$ reductive.

(VI) Characterizations of $r$:
(1) $r$ is the largest solvable ideal of $g$; (2) $r$ is the smallest ideal such that $g/r$ is semi-simple; (3) $r$ is the only solvable ideal such that $g/r$ is semi-simple; (4) $r$ is the orthogonal of $\mathscr{D}g$ relative to the Killing form.

(VII) Characterizations of $n$:
(1) $n$ is the largest nilpotent ideal of $g$; (2) $n$ is the largest nilpotent ideal of $r$;
(3) $n$ is the set of $x \in r$ such that $\operatorname{ad}_g x$ is nilpotent; (4) $n$ is the set of $x \in r$ such that $\operatorname{ad}_r x$ is nilpotent; (5) $n$ is the largest ideal of $g$ such that, for all $x \in n$, $\operatorname{ad}_g x$ is nilpotent; (6) $n$ is the set of $x \in g$ such that $\operatorname{ad}_r x$ belongs to the radical of the associative algebra generated by 1 and the $\operatorname{ad}_g y$ ($y \in g$).

(VIII) Characterizations of $s$:
(1) $s$ is the intersection of the kernels of the finite-dimensional simple representations of $g$; (2) $s$ is the smallest of the kernels of the finite-dimensional semi-simple representations of $g$; (3) $s$ is the intersection of the largest nilpotency ideals of the finite-dimensional representations of $g$; (4) $s$ is the smallest ideal of $g$ such that $g/s$ is reductive; (5) $s = r \cap \mathscr{D}g$; (6) $s = [r, g]$; (7) $s$ is the intersection of the orthogonals of $g$ relative to the bilinear forms associated with the finite-dimensional representations of $g$.
