§ 4

The conventions of § 4 remain valid unless otherwise mentioned.

1. Let $ g $ be a nilpotent Lie algebra and $ p $ (resp. $ q $) the smallest integer such that $ \mathcal{C}^p g = \{0\} $ (resp. $ \mathcal{C}_q g = g $). Show that $ p = q + 1 $ and that $ \mathcal{C}_i g \supset \mathcal{C}^{p-i} g $. (Use the argument of Proposition 1.)

2. Let $ g $ be a semi-direct product of an algebra $ b $ of dimension 1 and a commutative ideal $ g' $. Let $ x \in b, x \neq 0 $, and $ u $ be the restriction of $ \mathrm{ad}_x x $ to $ g' $.
   (a) For $ g $ to be nilpotent, it is necessary and sufficient that $ u $ be nilpotent.
   (b) For the Killing form of $ g $ to be zero, it is necessary and sufficient that $ \mathrm{Tr}(u^2) = 0 $.
   (c) Deduce from (a) and (b) that there exist non-nilpotent Lie algebras whose Killing forms are zero.
   (d) Deduce from (a) that in a nilpotent Lie algebra such that $ \mathcal{C}^{p-1} g \neq \{0\} $, $ \mathcal{C}^p g = \{0\} $ it is possible that $ \mathcal{C}_i g \neq \mathcal{C}^{p-i} g $.

3. (a) Let $ g $ be a nilpotent Lie algebra, $ z $ its centre and $ b $ a non-zero ideal of $ g $. Show that $ z \cap b \neq \{0\} $. (Consider $ b $ as a $ g $-module by means of the adjoint representation.)
   (b) If in a Lie algebra $ g $ an ideal $ b $ is contained in $ \mathcal{C}_{i+1} g $ but not in $ \mathcal{C}_i g $, show that the ideals $ b \cap \mathcal{C}_k g $ are all distinct for $ 0 \leq k \leq i + 1 $. (Apply (a).)

4. Let $ g $ be a nilpotent Lie algebra.
   (a) Every subalgebra of $ g $ is subinvariant. (Use Proposition 3.)
   (b) Let $ b $ be a vector subspace of $ g $ such that $ b + \mathcal{D}g = g $. Show that the subalgebra of $ g $ generated by $ b $ is $ g $. (Apply (a) to this subalgebra. Reduce it thus to the case where $ b $ is an ideal of $ g $ and use Proposition 4 of § 1.) Deduce that the minimum number of generators of $ g $ is $ \dim g / \mathcal{D}g $.

5. (a) Show that a Lie algebra $ g $ in which every subalgebra is subinvariant is nilpotent. (Show that if $ \dim g > 1 $ every $ x \in g $ belongs to an ideal $ h \neq g $ of $ g $ which has the same property as $ g $ and hence is nilpotent by induction on the dimension of $ g $; hence $ \mathrm{ad}_x x $ is nilpotent.)
   (b) Show that if in a Lie algebra $ g $ every subalgebra distinct from $ g $ is distinct from its normalizer, $ g $ is nilpotent. (Reduce it to (a).)
