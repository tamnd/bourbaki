(b) Suppose that G is compact. Let $ \phi $ be an étale endomorphism of G. Then Ker $ \phi $ is finite, $ \phi(G) $ is of finite index in G and
$$
\frac{\mathrm{Card}(\mathrm{Ker}\,\phi)}{\mathrm{Card}(G/\phi(G))} = \mathrm{mod}\,\det L(\phi).

$$

3. Let E be the complex vector space of symmetric complex matrices with 2 rows and 2 columns. For all $ s \in \mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{C}) $, we define an automorphism $ \rho(s) $ of E by $ \rho(s).m = s.m.s^t $.
(a) Show that $ \rho $ is an analytic linear representation of $ \mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{C}) $. Identifying $ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix} \in E $ with $ (a, b, c) \in \mathbf{C}^3 $, the matrix of $ \rho \left( \begin{pmatrix} \alpha & \beta \\ \gamma & \delta \end{pmatrix} \right) $ is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha^2 & 2\alpha\beta & \beta^2 \\
\alpha\gamma & \alpha\delta + \beta\gamma & \beta\delta \\
\gamma^2 & 2\gamma\delta & \delta^2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
(b) Show that $ m \mapsto \det(m) $ is the non-degenerate quadratic form
$$
q(a, b, c) = ac - b^2
$$
on E and that $ \rho $ is a morphism of $ \mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{C}) $ onto $ \mathbf{SO}(q) $ with kernel $ \{I, -I\} $. (For the surjectivity, observe that, in the matrix of an element of $ \mathbf{SO}(q) $, the 1st column can be expressed in the form $ (\alpha^2, \alpha\gamma, \gamma^2) $ and the 3rd column in the form $ (\beta^2, \beta\delta, \delta^2) $, where $ \alpha^2\delta^2 + \beta^2\gamma^2 - 2\alpha\gamma\beta\delta = 1 $. Then observe that 2 elements of $ \mathbf{SO}(q) $ which coincide on a non-isotropic plane are equal.)
(c) Deduce that the complex Lie group $ \mathbf{SO}(3, \mathbf{C}) $ is isomorphic to the complex Lie group $ \mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{C})/\{I, -I\} $.

4. (a) Let q be a quadratic form of signature (1, 2) on $ \mathbf{R}^3 $. Let P be the set of $ m \in \mathbf{R}^3 $ such that $ q(m) > 0 $. Then P is the union of two disjoint convex cones C and $ -C $. If $ s \in \mathbf{SO}(q) $, then either $ s(C) = C $ and $ s(-C) = -C $ or $ s(C) = -C $ and $ s(-C) = C $. Let $ \mathbf{SO}^+(q) $ be the set of $ s \in \mathbf{SO}(q) $ such that $ s(C) = C $. Then $ \mathbf{SO}^+(q) $ is an open normal subgroup of $ \mathbf{SO}(q) $ of index 2 in $ \mathbf{SO}(q) $.
(b) By imitating the method of Exercise 3, define a morphism of $ \mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{R}) $ onto $ \mathbf{SO}^+(q) $ with kernel $ \{I, -I\} $. Deduce that the real Lie group $ \mathbf{SO}^+(q) $ is isomorphic to the real Lie group $ \mathbf{SL}(2, \mathbf{R})/\{I, -I\} $.
