## APPENDIX

OPERATIONS ON LINEAR REPRESENTATIONS

Let G be a group, k a commutative field, E_1, E_2, ..., E_n vector spaces over k and π_i a linear representation of G on E_i (1 ≤ i ≤ n). The mapping

$$
g \mapsto \pi_1(g) \otimes \cdots \otimes \pi_n(g)
$$

is a linear mapping of G into the vector space E_1 ⊗ ... ⊗ E_n, called the tensor product of π_1, ..., π_n and denoted by π_1 ⊗ ... ⊗ π_n.

Let E be a vector space over k and π a linear representation of G on E. For all g ∈ G, let τ(g) (resp. σ(g), ε(g)) be the unique automorphism of the algebra T(E) (resp. S(E), ∧(E)) which extends π(g) (Algebra, Chapter III, § 5, no. 2, § 6, no. 2 and § 7, no. 2). Then τ (resp. σ, ε) is a linear representation of G on T(E) (resp. S(E), ∧(E)) denoted by T(π) (resp. S(π), ∧(π)). The subrepresentation of T(π) (resp. S(π), ∧(π)) defined by T^n(E) (resp. S^n(E), ∧^n(E)) is called the n-th tensor (resp. symmetric, exterior) power of π and is denoted by T^n(π) (resp. S^n(π), ∧^n(π)). Then

$$
T^n(\pi) = \pi \otimes \pi \otimes \cdots \otimes \pi
$$

(n factors). The representations S(π), ∧(π) are quotient representations of T(π) and hence S^n(π), ∧^n(π) are quotient representations of T^n(π).
