§ 6

The conventions of § 6 remain valid unless otherwise mentioned.

¶ 1. Let $g$ be a semi-simple Lie algebra and $\rho$ a finite-dimensional representation of $g$ on $M$.

(a) If $\rho$ is simple and non-zero, $H^p(g, M) = \{0\}$ for all $p$. (Use Proposition 1 and Exercise 12 (j) of § 3.)

(b) For all $\rho$, $H^1(g, M) = \{0\}$. (If $\rho$ is simple and non-zero, apply (a). If $\rho$ is zero, use the fact that $g = \mathscr{D}g$. In the general case, argue by induction on the dimension of $\rho$: if $N$ is a sub-$g$-module of $M$ distinct from $\{0\}$ and $M$, use the exact sequence:

$$
H^1(g, N) \to H^1(g, M) \to H^1(g, M/N)
$$

established in Exercise 12 (f) of § 3.) Hence recover Remark 2 of no. 2.

(c) Deduce from (b) a proof of Theorem 2. (Use Exercise 12 (g) of § 3.) Deduce also from (b) that every derivation of $g$ is inner. (Use Exercise 12 (h) of § 3.)

(d) For all $\rho$, $H^2(g, M) = \{0\}$. (Arguing as for (b), it suffices to consider the case where $\rho = 0$. Let $c \in H^2(g, M)$. Consider, following Exercise 12 (i) of § 3, the central extension $h$ of $g$ by $M$ defined by $c$. The adjoint representation of $h$ defines a representation of $g$ on $h$. By Theorem 2, $M$ admits in $h$ a supplement which is stable under $g$ and hence the extension is trivial. Hence $c = 0$ by Exercise 12 (i) of § 3.)

(e) Deduce from (b) and (d) a proof of Theorem 5. (As in the text, it can be reduced to the case where the radical is commutative.)

2. Let $g$ be a Lie algebra, $r$ its radical and $(a_0, a_1, \ldots)$ a sequence of ideals of $g$ defined as follows: (1) $a_0 = \{0\}$; (2) $a_{i+1}/a_i$ is a maximal commutative ideal of $g/a_i$. Let $p$ be the smallest integer such that $a_p = a_{p+1} = \cdots$. Show that $r = a_p$. (Show that $g/a_p$ is semi-simple.)

3. For a Lie algebra $g$ to be semi-simple, it is necessary and sufficient that it be reductive in every Lie algebra containing $g$ as a subalgebra. (If $g$ satisfies this condition, let $M$ be a $g$-module of finite dimension over $K$. Considering $M$ as a commutative algebra, form the semi-direct product of $g$ and $M$, in which $g$ is reductive. Deduce that $M$ is semi-simple.)

4. Let $g$ be a semi-simple Lie algebra and $\rho$ a non-zero simple representation of $g$ on a finite-dimensional space $M$. Let $h$ be the corresponding semi-direct product. Show that $h = \mathscr{D}h$, that the centre of $h$ is zero and that $h$ is not the product of a semi-simple algebra and a solvable algebra.

5. Let $a$ be a Lie algebra and $r$ its radical. If $r$ has a decreasing sequence of characteristic ideals $r = r_0 \supset r_1 \supset \cdots \supset r_n = \{0\}$ such that $\dim r_i/r_{i+1} = 1$ for $0 \leq i < n$, $g$ is the product of a semi-simple algebra and a solvable algebra.
