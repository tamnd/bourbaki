¶ 12. Suppose that $K$ is a field. Let $g$ be a Lie $K$-algebra and $M$ a $g$-module. Let $C^p(g, M)$ denote the space of alternating linear mappings of $g^p$ into $M$. We write $C^0(g, M) = M$ and, for $p < 0$, $C^p(g, M) = \{0\}$. Let $C^*(g, M)$ be the direct sum of the $C^p(g, M)$. The elements of $C^*(g, M)$ are called the *cochains of $g$ with values in $M$*; those of $C^p(g, M)$ are said to be of degree $p$. For all $y \in g$, we denote by $i(y)$ the endomorphism of $C^*(g, M)$ which maps each subspace $C^p(g, M)$ into $C^{p-1}(g, M)$ and which, for $p > 0$, is given by the formula:

$$
(i(y)f)(x_1, \ldots, x_{p-1}) = f(y, x_1, \ldots, x_{p-1}).
$$

We have $i(y)^2 = 0$.

(a) The adjoint representation of $g$ and the representation of $g$ on $M$ define a representation of $g$ on the space of multilinear mappings of $g^p$ into $M$. Show that $C^p(g, M)$ is stable under this representation. Let $\theta$ be the representation of $g$ on $C^*(g, M)$ thus defined. Show that:

$$
\theta(x)i(y) - i(y)\theta(x) = i([x, y])
$$

for all $x, y$ in $g$.

(b) Show that there exists one and only one endomorphism $d$ of $C^*(g, M)$, mapping $C^p(g, M)$ into $C^{p+1}(g, M)$, such that

$$
di(y) + i(y)d = \theta(y)
$$

for all $y \in g$. (Argue by induction on the degree of the cochains.) Show that, for $f \in C^p(g, M)$:

$$
df(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_{p+1}) = \sum_{i < j} (-1)^{i+j}f([x_i, x_j], x_1, \ldots, \hat{x}_i, \ldots, \hat{x}_j, \ldots, x_{p+1})
$$
$$
+ \sum_i (-1)^{i+1}(x_i)_M f(x_1, \ldots, \hat{x}_i, \ldots, x_{p+1})
$$
