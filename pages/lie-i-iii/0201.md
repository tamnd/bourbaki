7. Suppose that K is a field. Let l be a free Lie algebra with basic family $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ and let M be an l-module.
(a) Show that $H^2(l, M) = \{0\}$, cf. Chapter I, § 3, Exercise 12. (Use Corollary 2 to Proposition 1 and part (i) of the Exercise in question.)
(b) Show that, for every family $(m_i)_{i \in I}$ of elements of M, there exists a cocycle $\phi : l \to M$ of degree 1 such that $\phi(x_i) = m_i$ and that this cocycle is unique. Deduce an exact sequence:
$$
0 \to H^0(l, M) \to M \to M^l \to H^1(l, M) \to 0.
$$
If I is finite of cardinal n and M is of finite rank over K, then
$$
\operatorname{rg} H^1(l, M) - \operatorname{rg} H^0(l, M) = (n - 1) \operatorname{rg} M.
$$

8. Suppose that K is a field. Let l be a free Lie algebra with basic family $(\bar{x}_i)_{i \in I}$, let r be an ideal of l contained in $[l, l]$ and let g = $l/r$; let $x_i$ denote the image of $\bar{x}_i$ in g.
Show the equivalence of the following properties:
(i) g is a free Lie algebra.
(ii) g admits $(x_i)$ as basic family (i.e. $r = \{0\}$).
(iii) For every g-module M, $H^2(g, M) = \{0\}$.
(iv) If g operates trivially on K, then $H^2(g, K) = \{0\}$.
(The implications (ii) \Rightarrow (i) and (iii) \Rightarrow (iv) are obvious and (i) \Rightarrow (iii) follows from Exercise 7. To prove that (iv) \Rightarrow (ii) it suffices to show that $r = [l, r]$, cf. Exercise 5; otherwise take a hyperplane h of r containing $[l, r]$ and note that the extension $r/h \to l/h \to l/r = g$ is essential.)

9. Let $g = \bigoplus_{n \geq 1} g_n$ be a graded Lie algebra. If h is a graded subalgebra of g such that $g = h + [g, g]$, show that $h = g$. If $(x_i)$ is a family of homogeneous elements of g, deduce that $(x_i)$ is a generating family if and only if the images of the $x_i$ in $g/[g, g]$ generate $g/[g, g]$.
If K is a field on which g operates trivially and $H^2(g, K) = \{0\}$, show that the family $(x_i)$ is basic if and only if the images of the $x_i$ in $g/[g, g]$ form a basis of this vector space. (Use Exercise 8.)†

10. Let l be a free Lie algebra admitting a finite basic family with n elements and let u be a surjective endomorphism of l. Show that u is an isomorphism. (Write $\operatorname{gr}^n(l) = \mathcal{C}^n/\mathcal{C}^{n+1}$ and denote by $\operatorname{gr}^n(u)$ the endomorphism of $\operatorname{gr}^n(l)$ derived from u; note that $\operatorname{gr}^n(u)$ is surjective; as $\operatorname{gr}^n(l)$ is a free K-module of finite rank, deduce that $\operatorname{gr}^n(u)$ is bijective since the kernel of u is contained in $\bigcap_n \mathcal{C}^n l$, which is $\{0\}$.)

† When g is not graded, we do not know whether the condition
"H^2(g, M) = \{0\} for every g-module M"
implies that g is a free Lie algebra.
