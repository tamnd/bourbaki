8. Let g be a Lie algebra. An ideal h of g is called minimal if h ≠ {0} and every ideal of g contained in h is equal to {0} or h.
(a) Every simple ideal of g is minimal.
(b) Let h be a minimal ideal of g and r the radical of g. Then either h ⊂ r, in which case h is abelian, or h ∩ r = {0}, in which case h is simple. (Use the fact that the derived ideal of a Lie algebra and the simple components of a semi-simple Lie algebra are characteristic ideals.)

9. For a Lie algebra g to be reductive, it is necessary and sufficient that its centre c be equal to its largest nilpotent ideal. (If the condition holds, let r be the radical of g; Dr is contained in the centre of r, hence r is nilpotent and hence r = c.)

10. Let g be a Lie algebra such that the conditions x ∈ g, y ∈ g, [[x, y], y] = 0 imply [x, y] = 0. Show that g is reductive. (Show that the radical r of g is commutative using Exercise 3 of § 5. Then show that [g, r] = 0.)
