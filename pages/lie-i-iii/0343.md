λ: S' → Ġ be the canonical morphism. Then σ(F) ⊂ P and hence there exists one and only one R-analytic morphism γ of G into Ġ such that γ ∘ π = λ ∘ σ. If ψ: Ġ → H denotes the morphism derived from φ* when passing to the quotient, then

$$(φ ∘ γ) ∘ π = ψ ∘ (λ ∘ σ) = φ^* ∘ σ = φ ∘ π$$

whence ψ ∘ γ = φ. Clearly L(ψ), and hence ψ, are determined uniquely by the equality ψ ∘ γ = φ. Thus we have proved that the ordered pair (Ḡ, γ) has properties (i) and (iii).

(b) We pass now to the general case. Let F be the identity component of G, M = G/F and i: F → G and p: G → M be the canonical morphisms. We apply part (a) of the proof to F. We obtain an ordered pair (Ḟ, δ). For all g ∈ G, Int g|F = ω'(g) is an automorphism of F. By the universal property of Ḟ, there exists one and only one automorphism ω(g) of the complex Lie group Ḟ such that δ ∘ ω'(g) = ω(g) ∘ δ. Clearly ω is a morphism of G into Aut(Ḣ). If g ∈ G and f ∈ F, then

$$δ(gf g^{-1}) = (\delta ∘ ω'(g))(f) = (\omega(g) ∘ \delta)(f) = \omega(g)(\delta(f)).$$

If f ∈ F, then δ ∘ (Int_F f) = (Int_Ḟ δ(f)) ∘ δ and Int_Ḟ δ(f) is an automorphism of the complex Lie group Ḟ; hence Int_Ḟ δ(f) = ω(f).

Hence Lemma 7 can be applied, which gives a diagram

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
F & \xrightarrow{i} & G & \xrightarrow{p} & M \\
\downarrow{\delta} & & \downarrow{\gamma} & & \downarrow{\mathrm{Id}} \\
Ḟ & \xrightarrow{\tilde{i}} & Ġ & \xrightarrow{\tilde{p}} & M.
\end{array}
$$

Let Ḟ be identified with a normal subgroup of Ġ by means of ī. The group Ġ is generated by Ḟ and γ(G); hence the automorphisms of Ḟ defined by the elements of Ġ are automorphisms of the complex Lie group structure. By § 1, no. 9, Proposition 18, there exists one and only one complex Lie group structure on Ġ such that Ḟ is an open Lie subgroup of Ġ. Henceforth Ġ will have this structure. As δ is R-analytic, γ is R-analytic.

The ordered pair (Ḡ, γ) has property (iii) of the proposition. We show that it has property (i). Let H be a complex Lie group and ψ an R-analytic morphism of G into H. There exists a C-analytic morphism η of Ḟ into H such that η ∘ δ = φ|F. Let g ∈ G. The mappings

$$f \mapsto \eta(\omega(g)f), \quad f \mapsto \phi(g)\eta(f)\phi(g)^{-1}$$

of Ḟ into H are C-analytic morphisms; they coincide on δ(F), for, if f' ∈ F, then

$$\phi(g)\eta(\delta(f'))\phi(g)^{-1} = \phi(g)\phi(f')\phi(g)^{-1} = \phi(gf'g^{-1})$$
$$= \eta(\delta(gf'g^{-1})) = \eta(\omega(g)\delta(f'));$$
