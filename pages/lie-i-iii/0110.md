6. Let $g$ be a nilpotent Lie algebra and $a$ a commutative ideal of $g$. The following conditions are equivalent: (a) $a$ is a maximal commutative ideal of $g$; (b) $a$ is a maximal commutative subalgebra of $g$; (c) $a$ is equal to its centralizer $a'$ in $g$. (The implications not (a) $\Rightarrow$ not (b) $\Rightarrow$ not (c) are obvious. If $a' \neq a$, there exists by Proposition 1 an ideal $a''$ of $g$ such that $a \subset a'' \subset a'$ and $\dim a''/a = 1$. Then $a'' = a + Kx$, hence $[a'', a''] \subset [a, a] + [x, a] = \{0\}$ and hence (a) is false.)

(a) Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $K$, $g$ a Lie algebra of nilpotent endomorphisms of $V$ and $(V_r)_{0 \leq r < n}$ a decreasing sequence of vector subspaces of $V$, with $V_0 = V, V_n = \{0\}$ and $g(V_r) \subset V_{r+1}$ for $0 \leq r < n$. Show by induction on $i$ that $(\mathcal{D}^i g)(V_r) \subset V_{r+2^i}$. If $\dim V \geq 2^i$, the subspace of elements of $V$ annihilated by $\mathcal{D}^i g$ is of dimension $\geq 2^i$. If $\dim V \leq 2^i$, $\mathcal{D}^i g = \{0\}$.

(b) Let $g$ be a nilpotent Lie algebra and $i$ an integer $\geq 0$. If $\dim \mathcal{D}^i g > 2^i + 1$ the centre of $\mathcal{D}^i g$ is of dimension $\geq 2^i$. If $\dim \mathcal{D}^i g \leq 2^i + 1$, $\mathcal{D}^i g$ is commutative. (Apply (a) to the restrictions to $\mathcal{D}^i g$ of the ad$_g$ $x, x \in g$.)

(c) Let $g$ be a nilpotent Lie algebra and $i$ an integer $\geq 0$. If $\mathcal{D}^i g$ is not commutative, $\mathcal{D}^i g/\mathcal{D}^{i+1} g$ is of dimension $\geq 2^i + 1$. (Reduce it, by passing to the quotient, to the case where $\dim \mathcal{D}^{i+1} g = 1$ and use (b).)

8. Let $g$ be a Lie algebra, $m$ an ideal of codimension 1, $x$ an element of $g$ not belonging to $m$ and $z \in g$.

(a) Show that the linear mapping $D$ which reduce to 0 on $m$ and maps $x$ to $z$ is a derivation if $z$ belongs to the centralizer $a$ of $m$ in $g$.

(b) Let $q$ be the largest integer such that $a \subset \mathcal{C}^q g$. Show that if further $z \notin \mathcal{C}^{q+1} g$, $D$ is not an inner derivation of $g$.

(c) Deduce from (a) and (b) and Exercise 7 (c) that if $g$ is a nilpotent Lie algebra of dimension $> 1$, the vector space of inner derivations of $g$ is of co-dimension $\geq 2$ in the space of all derivations of $g$.
