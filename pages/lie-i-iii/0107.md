(c) Show that, for all $y \in g$,

$$
d \theta(y) = \theta(y)d.
$$

(Show first, using (2) and (3), that $d \theta(y) - \theta(y)d$ commutes with every $i(x)$. Then argue by induction on the degree of the cochains.)

(d) Show that $d^2 = 0$. (Show first, using (3) and (4), that $d^2$ commutes with every $i(x)$. The argue by induction on the degree of the cochains.)

(e) The restriction of $d$ to $C^p(g, M)$ has a kernel $Z^p(g, M)$ whose elements are called *cocycles* of degree $p$ with values in $M$. The restriction of $d$ to $C^{p-1}(g, M)$ has image $B^p(g, M)$, whose elements are called *coboundaries* of degree $p$ with values in $M$. Then $B^p(g, M) \subset Z^p(g, M)$. The quotient space

$$
Z^p(g, M)/B^p(g, M) = H^p(g, M)
$$

is called the *cohomology space* of $g$ of degree $p$ with values in $M$. The direct sum of the $H^p(g, M)$ is denoted by $H^*(g, M)$. Show that $H^0(g, M)$ is identified with the set of invariants of $M$. Let $\phi$ be a homomorphism of the $g$-module $M$ into the $G$-module $N$. For all $f \in C^p(g, M)$, $\phi \circ f \in C^p(g, N)$, so that $\phi$ can be extended to a K-linear mapping $\phi': C^*(g, M) \to C^*(g, N)$. Show that $\phi' \circ d = d \circ \phi'$. Deduce that $\phi'$ defines a homomorphism

$$
\tilde{\phi}: H^*(g, M) \to H^*(g, N)
$$

which is said to be associated with $\phi$.

(f) Let $L$ be a sub-$g$-module of $M$ and $N$ the quotient $g$-module $M/L$. The canonical homomorphisms $L \xrightarrow{i} M \xrightarrow{p} N$ define homomorphisms

$$
\begin{array}{cccccc}
C^*(g, L) & \xrightarrow{i'} & C^*(g, M) & \xrightarrow{p'} & C^*(g, N), \\
H^n(g, L) & \xrightarrow{i^n} & H^n(g, M) & \xrightarrow{p^n} & H^n(g, N).
\end{array}
$$

Show that $i'$ is injective and has image the kernel of $p'$ and that $p'$ is surjective. For all $c \in H^n(g, N)$, let $z \in Z^n(g, N)$ be a representative of $c$ and $a \in C^n(g, M)$ be such that $p'(a) = z$; show that $da \in Z^{n+1}(g, L)$ and that its class in $H^{n+1}(g, L)$ depends only on $c$; denote this class by $\delta^n c$. Show that the sequence of homomorphisms:

$$
\begin{array}{cccccccccc}
0 & \longrightarrow & H^0(g, L) & \xrightarrow{i^0} & H^0(g, M) & \xrightarrow{p^0} & H^0(g, N) & \xrightarrow{\delta^0} \\
& & \longrightarrow & H^1(g, L) & \xrightarrow{i^1} & H^1(g, M) & \xrightarrow{p^1} & H^1(g, N) & \xrightarrow{\delta^1} & \cdots
\end{array}
$$

is an exact sequence.
