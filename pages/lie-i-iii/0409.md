29. Let G be a finite-dimensional complex connected Lie group, G_0 the underlying real Lie group and H an integral subgroup of G_0. Show that there exists a smallest integral subgroup H^* of G containing H. Give an example where H is closed in G_0 but H^* is not closed in G (take G = \mathbf{C}^2/\mathbf{Z}^2).

30. Let G be a compact connected complex Lie group and hence of the form \mathbf{C}^n/D, where D is a discrete subgroup of \mathbf{C}^n of rank 2n.
(a) Show that every holomorphic differential 1-form $\omega$ on G is invariant. (Let $\pi : \mathbf{C}^n \to G$ be the canonical morphism. Let $\zeta_1, \ldots, \zeta_n$ be the coordinate functions on $\mathbf{C}^n$. Then $\pi^*(\omega)$ is of the form $\sum_j a_j d\zeta_j$, where the $a_j$ are holomorphic functions on $\mathbf{C}^n$ which are invariant under D and hence constant.)
(b) Let $G' = \mathbf{C}^n/D'$, where D' is a discrete subgroup of $\mathbf{C}^n$ of rank 2n. Show that every *analytic manifold* isomorphism $u : G \to G'$ is of the form $s \mapsto v(s) + a'$, where v is a *Lie group* isomorphism and $a' \in G'$. (Let $\pi' : \mathbf{C}^n \to G'$ be the canonical morphism. There exists an isomorphism of analytic manifolds $\tilde{u} : \mathbf{C}^n \to \mathbf{C}^n$ such that $u \circ \pi = \pi' \circ \tilde{u}$. For every holomorphic differential 1-form $\omega'$ on $G'$, $u^*({\pi'}^*(\omega'))$ is a 1-form on $\mathbf{C}^n$ which is invariant under translations by (a). Deduce that $\tilde{u}$ is an affine mapping.)

1. Let G be a Lie group and $\phi : U \to G$ an exponential mapping such that $\mathbf{Z}U \subset U$ and $\phi(rx) = \phi(x)^r$ for all $x \in U$ and all $r \in \mathbf{Z}$. If $p > 0$, $\phi$ is an analytic isomorphism of U onto $\phi(U)$. (If $\phi(x) = \phi(y)$, then $\phi(p^n x) = \phi(p^n y)$ for all $n \in \mathbf{N}$ and hence $x = y$. Let W be an open neighbourhood of 0 in L(G) such that $\phi^{-1}$ is analytic on $\phi(W)$. For all $s \in \phi(U)$, there exist $n \in \mathbf{N}$ and a neighbourhood V of s in $\phi(U)$ such that $t \in V \Rightarrow t^{p^n} \in \phi(W)$.)

2. Let G be a Lie group, $\phi : U \to G$ an exponential mapping with the properties of Proposition 3, V a neighbourhood of 0 in U such that $\mathbf{Z}V \subset V$ and $\psi : V \to G$ a tangent mapping at 0 to $\phi$ such that $\psi(nx) = \psi(x)^n$ for all $n \in V$ and all $n \in \mathbf{Z}$. If $p > 0$, then $\psi = \phi | V$. (Give L(G) a norm. Let $x \in V$. Then $p^n x$ tends to 0 as n tends to $+\infty$, hence there exists $\alpha_n > 0$ such that $\alpha_n$ tends to 0 and $\| (\phi^{-1} \circ \psi)(p^n x) - p^n x \| \leq \alpha_n \| p^n x \|$. But $\psi(p^n x) = \psi(x)^{p^n}$, whence $\| (\phi^{-1} \circ \psi)(x) - x \| \leq \alpha_n \| x \|$.)

3. Let U be the set of invertible elements of A and $U' = 1 + m \subset U$.
(a) Show that $U' \subset U_f$. (If $x = 1 + y$ with $y \in m$, then $x^{p^n}$ tends to 1 as n tends to $+\infty$ by the binomial theorem.)
(b) Show that $U_f$ is the set of elements of U whose image in $A/m$ is a root of unity. (Use (a).) Hence recover the fact that $U = U_f$ when K is locally compact (A/m is then finite).
