§ 8

We suppose that the residual characteristic $ p $ of the field $ K $ is >0. We denote by $ \mathfrak{o}_K $ the ring of the valuation $ v $ of $ K $.

1. (a) An element $ \pi $ of $ K $ is called admissible if $ v(\pi) = 0 $ and $ \pi^{p-1}/p \equiv -1 \pmod{\pi \mathfrak{o}_K} $.
(Note that $ \pi^{p-1}/p \in \mathfrak{o}_K $ since $ v(\pi) = 0 $.)
Show that there exist fields $ K $ satisfying the conditions of the paragraph and containing an admissible element (adjoin to $ \mathbf{Q}_p $ a $ (p-1) $-th root of $ -p $).

(b) Let $ \pi $ be an admissible element of $ K $. Consider the following formal power series with coefficients in $ K $:

$$
e_{\pi}(U) = \frac{1}{\pi} e(\pi U) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \pi^{n-1} U^n / n!,
$$

$$
l_{\pi}(U) = \frac{1}{\pi} l(\pi U) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-\pi)^{n-1} U^n / n.
$$

Show that these series are inverses of one another and that their coefficients belong to $ \mathfrak{o}_K $ (use Lemma 2).

(c) Let $ \{U, V\} $ be a set with two elements and let $ H(U, V) $ be the Hausdorff series. We write

$$
H_{\pi}(U, V) = \frac{1}{\pi} H(\pi U, \pi V).
$$

Then $ H_{\pi}(U, V) \in \hat{L}_K(\{U, V\}) $.

Show that

$$
H_{\pi}(U, V) = l_{\pi}(e_{\pi}(U) + e_{\pi}(V) + \pi e_{\pi}(U) \cdot e_{\pi}(V)).
$$

Deduce that $ H_{\pi} \in \hat{L}_{\mathfrak{o}_K}(\{U, V\}) $, i.e. that the coefficients of $ H_{\pi} $ belong to $ \mathfrak{o}_K $; hence another proof of Proposition 1.

(d) Let $ \tilde{e}_{\pi} $, $ \tilde{l}_{\pi} $ and $ \tilde{H}_{\pi} $ denote the series obtained by reducing $ e_{\pi} $, $ l_{\pi} $ and $ H_{\pi} $ modulo $ \pi \mathfrak{o}_K $; their coefficients belong to the ring $ \mathfrak{o}_K/\pi \mathfrak{o}_K $.

Show that $ \tilde{l}_{\pi}(U) = U - U^p $ (note that $ v_p(n) < (n-1)\theta $ if $ n \neq 1,\ p $). Deduce that

$$
\tilde{e}_{\pi}(U) = U + U^p + \cdots + U^{p^n} + \cdots
$$

and that

$$
\tilde{H}_{\pi}(U, V) = \tilde{e}_{\pi}(U) + \tilde{e}_{\pi}(V) - (\tilde{e}_{\pi}(U) + \tilde{e}_{\pi}(V))^p,
$$

or also

$$
\tilde{H}_{\pi}(U, V) = U + V - \Lambda_p \left( \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} U^{p^n}, \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} V^{p^n} \right),
$$

where $ \Lambda_p $ is defined by the formula

$$
\Lambda_p(U, V) = (U + V)^p - U^p - V^p, \quad \text{cf. Chapter I, § 1, Exercise 19.}
$$
