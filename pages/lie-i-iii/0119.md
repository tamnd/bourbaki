13. Let $g$ be a Lie Algebra and $\mathfrak{D}$ the Lie algebra of derivations of $g$. For $g$ to be characteristically nilpotent, it is necessary and sufficient that $\mathfrak{D}$ be nilpotent and that $\dim g > 1$. (To see that the condition is sufficient, write $g$ as a sum of subspaces $g^\lambda$, applying Exercise 12 to the identity mapping of $\mathfrak{D}$. Show that $[g^\lambda, g^\mu] \subset g^{\lambda+\mu}$ and that each $g^\lambda$ is an ideal of $g$. Deduce that $g^\lambda$ is commutative for $\lambda \neq 0$. Using again the fact that $\mathfrak{D}$ is nilpotent, show that $g = g^0$ if $\dim g > 1$. Otherwise $g = g^0 \times h$, where $h$ is commutative. Show first that $\dim h \leq 1$. If $\dim h = 1$, note that there would exist a derivation D of $g$ such that $D(g^0) = \{0\}$ and $D(h)$ is contained in the centre of $g^0$ (\S 4, Exercise 8 (a)).)

14. Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $K$ and $z$ an endomorphism of $V$. We adopt the notation $z_q^p$ of Exercise 3 of \S 3. An endomorphism $z'$ of $V$ is called a *replica* of $z$ if for all $p$ and $q$ every zero of $z_q^p$ is a zero of $z_q^{p'}$. Show that if $\operatorname{Tr}(zz') = 0$ for every replica $z'$ of $z$, then $z$ is nilpotent. (Use the proof of Lemma 3. In the notation of this proof, prove in particular that $t$ is a replica of $z$.)

15. Let $K$ be a field of characteristic 2. The identity representation of the nilpotent Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(2, K)$ on $K^2$ defines a semi-direct product $h$ of $\mathfrak{sl}(2, K)$ by $K^2$. Show that $h$ is solvable but that $\mathfrak{D}h$ is not nilpotent. Deduce that $h$ admits no faithful linear representation by triangular matrices. Show also that the conclusions of Exercise 5 are false.

16. Let $g$ be a Lie algebra over an arbitrary field $K$, $A$ a commutative associative algebra over $K$ and $g' = g \otimes_K A$, which can be considered as a Lie algebra over $K$.

(a) If $D$ is a derivation of $A$, show that there exists one and only one derivation $D'$ of $g'$ such that $D'(x \otimes a) = x \otimes Da$ for $x \in g, a \in A$.

(b) Let $p$ be a prime number, $G$ a cyclic group of order $p$ and $s$ a generator of $G$. Suppose that $K$ is of characteristic $p$ and henceforth let $A$ be the algebra of $G$ over $K$. Show that there exists one and only one derivation $D$ of $A$ such that $D(s^k) = ks^{k-1}$ for all $k \in \mathbf{Z}$. Show that the $K$-linear combinations of the $x \otimes (s-1)^k$ ($k = 1, 2, \ldots, p-1, x \in g$) form a solvable ideal $r$ of $g'$ and that $g'/r$ is isomorphic to $g$.

(c) Take $g$ to be simple (cf. \S 6, no. 2, Definition 2). Then $r$ is the radical of $g'$ but is not a characteristic ideal. (Observe that $D(x \otimes (s-1)) = 1 \otimes x$.)[^1]

17. Let $g$ be a Lie algebra. Suppose that for every simple $g$-module $M$ of finite dimension over $K$ the $x_M$ are permutable with one another. Show that $g$ is solvable. (Observe that $\mathfrak{D}g$ is contained in the nilpotent radical and hence is solvable.)

[^1] This result, unedited, was communicated to us by N. Jacobson.
