(c) Verify that the following multiplication table defines a (solvable) Lie algebra $g$ of dimension 5:

$$
[x_1, x_2] = x_5, \quad [x_1, x_3] = x_3, \quad [x_1, x_4] = -x_4, \quad [x_3, x_4] = x_5
$$

and the other $[x_i, x_j]$ are zero. Show that for this Lie algebra

$$
\mathcal{C}^\infty g = Kx_3 + Kx_4 + Kx_5
$$

but that there exists no supplementary subalgebra of $\mathcal{C}^\infty g$ in $g$.

¶ 13. Let $g$ be a Lie algebra and $\mathfrak{z}$ the centralizer of $\mathcal{C}^\infty g$ in $g$. If $\mathfrak{z} \not\subset \mathcal{C}^\infty g$, $g$ has non-zero centre. (Write $g = \mathcal{C}^\infty g + \mathfrak{t}$, where $\mathfrak{t}$ is a nilpotent subalgebra of $g$ (Exercise 12). Let $g_1 = \mathfrak{z} + \mathfrak{t}$. Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be a nilpotent subalgebra such that $g_1 = \mathcal{C}^\infty g_1 + \mathfrak{h}$. Show that $g = \mathcal{C}^\infty g + \mathfrak{h}$ and that $\mathcal{C}^\infty g_1 \subset \mathfrak{z} \cap \mathcal{C}^\infty g$. Let $y$ be an element of $\mathfrak{z}$ not belonging to $\mathcal{C}^\infty g$. Write $y = x + x'$ where $x \in \mathfrak{h}, x' \in \mathcal{C}^\infty g_1$; then $x \in \mathfrak{z}$ and $x \neq 0$ and hence $\mathfrak{z} \cap \mathfrak{h}$ is a non-zero ideal of $\mathfrak{h}$; using Exercise 3 (a), deduce that there exists a non-zero element of $g$ permutable with $\mathcal{C}^\infty g$ and $\mathfrak{h}$.)

¶ 14. Let $g$ be a Lie algebra and $\mathfrak{b}$ a subinvariant subalgebra of $g$ such that the centralizer $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{b})$ of $\mathfrak{b}$ in $g$ is zero.

(a) The centralizer $\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b})$ of $\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$ in $g$ is contained in $\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$. (If $\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}) \not\subset \mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$, $\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}) \not\subset \mathfrak{b}$ by Exercise 13; $\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$ is an ideal of $g$ (\S 1, Exercise 14); let $c = \mathfrak{b} + \mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b})$; $c$ is a subalgebra, $\mathfrak{b}$ is subinvariant in $c$; $\mathfrak{b}$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak{b}_1 \subset c$, where $\mathfrak{b} \neq \mathfrak{b}_1$; let $y \in \mathfrak{b}_1, y \notin \mathfrak{b}$; $y = x + x'$, where $x \in \mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b})$, $x' \in \mathfrak{b}$; then $x \in \mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}) \cap \mathfrak{b}_1, x \notin \mathfrak{b}$; $\mathfrak{d} = Kx + \mathfrak{b}$ is a subalgebra; $\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b} = \mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$ because $c \cap \mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}) \subset \mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$, whence $\mathcal{C}^k \mathfrak{b} \subset \mathcal{C}^k \mathfrak{b}$ for all $k$; $\mathfrak{z}(\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}) \cap \mathfrak{b} \not\subset \mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$ and hence the centre of $\mathfrak{d}$ is non-zero by Exercise 13; a fortiori, $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{b}) \neq \{0\}$, which is absurd.)

(b) Deduce from (a) that, if $\mathfrak{D}$ denotes the Lie algebra of derivations of $\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$ and $a$ the centre of $\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$, then $\dim g \leq \dim \mathfrak{D} + \dim a$ (let $g$ operate on $\mathcal{C}^\infty \mathfrak{b}$ by the adjoint representation).
