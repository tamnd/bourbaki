12. Let $ G $ be a finite-dimensional connected real or complex Lie group and $ \alpha $ a left invariant differential form of degree $ p $ on $ G $. For $ \alpha $ to be right invariant too, it is necessary and sufficient that $ d\alpha = 0 $. (Observe that the condition for right invariance can be written

$$
\wedge^p(t\mathrm{Ad}(s)) . \alpha(e) = \alpha(e)
$$

for all $ s \in G $ and hence it is equivalent to the condition

$$
\sum_{j=1}^f \langle \alpha(e), u_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge u_{j-1} \wedge [u, u_j] \wedge u_{j+1} \wedge \cdots \wedge u_p \rangle = 0
$$

for all $ u, u_1, \ldots, u_p $ in $ L(G) $.) In particular, the left and right invariant differential forms of degree 1 on $ G $ form a vector space of dimension

$$
\dim L(G) - \dim[L(G), L(G)].
$$
