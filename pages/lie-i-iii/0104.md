¶ 4. Suppose that K is a field. Let g be a Lie K-algebra.

(a) Let M and N be g-modules, where N is finite-dimensional over K. Let (f_j)_{1 \leq j \leq n} be a basis of N over K. If there are elements e_1, \ldots, e_n of M, not all zero such that $ \sum_{i=1}^n e_i \otimes f_i $ is invariant in M \otimes N, then the subspace M_1 of M generated by the e_i is stable under the x_M; if further N is simple the representation of g on M_1 is dual to the representation of g on N.

(b) Let M_1, M_2, N, N* be g-modules of finite dimension over K. Suppose that M_1 and M_2 are simple and that the representations of g on N and N* are dual. For the g-module M_1 to be isomorphic to a sub-g-module of M_2 \otimes N, it is necessary and sufficient that M_2 be isomorphic to a sub-g-module of M_1 \otimes N*. (Use Proposition 4; consider the representation of g on M_1^* \otimes M_2 \otimes N and apply (a) to the dual representation of the latter.)

¶ 5. Suppose that K is a field of characteristic 0. Let V be a finite-dimensional vector K-space, g = sl(V), U the enveloping algebra of g, U_n the set of elements of filtration \leq n in U, U^n the set of images in U of homogeneous symmetric tensors of order n over g and (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_m) a basis of the vector space g. Let W_s = \bigotimes^s V. The identity representation of g defines a representation of g on W_s which extends to a homomorphism \pi_s of U into the algebra M_s = \mathcal{L}(W_s).

(a) Let M'_s be the subspace of M_s generated by the elements of the form \beta_1 \otimes \beta_2 \otimes \cdots \otimes \beta_s, where \beta_i \in \mathcal{L}(V) and \beta_i = 1 for at least one i. Let z be an element of U_s and z_0 = \sum_{1 \leq i_1, \ldots, i_s \leq m} a_{i_1 \ldots i_s} \alpha_{i_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \alpha_{i_s} its component in U^s, where the a_{i_1 \ldots i_s} are symmetric with respect to permutation of indices. Show that
$$
\pi_s(z) \equiv s! \sum_{1 \leq i_1, \ldots, i_s \leq m} a_{i_1 \ldots i_s} \alpha_{i_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \alpha_{i_s} \pmod{M'_s}.
$$

(b) Deduce from (a) that for all z \in U there exists a finite-dimensional representation \pi of U such that \pi(z) \neq 0. (For a sequel to this exercise, cf. § 7, Exercise 3.)
