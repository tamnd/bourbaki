Recall (§ 3, no. 11, Corollary 5 to Proposition 41) that if a is a finite-dimensional algebra, Aut(a) is a Lie subalgebra of GL(a) and that L(Aut(a)) is the Lie algebra of derivations of a.

Definition 2. Let a be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. Let Ad(a) or Int(a) denote the integral subgroup of Aut(a) with Lie algebra ad(a). The elements of this group are called inner automorphisms of a.

By transport of structure, ad(a) is invariant under Aut(a) and hence Int(a) is normal in Aut(a). By § 4, no. 4, Corollary 1 to Proposition 8 and the fact that Int(a) is connected, the elements of Int(a) are the finite products of automorphisms of the form exp ad x where x ∈ a. In general, Int(a) is not a Lie subgroup of Aut(a) (Exercise 14).

### 3. PASSAGE FROM LIE ALGEBRAS TO LIE GROUPS

Theorem 3. (i) If L is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra, there exists a simply connected Lie group G such that L(G) is isomorphic to L.

(ii) Let G_1 and G_2 be two connected Lie groups, with G_1 simply connected. Let f be an isomorphism of L(G_1) onto L(G_2), φ the morphism of G_1 into G_2 such that L(φ) = f and N the kernel of φ. Then N is a discrete subgroup of the centre of G_1 and the morphism of G_1/N into G_2 derived from φ is a Lie group isomorphism. If G_2 is simply connected, φ is an isomorphism.
