(a) Let V be the vector space K[X]. Let D be the differential operator with respect to X on V and M the operator of multiplication by X on V. Show that if K is of characteristic 0 the mapping

$$
\alpha x_1 + \beta x_2 + \gamma x_3 \mapsto \alpha D + \rho M + \gamma
$$

is an infinite-dimensional simple representation $ \rho $ of g on V.

(b) If K is of characteristic $ p > 0 $, the ideal $ (X^p) $ of K[X] is stable under $ \rho(g) $. Under the quotient representation of g on $ K[X]/(X^p) $, no line is stable.

18. Let g be a 7-dimensional vector space over K with a basis $ (e_i)_{1 \leq i \leq 7} $. An alternating bracket is defined on g by the formulae:

(1)
$$
[e_i, e_j] = \alpha_{ij} e_{i+j} \quad (1 \leq i < j \leq 7, i + j \leq 7)
$$
and all the other brackets $ [e_i, e_j] $ are zero for $ i < j $.

(a) For the Jacobi identity to hold, it is necessary and sufficient that:

(2)
$$
-\alpha_{23}\alpha_{15} + \alpha_{13}\alpha_{24} = 0
$$
(3)
$$
\alpha_{12}\alpha_{34} - \alpha_{24}\alpha_{16} + \alpha_{14}\alpha_{25} = 0.
$$

(b) Suppose henceforth that all the $ \alpha_{ij} $ are $ \neq 0 $. Show that the ideals $ \mathcal{C}^2g, \mathcal{C}^3g, \mathcal{C}^4g, \mathcal{C}^5g, \mathcal{C}^6g $ admit the following bases: $ (e_3, e_4, e_5, e_6, e_7) $, $ (e_4, e_5, e_6, e_7) $, $ (e_5, e_6, e_7) $, $ (e_6, e_7) $, $ (e_7) $. Show that the centralizer $ h $ of $ \mathcal{C}^6g $ admits the basis $ (e_2, e_3, e_4, e_5, e_6, e_7) $.

(c) Let $ (e'_i)_{1 \leq i \leq 7} $ be another basis of g such that $ \mathcal{C}^6g $ admits the basis $ (e'_7) $, $ \mathcal{C}^5g $ admits the basis $ (e'_6, e'_7) $, $ \ldots $, $ h $ admits the basis $ (e'_2, e'_3, e'_4, e'_5, e'_6, e'_7) $. Show that
$$
[e'_i, e'_j] = \alpha'_{ij} e'_{i+j} + \beta e'_{i+j+1} + \gamma e'_{i+j+2} + \cdots + \lambda e'_7,
$$
where
$$
\alpha'_{14}\alpha'_{25}\alpha'_{16}^{-1}\alpha'_{24}^{-1} = \alpha_{14}\alpha_{25}\alpha_{16}^{-1}\alpha_{24}^{-1}.
$$

(d) Deduce that there exists, if K is infinite, an infinity of non-isomorphic nilpotent Lie algebras of dimension 7 over K and that there exist complex nilpotent Lie algebras which cannot be derived from real nilpotent Lie algebras by extending the base field from $ \mathbf{R} $ to $ \mathbf{C} $.

19. (a) Let g be a Lie algebra. Let $ \mathfrak{D} $ be the Lie algebra of derivations of g. Characteristic ideals $ g^{[k]} $ of g are defined inductively as follows: $ g^{[0]} = g $ and $ g^{[k+1]} $ is the subspace of g generated by the Dx ($ D \in \mathfrak{D}, x \in g^{[k]} $). The following conditions are equivalent: (1) every derivation of g is nilpotent; (2) $ g^{[k]} = \{0\} $ for k sufficiently large; (3) the holomorph of g is nilpotent. If they are fulfilled, g is said to be *characteristically nilpotent*. Such an algebra is nilpotent.
