COROLLARY 5. Suppose that $ \rho $ and $ \rho' $ are finite-dimensional. For $ \rho $ and $ \rho' $ to be equivalent, it is necessary and sufficient that $ L(\rho) $ and $ L(\rho') $ be equivalent.

This is a special case of Corollary 4.

COROLLARY 6. Suppose that $ G $ is finite-dimensional. Let $ t \in U(G) $. For $ L_t $ (resp. $ R_t $) to be right (resp. left) invariant, it is necessary and sufficient that $ t $ belong to the centre of $ U(G) $.

For $ L_t $ (resp. $ R_t $) to be right (resp. left) invariant, it is necessary and sufficient that $ \varepsilon_g * t = t * \varepsilon_g $ for all $ g \in G $, that is that $ (\mathrm{Int}\, g)_* t = t $. There exists an integer $ n $ such that $ t \in U_n(G) $. By Corollary 3 and Proposition 45 of § 3, no. 12, $ (\mathrm{Int}\, g)_* t = t $ for all $ g \in G $ if and only if $ [a, t] = 0 $ for all $ a \in L(G) $, that is if and only if $ t $ commutes with $ U(G) $.

### 6. NORMAL INTEGRAL SUBGROUPS

Lemma 3. Let $ G $ be a Lie group, $ H_1 $ and $ H_2 $ integral subgroups whose topology admits a countable base and $ g \in G $. Then

$$
g H_1 g^{-1} = H_2 \Leftrightarrow (\mathrm{Ad}\, g)(L(H_1)) = L(H_2).
$$

$ \mathrm{Ad}\, g = T_e(\mathrm{Int}\, g) $. Hence, by transport of structure, $ (\mathrm{Int}\, g)(H_1) $ has Lie algebra $ (\mathrm{Ad}\, g)(L(H_1)) $. As $ H_1 $ and $ H_2 $ have countable bases, to say that the sets $ H_2 $ and $ (\mathrm{Int}\, g)(H_1) $ are equal amounts to saying that the integral subgroups $ H_2 $ and $ (\mathrm{Int}\, g)(H_1) $ are equal (no. 2, Corollary 3 to Proposition 3). Then the lemma follows from Theorem 2 (i).
